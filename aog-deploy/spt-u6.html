School Fields and World Pitches

Origins

People kicked and carried balls for hundreds of years before anyone wrote a rule book. English towns played rough folk games in their streets. American college students argued over rules before each game. In this unit you will see how those games became two sports with written codes: American football and soccer.

Each sport got its shape from people who met and wrote things down. Walter Camp and the college rules meetings made the gridiron. The Football Association, IFAB and FIFA made a pitch the whole world could share. Injuries, bans and new laws changed who could play and how safely.

You will be able to put key dates in order and name who made each rule. You will explain how a school field and a world stadium are public places. You will also tell when women and girls were shut out of soccer, and when doors opened again.

The big question

How did two kinds of football grow from rough local games into written codes played on school fields and world stadiums?

Football
Big questionHow did a rough campus game become a marked field that towns and schools build and share?
The story

Bleachers Before Kickoff

Rows of empty bleachers face a rectangle of painted grass on a fall afternoon.

It is a Friday afternoon in an Illinois town. The bleachers beside the high school are still empty. White lines cross the grass every five yards. Numbers are painted every ten yards. A groundskeeper checks the goalposts at each end.

The field did not always look like this. In 1869, college students in New Jersey played a game with 25 players on each side. There were no painted lines across the ground. Each school argued about which rules to use.

Over the next forty years, college men wrote rule after rule. A Yale player named Walter Camp cut the teams to eleven. Rule makers drew lines across the field. After many players were hurt, they changed the rules again in 1906.

Later, towns built stands, lights and fences around these fields. Tonight the whole town may come to watch. The field belongs to the school district, which means it belongs to the public.

Campus Games Become a Code

11.1

Rutgers and Princeton, 1869

Main ideaOn 6 November 1869, Rutgers and Princeton played a game that is often called the first college football game.

On 6 November 1869, students from Rutgers met students from Princeton in New Brunswick, New Jersey. Each side had 25 players. Rutgers won, six goals to four. This game is often called the first football game.

That game looked more like soccer than modern American football. Players moved a round ball with their feet and bodies. The two schools had to agree on rules before they started. Rutgers was the home team, so its rules were used.

In 1874, Harvard played McGill University from Montreal, Canada. McGill played a game closer to , where players could pick up the ball and run. Harvard liked it. Soon other eastern colleges moved toward the running and carrying game.

Main ideaCollege rule makers shrank football teams over time, and in 1880 they settled on eleven players a side.

In 1876, several eastern colleges formed the Intercollegiate Football Association. They met in Springfield, Massachusetts. They agreed on rugby-style rules with fifteen players on each side. Now schools could play one another under one written code.

11.3

Downs and a Gridiron

Main ideaAn 1882 rule about downs led to chalk lines across the field, which gave it the name gridiron.

Soon after 1880, a new problem appeared. A team could keep the ball for a long time without moving forward. In 1882, the colleges passed another Camp idea. A team had three tries, called , to gain five yards.

To see five yards, officials needed marks on the grass. Workers chalked lines straight across the field every five yards. From above, the field looked like a cooking grill. People began to call it a .

In 1906, the distance grew to ten yards. In 1912, a fourth down was added. That same year the field became 100 yards long between the goal lines. These numbers were written into the rules, so every school could mark the same field.

A Crisis Changes the Rules

11.4

The Dangerous Years

Main ideaBy 1905, many college players had been badly hurt or killed, and people demanded safer rules.

In the 1890s and early 1900s, college football was very rough. Many players pushed forward together in a tight group. This was called . Players wore little padding. Some were badly hurt, and some died.

Newspapers printed lists of the injured and the dead each fall. Some college leaders wanted to end the game. Columbia University dropped football after the 1905 season. Other schools thought about doing the same.

In October 1905, President Theodore Roosevelt invited leaders from Harvard, Yale and Princeton to the White House. He liked football. He asked them to make the game safer so that it could survive.

The Forward Pass, 1906

Main ideaIn 1906, new college rules made the forward pass legal and set ten yards for a first down.

In 1906, college leaders formed a new group to oversee college sports. It was first called the IAAUS. In 1910 it took the name , the National Collegiate Athletic Association. It still runs many college sports today.

The 1906 rules made the legal. A forward pass is a throw toward the other team’s goal. The rules also said a team needed ten yards in three downs. The goal was to spread players out and end the worst piling up.

Rules were printed each year in Spalding’s Official Foot Ball Guide. Coaches, players and officials bought the same small book. At first, the passing rules had many limits. Over the next years, rule makers loosened them.

11.6

A Leather Helmet

Main ideaSoft leather helmets were used in the early 1900s, and hard plastic helmets came around 1940.

Early players often wore no helmet at all. Some grew their hair long to soften blows. In the early 1900s, some players began to wear soft . These looked like close-fitting caps with flaps over the ears.

Leather helmets were not required at first. Each player chose whether to wear one. They protected the ears and scalp from scrapes. They did little to stop a hard hit to the head.

Around 1940, companies began to sell hard plastic helmets. In 1943, the National Football League made helmets required for its players. Today, school rules require helmets that meet a written safety .

Stagg in Chicago

Main ideaAmos Alonzo Stagg coached at the University of Chicago from 1892 to 1932 and helped bring big college football to the Midwest.

The University of Chicago opened in 1892. Its president hired Amos Alonzo to lead its athletics. Stagg had played at Yale with Walter Camp. He became a full faculty member, not just a hired coach.

In 1895, leaders from several Midwest universities met in Chicago. They formed the Western , a group of schools that played one another under shared rules. Chicago was a founding member. The group later became known as the Big Ten.

Stagg coached at Chicago until 1932. The university’s field was named Stagg Field. Chicago later left the Big Ten, in 1946. Its early teams still show how a college game spread west from the eastern schools.

11.9

A Town's Friday Field

Main ideaA high school football field is public property, paid for and used by the whole community.

In the early 1900s, more American towns built public high schools. Many added football teams. At first, teams often played on open lots or city parks. Later, school districts built their own fields beside the school.

A school district gets much of its money from local property . Voters sometimes approve new bleachers or lights. Booster clubs of parents also raise money. So the field is a place, shared by the town.

Night lights let games move to Friday evenings, after work. Families could come after the workday. The same field may hold band shows, track meets and graduation. It works like a town square with goalposts.

Soccer
Big questionHow did a kicking game played many different ways become one set of laws that the whole world shares?
The story

Two Teams, One Set of Lines

Two teams that speak different languages walk onto the same marked field.

It is 13 July 1930 in Montevideo, Uruguay. The first World Cup has begun. France plays Mexico. The French players speak French. The Mexican players speak Spanish. Few of them can talk to each other.

Yet both teams know the field. They know the goal is eight yards wide. They know the halfway line and the penalty area. They know what the referee's whistle means. The rules are the same for both sides.

Long before, this was not true. Towns in England played rough kicking games with their own customs. Schools each had their own rules. Two teams from different places could argue for an hour before they started.

In 1863, clubs in London began to write one set of laws. Over the next seventy years, those laws crossed seas and borders. By 1930, teams from Europe and the Americas could meet on one shared field.

12.1

Folk Football in Towns

Main ideaFor hundreds of years, English towns played rough folk football games with few written rules.

In medieval England, whole towns played . A crowd pushed a ball through streets and fields toward a goal. There was no set number of players. Games could last for hours, and people were often hurt.

Rulers did not always like it. In 1314, a proclamation in London, made in the name of King Edward II, banned football in the city. It said the noise and pushing caused trouble. Later kings and towns passed similar bans.

Some old games never stopped. In Ashbourne, in Derbyshire, England, a game is still played each year. Hundreds of townspeople take part over two days. The goals are about three miles apart.

12.2

School Rules and Sheffield

Main ideaEnglish schools, a Cambridge group and Sheffield clubs wrote their own football rules before 1863.

In the 1800s, English such as Eton, Harrow and Rugby each played football their own way. Some let players carry the ball. Others allowed only kicking. When students went to college, they brought different rules with them.

In 1848, students at Cambridge University wrote a shared set of rules. These are called the Cambridge Rules. They tried to blend the school games so that everyone could play together. No copy of the 1848 rules survives today.

In 1857, Sheffield Football Club formed in northern England. It is often called the oldest football still playing. Its members wrote the Sheffield Rules in 1858. Other clubs in the Sheffield area used them for years.

12.3

Thirteen Laws, 1863

Main ideaThe Football Association published thirteen laws in December 1863, after a split over carrying and hacking.

Ebenezer Cobb Morley was a lawyer and a member of Barnes Football Club. He suggested that London clubs agree on one code. He became the first secretary of the , or FA. He wrote the first draft of its laws.

The clubs argued over two things. One was carrying the ball in the hands. The other was , which meant kicking an opponent’s shins. The final laws banned both. The Blackheath club disagreed and left the FA.

In December 1863, the FA published its laws. There were thirteen of them. They set the most a field could measure and how a match began. Because they were printed, any club anywhere could read and copy them.

By the 1880s, England, Scotland, Wales and Ireland each had a football association. Their laws were close but not always the same. Matches between them sometimes led to arguments. They needed one group to settle the words.

In 1886, the four associations formed the International Football Association Board, called . It meets to decide any change to the . A new law must be approved there before every nation uses it.

FIFA joined IFAB in 1913. Today, FIFA and the four British associations share the votes. IFAB publishes the Laws each year. They are translated into many languages, so referees everywhere read the same rules.

A Tournament That Grew

Main ideaThe men's World Cup grew from 13 teams in 1930 to 48 teams in 2026.

After 1930, more countries formed football associations and joined FIFA. Many former colonies in Africa and Asia joined after they became independent. Each new member could try to , or earn a place, in the World Cup.

FIFA made the tournament bigger several times. The 1934 World Cup in Italy had 16 teams. The 1982 World Cup in Spain had 24. The 1998 World Cup in France had 32.

The 2026 World Cup is shared by Canada, Mexico and the United States. It has 48 teams, the most ever. More teams give more a chance to play on the world stage.

12.9

Women Shut Out, Then Welcomed

Main ideaThe FA banned women's matches from its members' grounds in 1921, lifted the ban in 1971, and FIFA held the first Women's World Cup in 1991.

During World War I, many British women worked in factories. Some formed football teams. Dick, Kerr Ladies, from a factory in Preston, drew very large crowds. In 1920, a match at Everton’s ground drew over fifty thousand people.

In December 1921, the FA said football was not suitable for women. It women’s teams from playing on its members’ grounds. Women kept playing on other fields, but crowds and money shrank. The ban lasted fifty years.

The FA lifted the ban in 1971. In 1991, FIFA held the first Women&#x27;s World Cup, in China. The United States won. In 1999, the final at the Rose Bowl in California drew over ninety thousand fans.

Think it through
Two Dates, Fifty Years Apart

In 1921, a notice from the FA tells its clubs not to let women's teams use their grounds. In 1971, the FA takes that rule away. A woman who was a young player in 1921 would be about seventy in 1971.

What did the 1921 rule take away from women players, and for how long?

IHSA Soccer, 1972 and 1988

Main ideaThe IHSA crowned its first boys soccer state champion in 1972 and its first girls soccer state champion in 1988.

For most of the 1900s, soccer was a small sport in American schools. Football filled the fall in most high schools. Many soccer players learned the game in city clubs instead. Chicago had amateur soccer clubs for many decades, often started by immigrant families.

In 1972, the IHSA held its first boys soccer state . That same year Congress passed , a law that says schools that get federal money may not shut people out because of sex. Girls’ teams grew in the years after.

The IHSA held its first girls soccer state tournament in 1988, sixteen years after the boys. Today boys play in the fall and girls in the spring. Both use the same Laws of the Game, with some school changes.

The World Comes to Chicago

Main ideaThe 1994 World Cup opened at Soldier Field in Chicago, and the Chicago Fire club was founded in 1997.

The United States hosted the men’s World Cup in 1994. The opening match was played at Soldier Field in Chicago on 17 June 1994. Germany played Bolivia. Fans came from around the world to one lakefront .

When it won the right to host, the United States promised to start a new men’s professional league. began play in 1996. It brought pro soccer to many American cities.

The Chicago Fire was founded on 8 October 1997. That date was the 126th anniversary of the Great Chicago Fire of 1871. The club won the MLS Cup in its first season, 1998.
