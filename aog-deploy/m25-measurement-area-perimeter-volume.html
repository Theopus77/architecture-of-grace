Around it, or across it?

A rectangle, 6 long and 2 wide. Two different questions can be asked about it.

Around is perimeter, counted in lengths. Covering is area, counted in squares. Filling is volume, counted in cubes. Underline which one the question wants before you touch a formula.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

The perimeter of a 6 by 2 rectangle is 6 + 2 = 8.

A rectangle has FOUR sides, and you walk all of them: 6 + 2 + 6 + 2 = 16. Adding just two gives you half the fence and a yard full of loose dogs.

Trace it with your finger. Count every side you cross.

⚠️ People often think…

48 inches is 576 feet, because you multiply by 12.

Going from a small unit to a big one means FEWER of them, so you divide: 48 ÷ 12 = 4 feet. 576 feet is longer than a football field, which is your signal that something went the wrong direction.

Bigger unit, smaller number. Always check if it sounds crazy.

Helpful Hints

🤔 Which measure is it? — the routine

Read the problem and ask what the thing DOES → goes AROUND the edge (fence, frame, ribbon, a lap) → PERIMETER: add the sides → COVERS a flat surface (carpet, paint, tiles, seed) → AREA: length × width, square units → FILLS a box (water, sand, cubes, packing) → VOLUME: length × width × height, cubic units → write the number WITH its unit.

Around, cover, fill: add, multiply two, multiply three.

💡 The whole topic in one idea

Measuring is COUNTING UNITS. Perimeter counts unit lengths laid end to end around the edge. Area counts unit squares laid side by side to cover the inside. Volume counts unit cubes stacked to fill a box. The formulas are just fast ways to count: 4 rows of 7 squares is 7 × 4; 2 layers of 12 cubes is 12 × 2. Converting units is counting the same thing with a different-sized unit — smaller unit, bigger count.

Lengths, squares, cubes — count them.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Giving the area when the question asked for the perimeter, or the other way round. Underline the word first.
  • Adding only two sides for a perimeter: 6 + 2 = 8 for a 6 by 2 rectangle. It has FOUR sides: 16.
  • Adding the three edges of a box instead of multiplying: 4 + 3 + 2 = 9. Volume is 4 × 3 × 2 = 24.
  • Gluing units together: 2 h 15 min = 215 min, or 3 m 45 cm = 48 cm. Convert the big unit first, THEN add.
  • Converting the wrong direction: 48 inches = 576 feet. Small to big means fewer — divide.
  • Forgetting the unit or using the wrong one: writing 15 cm for an area. It is 15 cm² — square centimeters.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A rectangle with two sides labeled — “find the perimeter” or “find the area.” Read which one; write the unit.
  • A rectangle with the area (or perimeter) given and one side — “find the missing side.” Divide for area; subtract two lengths and halve for perimeter.
  • An L-shaped floor — “find the area.” Cut it into rectangles and add, or box it in and subtract.
  • A box built from unit cubes, or three edge lengths — “find the volume.” One layer, times the number of layers.
  • Two boxes — “which holds more?” Multiply all three edges of each; never judge by the longest side.
  • A story — “fence, carpet, or fill?” Decide perimeter, area or volume before touching a number.
  • “3 m 45 cm = ? cm” or “2 h 15 min = ? min” — convert the big unit, then add the small part.

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Find the perimeter AND the area of a 7 by 4 rectangle, with the right units on each?
  • Find the missing width when the area is 24 and the length is 6? When the perimeter is 20 and the length is 7?
  • Find the area of an L-shape by cutting it into two rectangles?
  • Find the volume of a 5 by 3 by 2 box, and explain it as layers of cubes?
  • Say which of two boxes holds more, using their volumes?
  • Convert 3 m 45 cm to cm, 48 in to ft, and 2 h 15 min to min — and say when to multiply and when to divide?
  • Tell, from a story, whether it is asking for perimeter, area or volume?
