Tener, Gustar and Stem Changes

Verb Patterns

Think about the questions you answer every day. Are you hungry? Do you want pizza or tacos? Can you come to practice? What kind of music do you like? In Spanish, every one of those questions uses a verb that does something unusual. Querer and poder change their middle vowel. Tener expresses hunger and cold. Gustar turns the sentence around so the food or the song becomes the subject.

This unit teaches those patterns as patterns, not as lists to memorize. You will learn the boot shape that stem-changing verbs follow, the three kinds of vowel change, the tener expressions that replace English to be, and the family of verbs that work like gustar. Many heritage speakers already say these forms by ear; this unit gives names to what you hear at home.

By the end, you will be able to say what you want, what you can do, how you feel, and what you and your family like, love, need and can't stand, in correct Spanish. You will also be able to agree and disagree politely: a mí también, a mí tampoco.

The big question

How do a few patterns let you talk about what you want, need, feel and like in Spanish?

Boot Verbs and Tener Expressions

Stem-Changing Verbs
Big questionWhy do some Spanish verbs change in the middle, and how can you predict where the change happens?
The story

Cold Rain, Hungry Team

After a muddy game, a soccer team needs food, blankets and the right Spanish verbs.

The rain started in the second half and never stopped. By the final whistle, the Pilsen under-16 team was soaked, muddy and tired. They had lost 2 to 1. Coach Ramírez waved everyone under the bleachers. "¿Tienen frío?" she asked (Are you cold?). Twenty voices answered at once: "¡Sí, tenemos frío!" (Yes, we're cold!).

Marisol wrung out her socks. "Tengo hambre" (I'm hungry), she said. "Y tengo sed" (And I'm thirsty). Her friend Diego laughed. "Siempre tienes hambre" (You're always hungry). Notice that Spanish says she HAS hunger. English says she IS hungry. That small difference trips up almost every new learner.

The coach checked her phone. "¿Quieren tacos o sopa?" (Do you all want tacos or soup?). "¡Queremos sopa!" (We want soup!), the team shouted. The coach said, "Yo pienso que el restaurante cierra a las nueve" (I think the restaurant closes at nine). Querer became quieren and queremos. Pensar became pienso. The middle of each verb kept changing.

At the restaurant, everyone ordered. "Pido el caldo de pollo" (I'm ordering the chicken soup), said Marisol. The server asked, "¿Qué piden ustedes?" (What are you all ordering?). Diego fell asleep on the booth. "Duerme como un bebé" (He sleeps like a baby), someone joked. Pedir became pido. Dormir became duerme.

On the bus home, Marisol listed the verbs in her notebook. Some changed e to ie. Some changed o to ue. Some changed e to i. But nosotros never changed: queremos, pedimos, dormimos. She drew a shape around the forms that changed. It looked like a boot.

Talk about itWhich verbs in the story changed their middle vowel, and which forms stayed the same as the infinitive?
Section 1

The Boot Shape

27.1

What a Stem Is

Main ideaA verb's stem is what is left after you drop -ar, -er or -ir, and boot verbs change a vowel in that stem.

Picture the coach asking, "¿Quieres sopa?" (Do you want soup?). The verb is (to want). Drop the -er ending and you have the : quer-. In regular verbs, the stem never moves. Hablar gives habl-, and every form keeps it: hablo, hablas, habla. You just swap the endings.

Querer is different. Watch it in the present tense: quiero, quieres, quiere, queremos, queréis, quieren. In four forms, the e of quer- turned into ie. The endings are normal -er endings. Only the stem vowel changed. A verb that does this is called a .

Why does this happen? Centuries ago, Latin short vowels that carried the stress split into two sounds in Spanish. So the change appears only where the stress falls on the stem. In queremos, the stress falls on -re-, the ending side, so the stem stays quer-. You do not need the history to use it, but it explains the pattern.

Think it through
Finding the stem

hablar → drop -ar → habl- comer → drop -er → com- querer → drop -er → quer- quer- + o → quiero (e became ie) quer- + emos → queremos (no change)

What is the stem of pensar, and what vowel in it would you expect to change?

Words to know
querer
to want; also to love a person — e→ie: quiero, quieres, quiere, queremos, queréis, quieren
el stem
grammar word: the part of a verb left after you drop -ar, -er or -ir (in Spanish, la raíz)
stem-changing verb
grammar word: a verb whose stem vowel changes in some present-tense forms, also called a boot verb
Check yourself

1. What is the stem of the verb querer?

Why: Drop the -er ending from querer and quer- is left. Quier- is the changed stem that appears only in some forms.

2. Which form of querer completes the sentence? Yo ___ sopa.

Why: The yo form takes -o, and the stressed stem vowel e becomes ie: quiero.

3. Why does queremos NOT change its stem?

Why: The stem changes only when the stress lands on the stem. In que-RE-mos the stress is on the ending side.
27.2

Drawing the Boot

Main ideaIn a stem-changing verb, yo, tú, él/ella/usted and ellos/ustedes change; nosotros and vosotros keep the original stem.

Write the six forms of querer in two columns, the way a conjugation chart looks. Singular on the left: quiero, quieres, quiere. Plural on the right: queremos, queréis, quieren. Now draw a line around every form with ie. The line wraps the whole left column and dips down to catch quieren at the bottom right.

That outline looks like a boot, so teachers call these boot verbs. The top of the right column, nosotros and vosotros, sits outside the boot. Those two forms keep the infinitive’s stem: queremos, queréis. Every stem-changing verb in the present tense follows this same boot shape.

A common mistake is changing every form: "quieremos" instead of queremos. English speakers do this because the change feels like part of the verb. Remember that (we) lives outside the boot. Saying the forms aloud helps. You will hear that the stress in queremos is not on the stem.

Think it through
The boot on a chart

[ quiero ] queremos [ quieres ] queréis [ quiere ] [ quieren ] The brackets mark the boot: four forms change e→ie. Queremos and queréis stay outside with the stem quer-.

Draw the same chart for pensar. Which two forms stay outside the boot?

Words to know
la bota
the boot — teachers use it for the shape of stem-changing verb charts
nosotros
we — in boot verbs this form keeps the original stem: queremos, podemos, pedimos
vosotros
you all, informal, used mainly in Spain — also outside the boot: queréis, podéis
Check yourself

1. Which form of querer is OUTSIDE the boot?

Why: Nosotros (queremos) and vosotros (queréis) sit outside the boot and keep the stem quer-.

2. A student writes: Nosotros quieremos pizza. What is wrong?

Why: Nosotros is outside the boot, so the stem stays quer-: queremos.

3. Which form of querer goes with ellos?

Why: Ellos/ellas/ustedes is inside the boot: quier- plus the -er ending -en gives quieren.
27.3

Endings Stay Regular

Main ideaBoot verbs change only the stem; their endings are the same regular -ar, -er or -ir endings you already know.

Take (to close), an -ar boot verb. The stem is cerr-, and its e becomes ie inside the boot. Now add the normal -ar endings: cierro, cierras, cierra, cerramos, cerráis, cierran. The endings -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an are exactly the endings of hablar.

That means you already know half of every boot verb. For -er verbs like querer, use -o, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en. For -ir verbs like (to sleep), use -o, -es, -e, -imos, -ís, -en: duermo, duermes, duerme, dormimos, dormís, duermen. The only new job is the stem.

Here is a safe two-step method. Step one: pick the ending from the subject, as you would for any regular verb. Step two: ask whether the form is inside the boot. If yes, change the stem vowel. If no, leave it. Students who try to memorize every form as a new word get lost. Students who use the two steps do not.

Think it through
Two steps for cerrar with ellos

Step 1 — ending: ellos with an -ar verb → -an. Step 2 — boot? Ellos is inside the boot → cerr- becomes cierr-. Result: cierran. "Ellos cierran la puerta." (They close the door.)

Use the two steps to build the nosotros form of cerrar.

Words to know
cerrar
to close — e→ie: cierro, cierras, cierra, cerramos, cerráis, cierran
dormir
to sleep — o→ue: duermo, duermes, duerme, dormimos, dormís, duermen
la terminación
the ending — the part of a verb that shows the subject (-o, -as, -a…)
Check yourself

1. Which form completes the sentence? Tú ___ la ventana. (cerrar)

Why: Tú takes the -ar ending -as, and tú is inside the boot, so cerr- becomes cierr-: cierras.

2. What are the endings of dormir in the present tense?

Why: Dormir is an -ir verb, so it uses -o, -es, -e, -imos, -ís, -en. Only the stem changes.

3. Which form completes the sentence? Nosotros ___ ocho horas. (dormir)

Why: Nosotros is outside the boot and dormir is an -ir verb: dorm- plus -imos gives dormimos.
Section 2

Three Kinds of Change

27.4

E Becomes IE

Main ideaVerbs like querer, pensar, empezar and preferir change the stem vowel e to ie inside the boot.

Imagine the team deciding when to leave. "¿A qué hora empieza el partido?" (What time does the game start?). The verb (to begin) has the stem empez-. Inside the boot, e becomes ie: empiezo, empiezas, empieza, empezamos, empezáis, empiezan. Say each form aloud as you write it.

(to think) works the same way: pienso, piensas, piensa, pensamos, pensáis, piensan. So do cerrar, entender (to understand) and preferir (to prefer). "Prefiero la sopa" means I prefer the soup. When a verb has two e’s, like preferir, only the stressed one changes: pre-FIE-ro.

How do you know which verbs change? You cannot tell from spelling alone. Pensar changes, but llevar (to carry) does not: llevo, not "llievo". Dictionaries and glossaries mark boot verbs with the change in parentheses: pensar (ie). Learn each verb with its label, and the chart builds itself.

Think it through
Planning after the game

Coach: ¿Qué piensan ustedes? (What do you all think?) Marisol: Pienso que tenemos hambre. (I think we're hungry.) Diego: Yo prefiero tacos. (I prefer tacos.) Coach: El restaurante empieza a servir a las seis. (The restaurant starts serving at six.)

Find every e→ie form in the dialogue. Then say what you prefer to eat after a game, using prefiero.

Words to know
pensar (ie)
to think — pienso, piensas, piensa, pensamos, pensáis, piensan
empezar (ie)
to begin or start — empiezo, empiezas, empieza, empezamos, empezáis, empiezan
preferir (ie)
to prefer — prefiero, prefieres, prefiere, preferimos, preferís, prefieren
entender (ie)
to understand — entiendo, entiendes, entiende, entendemos, entendéis, entienden
Check yourself

1. Which form completes the sentence? La clase ___ a las ocho. (empezar)

Why: La clase is third person singular, inside the boot: empiez- plus -a gives empieza.

2. What does "No entiendo la pregunta" mean?

Why: Entiendo is the yo form of entender (ie), to understand.

3. Which verb does NOT change its stem?

Why: Llevar is regular: llevo, llevas, lleva. You cannot guess from spelling; boot verbs are marked (ie) in glossaries.
27.5

O Becomes UE

Main ideaVerbs like poder, dormir, volver and almorzar change the stem vowel o to ue inside the boot.

Back at the bleachers, a player asks, "¿Puedo irme?" (Can I leave?). The verb is (to be able to, can). The stem pod- turns into pued- inside the boot: puedo, puedes, puede, podemos, podéis, pueden. The same boot, a different vowel pair. Say the forms aloud and listen for the ue.

(to return) gives vuelvo, vuelves, vuelve, volvemos, volvéis, vuelven. (to eat lunch) gives almuerzo, almuerzas, almuerza, almorzamos, almorzáis, almuerzan. Dormir, which you saw earlier, belongs here too. "¿A qué hora vuelves?" means What time are you coming back?

Poder is usually followed by an infinitive, just like can in English: "Puedo jugar" (I can play). Do not conjugate the second verb. A frequent error is "puedo juego", which doubles the conjugation. Only the first verb changes for the subject; the second stays in its -ar, -er or -ir form.

Think it through
Walking through poder

poder → stem pod- yo: pued- + o = puedo tú: pued- + es = puedes ella: pued- + e = puede nosotros: pod- + emos = podemos (outside the boot) ellos: pued- + en = pueden "¿Puedes venir?" (Can you come?)

Build the six forms of volver the same way, and circle the two that keep vol-.

Words to know
poder (ue)
to be able to, can — puedo, puedes, puede, podemos, podéis, pueden; followed by an infinitive
volver (ue)
to return, come back — vuelvo, vuelves, vuelve, volvemos, volvéis, vuelven
almorzar (ue)
to eat lunch — almuerzo, almuerzas, almuerza, almorzamos, almorzáis, almuerzan
Check yourself

1. Which sentence is correct?

Why: Poder changes o→ue in yo (puedo), and the verb after it stays an infinitive (jugar).

2. Which form completes the sentence? Nosotros ___ a casa a las diez. (volver)

Why: Nosotros is outside the boot, so the stem stays vol-: volvemos. Volvimos is past tense.

3. What does "¿Dónde almuerzas?" mean?

Why: Almuerzas is the tú form of almorzar (ue), to eat lunch.
27.6

Jugar: The Only U to UE

Main ideaJugar is the one common verb that changes u to ue: juego, juegas, juega, jugamos, jugáis, juegan.

The team plays soccer, so they need (to play a sport or game). Its stem jug- has a u, not an o, yet it follows the o→ue pattern: juego, juegas, juega, jugamos, jugáis, juegan. It is the only everyday verb that does this, so learn it on its own.

Spanish uses jugar a before the name of a sport or game in careful speech: "Juego al fútbol" (I play soccer). Al is a plus el. Many speakers, especially in Latin America, also say "Juego fútbol" without al. Both are used and understood. Just remember that (to play an instrument) is a different verb: "Toco la guitarra."

English uses one word, play, for sports and music. Spanish splits them. A common mistake is "Juego el piano", which sounds like you are playing a game with the piano. Use jugar for sports and games, tocar for instruments and music. Say "Juego al fútbol y toco el piano" to practice both.

Think it through
Sports or music?

Diego juega al fútbol los sábados. (Diego plays soccer on Saturdays.) Marisol toca la trompeta en la banda. (Marisol plays the trumpet in the band.) Nosotros jugamos al béisbol en el parque. (We play baseball in the park.)

Write two sentences about yourself: one with juego and one with toco.

jugar (ue)
to play a sport or game — juego, juegas, juega, jugamos, jugáis, juegan
tocar

el fútbol
soccer — in the U.S., football is el fútbol americano
Check yourself

1. Which form completes the sentence? Ellas ___ al básquetbol. (jugar)

Why: Ellas is inside the boot: jug- becomes jueg-, plus the -ar ending -an gives juegan.

2. How do you say "I play the guitar"?

Why: Spanish uses tocar for instruments. The yo form is toco.

3. Which form of jugar goes with nosotros?

Why: Nosotros is outside the boot, so the stem stays jug-, with the -ar ending -amos.
27.7

E Becomes I

Main ideaSome -ir verbs, like pedir, servir and repetir, change the stem vowel e to i inside the boot.

At the restaurant, Marisol says, "Pido el caldo" (I’m ordering the broth). The verb (to ask for, order) has the stem ped-. Inside the boot, e becomes i, not ie: pido, pides, pide, pedimos, pedís, piden. This third pattern appears only in -ir verbs.

(to serve) gives sirvo, sirves, sirve, servimos, servís, sirven. (to repeat) gives repito, repites, repite, repetimos, repetís, repiten. Your teacher may say "Repitan, por favor" (Repeat, please). You now know where that i comes from.

Pedir and preguntar both translate as ask, but they are not the same. Pedir means to ask FOR something: "Pido agua" (I ask for water). Preguntar means to ask a question: "Pregunto la hora" (I ask the time). Mixing them up is one of the most common errors English speakers make.

At the restaurant

Find the forms of pedir and servir. Which one is outside the boot, and how can you tell?

Words to know
pedir (i)
to ask for, to order — pido, pides, pide, pedimos, pedís, piden
servir (i)
to serve — sirvo, sirves, sirve, servimos, servís, sirven
repetir (i)
to repeat — repito, repites, repite, repetimos, repetís, repiten
preguntar
to ask a question — regular -ar verb; not the same as pedir
Check yourself

1. Which form completes the sentence? El mesero ___ la comida. (servir)

2. How do you say "I ask for help"?

Why: Asking FOR something uses pedir, which changes e→i: pido.

3. Which form completes the sentence? Ustedes ___ la palabra. (repetir)

Why: Ustedes is inside the boot, and repetir changes e→i: repiten.
Section 3

Tener Expressions

27.8

Tener: A Boot with a Twist

Main ideaTener is an e→ie verb with an irregular yo form: tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tenéis, tienen.

The coach counts players: "Tengo veinte jugadores" (I have twenty players). (to have) is one of the most used verbs in Spanish. It looks like a boot verb: tienes, tiene, tienen change e to ie, and tenemos, tenéis stay outside. You will use it in almost every conversation.

The twist is yo. Instead of "tieno", Spanish says tengo, with a g. A few other verbs share this yo-go pattern, such as venir (to come): vengo, vienes, viene, venimos, venís, vienen. So tener and venir are boot verbs with a special yo form. "Vengo a las seis" means I’m coming at six.

A mistake many learners make is "yo tiengo", mixing the two changes. Keep them separate. Yo gets the g and no ie: tengo. Tú, él, ellos get ie and no g: tienes, tiene, tienen. Nosotros and vosotros get neither: tenemos, tenéis. Practice all six forms until they sound natural.

Think it through
Three kinds of forms in tener

yo → g, no ie → tengo tú, él, ellos → ie, no g → tienes, tiene, tienen nosotros, vosotros → neither → tenemos, tenéis "¿Cuántos hermanos tienes?" "Tengo dos." (How many siblings do you have? I have two.)

Answer in Spanish: ¿Cuántas clases tienes hoy?

Words to know
tener (ie)
to have — tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tenéis, tienen; yo form is irregular
venir (ie)
to come — vengo, vienes, viene, venimos, venís, vienen
el jugador / la jugadora
the player (male / female)
Check yourself

1. Which form completes the sentence? Yo ___ un perro.

Why: The yo form of tener is irregular: tengo, with a g and no ie.

2. Which form completes the sentence? Mi hermana ___ quince años.

Why: Mi hermana is third person singular, inside the boot: tiene.

3. Which form of venir goes with nosotros?

Why: Venir is an -ir verb and nosotros is outside the boot: ven- plus -imos gives venimos.
27.9

Hungry, Thirsty, Cold, Hot

Main ideaSpanish uses tener plus a noun for many feelings English expresses with to be: tengo hambre means I am hungry.

Back in the rain, nobody says "estoy frío". They say "Tengo frío", literally I have cold. Spanish treats hunger, thirst, cold and heat as things you have. (to be hungry), (to be thirsty), (to be cold) and tener calor (to be hot).

Because hambre and sed are nouns, you make them stronger with mucho or mucha, not muy. Hambre is feminine, so it is "tengo mucha hambre" (I’m very hungry). Frío and calor are masculine: "tengo mucho frío", "tengo mucho calor". "Tengo muy hambre" is a common learner error; muy goes with adjectives.

Be careful with estar. "Estoy frío" does not mean I feel cold. It sounds like your body is cold to the touch, like food left out. "Estoy caliente" can even have a romantic or embarrassing meaning in many places. To talk about how you feel, use tener.

Think it through
Under the bleachers

Coach: ¿Qué tienen? (What's wrong with you all?) Marisol: Tengo mucho frío. (I'm very cold.) Diego: Yo tengo mucha hambre. (I'm very hungry.) Ana: Y nosotros tenemos sed. (And we're thirsty.)

It is July in Chicago and 95 degrees. Say how you feel with a tener expression and mucho or mucha.

Words to know
tener hambre
to be hungry — literally to have hunger; mucha hambre = very hungry
tener sed
to be thirsty — literally to have thirst; mucha sed = very thirsty
tener frío
to be (feel) cold — mucho frío = very cold; opposite of tener calor
tener calor
to be (feel) hot — mucho calor = very hot
Check yourself

1. How do you say "I'm very hungry"?

Why: Hunger uses tener, and hambre is a feminine noun, so it takes mucha, not muy.

2. It is snowing and you are outside without a coat. What do you say?

Why: To say you feel cold, Spanish uses tener frío. Estoy frío describes something cold to the touch.

3. What does "Los jugadores tienen sed" mean?

27.10

Fear, Being Right, Luck, Sleep

Main ideaTener miedo, tener razón, tener sueño, tener suerte and tener prisa also use tener where English uses to be.

On the dark bus ride home, Diego whispers, "Tengo miedo de los truenos" (I’m afraid of thunder). means to be afraid, and you add de before the thing you fear. The coach nods at Marisol’s plan to leave early. "Tienes razón" (You’re right). means to be right.

More of the family: (to be sleepy), tener suerte (to be lucky), tener prisa (to be in a hurry) and tener cuidado (to be careful). "¡Ten cuidado!" (Be careful!) is something you will hear from any abuela. Each one is tener plus a noun, conjugated for the subject.

Age belongs here too. "Tengo dieciséis años" means I am sixteen, literally I have sixteen years. English speakers often say "soy dieciséis", which sounds like I am the number sixteen. Always use tener with años. "¿Cuántos años tienes?" (How old are you?) is how you ask.

Gustar and Its Family

Gustar
Big questionHow does Spanish talk about likes and dislikes when the thing you like is the subject of the sentence?
The story

A Menu for Twenty

Planning one dinner for a big family means learning who likes what, and how to say it.

Abuela's seventieth birthday was two weeks away, and Lucía had volunteered to plan the menu. Twenty relatives were coming to the house in Little Village. She sat down with her notebook and her cousin Tomás. "Primero, ¿qué le gusta a Abuela?" (First, what does Grandma like?), she asked.

Tomás thought. "A Abuela le gustan los tamales" (Grandma likes tamales). "Y le encanta el mole" (And she loves mole). Lucía wrote it down. Then she frowned. "Pero a tío Raúl no le gusta el picante" (But Uncle Raúl doesn't like spicy food). "Y a las gemelas les molesta la cebolla" (And the onion bothers the twins).

Lucía noticed something strange. In English, she was the one doing the liking. In Spanish, the food seemed to be doing something to the person. "Me gustan los tamales" felt more like tamales are pleasing to me. The word for the food changed the verb: gusta for one thing, gustan for many.

They called their other cousins. "¿Te gusta el pozole?" (Do you like pozole?). "¡Sí, me encanta!" (Yes, I love it!). "A mí no me gusta" (I don't like it), said another. Tomás laughed. "A mí tampoco" (Me neither). The list grew with every call.

By the end of the night, Lucía had a menu: tamales, rice, beans, a mild mole and a spicy salsa on the side. "Nos falta el postre" (We're missing dessert), Tomás pointed out. Lucía smiled and wrote one more word: pastel. Everyone likes cake.

Talk about itWhy do you think Spanish says the food is pleasing to you instead of saying you like the food?
Section 1

How Gustar Works

28.1

The Thing Liked Is the Subject

Main ideaIn me gusta el mole, el mole is the subject; the sentence means mole is pleasing to me.

Picture Lucía’s grandmother tasting mole and smiling. In English, you would say She likes mole. The person is the subject, and mole is the object. Spanish flips it: "Le gusta el mole." The verb means to be pleasing, and the mole is doing the pleasing.

Read it word by word. Le (to her) gusta (is pleasing) el mole (the mole). So the subject of the sentence is el mole, the thing liked. The person who likes it appears as a small word before the verb that means to me, to you, to her. That word is called an .

This is why "Yo gusto el mole" is wrong, even though it copies English word order. Yo gusto would mean I am pleasing. The right sentence is "Me gusta el mole" (Mole is pleasing to me, I like mole). Once you think of gustar as to be pleasing, the rest of the pattern makes sense.

Think it through
Word by word

English: I like mole. Spanish: Me gusta el mole. me = to me gusta = is pleasing el mole = the mole (the subject) Literal: Mole is pleasing to me.

Translate word by word: Te gusta el pozole. What is the subject?

Words to know
gustar
to be pleasing — used to say like: Me gusta el arroz (I like rice; rice is pleasing to me)
el sujeto
the subject — grammar word for who or what does the verb; with gustar it is the thing liked
indirect object pronoun
grammar word: a small word (me, te, le, nos, os, les) that tells to whom or for whom something happens
Check yourself

1. In the sentence "Me gusta la música", what is the subject?

Why: With gustar, the thing liked is the subject. La música is what is pleasing.

2. How do you say "I like rice"?

Why: Gustar agrees with the thing liked (el arroz), and the person appears as me: Me gusta el arroz.

3. What is the literal meaning of "Me gusta el fútbol"?

Why: Gustar means to be pleasing: soccer is pleasing to me.
28.2

Gusta or Gustan

Main ideaUse gusta with one thing or an infinitive, and gustan with more than one thing.

Lucía writes "Me gustan los tamales" and "Me gusta el arroz." Why the n? Because the verb agrees with its subject, the thing liked. One rice: gusta. Many tamales: gustan. The person liking it never changes the verb ending. So the ending depends on what is liked, not on who likes it.

Activities work like one thing. When you like doing something, use gusta plus an infinitive: "Me gusta cocinar" (I like to cook). Even two activities stay singular: "Me gusta cocinar y bailar" (I like to cook and dance). Only plural nouns, like las canciones (the songs), take gustan.

The common mistake is choosing gustan because the PERSON is plural. "Nos gustan el pastel" is wrong, because el pastel is one thing: "Nos gusta el pastel" (We like the cake). Always look to the right of the verb, find the thing liked, and count it. Count it before you choose the ending.

Think it through
Count what is liked

Me gusta el mole. → el mole = one → gusta Me gustan las tortillas. → las tortillas = many → gustan Me gusta bailar. → an activity = one → gusta Nos gusta la salsa. → la salsa = one → gusta (even though nos is plural)

Write one sentence with gusta and one with gustan about foods at a family party.

Words to know
me gusta
I like (one thing or an activity) — Me gusta el pastel. Me gusta nadar.
me gustan
I like (more than one thing) — Me gustan los tamales.
el infinitivo
the infinitive — the -ar, -er, -ir form of a verb; with gustar it counts as one thing
Check yourself

1. Which form completes the sentence? Me ___ los frijoles.

Why: Los frijoles is plural, so gustar takes the plural form gustan.

2. Which form completes the sentence? Nos ___ el pastel de chocolate.

Why: The thing liked, el pastel, is singular, so the verb is gusta even though nos is plural.

3. Which sentence is correct?

Why: Infinitives count as one thing, even two of them together, so the verb is gusta.
28.3

Saying No and Asking

Main ideaPut no right before the pronoun to make a negative, and turn the sentence into a question with ¿ ? around it.

Uncle Raúl does not like spicy food. In Spanish, no goes in front of the whole pronoun-plus-verb group: "No me gusta el picante" (I don’t like spicy food). Never split them. "Me no gusta" is a common error, because English puts not after the subject.

To ask, keep the same order and add question marks at both ends. "¿Te gusta el pozole?" (Do you like pozole?). You can also ask about someone else: "¿Le gustan las enchiladas?" (Does he like enchiladas?). There is no word for do in Spanish questions, so do not try to translate it.

Answer with the same pattern. "Sí, me gusta" or "No, no me gusta." Notice the double no. The first one answers the question. The second one makes the verb negative. That double no sounds strange to English ears but is perfectly correct Spanish. Practice both answers aloud with a partner.

Think it through
Calling the cousins

Lucía: ¿Te gustan los tamales de rajas? (Do you like tamales with chile strips?) Primo: Sí, me gustan mucho. (Yes, I like them a lot.) Lucía: ¿Y te gusta el picante? Primo: No, no me gusta. (No, I don't like it.)

Ask a partner two questions with ¿Te gusta…? and ¿Te gustan…?, and answer theirs.

Words to know
no me gusta
I don't like — no goes before the pronoun: No me gusta la cebolla.
¿te gusta…?
do you like…? — informal question to one person: ¿Te gusta el pozole?
el picante
spicy food, heat from chile — No le gusta el picante (He doesn't like spicy food)
Check yourself

1. How do you say "I don't like onions"?

Why: No goes before me, and las cebollas is plural, so the verb is gustan.

2. How do you ask a friend "Do you like to dance?"

Why: Te means to you (informal), and an infinitive takes gusta.

3. Why does the answer "No, no me gusta" have two no's?

Why: The first no is the answer; the second no, before me gusta, makes the sentence negative.
Section 2

Who Likes It

28.4

The Six Pronouns

Main ideaThe person who likes something is shown by me, te, le, nos, os or les before the verb.

Lucía’s notebook needs a line for everyone. For herself: gusta. For her cousin: te gusta. For Abuela: le gusta. For herself and Tomás: nos gusta. For the twins: les gusta. These are the indirect object pronouns, and they tell who finds the thing pleasing.

Here is the full set: me (to me), te (to you, informal), (to him, to her, to you formal), nos (to us), os (to you all, informal in Spain), (to them, to you all). Notice that le and les cover several people each. Le can mean him, her or usted.

The verb stays gusta or gustan no matter which pronoun you use. "Les gusta el arroz" (They like rice) and "Me gusta el arroz" (I like rice) share the same verb, because the rice is the subject in both. Only the pronoun changes. Only the small pronoun in front changes.

Think it through
Lucía's family chart

Lucía → me gusta el pozole Tomás (to him directly) → te gusta el pozole Abuela → le gusta el mole Lucía y Tomás → nos gustan los tamales las gemelas → les gusta el pastel

Make the same chart for four people in your own family and one food each.

Words to know
me, te
to me, to you (informal) — Me gusta. ¿Te gusta?
le
to him, to her, to you (usted) — Le gusta el mole.
nos, os
to us; to you all (informal, mostly Spain) — Nos gusta. Os gusta.
les
to them, to you all (ustedes) — Les gustan los tamales.
Check yourself

1. Which pronoun completes the sentence? A mis padres ___ gusta el café.

Why: Mis padres is plural (them), so the pronoun is les.

2. What does "Nos gustan las películas de terror" mean?

Why: Nos means to us, so the sentence means we like horror movies.

3. Which sentence means "She likes the song"?

Why: To her is le, and la canción is singular, so the verb is gusta.
28.5

A Mí, A Ti, A Ella

Main ideaA phrase like a mí, a ti or a Abuela adds emphasis or makes clear who le or les refers to.

"Le gusta el mole" could mean he, she or you (usted) likes mole. To make it clear, add a plus the person at the start: "A Abuela le gusta el mole." You can use a name, a noun or a pronoun: a ella, a él, a usted, a mis tíos. This a phrase usually comes first in the sentence.

The pronouns after a are (with an accent), a ti (no accent), a él, a ella, a usted, a nosotros, a vosotros, a ellos, a ellas, a ustedes. They add emphasis or contrast: "A mí me gusta el pozole, pero a ti te gusta el menudo" (I like pozole, but you like menudo).

Here is the catch: the a phrase is added, not swapped. You still need the small pronoun. "A mí gusta" is wrong; it must be "A mí me gusta." Also, mí needs its accent. Without it, mi means my, as in mi casa. That accent is the whole difference. Check both every time you write a mí.

Think it through
Clearing up le

Le gusta el mole. (Who? Him? Her? You?) A Abuela le gusta el mole. (Grandma likes mole.) A tío Raúl no le gusta el picante. (Uncle Raúl doesn't like spicy food.) A usted le gusta el café, ¿verdad? (You like coffee, right?)

Write two sentences that contrast what you like with what a friend likes, using a mí and a ti.

Words to know
a mí
to me, for emphasis — A mí me gusta. Mí has an accent; mi (my) does not.
a ti
to you (informal), for emphasis — A ti te gusta. No accent on ti.
a él / a ella
to him / to her — clears up who le means: A ella le gusta.
Check yourself

1. Which sentence is correct?

Why: The a phrase adds emphasis but does not replace me, and mí needs its accent.

2. Which phrase makes clear that le means your teacher?

Why: Use a plus the person, and keep the pronoun le for one person.

3. Which pronoun completes the sentence? A mis primos ___ gustan los videojuegos.

Why: Mis primos means them, so the pronoun is les.
28.6

Me Too, Me Neither

Main ideaAgree with a positive using a mí también, and with a negative using a mí tampoco.

Tomás says, "Me gusta el pastel de tres leches." Lucía agrees: "" (Me too). Because gustar is not a yo verb, you cannot say "yo también" here in careful Spanish. The answer matches the a mí form of the sentence. "Yo también" is heard in casual speech, but a mí también matches the grammar.

When the first sentence is negative, the agreement changes. "No me gusta la cebolla." "" (Me neither). Tampoco means neither or not either. Answering a negative with también is a common mistake. It would mean you DO like it, which is the opposite of agreeing.

To disagree, flip it. If someone says "Me gusta el menudo" and you don’t, answer "A mí no." If someone says "No me gusta el menudo" and you do, answer "A mí sí." Four little answers cover every case: también, tampoco, no and sí. Practice them with a partner until they feel automatic.

Think it through
Four answers

Me gusta el menudo. → Agree: A mí también. Disagree: A mí no. No me gusta el menudo. → Agree: A mí tampoco. Disagree: A mí sí.

Your friend says "No me gustan las matemáticas." Answer twice: once agreeing, once disagreeing.

Words to know
a mí también
me too — agrees with a positive gustar sentence
a mí tampoco
me neither — agrees with a negative gustar sentence
a mí sí / a mí no
I do / I don't — disagrees with what someone else said
Check yourself

1. Your friend says "No me gusta el frío." You agree. What do you say?

Why: To agree with a negative, use tampoco: a mí tampoco.

2. Your friend says "Me gustan los perros." You don't like dogs. What do you say?

Why: To disagree with a positive, say a mí no.

3. What does "A mí también" mean?

Why: También means also or too, so a mí también means me too.
Section 3

Verbs Like Gustar

28.7

Encantar: To Love Something

Main ideaEncantar works exactly like gustar and means to love a thing or activity: me encanta el mole.

Abuela does more than like mole. "Le encanta el mole" (She loves mole). (to delight, to love a thing) follows the same pattern as gustar. The thing loved is the subject: encanta for one thing or an activity, encantan for many. "Me encantan los tamales" means I love tamales.

Because encantar already means very much, do not add mucho. "Me encanta mucho" is a learner habit carried over from me gusta mucho. Native speakers usually just say me encanta. Also, for people you love, use querer: "Quiero a mi abuela" (I love my grandmother).

Here is a scale from low to high. "No me gusta" (I don’t like it), "Me gusta" (I like it), "Me gusta mucho" (I like it a lot), "Me encanta" (I love it). Choosing the right step makes your Spanish sound more natural and more precise. Try placing your own favorite foods on this scale.

Think it through
How much?

No me gusta la cebolla. (I don't like onion.) Me gusta el arroz. (I like rice.) Me gustan mucho los frijoles. (I like beans a lot.) Me encanta el pastel de tres leches. (I love tres leches cake.)

Rank four foods on the scale from no me gusta to me encanta.

Words to know
encantar
to delight, to love (a thing or activity) — Me encanta bailar. Me encantan los tamales.
gustar mucho
to like a lot — Me gusta mucho el arroz.
querer a
to love a person — Quiero a mi familia.
Check yourself

1. Which form completes the sentence? A mi hermano le ___ los videojuegos.

Why: Los videojuegos is plural, so encantar takes encantan.

2. How do you say "I love my grandmother"?

Why: For people you love, Spanish uses querer a, not encantar.

3. Which sentence shows the strongest liking?

Why: Encantar is stronger than gustar; it means to love an activity.
28.8

Interesar and Molestar

Main ideaInteresar (to interest) and molestar (to bother) follow the gustar pattern: me interesa la historia; me molesta el ruido.

The twins do not like onion. More precisely, "Les molesta la cebolla" (The onion bothers them). (to bother) puts the annoying thing first as the subject. Loud music, homework, a long line: whatever bothers you is the subject of molestar. "Me molesta el tráfico" means traffic bothers me.

(to interest) works the same way. "Me interesa la historia de México" (Mexican history interests me). "¿Te interesan los deportes?" (Are you interested in sports?). English also uses this order: History interests me. That can make interesar feel easier than gustar.

Watch the plural. "Me molestan los mosquitos" (Mosquitoes bother me) takes the n because los mosquitos is plural. And watch the meaning of molestar. It means to bother or annoy, not the English word molest. False friends like this can cause real embarrassment.

Think it through
What bothers, what interests

A Tomás le molesta el tráfico. (Traffic bothers Tomás.) A Lucía le interesan las recetas. (Recipes interest Lucía.) A las gemelas les molesta la cebolla. (Onion bothers the twins.) Nos interesa la música mexicana. (Mexican music interests us.)

Name one thing that bothers you and one that interests you, using me molesta and me interesa.

Words to know
interesar
to interest — Me interesa el arte. ¿Te interesan las ciencias?
molestar
to bother, annoy — Me molesta el ruido. A false friend: not the English molest.
el ruido
the noise, the sound
Check yourself

1. Which form completes the sentence? Me ___ los ruidos fuertes.

Why: Los ruidos fuertes is plural, so molestar takes molestan.

2. What does "No me interesa el béisbol" mean?

Why: Interesar means to interest; me means to me, so the sentence means I'm not interested in baseball.

3. What does molestar mean?

Why: Molestar means to bother or annoy. It is a false friend of the English word molest.
28.9

Faltar: What's Missing

Main ideaFaltar means to be missing or lacking, and the missing thing is the subject: nos falta el postre.

At the end of the story, Tomás says, "Nos falta el postre" (We’re missing dessert). (to be missing, to lack) follows the gustar pattern. Word by word, it says Dessert is missing to us. The missing thing is the subject. It is the same pattern with a new meaning.

One thing: falta. More than one: faltan. "Me faltan dos platos" (I’m missing two plates, I need two more plates). "¿Qué te falta?" (What do you need? What are you missing?). You will hear this at stores, in kitchens and when a teacher counts papers. Faltar is everywhere.

Faltar also shows how much remains. "Faltan diez minutos" (There are ten minutes left) uses no pronoun at all. Minutes are the subject. English speakers often reach for necesitar, which is fine, but faltar is what native speakers use for something missing from a set.

Think it through
Setting the table for twenty

Tomás: Tenemos dieciocho sillas. (We have eighteen chairs.) Lucía: Somos veinte. Nos faltan dos sillas. (There are twenty of us. We're missing two chairs.) Tomás: ¿Y los platos? Lucía: Nos falta uno. (We're missing one.)

You are packing for a trip. Say two things you are missing with me falta and me faltan.

Words to know
faltar
to be missing, to lack — Nos falta el postre. Me faltan dos platos.
el postre
the dessert
necesitar
to need — a regular -ar verb: necesito, necesitas, necesita
Check yourself

1. Which form completes the sentence? Nos ___ tres sillas.

Why: Tres sillas is plural and is the subject, so faltar takes faltan.

2. What does "Me falta el lápiz" mean?

Why: Faltar means to be missing: the pencil is missing to me.

3. Which sentence is correct?

Why: Cinco minutos is plural and is the subject, so the verb is faltan.
28.10

Talking About Hobbies

Main ideaCombine gustar, encantar and interesar with infinitives to describe hobbies and ask about someone else's.

At the party, cousins who haven’t met in a year catch up. "¿Qué te gusta hacer?" (What do you like to do?). This question is the heart of every getting-to-know-you conversation. Hacer means to do, and it stays an infinitive after gusta. "¿Qué le gusta hacer?" is the polite version.

Answers use the verbs from this chapter with infinitives: "Me encanta dibujar" (I love to draw). "Me interesa aprender a cocinar" (I’m interested in learning to cook). You can also add nouns: "Me gusta la música de banda" (I like banda music). Remember: infinitives take the singular verb.

Keep the conversation going by asking back. "¿Y a ti?" (And you?) turns any statement into a question. For an adult, use usted: "¿Y a usted, tía, qué le gusta hacer?" (And you, Aunt, what do you like to do?). The le shows respect, just as usted does.

Think it through
Cousins catching up

Lucía: ¿Qué te gusta hacer, Sofía? Sofía: Me encanta nadar. ¿Y a ti? Lucía: A mí me gusta cocinar. Me interesan las recetas de Abuela. Sofía: ¡A mí también!

Write a four-line dialogue with a classmate about hobbies. Use ¿y a ti? at least once.

Words to know
¿qué te gusta hacer?
what do you like to do? — the classic question about hobbies
¿y a ti?
and you? — informal; with an adult, ¿y a usted?
el pasatiempo
the hobby, pastime — Mi pasatiempo favorito es leer.
Check yourself

1. How do you ask your aunt what she likes to do, politely?

Why: For usted, the pronoun is le: ¿Qué le gusta hacer?

2. Which sentence is correct?

Why: Infinitives count as one thing, so the verb is encanta.

3. Your friend says "Me gusta nadar." How do you ask the same question back?

Why: ¿Y a ti? (And you?) turns the statement back to your friend.
