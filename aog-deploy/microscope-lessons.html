Lesson 3 of 17

Set the light

Too much light washes a clear cell away. Close the iris until the edges show. Real labs call the full routine Köhler lighting. The iris is the part you can set here.

At the eyepiece
  1. Set the iris between a quarter and three quarters open, in focus.
  2. Unstained cheek cell at 40×, iris closed to half or less.

Open the microscope

Key words

iris diaphragmcontrastKöhler lighting

After the lesson

Draw or describe the cheek cell with the iris wide open, then half closed.

Where was the iris when the edge showed best? Write the number from the knob.

Why does less light show more of a clear cell?

Which other clear slide would you try this on?

Lesson 5 of 17

Stains tell parts apart

Each stain grabs different parts. Blue shows a cheek cell's nucleus. Red shows an onion's walls. On yeast, blue tells the living from the dead.

At the eyepiece
  1. Cheek cells with methylene blue, sharp at 40×.
  2. Onion with safranin, sharp at 40×.
  3. Yeast with methylene blue, sharp at 40× or 100×.

Open the microscope

Key words

stainnucleuscell wall

After the lesson

What part turned blue in the cheek cell? What turned red in the onion?

Count 20 yeast cells. How many were blue? Blue ÷ 20 × 100 = ___% dead.

Why do living yeast cells stay clear in methylene blue?

Which stain would you use to find a nucleus, and why?

Lesson 8 of 17

Field of view math

The circle you see is 18 mm divided by the objective. At 4× that is 4.5 mm. See it on the ruler, then measure at 10×.

At the eyepiece
  1. Stage micrometer sharp at 4×.
  2. Measure something at 10× (two taps).

Open the microscope

Key words

field of viewfield numbermillimeter

After the lesson

How much of the 1 mm ruler fit across the circle at 4×?

Field = 18 mm ÷ objective. 4×: ___ mm. 10×: ___ mm. 40×: ___ mm.

If a thing fills half the field at 10×, how big is it?

What did you measure at 10×, and how long was it?

Oil immersion

At the eyepiece
  1. Oil the 100× and sharpen a cheek cell.

Open the microscope

Key words

oil immersionresolution1,000×

What did the bacteria look like at 1,000×? Rods, balls or spirals?

Total power: 10 × 100 = ___×. Field: 18 mm ÷ 100 = ___ µm.

Why does the oil help? (Hint: light bends when it leaves glass for air.)

Why use only the fine knob at 100×?

Lesson 15 of 17

Polarized light

Crossed filters make the field black. Crystals that bend light glow. Try salt and sugar, sand, and cloth. Turn the angle and watch them go dark.

At the eyepiece
  1. Polarize salt and sugar.
  2. Polarize the sand.
  3. Polarize the cloth.

Open the microscope

Key words

polarized lightcrystalextinction

After the lesson

Which things glowed? Which stayed black?

Turn the angle all the way. How many times did one grain go dark?

Salt stays black, sugar glows. What does that say about their crystals?

How could polarized light help tell two white powders apart?
