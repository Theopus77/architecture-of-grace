The book does the work

In English the person does the liking. The arrow runs from the person to the thing.

Gustar does not mean to like. It means to be pleasing. The thing you like is the subject, the person is an indirect object pronoun — me, te, le, nos, os, les — and the verb agrees with the thing: me gusta el libro, me gustan los libros.

What people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

To say I like pizza in Spanish, you say Yo gusto la pizza.

Yo gusto says that I am pleasing — that other people like me. The pizza is what pleases, so the pizza takes the verb and you take the small word: Me gusta la pizza.

Gusto means somebody likes me.

⚠️ People often think…

Gustar is an irregular verb, so its forms have to be memorized one by one.

Gustar is a regular -ar verb: gusto, gustas, gusta, gustamos, gustáis, gustan. Nothing in the verb is odd. What is turned around is the sentence — the thing is the subject, so you almost always need the third-person forms, gusta and gustan.

The verb is ordinary. The sentence is what is turned around.

⚠️ People often think…

If more than one person likes it, the verb is gustan.

The people never choose the ending. A mis primos les gusta el fútbol — several cousins, one sport, so the verb is gusta. Only the thing is counted.

Count the thing, not the people.

⚠️ People often think…

Once you name the person, A Marta, the le is not needed any more.

Spanish keeps both. A Marta le gusta el arte. The name says who; le is the pronoun the verb still needs. The same holds for a mí me gusta el café.

The name is extra. The pronoun is required.

Helpful Hints

🧭 The unit in one line

Tener means to have — and Spanish uses it for age, hunger, fear and everything you have to do. Some verbs change the vowel inside the stem in four of six forms, and those four draw a boot. Gustar runs backwards: the thing you like is the subject, so it picks gusta or gustan, and a little word — me, te, le, nos, os, les — says who is pleased.

In the boot: yo, tú, él, ellos. Outside the boot: nosotros, vosotros.

🔑 The one idea

Spanish does not always put the sentence together the way English does. You HAVE fifteen years. You HAVE hunger. The book is PLEASING to you. Learn to hear the Spanish sentence on its own terms and the endings stop fighting you.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • Age is tener, not ser. Tengo quince años — never Soy quince años.
  • Hambre and sed are nouns, so you say mucha hambre, not muy hambre.
  • Tener que never loses the que, and the verb after it stays plain: tengo que estudiar, not tengo que estudio.
  • Nosotros and vosotros never change their stem. Podemos, never puedemos.
  • Gustar agrees with the THING, not with you. Me gustan los libros, because libros is plural.
  • Yo gusto la pizza says that you are pleasing to someone. The sentence you want is Me gusta la pizza.
  • A María le gusta… keeps the le. The name tells you who; the le is still required.

🎯 How you will be asked

  • “Write all six forms of dormir and mark the boot.”
  • “Gusta or gustan?” — and the answer is decided by the thing, not by you.
  • “Say it with tener” — your age, your hunger, what you have to do tonight.
  • A wrong sentence to fix: “Yo gusto los perros.” Name the mistake, then correct it.
  • “Why do nosotros and vosotros not change?” — an explanation, not a form.

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Say all six forms of tener without stopping?
  • List eight tener expressions and translate each one?
  • Sort a new verb into e→ie, o→ue or e→i by looking at its yo form?
  • Draw the boot on a blank grid and say why two cells sit outside it?
  • Choose gusta or gustan for any sentence, and explain the choice?
  • Use encantar, interesar, molestar and doler the same way you use gustar?
