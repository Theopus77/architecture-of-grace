A More Perfect Union

Sixteen activities — eight on one era, the Constitution to Appomattox; three on the branches of government it built; and five on the Constitution unit — the document, the rights, the road a bill walks, the Illinois plan beside them, and the review that pulls the whole sheet together. History, civics, economics, and geography, and every one works on this screen or on paper. Nothing a student does leaves their computer unless they tap Send.

Civics

The three branches, one card each

Write, execute, interpret — one sentence carries the whole government, and these three practice it from all three chairs. Items 1–5 of each card are the branch itself; items 6–8 are the branches working on each other, so the other two stay warm while one is being studied. Built for quiz review, honest as evidence.

The cards run 10 → 11 → 9 on purpose: that is the order the Constitution names the branches — Article I, the legislative; Article II, the executive; Article III, the judicial.

Civics · The Constitution unit

The document, the rights, and the road

The unit in the order it holds together: first the plan itself, then the promises added to it, then how a law travels under it — and then the Illinois plan, the state mirror, for the second constitution test. Activities 12–14 are built along the class's own study outline — the same names, the same numbers, the same road — so practice never contradicts the test. Activity 14 opens with the class simulation, made digital: walk your own bill and take the wrong turns on purpose. Activity 16 is the review that gathers all of it — the class review sheet question by question, the four charts made live, and a fill-in-the-blank rehearsal with word banks.

The era

Eight ways into one era

An era is not learned by being read either. It is learned by being put in order, traced cause to effect, heard from both chairs, boiled to its main ideas, spoken in its own vocabulary, found on the map, argued with evidence, and met person by person. That is what these eight do — one era, eight different jobs, each with three sets and three doors.

Order the Story

Four event cards, one order. History as one thing making the next thing possible — the years appear only after the story lands.

Open →

Because of That

One cause, four true effects, one that grew from it. Items 6–8 are chains: the effect turns around and causes the next thing.

Whose Voice?

A line in the manner of the time — a mill worker, a planter, a soldier of each side. Hear whose chair it comes from. Perspective, practiced.

The Main Idea

Short informational passages, four answers in four sizes: just right, too small, too big, not said. The traps are named out loud.

Words of a New Nation

Map of a Growing Nation

Back It Up

A claim and four sentences: the true support, the other side's evidence, an opinion in new clothes, a fact that never connects. Position writing, made tappable.

Washington to Tubman to Sequoyah: read the risk each person chose, name them, then name the ripple. Positive risk-taking, straight from the record.

The essential questions

Every activity keeps landing on the same four questions. They have no final answers — that is what makes them essential — and a student who leaves this unit still arguing about them has learned it.

1Can a country disagree and still hold together?
2Who gets a say — and who decides who gets a say?
3Can one person change a nation?
4Does where you live change how you live?
For the teacher

<b>Eight items, three sets, on every activity.</b> Items 1–5 and 6–8 are two named sub-skills — the same split in every set, so the band grid on the printed record stays true when you switch a student to Set 2 mid-week. Sets 2 and 3 are the same eight jobs on different content, so the same student can be probed three weeks running without being measured on memory.

<b>Three doors on every page.</b> Show me one first is a worked example — instruction, never a trial. The eight items are the middle door. A finished set opens One more, if you want it: a stretch with two tries and the thinking shown warmly either way, and declining it is named a strong move. One page serves the student who needs a model, the student who needs the walk, and the student who needs one more hill.

<b>The three branch cards are quiz review.</b> Items 1–5 of each drill the branch itself; 6–8 fold the other two branches back in, so earlier quizzes stay warm while the next one is studied. Every wrong answer's feedback names the branch that answer really belongs to — the miss is the review.

<b>Independent, supported, hints, confidence.</b> The teacher panel at the bottom of each activity builds the probe row for you, and Print this record turns it into a one-page probe sheet with the item-by-item map. A hint is never a penalty — it is the thing that tells you what to teach next.

1

<b>Send a link, get a row back.</b> Share an activity's address, or put it on the board with the button under each card. When the page can reach your Sheet, a Send button appears after a finished set — one tap posts one row to the <b>Practice</b> tab: activity, set, independent, supported, hints, confidence, and the first name the student types. Whether the demo and the stretch were used rides along as information, never as a score.

2

<b>A bare link stays private.</b> On a device with no path to your Sheet the Send button never appears, and the lock section on every page tells the student the truth for the mode it is in — including that it has no way to send anything at all.

3

<b>Paper is a full citizen.</b> Every page prints as a real worksheet — the same eight asks with lines to write on — and the timeline above prints as the plain dated list. A student with no device and a student with one are doing the same work.
