Dissolved is not gone

A cup of water on a scale, and a spoon of salt beside it. Weigh them together: 210 grams.

Dissolved is not gone — it is just too small to see. The scale proves it: salt water weighs the salt plus the water. If the scale ever reads LESS, something escaped as a gas.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

A gas has no weight — air weighs nothing.

Weigh a flat basketball, then pump it up and weigh it again. It is heavier. Gases are matter, and matter has mass, even the kind you cannot see.

Gases have mass. Weigh the balloon.

⚠️ People often think…

Big things are heavy and small things are light.

Size and mass are two different questions. A beach ball takes up more room than a brick and weighs far less. Mass is how MUCH matter, not how much room it takes.

Mass is how much matter, not how much room.

Particles, states, properties, changes

1 Watch one

A log burns and the ash weighs much less. Was matter destroyed?

  1. Ask what you can still see: ash. Much lighter than the log.
  2. Ask what you cannot see: smoke and gases went up into the air.
  3. Those gases have mass. They just left the scale.
  4. Nothing was destroyed — it moved. Catch the gases and the weight matches. ✓

Helpful Hints

🪜 From a particle to a burning log — the ladder

Matter is made of particles → packed tight (solid), sliding (liquid), flying apart (gas) → heat and cold move it between the three → properties tell substances apart → mixtures can be separated by those properties → physical changes keep the substance, chemical changes make a new one → and the weight before always equals the weight after.

Particles, states, properties, changes, weight.

🤝 The whole topic in one idea

Matter is never made or lost — only rearranged. Melt it, dissolve it, mix it, even burn it: the same particles are all still somewhere, and a good scale will find every one of them.

Rearranged, never gone.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Dissolved is NOT gone. Salt water weighs the salt plus the water.
  • A gas has weight. Air in a balloon, fizz from a cup — it all counts on the scale.
  • Melting and dissolving are PHYSICAL. Burning, rusting and baking are CHEMICAL.
  • If the scale reads less, a gas escaped — matter wasn’t destroyed.
  • Steam is still water. Ice is still water. The particles never changed.
  • Big isn’t heavy. Mass is how much matter, not how much room.

🧊 Three states — the chart

StateParticlesShape and sizeExample
solidpacked tight, vibrating in placekeeps bothice, rock
liquidclose, sliding past each othertakes the container’s shape, keeps its sizewater, milk
gasfar apart, flying fastfills any containerair, steam
Heat it: solid → liquid → gas. Cool it: back again.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • Three boxes of dots — “which is the solid, liquid, gas?” (tight, sliding, spread).
  • Salt plus water on a scale — “what does it weigh after?” (add them).
  • A list of changes — “physical or chemical?” (same substance, or a new one?).
  • A mixture — “how would you separate it?” (find the property only one part has).
