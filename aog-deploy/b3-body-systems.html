Follow one breath of air

Air comes into the lungs. That part is easy — but the lungs are not where it is used.

Three systems did one job: respiratory, circulatory, and the cells that use the oxygen. No system works alone — and that is what almost every question on this test is really asking.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Blood without oxygen in it is blue.

Blood is never blue. Low-oxygen blood is dark red. Veins look blue through skin, which is about light, not about blood.

Bright red or dark red. Never blue.

⚠️ People often think…

The stomach does the digesting.

The stomach starts it. The small intestine does most of the breakdown AND nearly all of the absorption into the blood.

Breakdown and absorption: small intestine.

⚠️ People often think…

You breathe so your lungs get oxygen.

The lungs are a doorway, not a destination. You breathe to get oxygen to your CELLS, where respiration releases energy — and to carry away the CO₂ that makes.

The oxygen is for the cells.

⚠️ People often think…

Your brain decides to pull your hand off something hot.

A reflex skips the brain. The signal loops through the spinal cord and back, which is why your hand is already moving before it hurts.

Moved first, felt it second. That is the spinal cord.

Name the systems, name the teamwork

You run up the stairs. Why do you breathe harder?

  1. Your muscles need more energy.
  2. Energy comes from respiration inside cells.
  3. That needs oxygen and makes CO₂.
  4. Breathing harder brings oxygen in and dumps CO₂ out.

Helpful Hints

🪜 Levels of organization — smallest to biggest

Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ System → Organism. A tissue is a team of similar cells; an organ is different tissues working together; a system is organs teaming up.

You’re built like LEGO — small pieces, bigger pieces, whole build.

🤝 The whole topic in one idea

No system works alone. Every question is secretly asking: which systems team up, and how does the teamwork keep conditions stable (homeostasis)?

Name the systems. Name the teamwork. Name the balance.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Your blood is never blue — low-oxygen blood is dark red.
  • Digestion isn’t finished in the stomach — the small intestine does most breakdown AND absorption.
  • You breathe to deliver oxygen to CELLS for respiration — and to dump the CO₂ it makes.
  • Nerves are fast, hormones are slow — but hormone effects last much longer.
  • Reflexes skip the brain — they loop through the spinal cord.

🚚 Who delivers what

  • Digestive: breaks food into nutrients the blood can carry.
  • Respiratory: loads oxygen in, dumps carbon dioxide out.
  • Circulatory: the delivery service — carries everything everywhere.
  • Excretory: filters the blood and removes liquid waste (kidneys!).
  • Nervous + endocrine: the managers — electric messages and chemical messages.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • Expect a “trace the path” question: follow a bite of food, a breath, or a nerve signal in order.
  • Exercise scenarios are THE systems-interacting question: heart up, breathing up, sweating — name the systems.
  • “Maintain stable internal conditions” = homeostasis + at least two systems.

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Order the five levels of organization?
  • Trace a bite of pizza from mouth to bloodstream?
  • Trace a breath from nose to a muscle cell?
  • Name the stimulus→response path through the nervous system?
  • Explain sweating AND shivering as homeostasis?
