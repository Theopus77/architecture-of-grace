Which way does the arrow point?

The land first: mountains, a valley with a river, and a flat coast where the river meets the sea.

Place shapes people, and people shape place. Every question in this unit is one of two: what did the land make possible here, or what did the people do to the land?

What people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Latitude and longitude are easy to tell apart by their names.

Most people mix them up under pressure. Latitude lines run flat across, like the rungs of a ladder, and tell you how far north or south. Longitude lines are the long ones running pole to pole.

Latitude = ladder rungs = north/south.

⚠️ People often think…

Africa is a country.

Africa is a continent of 54 countries, with thousands of languages and climates ranging from desert to rainforest. And a region is not a country either — a region can sit inside one country or stretch across many.

Continent, country, region — three different sizes.

⚠️ People often think…

Push and pull factors are two names for the same reason.

Push factors make people LEAVE: war, drought, no work. Pull factors draw people IN: jobs, safety, family already there. Most moves have both, and the test wants you to name which is which.

Push is behind you. Pull is ahead of you.

⚠️ People often think…

If a city is near the equator, it is hot.

Altitude cools things down. Quito sits almost exactly on the equator and stays in the 60s all year, because it is nearly two miles up. Latitude is one factor, not the only one.

How far north, AND how high up.

⚠️ People often think…

You can read a graph by looking at which bar is tallest.

Read the axis first. One graph counts in millions and the next in billions, and a shorter bar on the second can be a thousand times bigger than a taller bar on the first. The bars are a picture; the axis is the fact.

Axis before bars. Every single time.

Worth knowing cold

The word pairs that decide the question

Almost every mistake in this unit is one of these seven mixed up with its partner.

  1. Latitude Flat lines running across. Tells you how far north or south.
  2. Longitude Long lines running pole to pole. Tells you how far east or west.
  3. Continent One of seven huge landmasses. Africa is one of them.
  4. Country A nation with a government. Africa holds 54 of them.
  5. Region An area that shares something. It can cross borders.
  6. Push factor A reason to leave: war, drought, no work.
  7. Pull factor A reason to come: jobs, safety, family already there.

Helpful Hints

🪜 From a spot on the map to a way of life — the ladder

Latitude and longitude (where) → landforms, water and climate (what’s there) → resources (what can be used) → population (who came) → culture and economy (how they live) → region (what they share).

Where, what, who, how, shared.

🤝 The whole topic in one idea

Place shapes people and people shape place. Every question is asking one of two things: what did the land make possible here, or what did the people do to the land?

Land → people, or people → land?

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Latitude = north/south (the flat rungs). Longitude = east/west (the long lines).
  • A region isn’t a country — it can be part of one or cross many.
  • Push factors make people LEAVE; pull factors draw them IN.
  • Near the equator isn’t always hot — altitude cools Quito and Nairobi.
  • Africa is a continent of 54 countries, not a country.
  • Read a graph’s axis before its bars — millions and billions are very different.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A population map with dark clusters — “why here?” (water, flat land, mild climate, jobs).
  • Two cities at the same latitude with different climates — “why?” (altitude, ocean, mountains).
  • A list of features — “physical or human?” (was it there before people?).
  • A migration story — “name the push and the pull.”

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Name the seven continents and five oceans?
  • Sort ten features into physical and human?
  • Explain why people cluster where they do, in one sentence?
  • Give a push and a pull factor for one real migration?
  • Read a bar graph of population by continent and say what it shows?
