Shapes and Equal Shares

Geometry

Look around you. A book is a rectangle. A clock is a circle. A ball is a sphere. Shapes are in every room and on every street. In this unit you learn to name them. You count sides and corners. You find solids that roll and solids that stack.

Then you learn to cut shapes into equal parts. Two kids share a sandwich. Four kids share a pizza. Each one wants a fair share. You will cut halves, thirds and fourths. You will see that a fair share is an equal share.

By the end you can name a shape by its sides. You can build a big shape from small ones. You can cut a whole into equal parts and name each part. That is a lot of power for a pair of scissors.

The big question

How do we name a shape, and how do we cut it so every share is fair?

How we figured it out
Step 1

Look at a flat shape. Is it round or does it have straight sides?

Step 2

Count the sides. Three, four or six?

Step 3

Count the corners. Every straight side meets another at a corner.

Step 4

Say the name: triangle, square, rectangle, hexagon or circle.

Step 5

Pick up a solid. Does it roll, stack or both?

Step 6

Name the solid: cube, sphere, cone or cylinder.

Step 7

Join small shapes to build a bigger one.

Step 8

Fold a shape in half. Check that the two parts match.

Step 9

Cut thirds and fourths. Count the equal parts.

Step 10

Share with friends. Give everyone an equal part.

Flat and Solid Shapes

Geometry
Big questionHow do we know a shape is a triangle, no matter how big it is or how it turns?
The story

The Block City

A class builds a city out of blocks and starts to see shapes everywhere.

On Friday the blocks came out. Mia and Jon built a tall tower. Ava built a bridge. Sam made a road with long flat blocks. Soon the rug was a whole city. It had houses, a school and a park.

Then Ms. Lee asked a question. "What shapes do you see?" Mia looked at the side of her tower. "A square!" she said. Jon looked at the roof. "A triangle. It has three sides." Ava found a circle on the end of a round block.

Sam held up a block. It looked like a can of soup. "What is this one?" No one knew the name. Ms. Lee smiled. "That is a cylinder. It rolls and it stacks." Sam rolled it down the road.

The class walked around the city. They found squares, rectangles, triangles and circles. They found cubes, cones and spheres. Shapes were hiding in every block. They just had to look.

Talk about itLook around your room right now. What is one shape you can see, and how do you know its name?
Section 1

Naming Flat Shapes

9.1

Sides and Corners

Main ideaWe name a flat shape by counting its sides and corners.

Look at a slice of pizza. It has three straight edges. A shape with three straight is a . Where two sides meet, there is a . A triangle has three corners too.

Now look at a cracker. It has four sides. All four are the same length. All four corners are square, like the corner of a book. That shape is a square.

A door has four sides too. But two sides are long and two are short. That shape is a rectangle. To name a shape, count the sides. Then look at the corners.

Think it through
Count the Sides

Picture a yield sign on the road. It has three straight sides. Now picture a stop sign next to it. Count its sides in your head. It has eight. A stop sign is not a triangle. It is a shape called an octagon.

How many corners does the yield sign have? How do you know without counting them?

Words to know
side
one straight edge of a flat shape
corner
the point where two sides of a shape meet
triangle
a flat shape with three straight sides and three corners
Check yourself

1. A shape has three straight sides. What is it?

Why: Three straight sides make a triangle. A square has four sides. A circle has no straight sides.

2. How many corners does a square have?

Why: A square has four sides, and each pair of sides meets at a corner. That makes 4 corners.

3. A shape has four sides. Two are long and two are short. What is it?

Why: Four sides with two long and two short make a rectangle. A triangle has three sides. A circle has none.
9.2

Hexagons and Circles

Main ideaA hexagon has six sides; a circle is round with no sides at all.

Look at a honeycomb. Each little room has six sides. A shape with six straight sides is a . It has six corners too. Count them: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.

Now look at a clock face or a coin. It is round. It has no straight sides. It has no corners. That shape is a . Every spot on the edge is the same distance from the middle.

Here is a common mistake. Some kids think a big triangle is a different shape than a small one. It is not. Size does not change the name. Only the sides and corners do.

Read the numbers
Sides on Each Shape

standard counts of straight sides for each flat shape

Which two shapes have the same number of sides? How can you tell them apart?

Words to know
hexagon
a flat shape with six straight sides and six corners
circle
a round flat shape with no straight sides and no corners
Check yourself

1. How many sides does a hexagon have?

Why: A hexagon has six sides and six corners, like one room in a honeycomb.

2. Which shape has no corners?

Why: A circle is round with no straight sides, so it has no corners. Triangles have 3 and hexagons have 6.

3. A tiny triangle and a huge triangle. Are they the same shape?

Why: Both have three straight sides and three corners. Size does not change a shape's name.
Section 2

Solid Shapes

9.3

Cubes and Spheres

Main ideaSolid shapes take up space; a cube has flat square faces and a sphere is round all over.

A drawing of a square is flat. A dice is not flat. You can hold it. It is a shape. Solid shapes take up space. A dice is a . Every face is a square.

Count the faces on a dice. Top, bottom, front, back, left, right. That is 6 faces. A cube has 6 square faces. It stacks well because the faces are flat.

A ball is a solid shape too. It is round all over. It has no flat faces and no corners. That shape is a . A sphere rolls any way you push it.

Think it through
Roll or Stack?

Picture a cube and a sphere on a table. Push the cube. It slides and stops. Push the sphere. It rolls away. Now try to put a cube on top of another cube. It stays. Try to put a sphere on top of a sphere. It falls off.

Why does a cube stack but a sphere does not? What part of each shape makes the difference?

Words to know
solid
a shape that takes up space, not flat, like a block or a ball
cube
a solid shape with 6 flat square faces, like a dice
sphere
a solid shape that is round all over, like a ball
Check yourself

1. How many faces does a cube have?

Why: A cube has 6 faces: top, bottom, front, back, left and right. Each face is a square.

2. Which solid shape is round all over?

Why: A sphere is round all over, like a ball. A cube has flat faces. A square is flat, not solid.

3. What shape is each face of a cube?

Why: Every face of a cube is a square with four equal sides. That is what makes it a cube.
9.4

Cones and Cylinders

Main ideaA cylinder has two flat circle faces; a cone has one circle face and a point.

Look at a can of soup. The top is a circle. The bottom is a circle. The side is curved. That solid shape is a . A cylinder stacks on its flat end. It rolls on its side.

Now look at a party hat or an ice cream cone. The bottom is a circle. The top comes to a point. That shape is a . A cone stands on its circle. It cannot stack on its point.

Some kids mix up a cylinder and a cone. Check the ends. Two circles means a cylinder. One circle and a point means a cone.

Read the numbers
Flat Faces on Solids

standard counts of flat faces on each solid shape

Which solid has the most flat faces? Which has none? Which one could you not stand a cup on?

Words to know
cylinder
a solid shape with two flat circle ends and a curved side, like a can
cone
a solid shape with one flat circle and a point, like a party hat
Check yourself

1. Which solid shape has two flat circle ends?

Why: A cylinder has a circle on top and a circle on the bottom, like a can. A cone has only one.

2. A party hat has one circle and a point. What shape is it?

Why: One flat circle plus a point on top makes a cone. A sphere has no flat face. A cylinder has two.

3. How many flat faces does a cone have?

Why: A cone has just 1 flat face, the circle it stands on. The rest is curved and ends at a point.
Section 3

Building With Shapes

9.5

Putting Shapes Together

Main ideaSmall shapes can join to make a bigger shape.

Take two squares that are the same size. Put them side by side. Now you have one long shape. It has four sides. Two are long and two are short. Two squares make a rectangle.

Take a square and cut it from corner to corner. You get two triangles. Put them back together. The square is back. Shapes can split apart and up again.

Try this with six triangles. Put their points in the middle. They make a hexagon. Building shapes from shapes is how blocks make a city.

Think it through
Two Triangles

Picture two triangles that are the same size. Each one looks like half a square. Put the two long sides together. What do you see? Now turn one of them around. Put the short sides together. You get a different shape. Same two pieces, two different results.

What shape do two of these triangles make when the long sides touch? What new shape could you make with four of them?

Words to know
join
to put two or more shapes together to make a new shape
Check yourself

1. Two same-size squares side by side make what shape?

Why: Two squares in a row make a shape with two long sides and two short sides. That is a rectangle.

2. Cut a square from corner to corner. What do you get?

Why: The cut makes two shapes with three sides each. Each one is a triangle.

3. Six triangles with points in the middle make what shape?

Why: The six outside edges become six sides. Six sides make a hexagon.
9.6

Drawing a Shape

Main ideaTo draw a shape, decide the number of sides and corners first.

Ms. Lee says, "Draw a shape with four sides." You could draw a square. You could draw a rectangle. Both have four sides. Both are right. The tells you what counts.

Now she says, "Draw a shape with three corners." That is a triangle. It can be tall or short. It can point up or sideways. It is still a triangle if it has three sides and three corners.

Here is a trap. A shape with four sides but one side bent is not a square. Sides must be straight. Check each side with a ruler if you are not sure.

Think it through
Follow the Rule

Jon drew a shape with four straight sides. All the sides are the same length. All the corners are square. Ava drew a shape with four straight sides too. Two sides are longer than the others. Both followed the rule "four sides." But they drew different shapes.

What did Jon draw? What did Ava draw? Could both drawings be right?

Words to know
rule
what a shape must have, like four sides or three corners
Check yourself

1. The rule is "three sides." Which shape fits?

Why: A triangle has three sides. A square has four and a hexagon has six.

2. Which shape fits the rule "four sides"?

Why: A rectangle has four straight sides. A circle has none. A triangle has three.

3. A shape has four sides, but one side is curved. Is it a square?

Why: A square needs four straight sides. A curved side breaks the rule, so it is not a square.
9.7

Shapes Around Us

Main ideaShapes are everywhere: in signs, buildings and floors.

Walk down a street in Chicago. A stop sign has eight sides. A window is a rectangle. A manhole cover is a circle. Shapes are on every block.

Look at a floor made of . The tiles are often squares. They fit with no gaps. Squares fit together well because their corners are square. Hexagon tiles fit too.

Look up at a tall building. Its sides are rectangles. A roof may be a triangle. Now find a solid. A trash can is a cylinder. A ball in the park is a sphere.

Think it through
A Walk to School

Picture your walk to school. You pass a red stop sign with eight sides. You pass a house with a door. The door has four sides. The roof comes to a point with three sides. At school the clock on the wall is round. The tiles on the floor are squares.

Name the shape of the door, the roof and the clock. Which solid shape is a trash can?

Words to know
tile
a flat shape used to cover a floor or wall with no gaps
Check yourself

1. A window with four sides, two long and two short, is what shape?

Why: Four sides with two long and two short make a rectangle.

2. A trash can with a round top and bottom is what solid?

Why: A round top, a round bottom and a curved side make a cylinder, like a can.

3. Why do square tiles cover a floor with no gaps?

Why: Four square corners meet at a point and fill the space, so square tiles leave no gaps.

Halves, Thirds and Fourths

Fractions Begin
Big questionWhat makes a share fair, and how can we cut a shape so every share is the same?
The story

One Sandwich, Two Kids

Two hungry kids, one sandwich, and a fight over the word fair.

Ben and Rosa got home from school. They were hungry. There was one sandwich on the plate. Ben grabbed a knife. He cut the sandwich. One piece was big. One piece was small. Ben took the big one.

"That is not fair!" said Rosa. "Your piece is bigger." Ben looked at the two pieces. He knew she was right. Two pieces is not the same as two equal pieces. Fair means the same size.

Their dad came in. He put the pieces back together. "Let's try again," he said. He cut the sandwich down the middle. Now both pieces were the same. "Each of you gets one half," he said.

Rosa held up her piece. "Half," she said. Ben held up his. "Half." Two halves. One whole sandwich. Both kids were happy. And both learned a new word for fair.

Talk about itBen cut the sandwich into two pieces. Why was that still not fair? What did the second cut do differently?
Section 1

Two Equal Parts

10.1

What Half Means

Main ideaA half is one of two equal parts of a whole.

Take a paper circle. Fold it so the edges match. Open it up. The fold cuts the circle into two parts. The two parts are the same size. They are . Each part is one .

Now take a paper square. Cut it into two parts. But make one part big and one part small. Are those halves? No. Halves must be equal. Two unequal pieces are not halves.

Two halves make one whole. Put the two halves of the circle back together. The whole circle is back. Say it like this: one half and one half make one whole.

Think it through
Fold and Check

Picture a square piece of paper. Fold it so the top edge lands right on the bottom edge. Press the fold. Open it. You see two parts. Put one part on top of the other in your mind. They match. That is how you know they are equal.

What is each part called? If you fold the paper the other way, from left to right, are the parts still halves?

Words to know
equal
the same size or the same amount
half
one of two equal parts of a whole
whole
the full thing before it is cut into parts
Check yourself

1. A circle is cut into two equal parts. What is each part called?

Why: Two equal parts of a whole are halves. Each part is one half.

2. A square is cut into one big piece and one small piece. Are they halves?

Why: Halves are two equal parts. A big piece and a small piece are not equal, so they are not halves.

3. How many halves make one whole?

Why: One half and one half make one whole. That is 2 halves.
10.2

Halves Can Look Different

Main ideaTwo halves of the same whole are equal even if they are different shapes.

Take two paper squares the same size. Cut the first one straight down the middle. You get two tall rectangles. Each one is a half.

Cut the second square from corner to corner. You get two triangles. Each one is a half too. A rectangle half and a triangle half look different. But they came from the same square. They are the same size.

Here is the rule. Halves of the same whole are always equal in size. Their shape can be different. Kids often think the triangle half is smaller. Check by cutting. It is not.

Think it through
Two Ways to Cut

Picture a square brownie. Mia cuts hers straight across. Her two halves are rectangles. Jon cuts his from corner to corner. His two halves are triangles. Both brownies were the same size to start. Mia takes one of her halves. Jon takes one of his.

Who has more brownie, Mia or Jon? How do you know?

Words to know
shape
the outline of a part, like a triangle or a rectangle
Check yourself

1. A square cut corner to corner makes two halves. What shape are they?

Why: A corner-to-corner cut makes two pieces with three sides each. They are triangles.

2. A rectangle half and a triangle half come from the same square. Which is bigger?

Why: Both are one half of the same square, so they are the same size even though they look different.

3. Can two halves of one whole be different shapes?

Why: Halves must be equal in size, not in shape. A square can be halved into rectangles or into triangles.
Section 2

Three and Four Parts

10.3

Thirds

Main ideaA third is one of three equal parts of a whole.

Three friends share one granola bar. They cut it into three equal pieces. Each piece is one . Each friend gets one third of the bar.

To cut a rectangle into thirds, make two cuts. Not three. Two cuts make three pieces. Make the pieces the same size. Check by laying one piece on another.

Three thirds make one whole. Put the three pieces back. The bar is whole again. A common mistake is to make three cuts. That gives four pieces, not three.

Read the numbers
Cuts and Pieces

cutting a bar straight across, one cut at a time

How many cuts make thirds? How many cuts would you need for five equal pieces?

Words to know
third
one of three equal parts of a whole
Check yourself

1. A bar is cut into three equal parts. What is each part called?

Why: Three equal parts of a whole are thirds. Each part is one third.

2. How many cuts across a bar make three pieces?

Why: Each cut adds one piece. Start with 1 piece. Two cuts make 1 + 2 = 3 pieces.

3. How many thirds make one whole?

Why: One third plus one third plus one third makes one whole. That is 3 thirds.
10.4

Fourths and Quarters

Main ideaA fourth, also called a quarter, is one of four equal parts.

Four kids share a small pizza. They cut it into four equal slices. Each slice is one . Another name for one fourth is one . Both words mean the same thing.

To cut a square into fourths, fold it in half. Then fold it in half again. Open it. You see four equal squares. Four fourths make one whole.

Fourths are smaller than halves. Think about the pizza. Cut it in half, and you get a big slice. Cut it in fourths, and you get a smaller slice. More parts means smaller parts.

Read the numbers
Slices in One Pizza

one whole pizza cut into equal slices

Which cut gives the smallest slice? Which cut gives the biggest? Why?

Words to know
fourth
one of four equal parts of a whole
quarter
another name for one fourth, like a quarter coin is one fourth of a dollar
Check yourself

1. What is another name for one fourth?

Why: One fourth and one quarter both mean one of four equal parts.

2. How many fourths make one whole?

Why: Four equal parts put together make the whole. So 4 fourths make one whole.

3. Which is smaller, one half of a pizza or one fourth of the same pizza?

Why: Cutting into more parts makes smaller parts. Four parts are smaller than two, so one fourth is smaller.
Section 3

Rows, Columns and Fair Shares

10.5

Squares in Rows and Columns

Main ideaA rectangle can be cut into rows and columns of same-size squares.

Look at a chocolate bar. It is made of small squares. The squares go across in . They go up and down in . Count the rows: 2. Count the squares in each row: 3.

Now count all the squares. Row one has 3. Row two has 3. That is 3 + 3 = 6 squares. You can also add the columns: 2 + 2 + 2 = 6. Same answer.

Try a bigger bar. It has 3 rows. Each row has 4 squares. Add: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. The bar has 12 squares. Counting by rows is faster than counting one by one.

Read the numbers
Squares in a Bar

a chocolate bar with 4 squares in each row

What pattern do you see as you add a row? How many squares would 5 rows of 4 have?

Words to know
row
a line of squares that goes across, from left to right
column
a line of squares that goes up and down
Check yourself

1. A bar has 2 rows. Each row has 3 squares. How many squares in all?

Why: Add the rows: 3 + 3 = 6 squares. Adding 2 + 3 = 5 is a mistake, since that mixes rows and squares.

2. A bar has 3 rows of 4 squares. How many squares in all?

Why: Add 4 three times: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. Adding 3 + 4 = 7 is the common mistake.

3. A line of squares going across a bar is called what?

Why: A row goes across, left to right. A column goes up and down.
10.6

Sharing Fairly

Main ideaA fair share means every person gets an equal part of the whole.

Two kids share one pancake. Cut it into halves. Each kid gets one half. Three kids share one pancake. Cut it into thirds. Each kid gets one third.

Four kids share one pancake. Cut it into fourths. Each kid gets one fourth. Count the people. Cut that many equal parts. That is a .

Watch out for this mistake. Ben cut a pancake into two big pieces and one small one for three kids. Three pieces, but not equal. That is not thirds. Not fair.

Think it through
The Birthday Cake

Picture a square birthday cake. Four cousins want cake. Their aunt cuts it once down the middle. Then she cuts it once across the middle. Now there are four equal squares. Each cousin gets one. Then a fifth cousin walks in. Everyone looks at the cake.

What is each cousin's share called? What could the aunt have done if she knew five cousins were coming?

Words to know
fair share
a part that is the same size as everyone else's part
Check yourself

1. Three kids share one pancake fairly. What does each kid get?

Why: Three people means three equal parts. Each part is one third.

2. Four kids share one cake. How many equal pieces should it have?

Why: One piece for each of the 4 kids. So the cake needs 4 equal pieces, or fourths.

3. A pancake is cut into three pieces. Two are big and one is small. Is that fair for three kids?

Why: Fair shares must be equal. Three pieces of different sizes are not thirds, so it is not fair.
