Then and Now, and Our Country

History and Civics

Grandma was a kid once. She rode a train with a paper ticket. She dialed a phone with her finger. Her school had a bell and a chalkboard. Some things she did, you still do. Some things are gone. Old photos and old objects are clues. They show us the past.

Our country has a past too. It has a flag with fifty stars. It has an eagle, a bell and a statue. It has holidays that remember people and big days. And it has citizens. That is you. In this unit you will learn how to read clues from long ago, and what it means to vote, help and speak up.

The big question

How do we know about long ago, and what do we share as a country today?

The story

Grandma's Shoebox

A box under the bed holds a phone with a dial, an old ticket and a photo of a girl.

Maya is at Grandma's house in Chicago. She finds a shoebox under the bed. The box is dusty. Grandma smiles. "Open it," she says. "Those are my things from long ago."

Inside is a heavy black phone. It has a round dial with holes. No screen. No buttons. "You put your finger in a hole and turn it," Grandma says. "One number at a time. It took a while."

Under the phone is a paper ticket. It is small and yellow. It says "Chicago, Illinois." Grandma rode a train with it when she was six. Under the ticket is a photo. A girl stands in front of a school. She has braids.

"Who is that?" Maya asks. "That is me," says Grandma. "That was my school." Maya looks at the girl in the photo. Then she looks at Grandma. Same smile. Long ago, Grandma was a kid too.

Talk about itWhat is one old thing in your home? What can it tell you about long ago?

Yesterday, Today, Tomorrow

Main ideaThe past is before now, the present is now, and the future is after now.

Think about yesterday. What did you eat? What did you play? Yesterday is over. It is in the . The past is all the time before now.

Now think about right now. You are reading. You are sitting. This is the . The present is now, this very minute.

Tomorrow has not come yet. It is in the . The future is the time after now. Long ago is the past. Someday is the future. Today is the present.

Main ideaOld photos and objects are clues that tell us about the past.

An old photo is a clue. Look at the clothes. Look at the cars. Look at the street. Each one tells you something. A photo is a piece of the past you can hold.

Grandma’s dial phone is a clue too. It shows how people called each other. Old things like this are called . An artifact is an object made by people long ago.

Things That Changed

7.3

Getting From Here to There

Main ideaHow people travel has changed a lot over time.

Long ago, there were no cars. People walked. Or they rode a horse. Or a horse pulled their wagon. A trip to the next town took all day.

Then came trains. A train ran on rails. It carried many people fast. In Chicago, trains ran on tracks up in the air. People called it the "L." It is still there today.

Then came cars, buses and planes. Today a car ride to the next town takes minutes. A plane can cross the country in one day. got faster and faster.

Think it through
The Same Trip

Look at the road to Grandma's town. Long ago, a boy rode there in a wagon. The horse walked all day. The boy got there at night. Today, a girl rides there in a car. She leaves after lunch. She gets there before dinner.

What changed? What stayed the same about the trip?

7.4

Cooking and Talking

Main ideaCooking and talking to far-away people changed as new tools were made.

Long ago, people cooked on a wood . First you had to chop wood. Then you lit a fire. Then you waited for the stove to get hot. Cooking took a long time.

Today many people cook on a gas or electric stove. You turn a knob. It gets hot right away. Some people use a microwave. Dinner can be ready in minutes.

Talking changed too. Long ago, people wrote letters. A letter took days to arrive. Then came the phone with a dial. Today we use cell phones. We can talk or text right away.

Think it through
Two Kitchens

Which kitchen is from long ago? How can you tell?

2. How did people talk to far-away friends long ago?

Why: Long ago there were no phones. People wrote letters that took days to arrive.

3. Why is cooking faster today?

Why: Gas and electric stoves get hot right away. No wood to chop, no fire to build.
7.5

School Long Ago

Main ideaSchools long ago had one room, one teacher and no computers.

Long ago, many towns had a . All the kids sat in one room. Little kids and big kids. One teacher taught them all.

Kids wrote on small boards called slates. They used chalk. Then they wiped it off. There were no computers. There was no bus. Kids walked to school, even in snow.

Some things are the same. Kids learned to read. Kids did math. Kids played at recess. Ask a grown-up what their school was like. What did they have? What did they not have?

A Town in One Hundred Years

Main ideaA town can change a lot in one hundred years, and old maps and photos show how.

Picture a small town one hundred years ago. It has a dirt road. It has a store, a church and a school. Farms are all around it. Horses stand by the store.

How do we know the town ? We look at old maps. We look at old photos. We count the people. Chicago was a small town once. Now it is a big city.

7.7

Families, Play and Food

Main ideaSome things stay the same over time, like families, play and eating together.

Not everything changes. Long ago, kids lived with families. Families cared for each other. That is still true today. Families are a thing that stayed the .

The Class Pet Gets a Name

A new hamster needs a name. Twenty-two kids have ideas. How will they choose?

Room 12 has a new class pet. It is a small brown hamster. It runs on its wheel. It stuffs seeds in its cheeks. Everyone loves it. But it has no name.

"I want Peanut!" says Jae. "No, Biscuit!" says Ana. "Rocket!" says Sam. Soon everyone is shouting. Ms. Ortiz raises her hand. The room gets quiet. "We will vote," she says.

Ms. Ortiz writes three names on the board: Peanut, Biscuit, Rocket. Each kid gets one slip of paper. Each kid writes one name. No peeking. The slips go in a jar.

Ms. Ortiz counts. Peanut gets six. Rocket gets seven. Biscuit gets nine. "Biscuit wins," she says. Jae wanted Peanut. But he shrugs. "That was fair," he says. "Hi, Biscuit."

8.2

The Pledge Is a Promise

Main ideaThe Pledge of Allegiance is a promise to be loyal to our country.

Many schools say the in the morning. Kids stand up. They face the flag. They put a hand on their heart. They say the words together.

A pledge is a promise. The Pledge says we will be to our country. Loyal means you stick with something and care for it. It says our country is one country, all together.

The Pledge ends with "liberty and justice for all." Liberty means being free. Justice means being fair. For all means everyone. That is the promise.

Symbols of Our Country

8.3

The Bald Eagle

Main ideaThe bald eagle is a symbol of our country because it is strong and free.

A big bird flies over a river. It has a white head. It has a yellow beak. Its wings are wide. It is a . It is not really bald. Its head feathers are white.

Long ago, the leaders of our country picked the eagle as a symbol. The eagle is strong. It flies high and free. Eagles live only in North America. That made it a good pick.

Look for the eagle. It is on coins. It is on dollar bills. It is on the seal of the president. Bald eagles live in Illinois too. You can see them by the Mississippi River in winter.

8.6

Days for Our Country

Main ideaIndependence Day is our country's birthday, and Veterans Day thanks people who served.

On the Fourth of July we have . It is our country’s birthday. On July 4, 1776, our leaders signed a paper. It said we were our own country. That was about 250 years ago.

Being a Good Citizen

8.7

People Who Made a Difference

Main ideaLincoln, Tubman, Chavez and Addams each helped other people and made things fairer.

Abraham Lincoln lived in Illinois. He became president. Back then, some people were held as . A slave is a person who is owned by someone else and is not free. Lincoln worked to end slavery.

Harriet Tubman was a slave. She ran away to be free. Then she went back. Again and again. She led many other people to freedom. It was very dangerous.

Cesar Chavez helped farm workers get fair pay. Jane Addams opened a big house in Chicago. Poor families came there to learn and get help. Each of these people saw a problem and helped.

Vote, Help, Speak Up

Main ideaA good citizen votes, helps others and speaks up when something is wrong.

A is a member of a country or a community. You are a citizen of your class. A good citizen helps. Pick up trash. Share. Help a friend who fell.

A good citizen . To vote is to choose. Each person gets one vote. The choice with the most votes wins. Grown-ups vote for leaders. Room 12 voted for Biscuit.

A good citizen speaks up. If something is not fair, say so. Say it kindly. Say it clearly. Dr. King spoke up. Jane Addams spoke up. You can too.

citizen
