Lesson 1 · Skill

Where recordings come from

A mixing desk puts recordings together into one song. You make the recordings on the site's instruments: the piano, the guitar, the bass, the drums, the Beat Lab or the band.

  1. Record on any instrument, then press Send to the Mixing Desk. Your take shows up here.
  2. Press Put it on track

Try this: Open the Music tools menu at the top to find an instrument. Come back here when you have sent a take.

Think about it

Which instrument did you record first? Why that one?

Your turn

Write the names of two instruments you want in your song.

Lesson 2 · Skill

Play and stop

Play starts your song from the start. Stop ends it at once. Space does the same on a keyboard.

  1. Press ▶ Play
  2. Press ■ Stop
  3. Press Play again and let it play to the end

Try this: Watch the bar number move while it plays.

Think about it

How long is your song right now, in bars?

Your turn

Draw a line for your song. Mark where each bar starts.

Lesson 3 · Skill

A song in layers

There are eight tracks. Each track holds one recording. They all play at the same time.

  1. Pick another track in the Track menu
  2. Put a recording on a second track
  3. Press Play to hear both tracks together

Try this: Put the same take on two tracks. It sounds bigger.

Think about it

What changes when two recordings play together?

Your turn

Plan four tracks: write what goes on each one.

Lesson 4 · Skill

Volume and balance

Volume makes a track louder or quieter. A good mix lets you hear every part.

  1. Move a track's Volume
  2. Turn one track down to 50 or less

Try this: Play the song. Move one Volume until the two tracks feel even.

Think about it

Which part should be the loudest in your song?

Your turn

Write each track and the volume you picked for it.

Lesson 5 · Skill

Left and right

Side moves a track to your left ear or your right ear. Headphones make it easy to hear.

  1. Move a track's Side to the left
  2. Move a track's Side to the right

Try this: Put one track a little left and one a little right. Keep drums and bass in the middle.

Think about it

Why might a band put the guitar on one side?

Your turn

Draw a stage. Put each of your tracks where it stands.

Lesson 6 · Skill

Mute and solo

Mute turns one track off. Solo lets you hear only that track. Both help you listen closely.

  1. Press Mute on a track
  2. Press Mute again to bring it back
  3. Press Solo on a track
  4. Press Solo again to hear everything

Try this: Play the song and Solo each track, one at a time.

Think about it

What did you hear in Solo that you missed before?

Your turn

Write one thing you like about each track.

Lesson 7 · Skill

Shape the sound: low, middle, high

Low is the deep part of a sound. High is the bright part. Middle is in between. Punch, Room and Echo add more color.

  1. Move a track's Low
  2. Move a track's Middle
  3. Move a track's High
  4. Try Punch, Room or Echo on a track

Try this: Take a little Low off the piano so the bass has room.

Think about it

Which instrument sounds best with more High? Why?

Your turn

Pick words for your mix: warm, bright, deep, big. Which fit?

Lesson 8 · Skill

Move a recording in time

Starts at bar says when a track begins. A part that comes in later makes the song grow.

  1. Under Starts at bar, press Later +
  2. Press − Earlier to bring it back

Try this: Let the drums start alone. Bring the piano in at bar 3.

Think about it

Why do many songs start with just one instrument?

Your turn

Write the bar where each of your tracks comes in.

Lesson 9 · Skill

Repeat a loop

Plays says how many times a recording plays in a row. A short loop can fill a whole song.

  1. Under Plays, press More +
  2. Press − Fewer
  3. Press Copy this piece

Try this: Make a drum loop play 4 times under everything else.

Think about it

How many times can a loop repeat before you want a change?

Your turn

Count the bars: loop length × times = song length.

Lesson 10 · Skill

Cut into pieces

Cut splits a recording in two. Each piece can move, repeat or go away on its own. Undo always brings it back.

  1. Pick a place in Cut at, then press ✂ Cut here
  2. Pick a piece in the Piece menu
  3. Press Remove this piece
  4. Press ↶ Undo to bring it back

Try this: Cut out your favorite bar and copy it three times.

Think about it

Which part of your take is the best part?

Your turn

Write your pieces in the order you want them.

Lesson 11 · Skill

Trim and fade

Trim cuts quiet bits or mistakes off the start and end. A fade lets a track come in or go out gently.

  1. Under Cut from the start, press +1 beat or +1 bar
  2. Under Cut from the end, press +1 beat or +1 bar
  3. Pick a Fade in
  4. Pick a Fade out

Try this: Give the last track in your song a long fade out.

Think about it

When is a fade better than a sudden stop?

Your turn

Mark on your song line where each fade goes.

Lesson 12 · Skill

Speed

Speed makes a recording slower or faster. Keep the pitch keeps the notes in tune. Off, it works like a tape.

  1. Slide Speed below 100 %
  2. Slide Speed above 100 %
  3. Press Keep the pitch to hear the difference

Try this: Slow a take to 75 % with Keep the pitch off. It sounds deep and dreamy.

Think about it

Why do the notes go lower when a tape plays slower?

Your turn

Write the speed you liked best, and why.

Lesson 13 · Skill

Loop bars while you work

Loop plays the same few bars again and again. You can fix one spot while you listen.

  1. Press Loop under Loop bars
  2. Pick the bars to loop in the two menus
  3. Press Play with Loop on
  4. Pick a bar in Start at bar

Try this: Loop bars 1 to 2 and move a Volume while it plays.

Think about it

Which part of your song needs the most care?

Your turn

Write the bars you looped and what you changed.

Lesson 14 · Skill

The whole song: Glue and Song volume

The whole song has its own controls. Glue holds the parts together. Song volume sets how loud it all is.

  1. In The whole song, move Low, Middle or High
  2. Move Glue
  3. Move Song volume

Try this: Turn Glue up and down while it plays. Listen to the drums.

Think about it

What does it mean for a song to sound together?

Your turn

Write your Glue and Song volume numbers.

Lesson 15 · Skill

Make your song into a file

Make the mix turns every track into one recording. Save as .wav keeps it on your device.

  1. Press Make the mix
  2. Play your mix in the player
  3. Press Save as .wav

Try this: Change one thing, make the mix again and compare.

Think about it

Who would you like to play your song for?

Your turn

Give your song a name.

Lesson 16 · Skill

Send to the turntables

Your mix can go to the turntables. There you can scratch it, mix it with a record or loop it.

  1. Press Send to the turntables

Try this: Open the turntables from the Music tools menu and play your song there.

Think about it

What would a DJ do with your song?

Your turn

Write one idea for your song on the turntables.

Lesson 17 · A song to make

Song: Drums and piano

Two instruments, two tracks, one song. Record a drum beat and a piano part, then mix them.

  1. Make a mix with two instruments in it
  2. In that mix, one track is quieter than the other
  3. Press Save as .wav

Try this: Keep the drums a little quieter than the piano, so the tune comes through.

Think about it

Which track did you make louder, and why?

Your turn

Write the two volumes you picked.

Lesson 18 · A song to make

Song: The band arrangement

Build an intro, a verse and a chorus from Beat Lab loops and other takes. Parts come in one by one.

  1. Put recordings on four tracks
  2. Make one track start at bar 3 or later
  3. Make the song 8 bars long or longer
  4. Make the mix with four tracks playing

Try this: Intro: drums alone. Verse: add bass. Chorus: add chords and a tune.

Think about it

How does a listener know the chorus has started?

Your turn

Write your song's parts and the bar where each starts.

Lesson 19 · A song to make

Song: Remix

Same takes, new order. Cut a recording into pieces and play them in a new way.

  1. Cut a recording and move a later piece in front of an earlier one
  2. Make the mix of your remix
  3. Send your remix to the turntables

Try this: Change the speed of one track too. Remixes often do.

Think about it

What stays the same in your remix? What is new?

Your turn

Write the order of your pieces, before and after.

Lesson 20 · A song to make

Song: Music under a story

Podcasts and videos use soft music under a voice. Make a quiet bed of music you could talk over.

  1. Turn a music track down to 40 or less and give it a fade in and a fade out
  2. Make the mix with that quiet track in it

Try this: Play your mix and tell a short story out loud over it. Is the music quiet enough?

Think about it

Why should music under a voice be quiet and simple?

Your turn

Write the first two lines of the story you would tell.

Lesson 21 · A song to make

Song: Your own, start to finish

A whole song, your way: three or more instruments, 16 bars or more, mixed and finished.

  1. Use recordings from three or more instruments
  2. Make the song 16 bars long or longer
  3. Make the mix of your song

Try this: Use everything you learned: balance, sides, a part that comes in later, a fade out.

Think about it

What would you change if you made it again?

Your turn

Write your song's name, its instruments and its parts in order.
