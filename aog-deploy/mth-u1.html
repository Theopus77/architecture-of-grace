Counting and Numbers

Number

How many kids are on the bus? How many pennies are in the jar? Every day we need to know how many. Counting is how we find out. In this unit you will learn to count the right way. One number for each thing. The last number tells how many.

Big numbers are hard to count by ones. So we make groups of ten. Ten pennies go in a bag. Ten straws go in a bundle. Then we count the bags. A number like 64 tells us six tens and four ones.

By the end you will count to 100. You will count by twos, fives and tens. You will tell which group has more. You will read and write numbers with tens and ones. And you will compare them with >, < and =.

The big question

How do numbers tell us how many, and why do we group things in tens?

Counting and Comparing

Counting
Big questionHow do we know for sure how many things are in a group?
The story

The Bus Count

The class is going to the zoo, but first Mr. Ortiz has to know how many kids are on the bus.

The bus is here. The whole class is going to Lincoln Park Zoo in Chicago. Mr. Ortiz stands by the door. He needs to know how many kids get on. If he loses count, someone might get left behind.

He touches each head as a kid climbs on. One. Two. Three. He goes slow. He says one number for each kid. Nobody gets skipped. Nobody gets counted twice.

The last kid gets on. Mr. Ortiz says twenty-two. So there are 22 kids on the bus. He writes 22 on his clipboard. He does not need to count again.

At the zoo, the kids move all around. But the number stays the same. When it is time to go home, Mr. Ortiz counts again. He needs to hear twenty-two.

Talk about itWhy did Mr. Ortiz touch each head as he counted? What could go wrong if he counted fast?
Section 1

Counting Things

1.1

One Number for Each Thing

Main ideaSay one number for each thing you count. Do not skip any. Do not count one twice.

Mia has some apples on the table. She touches one apple and says one. She touches the next and says two. Then three. Each apple gets one number. This is counting.

Sometimes we count too fast. We say five but only touch four apples. Slow down. Touch each thing. Move it to the side after you count it. Then you will not count it twice.

Think it through
Buttons in a Row

Look at a row of buttons. There are six buttons. Ben touches the first button and says one. He touches the second and says two. He skips the third button. He says three on the fourth button. What happened?

Count the buttons again the slow way. Touch each one. What number do you say last?

Words to know
one-to-one
one number for each thing you count, with no skips and no repeats
Check yourself

1. You count five toy cars. What should you do with each car?

Why: Each thing gets one number. Five cars get the numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.

2. Sam has 4 blocks. He counts 1, 2, 3, 3. What did he do wrong?

Why: He said 3 twice. Four blocks should get 1, 2, 3, 4.

3. Ana counts 3 pencils. Which count is right?

Why: The numbers go in order: 1, 2, 3. The last number is 3, so there are 3 pencils.
1.2

The Last Number Tells How Many

Main ideaThe last number you say tells how many things there are in all.

Leo counts his crayons. He says one, two, three, four, five. He stops at five. So Leo has five crayons. The last number tells .

Now Leo moves the crayons around. He puts them in a circle. Does he have to count again? No. It is still five. Moving things does not change how many.

Try it. Count seven coins in a row. Now spread them out. Count again. Still seven. The number does not change.

Read the numbers
Toys in the Bin

an example count from a classroom toy bin

Which toy has the most in the bin? How many balls are there?

Words to know
how many
the total number of things in a group
Check yourself

1. Kim counts 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. How many things does she have?

Why: The last number she said was 6. So she has 6 things.

2. Ray counts 8 rocks in a row. He moves them into a pile. How many rocks now?

Why: Moving things does not change how many. There are still 8 rocks.

3. In the toy bin table, how many balls are there?

Why: The balls row says 3. Cars are 4 and dolls are 2.
1.3

Zero Means None

Main ideaZero means there is nothing to count. We write it as 0.

Jo has a plate. There are three cookies on it. She eats one. Now there are two. She eats one more. Now there is one. She eats the last cookie. Now there are none. We say . We write 0.

Zero is a number. It comes before one. Count down: 3, 2, 1, 0. An empty box has zero toys. An empty hand has zero coins.

Think it through
The Empty Cup

Look at two cups. The first cup has four marbles. The second cup has no marbles at all. Point to the cup with zero marbles. Now take one marble out of the first cup. How many are left there?

How many marbles are in the empty cup? Say the number and write it.

Words to know
zero
the number for none; we write it as 0
Check yourself

1. A box has no crayons in it. How many crayons is that?

Why: None means zero. We write 0.

2. Count down: 3, 2, 1, what comes next?

Why: Counting down, the number after 1 is 0. Zero comes before one.

3. Which one shows zero?

Why: An empty plate has no cookies. No cookies is zero cookies.
Section 2

Counting Higher

1.4

Counting On

Main ideaYou can start counting from any number. You do not have to start at one.

Max has 5 stickers in a pile. He knows there are 5. He gets 3 more. He does not start at one. He says five, then six, seven, eight. Now he has 8 stickers. This is counting on.

Start at the number you know. Say the next numbers. Start at 12: 13, 14, 15. Start at 27: 28, 29, 30. Counting on saves time.

Think it through
Stickers on a Page

Look at a page of stickers. The first row has 10 stickers. You know that. The second row has 4 more. Put your finger on the last sticker in the first row. Say ten. Now count on: eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen.

Start at 10 and count on 4 more. What number do you land on?

Words to know
count on
start at a number you know and keep counting up from there
Check yourself

1. Start at 6. Count on 2 more. What number do you say last?

Why: Start at 6, then say 7, 8. The last number is 8.

2. What number comes right after 19?

Why: Counting up from 19, the next number is 20.

3. Ava has 14 beads. She adds 3 more. She counts on: 15, 16, 17. How many beads?

Why: Start at 14 and count on 3: 15, 16, 17. She has 17 beads.
1.5

Counting by Tens

Main ideaCount by tens: 10, 20, 30, up to 100. Each jump adds ten.

A dime is worth ten cents. Ten pennies make one dime. Count dimes by tens: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50. Five dimes are 50 cents. Say it fast. Ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty.

Keep going up. 60, 70, 80, 90, 100. Ten tens make one hundred. Counting by tens is a . You skip the numbers in between.

Hear the pattern. Each word ends the same way. Twenty, thirty, forty. Each one is ten more than the last.

Read the numbers
Dimes and Cents

U.S. coin values: one dime is 10 cents

How many cents would 5 dimes be? What comes next in the table?

Words to know
skip count
count by jumps, like 10, 20, 30, instead of by ones
hundred
the number 100; ten groups of ten
Check yourself

1. Count by tens: 10, 20, 30, what comes next?

Why: Each jump adds ten. 30 and 10 more is 40.

2. How many cents are 3 dimes?

Why: Each dime is 10 cents. Count 10, 20, 30. That is 30 cents.

3. What number is ten more than 70?

Why: Counting by tens, 70 then 80. Ten more than 70 is 80.
1.6

Counting by Twos and Fives

Main ideaCount by twos: 2, 4, 6, 8. Count by fives: 5, 10, 15, 20.

Socks come in pairs. A is two socks. Count socks by twos. 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. Five pairs are 10 socks. Each jump adds two.

Your hand has five fingers. Count fingers by fives. 5, 10, 15, 20. Four hands are 20 fingers. A nickel is worth five cents. Count nickels by fives too.

Think it through
Hands in the Air

Look at three friends. Each friend holds up one hand. Each hand has five fingers. Count by fives. Five, ten, fifteen. Now a fourth friend holds up a hand. Count on by five one more time.

How many fingers are up when four hands are raised? Count by fives to check.

Words to know
pair
two things that go together, like two socks
Check yourself

1. Count by twos: 2, 4, 6, what comes next?

Why: Each jump adds two. 6 and 2 more is 8.

2. Count by fives: 5, 10, 15, what comes next?

Why: Each jump adds five. 15 and 5 more is 20.

3. How many socks are in 3 pairs?

Why: Count by twos: 2, 4, 6. Three pairs are 6 socks.

Comparing and Writing Numbers

1.7

More, Fewer, the Same

Main ideaMatch things up one to one to see which group has more, fewer, or the same.

Tia has 4 red cubes. Ben has 6 blue cubes. Line them up. Put one red next to one blue. Two blue cubes have no partner. So Ben has . Tia has .

Now count instead. 6 is bigger than 4. So 6 cubes is more than 4 cubes. If both had 4, the groups would be the . Matching and counting both work.

Read the numbers
Fruit on the Snack Table

an example snack count

Which two fruits have the same number? Which fruit has the fewest?

Words to know
more
a bigger amount than another group
fewer
a smaller amount than another group
same
the two groups have an equal number
Check yourself

1. Lee has 7 cars. Kay has 9 cars. Who has more?

Why: 9 is bigger than 7. Kay has more cars.

2. Which group has fewer: 3 dogs or 5 cats?

Why: 3 is smaller than 5. The dogs are the smaller group.

3. In the fruit table, which fruit has the fewest?

Why: Bananas are 3. Apples and oranges are 5. Three is the smallest.
1.8

Writing Numbers and Number Words

Main ideaEvery number has a numeral and a word. 7 is seven. 12 is twelve.

We write numbers two ways. We write the 5. We write the five. Both mean the same amount. Count 5 fingers. Write 5. Write five.

Here are the words from 0 to 10. Zero, one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten. Then come the teens. Eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen.

Some numbers are tricky to write. A 6 and a 9 look alike upside down. Watch the direction. Practice writing 0 to 20 on a line.

Think it through
Numbers on the Lockers

Look at a row of lockers. Each one has a numeral. The first says 11. The next says 12. Then 13, 14, 15. One locker tag fell off. It goes between 15 and 17. Say the number words as you walk down the row.

What numeral is on the missing tag? Write it and say its word.

Words to know
numeral
a written number sign, like 5 or 12
number word
a number written in letters, like five or twelve
Check yourself

1. Which numeral matches the word eight?

Why: Eight is written 8. Eighteen is 18.

2. What is the number word for 12?

Why: 12 is twelve. 2 is two and 20 is twenty.

3. What number is missing? 15, 16, __, 18

Why: Counting up: 15, 16, 17, 18. The missing number is 17.

Place Value
Big questionWhy do we group things in tens, and what does each digit in a number tell us?
The story

The Penny Bags

The class store jar is full of pennies, and nobody wants to count them one by one.

The class store has a big jar of pennies. Kids pay with them. Now the jar is full. Ms. Bell asks, how many pennies do we have? Counting by ones would take all day.

Rosa has an idea. Count ten pennies. Put them in a small bag. Then count ten more. Make another bag. Soon the table has bags of ten. A few loose pennies are left over.

The class counts the bags by tens. Ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty. Then they count on the loose pennies. Sixty-one, sixty-two, sixty-three, sixty-four. There are 64 pennies. Six bags of ten and four ones.

Ms. Bell writes 64 on the board. The 6 stands for six bags. The 4 stands for four loose pennies. Two little numbers tell the whole story.

Talk about itWhy did the class bag the pennies in tens instead of counting all of them one by one?
Section 1

Making a Ten

2.1

A Bundle of Ten

Main ideaTen ones make one ten. We can count a bundle of ten as one group.

Ali has ten straws. She counts them. 1, 2, 3, up to 10. She puts a rubber band around them. Now she has one . One bundle is one . It is still ten straws.

Ten ones make one ten. That is the big idea. When we have ten small things, we can group them. Then we count groups. It is faster.

Think it through
Straws on the Desk

Look at a desk with straws. There is one bundle with a rubber band. It holds ten straws. Next to it are three loose straws. Say the bundle as ten. Then count on the loose straws. Eleven, twelve, thirteen.

How many straws are on the desk in all? How many bundles could you make from 20 straws?

Words to know
bundle
a group of things held together; a bundle of ten is one ten
ten
a group of ten ones
Check yourself

1. How many ones make one ten?

Why: A ten is a group of ten ones. Count 1 to 10 and bundle them.

2. Ali has 2 bundles of ten straws. How many straws?

Why: Count the bundles by tens: 10, 20. Two tens are 20 straws.

3. You have 30 pennies. How many bags of ten can you make?

Why: Count by tens: 10, 20, 30. That is 3 bags of ten.
2.2

Teen Numbers

Main ideaA teen number is one ten and some more ones. 14 is ten and four.

Ken has 14 grapes. He makes a group of ten. Four grapes are left over. So 14 is one ten and four ones. Say it: ten and four more.

Every works this way. 11 is ten and one. 12 is ten and two. 15 is ten and five. 19 is ten and nine. The 1 in front means one ten.

Watch the words. Fourteen sounds like four and ten. Sixteen sounds like six and ten. Eleven and twelve are the odd ones. Just remember them.

Read the numbers
Teen Numbers as Ten and More

each teen number is one ten plus some ones

What would ten and 9 be? Which row shows 15?

Words to know
teen number
a number from 11 to 19; one ten and some ones
Check yourself

1. 17 is one ten and how many ones?

Why: 17 is ten and 7 more. So it has 7 ones.

2. What is ten and 3 more?

Why: Start at ten and count on 3: 11, 12, 13. That is 13.

3. Which number is one ten and 8 ones?

Why: One ten and 8 ones is 18. The 1 means one ten.
2.3

The Ten Frame

Main ideaA ten frame has ten boxes. A full frame is one ten. Extra dots are the ones.

A is a box with ten spaces. Two rows of five. Put one dot in each space. When it is full, you have ten. You do not need to count.

Rae shows 13 with frames. She fills one frame. That is ten. She puts 3 dots in a second frame. Ten and three is 13. One full frame and 3 more.

A ten frame also shows how far to ten. If 7 spaces are full, 3 are empty. So 7 needs 3 more to make ten.

Think it through
Dots in the Frames

Look at two ten frames. The top frame is full. All ten spaces have a dot. The bottom frame has dots in six spaces. Four spaces are empty. Say the top frame as ten. Then count on the dots in the bottom frame.

What number do the two frames show? How many more dots would fill the bottom frame?

Words to know
ten frame
a box with ten spaces in two rows of five, used to show numbers
Check yourself

1. A ten frame has 8 dots. How many spaces are empty?

Why: The frame has 10 spaces. 8 are full, so 2 are empty. 8 and 2 make 10.

2. One full frame and 5 more dots show what number?

Why: A full frame is ten. Ten and 5 more is 15.

3. How many spaces are in one ten frame?

Why: A ten frame has two rows of five. That is 10 spaces.
Section 2

Tens and Ones Together

2.4

Two-Digit Numbers

Main ideaIn a two-digit number, the first digit counts tens. The second digit counts ones.

The class has 64 pennies. Six bags of ten. Four loose ones. We write 64. The 6 is in the . The 4 is in the . Six tens and four ones.

Look at 37. The 3 means three tens. That is 30. The 7 means seven ones. 30 and 7 is 37. Now look at 73. Is that three tens? No. 73 is seven tens and three ones. Order matters.

A common mistake is to mix up the places. 25 is not the same as 52. Say the tens first. Two tens and five ones is 25. Each has its own job.

Read the numbers
Tens and Ones

each number written as its tens and ones

What is 2 tens and 4 ones? Which row has zero ones?

Words to know
digit
one of the number signs 0 to 9; the number 47 has two digits
tens place
the spot on the left in a two-digit number; it counts tens
ones place
the spot on the right in a two-digit number; it counts ones
Check yourself

1. In the number 58, what does the 5 mean?

Why: The 5 is on the left, in the tens place. It means 5 tens, or 50.

2. What number is 3 tens and 9 ones?

Why: 3 tens is 30. 30 and 9 is 39. Tens go first, so it is 39, not 93.

3. What number is 6 tens and 0 ones?

Why: 6 tens is 60. Zero ones adds nothing. The number is 60.
2.5

Base-Ten Blocks

Main ideaA long rod is one ten. A small cube is one one. Rods and cubes show any number.

Math blocks called help us count. A small cube is one. A long is ten. The rod is ten cubes stuck in a row. Count rods by tens. Count cubes by ones.

Show 45 with blocks. Take 4 rods. That is 40. Take 5 cubes. Count on: 41, 42, 43, 44, 45. Four rods and five cubes show 45.

Now show 45 another way. Use 3 rods and 15 cubes. 30 and 15 is 45. Same number. You can trade ten cubes for one rod.

Think it through
Rods and Cubes on the Mat

Look at a place-value mat. On the left side sit 5 rods. On the right side sit 2 cubes. Count the rods by tens. Ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty. Now count on the cubes. Fifty-one, fifty-two.

What number do the blocks show? What would you have if you added one more rod?

Words to know
base-ten blocks
blocks for counting; a cube is one and a rod is ten
rod
a long block worth ten; the same as ten cubes
Check yourself

1. 2 rods and 7 cubes show what number?

Why: 2 rods are 20. Count on 7 cubes: 21 to 27. The number is 27.

2. How many cubes equal one rod?

Why: A rod is ten cubes in a row. Ten cubes equal one rod.

3. What number do 8 rods and 0 cubes show?

Why: Count 8 rods by tens: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80. No cubes, so it is 80.
2.6

Comparing with Symbols

Main ideaCompare tens first. If tens are the same, compare ones. Use >, < or =.

Which is bigger, 52 or 38? Look at the tens. 5 tens beats 3 tens. So 52 is 38. Write 52 > 38. The open side faces the bigger number.

Now 43 and 47. The tens are the same. Four and four. Look at the ones. 3 is less than 7. So 43 is 47. Write 43 < 47.

If both digits match, the numbers are . 29 = 29. A common mistake is to look at ones first. 19 has a 9, but 21 is bigger. Tens come first.

Think it through
The Sign Points

Think of the sign as a hungry mouth. It opens toward the bigger number. 8 > 3. The mouth opens to the 8. 3 < 8. The mouth still opens to the 8. Now try 61 and 59. Which number is bigger? Which way should the mouth open?

Write the sign between 61 and 59. Then write the sign between 34 and 34.

Words to know
greater than
bigger than; the sign is >
less than
smaller than; the sign is <
equal
the same amount; the sign is =
Check yourself

1. Which sign goes between 46 and 64?

Why: 4 tens is less than 6 tens. So 46 < 64.

2. Which number is greater, 19 or 21?

Why: Look at tens first. 2 tens beats 1 ten. So 21 > 19.

3. Which one is true?

Why: 88 and 88 have the same digits, so they are equal. 35 is less than 53, and 72 is greater than 27.
Section 3

Patterns with Tens

2.7

Ten More and Ten Less

Main ideaTen more changes the tens digit up by one. Ten less changes it down by one.

Nia has 34 stickers. She gets 10 more. Add one rod. 3 tens become 4 tens. The ones stay at 4. So 34 and 10 more is 44. Only the tens digit changed.

Now she gives away 10. Take away one rod. 4 tens become 3 tens. 44 take away 10 is 34. That is . The tens digit goes down by one.

You do not need to count by ones. Find than 57. It is 67. Ten less than 57 is 47. A common mistake is to change the ones digit. The ones stay the same.

Read the numbers
Ten More Each Time

example: adding 10 to 23 again and again

What comes next after 53? What is ten less than 23?

Words to know
ten more
the number you get by adding 10; the tens digit goes up by one
ten less
the number you get by taking away 10; the tens digit goes down by one
Check yourself

1. What is 10 more than 42?

Why: 4 tens become 5 tens. The ones stay 2. So it is 52.

2. What is 10 less than 78?

Why: 7 tens become 6 tens. The ones stay 8. So it is 68.

3. In the table, what comes after 53?

Why: Each row adds ten. 53 and 10 more is 63.
2.8

The Hundred Chart

Main ideaOn a hundred chart, each step right adds one. Each step down adds ten.

A shows 1 to 100. It has ten rows. Each row has ten numbers. The first row is 1 to 10. The second row is 11 to 20. The last row ends at 100.

Find 25. Move one box right. You see 26. That is one more. Move one box down. You see 35. That is ten more. Move up instead. You see 15. Ten less.

Look at a . 4, 14, 24, 34, 44. The ones digit stays 4. The tens digit goes up by one. The chart shows the pattern.

Think it through
A Trip on the Chart

Put your finger on 41 on a hundred chart. Move down one box. Now you are on 51. Move down again. Now 61. Move one box to the right. Now 62. Move up one box. Where are you now?

What number is one box below 62? What number is one box to the left of 41?

Words to know
hundred chart
a grid of numbers from 1 to 100 in ten rows of ten
column
a line of boxes going up and down on a chart
Check yourself

1. On a hundred chart, what is one box below 27?

Why: One box down is ten more. 27 and 10 more is 37.

2. What number comes right after 49 on the chart?

Why: One more than 49 is 50. It starts the next row.

3. Which numbers are in the same column as 6?

Why: Going down a column adds ten each time: 6, 16, 26. The ones digit stays 6.
