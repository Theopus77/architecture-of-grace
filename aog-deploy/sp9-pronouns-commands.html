Pronouns, Reflexives and Commands

Spanish keeps its small words in front of the verb — and sometimes glues them onto the end of it. This unit shows which pronoun to pick, where to put it and how to give an order, across twenty-four study cards, a page of hints, a three-tier quiz and four workshop labs.

A pronoun has two homes

Me lo das. In front of the conjugated verb there is a slot. The pronouns stand there, as their own words.

An object pronoun has two homes: in front of the conjugated verb, or attached to the end of an infinitive, a gerund or an affirmative command. When you attach one, a written accent appears to keep the stress where it already was.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

With two verbs, the pronoun goes between them: Quiero lo comprar.

The pronoun may never stand between a conjugated verb and an infinitive. It goes in front of the pair, or onto the end of the infinitive: Lo quiero comprar, or Quiero comprarlo. Both are correct.

In front of both, or stuck to the end. Never in the middle.

⚠️ People often think…

A pronoun attaches to the end of any command: No hagaslo.

Only an affirmative command lets a pronoun stick. A negative command sends it back out in front, as its own word: No lo hagas. No te levantes.

Yes pulls it in. No pushes it out.

⚠️ People often think…

With two pronouns, ‘Le doy el libro’ becomes Le lo doy.

Le and les cannot stand in front of lo, la, los or las. They turn into se: Se lo doy. The meaning does not change at all — only the sound does.

Two l-words in a row: the first one becomes se.

⚠️ People often think…

Dame has no accent, so damelo does not need one either.

Attaching a pronoun adds a syllable, and the stress stays on da. DA-me needs no mark. DÁ-me-lo is stressed three syllables from the end, so the mark is required to hold it there.

Count the syllables after you attach, not before.

Two homes, one verb

1 Watch one

Me lo das means you give it to me. Turn it into a command.

  1. Das is conjugated, so the pronouns go in front.
  2. Me is indirect, lo is direct. Me lo das.
  3. The tú command of dar is da.
  4. A yes-command pulls them on: da + me + lo.
  5. Da was stressed on DA. So is DÁ-me-lo — write the accent. Dámelo ✓
2 Do one with me

One verb, comprar, in three shapes: Lo compro · Cómpralo · No lo compres.

  1. In ‘Lo compro’ the pronoun stands
  2. In ‘Cómpralo’ the pronoun is
  3. In ‘No lo compres’ the pronoun goes back

Helpful Hints

🧭 The unit in one line

Find the object → pick the pronoun (lo, la, le, se…) → put it in front of the conjugated verb, or glue it to an infinitive, a gerund or a yes-command → add the accent if the word grew → and on a no-command send it back out in front.

In front, unless it can stick. Yes sticks, no does not.

🔑 The one idea

A pronoun is a short word that stands in for a noun you already said. Spanish never leaves it loose at the end of the sentence: it either stands in front of the verb or becomes part of the verb. Every rule in this unit answers one question — which of those two.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • Never write ‘le lo’ or ‘les las’. Le and les turn into se in front of lo, la, los and las.
  • The pronoun may not sit between a conjugated verb and an infinitive. ‘Quiero lo comprar’ is wrong; write ‘Lo quiero comprar’ or ‘Quiero comprarlo’.
  • A negative command never takes the pronoun on its end. ‘No lo hagas’, never ‘No hagaslo’.
  • Build the negative tú command from the yo form, not the infinitive. Tengo gives ‘no tengas’, not ‘no tienes’.
  • With body parts and clothing, Spanish uses the article: ‘me lavo las manos’, not ‘me lavo mis manos’.
  • The personal a goes before a specific person, not before a thing. ‘Veo a María’ but ‘Veo la casa’.
  • When you attach a pronoun, check the accent. Da → dame → dámelo.
