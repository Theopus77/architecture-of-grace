Advanced Punctuation

In Grades 9–10 punctuation decided what your own sentence asserted. Here it decides what somebody else's sentence is allowed to have said. Three dots can make a source say the opposite of what it said, and the quotation remains word-for-word accurate. Square brackets let you put a gloss inside another person's sentence. A single [sic] delivers a verdict on a writer while pretending to be a courtesy. So this room is partly a set of conventions — the colon's three jobs, the semicolon in a complex series, the three levels of interruption — and partly the first place in this course where a punctuation mark is an ethical decision. Five tabs — cards for the marks and the rules of quotation, hints with the honest-cut test, a quiz built on quotations that have been trimmed, and a workshop where you judge cuts and defend one.

Every word genuine, the claim reversed

What a researcher actually wrote. One sentence, two halves — and the second half is not an afterthought. It is the limit she put on her own claim.

A quotation can be word-for-word accurate and still be a lie, because accuracy and fairness are separate properties and an ellipsis operates on the second. Apply the honest-cut test: would the person you are quoting accept your shortened version as a fair account of what they said? If not, the cut is not saving space — it is editing their position.

What people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

I checked every word against the source, so the quotation is sound.

Word-checking verifies accuracy and says nothing about fairness. The misrepresentation is entirely in the absence — nothing false is present to be found, and a reader has no way of knowing a though ever existed. The only check that works is to ask whether the person quoted would accept your version as a fair account.

Ask not whether the words are real, but whether the claim survived the cut.

⚠️ People often think…

[sic] is just a scholarly courtesy, so it is always neutral.

It is a courtesy when the error might otherwise be blamed on you. On a typo nobody would have attributed to you it does no protective work at all, and its only remaining effect is to display a stranger’s slip in front of an audience — a verdict delivered inside a convention, which makes it almost impossible to object to.

Ask what it was protecting you from. If nothing, that was not why it was there.

⚠️ People often think…

I kept the quotation short to save space.

A quotation too short to be checked is not evidence — it is an allusion wearing quotation marks. If a reader cannot recover what the source claimed, they cannot judge whether your use of it is fair, so you have asked for trust while removing the means to verify it. The whole sentence costs one line.

Citation exists to be checked. One nobody can check is decoration.

Read it whole, cut, then read it cold

1 Watch one

Judge this cut: a source wrote “We support the scheme only if funding is found.” A leaflet prints: they “support the scheme…”.

  1. Read the source whole. The claim is conditional — support exists only under one condition.
  2. Notice where the ellipsis sits: immediately before only, which is the word carrying the condition.
  3. Read the printed version cold: they support the scheme. Unconditional. The opposite of a conditional.
  4. Apply the test: would they accept it as fair? No — they would say we never said that, and they would be right, in every word.

Helpful Hints

⚡ The one idea

A quotation can be word-for-word accurate and still be a lie. “The evidence is strong, though the sample was small and the effect disappeared at six months” becomes “The evidence is strong…” — every word genuine, the claim reversed. The honest-cut test is the whole room: would the person you are quoting accept your shortened version as a fair account of what they said? If not, the cut is not a saving of space, it is an edit of their position.

📐 The colon's three jobs

JobExample
Introduce a listShe brought three things: a map, a torch and a receipt.
Explain what precededThe reason was simple: nobody had been asked.
Amplify — restate with forceHe had one talent: patience.

In all three, what comes before the colon must be a complete sentence. What comes after does not have to be.

🔧 Three levels of interruption

MarkVolumeUse it when
Commasquietthe aside belongs in the flow
Parenthesesquieter stillthe reader could skip it entirely
Dashesloudyou want the interruption noticed

✂️ Quoting honestly

  • Ellipsis (…) marks words removed. It must not remove a qualification that changes the claim.
  • Square brackets [ ] mark your words inside theirs — for grammar or a needed name only.
  • [sic] means the error is theirs. Used on a trivial slip, it is a sneer wearing a convention.
  • A block quotation takes no quotation marks — the indentation does that job.

➖ Hyphen, en dash, em dash

MarkJobExample
Hyphen -joins words into one ideaa well-known writer
En dash –spans a range, or links a pairpages 40–52 · the Paris–Berlin line
Em dash —interrupts a sentenceHe agreed — eventually.

🎯 How you will be asked

  • “Which choice most effectively punctuates the list?”
  • “Is this quotation a fair representation of the source?”
  • “What is the effect of the dashes rather than parentheses?”
  • “NO CHANGE / colon / semicolon / dash”
  • “Why has the writer placed [sic] after this word?”

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Apply the honest-cut test to a shortened quotation?
  • Name the colon's three jobs and check the left-hand side?
  • Punctuate a series whose items already contain commas?
  • Choose between commas, parentheses and dashes by how loud you want it?
  • Say when [sic] is a courtesy and when it is a weapon?
