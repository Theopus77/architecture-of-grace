Sounds and Letters

Foundations

Say your name out loud. Say it slowly. Can you hear the pieces? Every word you say is made of small sounds. Your ears can find them. Your mouth can say them one at a time. That is where reading starts.

Letters are a code for those sounds. The letter m stands for /m/. Put letters in a row and they spell a word. Learn the code and the letters tell you what to say. Cat. Ship. Cape. Words you can read all by yourself.

In this unit you will hear the first sound, the last sound and the middle sound. You will rhyme and clap. You will blend sounds into words and take words apart. Then you will learn the letters, the vowels, the letter teams and the silent e. By the end, you will crack the code.

The big question

How do the sounds you hear become words you can read?

The story

I Spy With My Ears

The clue is not a color. The clue is a sound.

It is rug time in Ms. Ortiz's class. She says, "Let's play I Spy. But today the clue is a sound." The kids lean in. Ms. Ortiz says, "I spy something that starts with /b/." She says the sound, not the letter. Just /b/, like a bubble popping.

Hands go up. "Ball!" says Devon. Ms. Ortiz shakes her head. "Good try. Ball does start with /b/. But I do not see a ball." Kids look around the room. There is a clock. There is a door. There is a book.

Priya says the word slowly. "Buh... book. Book starts with /b/!" Ms. Ortiz smiles. "Yes! The first sound in book is /b/." Now it is Priya's turn. She looks around. She says, "I spy something that starts with /d/."

Devon says, "Door!" He is right. Every word in the room has a first sound. Clock. Rug. Window. Once you hear the first sound, you can play this game anywhere.

Talk about itLook around your room. Pick one thing. What sound does its name start with?
Section 1

Hearing Sounds

1.1

Words Are Made of Sounds

Main ideaEvery word is made of small sounds put together.

Say the word cat. Now say it very slowly. Stretch it out. Do you hear it? C... a... t. Cat is not one sound. Cat is three sounds. They are stuck together.

Every word is like this. A is a set of said in a row. Say sun slowly. S... u... n. Three sounds. Say go slowly. G... o. Two sounds.

Your ears can pull a word apart. Your mouth can say each piece. This is a big skill. When you hear the sounds, you are ready to read them.

1.2

The First Sound

Main ideaThe first sound is the sound you hear at the start of a word.

Say mom. What sound comes out first? /m/. Your lips press together. That is the . Now say dog. The first sound is /d/. Your tongue taps the top of your mouth.

The first sound is the start of the word. It is the sound you hear before all the others. Say sun. /s/ comes first. Say fish. /f/ comes first.

Play a game. Say your name slowly. What sound comes first? Now say a friend’s name. Do they start the same? Maria and Max both start with /m/.

Think it through

Which toys go in the /b/ bag? How do you know?

1.3

Last Sound, Middle Sound

Main ideaWords have a last sound and a middle sound too.

Say bus. Say it slowly. B... u... s. The is /s/. It is the sound at the end. Say cup. The last sound is /p/. Your lips pop.

Now find the . Say cat slowly. C... a... t. The middle sound is /a/. It sits between /c/ and /t/. Say pig. The middle sound is /i/.

The middle sound is the hard one. It is short. It goes by fast. So stretch the word out. Say it like a slow song. Caaaat. Now you can hear the /a/.

Think it through
Cat, Cot, Cut

Say these three words slowly. Cat. Cot. Cut. They all start with /c/. They all end with /t/. But they are not the same word. Listen to the middle. Cat has /a/. Cot has /o/. Cut has /u/. The middle sound changes everything.

Which sound is different in cat, cot and cut?

Why: The middle sound is short. Stretching the word helps you hear it.
Section 2

Rhymes and Beats

1.4

Words That Rhyme

Main ideaWords rhyme when their ends sound the same.

Say cat. Say hat. Listen to the ends. C-at. H-at. Both end with -at. Cat and hat . Two words rhyme when their ends sound the same.

Try more. Dog and log. Sun and fun. Bed and red. The first sound is different. The rest of the word matches. That is why they sound like a pair.

Rhymes are all over songs and poems. Twinkle, twinkle, little star. How I wonder what you are. Star and are rhyme. Your ears already know this game.

1.5

Clap the Parts

Main ideaYou can clap the beats in a word to count its parts.

Say apple. Clap as you say it. Ap-ple. Two claps. Now say cat. One clap. Say banana. Ba-na-na. Three claps. Each clap is one . A syllable is one beat of a word.

Try your name. Say it and clap. Sam gets one clap. Ma-ri-a gets three claps. Long names get more claps. Short names get one.

Put your hand under your chin. Say elephant. El-e-phant. Your chin drops three times. That is three syllables. Your chin can count for you.

Section 3

Building Words

1.6

Blend the Sounds

Main ideaPut sounds together in a row and they make a word.

Listen. /m/... /a/... /p/. Three sounds, one at a time. Now say them fast. Push them together. Map! You just made a word. This is called .

Blending is like gluing beads on a string. Each sound is a bead. Say them close together. /s/ /u/ /n/. Sun. /d/ /o/ /g/. Dog.

Go slow at first. Say the first sound. Hold it. Slide into the next one. Sssuuunnn. Then say it fast. Sun. Readers do this all day long.

Think it through
The Robot Talks

A toy robot says words in pieces. It says, "/p/... /i/... /g/." Its friend Lena hears the sounds. She pushes them together in her head. She says, "Pig!" The robot beeps. It says, "/h/... /a/... /t/." Lena smiles. She knows this one too.

What word did the robot say the second time?

Words to know
blending
saying sounds together fast so they make a word
Check yourself

1. What is blending?

Why: Blending pushes sounds together. /m/ /a/ /p/ becomes map.

2. Blend these sounds: /s/ /u/ /n/. What word is it?

Why: Say the sounds fast and close. They make the word sun.

3. What should you do when blending is hard?

Why: Slow blending works. Hold the first sound and slide to the next.
1.7

Take a Word Apart

Main ideaYou can break a word into its sounds, one at a time.

Blending puts sounds together. Now go the other way. Say the word hop. Pull it apart. /h/... /o/... /p/. You broke hop into three sounds. That is .

Use your fingers. Say sit. Hold up one finger for each sound. /s/ is one. /i/ is two. /t/ is three. Three fingers. Three sounds.

This helps you spell. To write a word, you need its sounds. Say the word. Take it apart. Then write one letter for each sound you hear.

Think it through
Fingers Up

Jamal wants to write the word bug. He says it slowly. He puts up one finger. /b/. Two fingers. /u/. Three fingers. /g/. Now he has three fingers up. He knows bug has three sounds. He can write one letter for each one.

Why does Jamal hold up his fingers?

Words to know
taking apart
saying each sound in a word one at a time
Check yourself

1. Take apart the word hop. What are the sounds?

Why: Hop has three sounds in this order: /h/, /o/, /p/.

2. How many fingers do you hold up for sit?

Why: Sit has three sounds: /s/, /i/, /t/. One finger for each.

3. How does taking a word apart help you?

Why: When you hear each sound, you can write a letter for each one.
1.8

Change One Sound

Main ideaChange one sound in a word and you get a new word.

Say cat. Now take off the /c/. Put /h/ in its place. Hat! Take off the /h/. Put on /b/. Bat! You changed one sound. You made a new word each time.

You can change the end too. Say cat. Take off the /t/. Put on /p/. Cap! You can change the middle. Cat. Cot. Cut. One small change makes a whole new word.

This is a you can play anywhere. Start with pin. Change one sound. Pan. Change one more. Man. Keep going. How far can you get?

Think it through
The Word Ladder

Look at the word ladder. On the first step is the word cat. Change one sound. Step up to hat. Change one sound. Step up to hot. Change one sound. Step up to pot. Each step is a new word. Each step is only one sound away from the last one.

Which sound changed to go from hat to hot?

Words to know
word game
a game where you play with the sounds in words
Check yourself

1. Change the /c/ in cat to /h/. What word do you get?

Why: Take off /c/ and put on /h/. C-at becomes h-at, hat.

2. Cat becomes cap. Which sound changed?

Why: The /t/ at the end became /p/. The first and middle sounds stayed.

3. What happens when you change one sound in a word?

Why: One sound change makes a new word. Pin becomes pan.

Phonics
Big questionHow do letters turn sounds into words you can read?
The story

Cracking the Code on Ship

Four letters. Three sounds. One class that would not give up.

Mr. Bell writes a word on the board. S-h-i-p. "Who can read this?" he asks. Nobody says a thing. The class knows /s/. They know /h/. But together the letters make a strange word. Suh-hip? That is not a word.

Then Ava says, "Wait. What if s and h are a team?" Mr. Bell nods. He puts his finger to his lips. "Shhh." The class laughs. That is the sound! Two letters, one sound. /sh/.

Now they try again. Ava points to each letter, left to right. /sh/... /i/... /p/. She says it faster. Ship! The whole class says it. Ship! A boat on the lake. They just cracked the code.

Mr. Bell writes more words. Shop. Fish. Dish. Now the class can read them all. Once you know the code, the letters tell you what to say.

Talk about itWhat made ship hard to read at first? What made it easy at the end?
Section 1

What a Letter Is

2.1

A Name and a Sound

Main ideaEvery letter has a name and a sound, and the sound is what you read.

Look at the letter m. Its is em. But when you read, you do not say em. You say its : /m/. Say it. Your lips press together. Mmm. That is the sound of m.

Every letter has both. The letter s is called ess. Its sound is /s/, like a snake. The letter t is called tee. Its sound is /t/, a quick tap.

The name helps you talk about the letter. The sound helps you read the word. Map is not em-ay-pee. Map is /m/ /a/ /p/. Sounds make words.

Think it through
Two Ways to Say It

Look at a box of cereal. On the front is one big letter: S. Ben says, "That is an ess." His sister says, "It says /s/." They are both right. Ess is the name. /s/ is the sound. When Ben reads the word sun, he does not say ess. He says /s/.

When you read a word, do you use the letter's name or its sound?

Words to know
name

Check yourself

2. Which one helps you read a word?

2.2

Big Letters, Small Letters

Main ideaEach letter comes in a capital and a small form, and both say the same sound.

Look at B and b. They look different. One is big. One is small. But they are the same letter. Both are bee. Both say /b/. The big one is a letter. The small one is a letter.

Capitals do a special job. A name starts with a capital. Maria. Chicago. The first word in a sentence starts with a capital too. The dog ran.

Most letters in a book are small. Capitals show up at the start of things. Look at your name. The first letter is a capital. The rest are small.

Think it through
Matching Socks

Look at a pile of letter cards. Some are big. Some are small. Your job is to match them like socks. A goes with a. G goes with g. Some pairs look alike, like S and s. Some pairs look very different, like A and a. But every pair says the same sound.

Which pairs look alike? Which pairs look different?

Words to know

Check yourself

1. What is the big form of a letter called?

Why: The big form is the capital. B is a capital. b is lowercase.

2. Do B and b say the same sound?

Why: B and b are the same letter in two forms. Both say /b/.

3. Where do you see a capital letter?

Why: Names start with a capital. So does the first word of a sentence.
2.3

Vowels and Consonants

Main ideaFive letters are vowels, and every word needs at least one.

Say /a/. Your mouth is open. Air comes out with nothing in the way. Now say /t/. Your tongue taps. Something blocks the air. Open sounds are . Blocked sounds are .

The vowel letters are a, e, i, o and u. Just five. All the other letters are consonants. B, c, d, f, g and more.

Every word needs a vowel. Look at cat. The vowel is a. Look at dog. The vowel is o. Try to say a word with no vowel. Bkt? It is not a word. The vowel holds the word together.

Section 2

Reading Short Words

2.4

Short Vowels

Main ideaIn a short word like cat, the vowel says its short sound.

Look at the word cat. The vowel is a. Here it says /a/, like the start of apple. This is the of a. Say cat, hat, bat, map. Every one has short a.

Each vowel has a short sound. E says /e/ in bed. I says /i/ in pig. O says /o/ in hot. U says /u/ in cup. Learn these five and you can read hundreds of words.

Here is the rule. A word with one vowel in the middle usually says the short sound. Cat. Bed. Pig. Hot. Cup. Consonant, vowel, consonant. Say the sounds and blend.

2.5

Two Letters, One Sound

Main ideaSome letter pairs, like sh, ch and th, make one new sound together.

Look at ship. Four letters. But only three sounds. The letters s and h work as a . Together they say /sh/. Put your finger to your lips. Shhh. That is the sound.

Other teams do this too. C and h say /ch/, like in chip. Say it. Your tongue clicks. T and h say /th/, like in thin. Your tongue pokes out a little.

When you see sh, ch or th, do not say each letter. Say the team sound. Fish. Chop. Bath. Look for the teams, and the words get easier.

2.6

The Silent e

Main ideaAn e at the end of a word makes the vowel say its name.

Look at cap. Short a. /cap/. Now add an e to the end. Cape. The a changed! Now it says its name, ay. And the e says nothing at all. It is a .

The silent e is a helper. It sits at the end. It makes no sound. But it tells the vowel to say its name. Kit becomes kite. Hop becomes hope. Cub becomes cube.

Watch for the e at the end. Cover it with your finger. Read the short word. Then let the e back in. Say the vowel’s name. Pin. Pine. You can hear the change.

What did the silent e change in the word kite?

Section 3

Using the Code

2.7

Read Left to Right

Main ideaRead the letters in order from left to right, and you get the right word.

Look at the word pat. Point to the p. That is the side. Now slide your finger to the right. P... a... t. Pat. You read the letters in order.

Order matters. The letters t, a and p can spell tap. Or pat. Or apt. Same letters, different words. Start on the left. Go to the right. Then you get the right word.

Lines work the same way. Read a line from left to right. When you reach the end, jump down. Start again on the left. Your finger can lead the way.

Think it through
Same Letters, Two Words

Look at three letter cards on a table: t, o, p. Read them left to right. Top. Now flip the order: p, o, t. Read left to right again. Pot. A top spins. A pot holds soup. Same three letters, but a different word each time.

Why is top a different word from pot?

Words to know
left
the side where you start reading
Check yourself

2. Read these letters in order: p, a, t. What word is it?

Why: Left to right, the sounds are /p/ /a/ /t/. That is pat.

3. What happens when you reach the end of a line?

Why: Each new line starts on the left again. Your finger can lead the way.
2.8

Write the Letters in Order

Main ideaThe alphabet has 26 letters in a set order, and you can write each one.

Start at the top. Pull down. Lift. Cross. That is a t. Every letter has a way to be made. Start at the top for most letters. Sit the letter on the line. Make small letters small.

All the letters together are the . There are 26. They go in an order. A, b, c, d, e, f, g. The order never changes. The song you know puts them in a row.

The order is useful. Names on a list go in alphabet order. Books on a shelf go that way too. Write a, b and c on a paper. Then keep going. Can you get all the way to z?
