THE BASS · Architecture of Grace · where the recorded basses come from

The recorded electric bass is a real bass guitar, recorded note by note:

  Growlybass by Karoryfer Samples
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/karoryfer.growlybass
  https://www.karoryfer.com

  A Squier Jazz Bass with roundwound strings, tuned E A D G, plugged
  straight into the recorder (both pickups, every knob on 10). The notes
  are played with the fingers.

The recorded upright bass is a real double bass, plucked with the fingers:

  A 1958 Otto Rubner double bass, played and recorded by D. Smolken
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/dsmolken.double-bass

  Thomastik-Infeld Spirocore strings, tuned in fifths (C G D A).

  Four of its notes come from Meatbass by Karoryfer Samples: the same
  double bass and the same player, recorded again in 2016 by Ludwik
  Zamenhof.
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/karoryfer.meatbass

All three libraries are given to everyone under CC0 1.0 Universal
(public domain):
  https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
So nothing here needs permission. We name them because we are grateful.

What we changed (electric bass):
  - We kept two strengths of held note: the library's soft one and its
    strong one. Low and middle notes have two takes, so a repeated note
    does not sound the same twice. High notes have one.
  - We kept the short (staccato) notes, two takes of each, for fast lines.
  - We kept five release noises (a finger stopping the string) and four
    pick scrapes. We chose the release noises with the least ring in them,
    so they fit after any note.
  - The library names its notes one octave above where they sound. Its
    "E2" is the open low E string. We measured every note's pitch.
  - We worked out which string each note was played on. A string's high
    overtones stretch a little sharp, and a thicker string stretches them
    more. The library does not say.
  - Some short notes were recorded so loud that the tops of their waves
    were cut flat. We rebuilt those tops.
  - The page's picked bass sounds play these fingered notes with the
    click of a pick added at the start. That click is made on the page.

What we changed (upright bass):
  - We kept two strengths of plucked note: the library's middle one and
    its strong one. Low and middle notes have two takes. High notes have
    one.
  - The library has a note every 3 or 4 semitones. Where it skips 4, the
    note comes from Meatbass: F sharp 1, C 2, F sharp 2 and D sharp 3.
    Those four sound darker in Meatbass, so we brightened them to sit
    with the notes around them. The start of each note and its ring were
    matched one at a time. We also took away a low rumble under them.
  - We kept five of the player's string mutes as release noises. They
    were played hard, so they are set to sound 12 dB softer.

For every note:
  - It is mono, 44,100 samples a second, saved as a 128 kbps MP3.
  - It starts right at the pluck. It keeps its natural ring for up to
    6 seconds and ends with a short fade.
  - The notes of each strength were brought to one loudness, so low and
    high notes sit evenly. Each strength keeps its own level, so soft
    notes stay softer.
  - We did not retune any note. We measured its pitch, and the page tunes
    it as it plays.

EL BAJO · de dónde vienen los bajos grabados

El bajo eléctrico grabado es un bajo de verdad, grabado nota por nota:
Growlybass de Karoryfer Samples, un Squier Jazz Bass con cuerdas
entorchadas, afinado Mi La Re Sol, conectado directo a la grabadora. Las
notas están tocadas con los dedos.

El contrabajo grabado es un contrabajo Otto Rubner de 1958, pulsado con
los dedos, tocado y grabado por D. Smolken, con cuerdas Spirocore,
afinado en quintas (Do Sol Re La). Cuatro de sus notas vienen de Meatbass
de Karoryfer Samples: el mismo contrabajo y el mismo músico, grabados de
nuevo en 2016 por Ludwik Zamenhof.

Las tres colecciones están regaladas a todos bajo CC0 1.0 Universal
(dominio público). No hace falta pedir permiso; las nombramos porque
estamos agradecidos.

Lo que cambiamos en el bajo eléctrico: guardamos dos fuerzas de nota
larga (la suave y la fuerte de la colección), dos tomas de cada nota grave
y media y una de las agudas; las notas cortas (staccato), dos tomas de
cada una; cinco ruidos de soltar la cuerda y cuatro raspados de púa. La
colección nombra sus notas una octava más arriba de como suenan; medimos
la altura de cada nota. Averiguamos en qué cuerda se tocó cada nota.
Algunas notas cortas se grabaron tan fuertes que la cima de la onda quedó
cortada; la reconstruimos. Los sonidos de bajo con púa de la página tocan
estas notas con el clic de una púa al principio; ese clic se crea en la
página.

En el contrabajo guardamos dos fuerzas (la media y la fuerte), dos tomas
de cada nota grave y media y una de las agudas. La colección tiene una
nota cada 3 o 4 semitonos; donde salta 4, la nota viene de Meatbass (Fa
sostenido 1, Do 2, Fa sostenido 2 y Re sostenido 3). Esas cuatro suenan
más oscuras, así que las aclaramos para que suenen como las notas
vecinas, el ataque y la resonancia por separado, y les quitamos un
retumbo grave. Cinco apagados de cuerda del músico sirven como ruido al
soltar la nota; están marcados para sonar 12 dB más suaves.

Cada nota está en mono y en MP3 de 128 kbps. Empieza justo al pulsar la
cuerda, conserva su resonancia natural hasta 6 segundos y termina con un
desvanecimiento corto. Igualamos el volumen de las notas de cada fuerza,
y cada fuerza conserva su propio nivel. No reafinamos ninguna nota: medimos
su altura y la página la afina al tocarla.
