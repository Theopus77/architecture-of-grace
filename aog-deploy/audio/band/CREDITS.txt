THE BAND (architectureofgrace.org/band) — where the sounds come from

Every note is a recording of a real player, from
  VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition (VSCO 2 CE)
  by Versilian Studios: recorded by Sam Gossner and Simon Dalzell,
  samples cut by Elan Hickler (Soundemote).
  https://github.com/sgossner/VSCO-2-CE

The library is given to everyone under CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain):
  https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
So nothing here needs permission. We name them because we are grateful.

The instruments: trumpet, tenor trombone, French horn, tuba, flute, clarinet,
oboe and bassoon. For each one: a soft and a loud held note, and one short
note, at every pitch the library recorded.

What we changed:
  - Each note is mono, 32,000 samples a second, saved as an MP3.
  - The quiet moment before each note is trimmed. Held notes last 5 seconds
    and short notes less than 1 second, each with a gentle fade at the end.
  - The notes of each instrument were brought to one loudness.
  - We measured the pitch of every note. A few were a little off; the page
    tunes them as it plays.
  - The clarinet's highest note is an F6. Its file name said F sharp.
  - The library has no French horn notes between middle C (C4) and D5.
    The horn's E flat 4, F sharp 4 and B flat 4 (held and short) were made
    from its own C4 and D5: the pitch was moved and the horn's tone kept
    (a method called PSOLA).

THE SAXOPHONE

The alto saxophone comes from
  Weresax by Karoryfer Samples
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/karoryfer.weresax
also given to everyone under CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain).
It was recorded on every note, soft and loud. We use every other note,
soft and loud, from the condenser microphone, the first take, made the
same way as the others: mono, 32,000 samples a second, an MP3, held for
5 seconds (its own loop carries a short note on), the quiet moment before
it trimmed, brought to the band's loudness, with a gentle fade at the end.

MORE PLAYERS (October 2026)

Also from VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition, made the same way:
  - Strings: the violin, viola and cello sections and a solo double bass,
    each held (with vibrato: a soft and a loud note) and short (bounced on
    the bow, spiccato), and each plucked (pizzicato) as its own sound.
    The double bass plucked uses its soft notes only.
  - The harp, at every pitch the library recorded.
  - The trumpet with two mutes (harmon and straight), and the French horn
    with a mute.
  - The piccolo, held and short.
  - Trumpet, trombone, flute, oboe and bassoon with vibrato. Their short
    notes are the same instrument's notes without vibrato.
  - Percussion: the timpani (five drums: a roll for held notes, single
    strokes for short ones), marimba, xylophone and glockenspiel, and a
    beat section: bass drum, snare drum and its roll, suspended cymbal,
    triangle and tambourine.

What we changed, besides what is said above:
  - A plucked or struck note keeps its own ring until it has died away
    (at most 3 seconds; harp, glockenspiel and the timpani rolls 5, the
    cymbal 4).
  - Where the library recorded a pluck or a short note twice, we kept the
    take whose pitch is steadier.
  - We measured the pitch of every note over the part the ear listens to
    (a held note after its first quarter second, a pluck over its ring),
    and the page tunes each note as it plays. The library names no pitch
    for the timpani: we measured each drum's tone.
  - The first players' tuning was measured again the same way where the
    first measure had caught a player sliding up into a note (the French
    horn's G2 had been raised by mistake).

THE STRING SECTIONS IN STEREO (October 2026)

The violin, viola and cello sections (held and short notes) were made again
from the same recordings, so they sound like real players in a room:
  - Both microphones are kept, left and right, as the library recorded
    them. Before, the two were added together into one channel. They stand
    apart, so adding them cancelled part of the sound, differently on every
    note, and the strings sounded hollow.
  - Held notes last 6.5 seconds (they were 5), with a gentle fade at the end.
  - Each note is as loud as the note it replaces, and we measured the pitch
    of every note again from the new files.
  - They are in the folders violins2, violas2 and cellos2.

THE WHOLE BAND IN STEREO (October 2026)

Every other player from VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition was made
again from the same recordings, the same way as the string sections: the
brass, the woodwinds, the double bass, the plucked strings, the harp, the
timpani, the marimba, xylophone and glockenspiel, and the beat section.
  - Both microphones are kept, left and right, as the library recorded
    them. Before, the two were added together, and many notes sounded
    hollow.
  - Each note starts, lasts and fades as before, and is as loud as the
    note it replaces. We measured the pitch of every note again.
  - In a big group (the brass or woodwind section, the jazz groups, the
    marching band, the orchestra), these players use one microphone only,
    so a phone or tablet has enough memory. One microphone sounds just as
    it was recorded.
  - They are in folders whose names end in 2 (trumpet2, flute2, kit2 and
    the others).

THE TENOR AND SOPRANO SAXOPHONES (October 2026)

The tenor saxophone and the soprano saxophone (a saxello, which is a
curved soprano saxophone) come from
  the Versilian Community Sample Library (VCSL)
  by Versilian Studios
  https://github.com/sgossner/VCSL
also given to everyone under CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain).

What we use:
  - The tenor: held notes without vibrato, soft and loud, on every
    whole step from A flat 2 to E6 (23 notes), and a short note on
    23 notes.
  - The tenor with vibrato: held notes with vibrato, one strength
    (19 notes). Its short notes are the tenor's.
  - The soprano: held notes without vibrato, soft and loud, from
    B flat 3 to E6 (8 notes), and a short note on 8 notes.

What we changed, the same way as the others:
  - Each note is mono, 32,000 samples a second, saved as an MP3. The
    quiet moment before it is trimmed. Held notes last 5 seconds (a few
    were played a little shorter, at least 4) and short notes less than
    1 second, each with a gentle fade at the end.
  - The library names each note one octave lower than it sounds (its
    "G#1" is the tenor's lowest note, A flat 2). We measured every note
    and named it by the pitch it sounds.
  - The library recorded with two microphones set apart. Mixed together,
    they cancel part of the sound on some notes, and differently on each
    one. So every note uses one microphone, the left one.
  - The notes of each instrument were brought to one loudness, measured
    on the held part of the note (after its first quarter second).
  - We measured the pitch of every note. The page tunes each note that
    was 3 cents or more off (a cent is one hundredth of a half step).

LA BANDA — de dónde vienen los sonidos

Cada nota es la grabación de un músico de verdad, de VS Chamber Orchestra:
Community Edition (VSCO 2 CE), de Versilian Studios, regalada a todos bajo
CC0 1.0 Universal (dominio público). No hace falta pedir permiso; los
nombramos porque estamos agradecidos.

Lo que cambiamos: cada nota está en mono y en MP3; quitamos el silencio del
principio; igualamos el volumen de las notas de cada instrumento; medimos la
afinación de cada nota y la página corrige las pocas que estaban un poco
desafinadas. Al corno francés le faltaban notas entre el do central (do 4) y
el re 5: hicimos su mi bemol 4, fa sostenido 4 y si bemol 4 a partir de su
propio do 4 y re 5, moviendo la altura y guardando su sonido.

El saxofón alto viene de Weresax de Karoryfer Samples, también regalado a
todos bajo CC0 1.0 Universal (dominio público), y se preparó igual que los
demás.

MÁS MÚSICOS (octubre de 2026)

También de VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition, preparados igual:
  - Cuerdas: las secciones de violines, violas y violonchelos y un
    contrabajo solo, cada uno con notas largas (con vibrato: una suave y una
    fuerte) y cortas (con el arco que rebota, spiccato), y cada uno pulsado
    (pizzicato) como sonido aparte. El contrabajo pulsado usa solo sus notas
    suaves.
  - El arpa, en cada altura que grabó la biblioteca.
  - La trompeta con dos sordinas (harmon y recta) y el corno francés con
    sordina.
  - El flautín, con notas largas y cortas.
  - Trompeta, trombón, flauta, oboe y fagot con vibrato. Sus notas cortas
    son las del mismo instrumento sin vibrato.
  - Percusión: los timbales (cinco tambores: un redoble para las notas
    largas y golpes sueltos para las cortas), marimba, xilófono y
    glockenspiel, y una sección de ritmo: bombo, caja y su redoble, platillo
    suspendido, triángulo y pandereta.

Lo que cambiamos, además de lo de arriba:
  - Una nota pulsada o golpeada guarda su propio sonido hasta que se apaga
    (como mucho 3 segundos; el arpa, el glockenspiel y los redobles de
    timbal 5; el platillo 4).
  - Cuando la biblioteca grabó dos veces una nota pulsada o corta, usamos la
    toma con la afinación más estable.
  - Medimos la afinación de cada nota en la parte que escucha el oído (una
    nota larga después de su primer cuarto de segundo, una nota pulsada
    mientras suena) y la página corrige cada nota al tocarla. La biblioteca
    no dice la altura de los timbales: medimos el tono de cada tambor.
  - Volvimos a medir así la afinación de los primeros músicos donde la
    primera medida había captado al músico subiendo hacia la nota (el sol 2
    del corno francés se había subido por error).

LAS SECCIONES DE CUERDAS EN ESTÉREO (octubre de 2026)

Las secciones de violines, violas y violonchelos (notas largas y cortas) se
hicieron de nuevo con las mismas grabaciones, para que suenen como músicos de
verdad en una sala:
  - Se conservan los dos micrófonos, izquierdo y derecho, como los grabó la
    biblioteca. Antes se sumaban en un solo canal. Están separados, así que
    al sumarlos se cancelaba parte del sonido, de forma distinta en cada
    nota, y las cuerdas sonaban huecas.
  - Las notas largas duran 6,5 segundos (antes 5), con un final suave.
  - Cada nota suena tan fuerte como la que reemplaza, y volvimos a medir la
    afinación de cada nota en los archivos nuevos.
  - Están en las carpetas violins2, violas2 y cellos2.

TODA LA BANDA EN ESTÉREO (octubre de 2026)

Todos los demás músicos de VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition se
hicieron de nuevo con las mismas grabaciones, igual que las secciones de
cuerdas: los metales, las maderas, el contrabajo, las cuerdas pulsadas, el
arpa, los timbales, la marimba, el xilófono, el glockenspiel y la batería.
  - Se conservan los dos micrófonos, izquierdo y derecho, como los grabó la
    biblioteca. Antes se sumaban, y muchas notas sonaban huecas.
  - Cada nota empieza, dura y termina como antes, y suena tan fuerte como
    la que reemplaza. Volvimos a medir la afinación de cada nota.
  - En un grupo grande (la sección de metales o de maderas, los grupos de
    jazz, la banda de marcha, la orquesta), estos músicos usan un solo
    micrófono, para que al teléfono o a la tableta le alcance la memoria.
    Un solo micrófono suena tal como se grabó.
  - Están en carpetas cuyo nombre termina en 2 (trumpet2, flute2, kit2 y
    las demás).

LOS SAXOFONES TENOR Y SOPRANO (octubre de 2026)

El saxofón tenor y el saxofón soprano (un saxello, que es un saxofón
soprano curvo) vienen de la Versilian Community Sample Library (VCSL), de
Versilian Studios (https://github.com/sgossner/VCSL), también regalada a
todos bajo CC0 1.0 Universal (dominio público).

Lo que usamos:
  - El tenor: notas largas sin vibrato, suaves y fuertes, en cada tono
    entero de la bemol 2 a mi 6 (23 notas), y una nota corta en 23 notas.
  - El tenor con vibrato: notas largas con vibrato, de una sola fuerza
    (19 notas). Sus notas cortas son las del tenor.
  - El soprano: notas largas sin vibrato, suaves y fuertes, de si bemol 3
    a mi 6 (8 notas), y una nota corta en 8 notas.

Lo que cambiamos, igual que con los demás:
  - Cada nota está en mono y en MP3; quitamos el silencio del principio.
    Las notas largas duran 5 segundos (unas pocas se tocaron un poco más
    cortas, de al menos 4) y las cortas menos de 1 segundo, cada una con
    un final suave.
  - La biblioteca nombra cada nota una octava más abajo de como suena (su
    "G#1" es la nota más grave del tenor, la bemol 2). Medimos cada nota y
    la nombramos por cómo suena.
  - La biblioteca grabó con dos micrófonos separados. Juntos, se cancelan
    en parte en algunas notas, y de forma distinta en cada una. Por eso
    cada nota usa un solo micrófono, el izquierdo.
  - Igualamos el volumen de las notas de cada instrumento, medido en la
    parte sostenida de la nota (después de su primer cuarto de segundo).
  - Medimos la afinación de cada nota. La página corrige cada nota que
    estaba 3 cents o más desafinada (un cent es la centésima parte de un
    semitono).


THE BAGPIPES

A real Great Highland bagpipe, from
  "Great Highland bagpipe samples" by IowaSpaceWizard (Freesound, pack 29644)
  https://freesound.org/people/IowaSpaceWizard/packs/29644/
Every file is given to everyone under CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain).

  - The chanter: its nine notes, Low G to High A, each one held.
  - The drones: one recording of the drones, made into a held note every
    three semitones from E3 to E4 by changing its speed (never more than four
    semitones), so the drones can be held under a tune in any key.

What we changed, as with the others:
  - Each note is mono, 32,000 samples a second, saved as an MP3. The quiet
    moment before it is trimmed; it lasts 5 seconds, with a gentle fade.
  - The notes were brought to one loudness; the drones sit a little under.
  - A Highland bagpipe is tuned well above the orchestra's A (its A sounds
    near 488 Hz). We measured every note and named it for the note it is
    nearest; the page tunes each one as it plays, so the bagpipes play in
    tune with the rest of the band.

LA GAITA

Una gaita escocesa de verdad, de "Great Highland bagpipe samples" de
IowaSpaceWizard (Freesound). Cada archivo es un regalo para todos (CC0 1.0,
dominio público): las nueve notas del puntero y los roncones. Medimos cada
nota y la página la afina al tocar, para que la gaita suene afinada con el
resto de la banda.
