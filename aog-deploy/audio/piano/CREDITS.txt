TAPE FLUTE (audio/piano/flute/)
  The flute from VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition (VSCO 2 CE) by
  Versilian Studios (CC0, as above): the same recordings The Band plays
  (audio/band/flute/), 10 notes, C4 to C7, soft and held, without vibrato.
  The tape gives it the tape strings' haze, slow wobble and eight seconds.

  Changes made here, for all of these: each note mixed to mono (the two
  microphones lined up first) and saved as a 96 kbps MP3 at 44,100 samples
  a second, like the pianos; the quiet moment before it trimmed (on the
  harpsichord's low keys, also the knock of the key just before the
  pluck); shortened and faded at the end; the rumble below each note taken
  away; every note of an instrument brought to one loudness; on the
  kalimba, the sharpest click of a few tines softened a little, so those
  tines play as loud as the others. We measured the pitch of every note
  and retuned each one to A = 440: the kalimba's tines are tuned the
  traditional way, up to about 60 cents from the piano's notes, and the
  glockenspiel sat 8 to 24 cents sharp. A bell's note is its strike note,
  which the ear hears an octave below the bell's fourth partial. On the
  page, the organ's and the flute's held notes loop smoothly while a key
  is held; each note's loop is a whole number of its own waves long. In
  the library, the harpsichord's, the vibraphone's, the glockenspiel's and
  the kalimba's file names are an octave below the notes they sound; the
  files here are named by the note you hear.

MORE RECORDED SOUNDS (October 2026)

How these were made, for all of them: each note is mixed to mono and
saved as a 96 kbps MP3 at 44,100 samples a second, like the pianos; its
pitch is measured and the note retuned to A = 440; the quiet moment
before it is trimmed; every note of a sound is brought to one loudness.
Each sound has a note on every third key from C1 to C7, so no key is more
than one key away from a recording. Where an instrument has no recording
of a note, the nearest recording is moved in pitch; where the keyboard
goes past the instrument's own range, its lowest or highest recording is.
The programs that made them are in the site's source
(music-handoff/tools/piano_real_sets.py).

  License: the Unlicense (public domain).

ROCK AND JAZZ ORGAN (organ/), GOSPEL ORGAN (gospel/), '70s ROCK ORGAN
(rockorgan/), '70s STRING SYNTH (strsynth/), WARM SYNTH (pad/), '80s SYNTH
BRASS (brass/) and SYNTH LEAD (lead/)
  No recording of a real Hammond organ, or of a real 1970s or 1980s
  synthesizer, with a license that lets us share it was found. These are
  made from the next best thing: a real Unitra B-11, a transistor organ
  made in Poland in 1983, recorded by Karoryfer Samples as "Caveman
  Cosmonaut". https://github.com/sfzinstruments/karoryfer.caveman-cosmonaut
  License: CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain).
  Which of its voices make each sound:
  - Rock and jazz organ: its clarinet voice, softened, on four keys at
    once, like a drawbar organ's 16', 5 1/3', 8' and 4'.
  - Gospel organ: the same voice on six keys at once (16' to 2'),
    brighter, with its violin voice.
  - '70s rock organ: the same voice on five keys (16', 5 1/3', 8', 4' and
    a little 2 2/3'), with its trumpet voice.
  - '70s string synth: its violin voice, with the same voice an octave
    higher, swelling in.
  - Warm synth: its clarinet and violin voices a little apart in pitch,
    so they beat slowly, darkened, opening and swelling in slowly.
  - '80s synth brass: its trumpet and trombone voices a few cents apart;
    the tone opens quickly and settles, and the pitch scoops up a little.
  - Synth lead: its clarinet voice with a little of its trumpet voice,
    with a vibrato that grows in while the note is held.
  The spinning speaker, the amplifier and the chorus are still added on
  the page.
  A held note repeats smoothly: each note is retuned (by at most 2 cents)
  so that a whole number of its waves fits the repeat.

MÁS SONIDOS GRABADOS (octubre de 2026)

Cómo los hicimos, para todos: cada nota está en mono y en MP3, como los
pianos; medimos su afinación y la afinamos a la = 440; quitamos el
silencio del principio; igualamos el volumen de las notas de cada sonido.
Cada sonido tiene una nota cada tres teclas, de do 1 a do 7, así que
ninguna tecla está a más de una tecla de una grabación. Donde un
instrumento no tiene grabada una nota, usamos la grabación más cercana
movida de tono; donde el teclado pasa del registro del instrumento, su
grabación más grave o más aguda. Los programas que los hicieron están en
el código del sitio (music-handoff/tools/piano_real_sets.py).

CAJA DE MÚSICA (audio/piano/musicbox/): no encontramos la grabación de una
caja de música de verdad que podamos compartir. Es la celesta de arriba,
con golpes suaves, cortos y brillantes; desde do 5 hacia arriba, cada nota
sale de la octava más aguda de la celesta.

ACORDEÓN (audio/piano/accordion/): un acordeón Hohner grabado por Jeff
Stauffer en noviembre de 2023 y preparado por michael02022 para FreePats,
bajo CC0 1.0 Universal (dominio público). Cada nota tiene dos lengüetas
un poco separadas (el brillo del acordeón); afinamos cada nota por el
punto medio entre las dos.

CORO (audio/piano/choir/): no encontramos la grabación de un coro de
verdad que podamos compartir. Es un cantante de verdad cantando "a", de
la biblioteca Hadzi-Fia de Karoryfer Samples, bajo CC0 1.0 Universal
(dominio público). En cada nota cantan juntas cuatro de sus grabaciones,
un poco separadas, como un grupo pequeño. Cada una se mueve a la nota con
PSOLA, un método que conserva la vocal tal como la cantó.

PIANO DE JUGUETE (audio/piano/toy/): no encontramos la grabación de un
piano de juguete de verdad que podamos compartir. Es la celesta de arriba,
con golpes fuertes, cortos y más brillantes, y cada tecla un poco
desafinada, como en un piano de juguete.

Ahora todos los sonidos del piano son grabaciones. Donde no pudimos
compartir una grabación del instrumento mismo, las secciones de arriba
dicen qué instrumento de verdad lo toca en su lugar.
