GRAND PIANO (audio/piano/grand/) — also used for the bright piano
  Salamander Grand Piano V3 by Alexander Holm (a Yamaha C5).
  https://archive.org/details/SalamanderGrandPianoV3
  SFZ edition: https://github.com/sfzinstruments/SalamanderGrandPiano
  License: Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 Unported (CC BY 3.0)
  https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/
  Changes made here: 30 notes (every minor third, A0 to C8) at velocity
  layers 4, 8 and 12 of 16; the two microphones time-aligned and mixed to
  mono; silence before the hammer trimmed; each note shortened and faded
  out; one shared level for the whole set; encoded as 96 kbps MP3.
  The bright piano is these same notes with their high end turned up.

UPRIGHT PIANO (audio/piano/upright/) — also used for the honky-tonk and
the soft felt piano
  "Upright Piano" sampled by Simon Dalzell of Ivy Audio, from
  VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition by Versilian Studios.
  https://github.com/sgossner/VSCO-2-CE
  License: CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain). Versilian Studios grants the
  right to redistribute; credit to Simon Dalzell (Ivy Audio) and Versilian
  Studios is encouraged, and given here with thanks.
  Changes made here: 23 notes (every major third, A0 to C8) at all three
  dynamic layers; time-aligned mono; trimmed, shortened and faded; one shared
  level; encoded as 96 kbps MP3. The soft felt piano is these same notes,
  softened, as if a strip of felt hung between the hammers and the strings.

HARP (audio/piano/harp/), MARIMBA (audio/piano/marimba/) and
SOFT STRINGS (audio/piano/strings/)
  From VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition (VSCO 2 CE) by Versilian
  Studios: recorded by Sam Gossner and Simon Dalzell, samples cut by
  Elan Hickler (Soundemote).
  https://github.com/sgossner/VSCO-2-CE
  http://vis.versilstudios.net/vsco-community.html
  License: CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain).
  https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
  So nothing here needs permission. We name them because we are grateful.
  - Harp: 21 notes, E1 to F7, about every third note, played mezzo-forte.
  - Marimba: 10 notes, F2 to C7, struck loud with outrigger mallets.
  - Soft strings: 16 notes, C2 to D6, played softly with vibrato: the
    cello section from C2 to A3, the violas from B3 to A4, the violins
    from C5 to D6.
  Changes made here: each note mixed to mono and saved as a 96 kbps MP3 at
  44,100 samples a second, like the pianos; the quiet moment before it
  trimmed; shortened and faded at the end; the rumble below each note and,
  on the softly recorded low harp strings, the hiss above them taken away;
  every note of an instrument brought to one loudness. We measured the
  pitch of every note: the harp sat about 10 cents flat and a few notes
  were further off, so each note was retuned to A = 440. In the library the
  marimba's and the string sections' file names are an octave below the
  notes they sound; the files here are named by the note you hear. On the
  page, a held string note loops smoothly from 2.4 to 5.4 seconds.
  The tape strings (a 1970s tape keyboard, for prog rock) are the soft
  strings, with a hazier tone, a slow wobble, and a tape that runs out
  after eight seconds.

HARPSICHORD (audio/piano/harpsichord/), CHURCH ORGAN (audio/piano/pipeorgan/),

BELLS (audio/piano/bells/), KALIMBA (audio/piano/kalimba/) and
'80s ELECTRIC PIANO (audio/piano/fmpiano/)
  From the Versilian Community Sample Library (VCSL) by Versilian Studios.
  https://github.com/sgossner/VCSL
  License: CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain).
  https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
  The church organ was sampled by Simon Dalzell of Ivy Audio. Versilian
  Studios grants the right to redistribute it; credit to Simon Dalzell
  and Versilian Studios is encouraged, and given here with thanks.
  - Harpsichord: the English harpsichord (a Zuckermann kit harpsichord),
    its normal stop: 28 notes, every whole tone from A#1 to E6.
  - Church organ: the pipe organ, all its loud stops together: 21 notes,
    every minor third from C1 to C6.
  - Vibraphone: soft mallets, 11 notes, F3 to E6. It was recorded with its
    motor off; the page adds the motor's gentle pulse.
  - Glockenspiel: struck loud, 6 notes, G5 to C8.
  - Bells: tubular bells ("Tubular Bells 2"), the louder strokes, 11 notes,
    C4 to F5.
  - Kalimba: the kalimba from Tanzania, 19 of its tines, G#2 to C#7.
  - '80s electric piano: a real Yamaha TX81Z synthesizer playing its
    "FM Piano" sound, the loud layer: 18 notes, every major third from E1
    to C7.

TAPE FLUTE (audio/piano/flute/)
  The flute from VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition (VSCO 2 CE) by
  Versilian Studios (CC0, as above): the same recordings The Band plays
  (audio/band/flute/), 10 notes, C4 to C7, soft and held, without vibrato.
  The tape gives it the tape strings' haze, slow wobble and eight seconds.

  Changes made here, for all of these: each note mixed to mono (the two
  microphones lined up first) and saved as a 96 kbps MP3 at 44,100 samples
  a second, like the pianos; the quiet moment before it trimmed (on the
  harpsichord's low keys, also the knock of the key just before the
  pluck); shortened and faded at the end; the rumble below each note taken
  away; every note of an instrument brought to one loudness; on the
  kalimba, the sharpest click of a few tines softened a little, so those
  tines play as loud as the others. We measured the pitch of every note
  and retuned each one to A = 440: the kalimba's tines are tuned the
  traditional way, up to about 60 cents from the piano's notes, and the
  glockenspiel sat 8 to 24 cents sharp. A bell's note is its strike note,
  which the ear hears an octave below the bell's fourth partial. On the
  page, the organ's and the flute's held notes loop smoothly while a key
  is held; each note's loop is a whole number of its own waves long. In
  the library, the harpsichord's, the vibraphone's, the glockenspiel's and
  the kalimba's file names are an octave below the notes they sound; the
  files here are named by the note you hear.

WARM ELECTRIC PIANO (audio/piano/rhodes/)
  "jRhodes GM" by Jeff Learman: his own 1977 Rhodes Mark I Stage 73
  electric piano, mapped and relooped by him for the Discord SFZ General
  MIDI Bank.
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/Discord-SFZ-GM-Bank
  (Discord GM/Melodic/005-Electric Piano 1)
  License: CC0. The instrument's own file says "License: Creative Commons
  CC0, Jeff Learman". So nothing here needs permission; we name him with
  thanks.
  - 15 notes, F1 to C7, about every fifth note, at one strength.
  Changes made here: each note saved as a 96 kbps MP3 at 44,100 samples a
  second, like the pianos. Each recording is short and ends in a loop that
  a player repeats while the note fades; here that loop is repeated
  before the note is saved, and the note keeps fading at the rate it was
  fading when it reached the loop, until it has died away (at most 8
  seconds). The rumble below each note taken away; every note brought to
  one loudness; every note retuned to A = 440 (most sat 2 to 10 cents
  sharp). On the page, a note played softly sounds rounder and a note
  played hard sounds brighter, with more bark.

Every other sound on the piano is not a recording: the bright electric
piano, the clavinet, the rock and jazz, gospel, '70s rock and cinema
organs, the accordion, the toy piano, the celesta, the steel drums, the
music box, the choir and the synths. They are built on the page from
simple waves, the same way the drum machine builds its kits.

PIANO DE COLA (audio/piano/grand/), que también es el piano brillante:
  Salamander Grand Piano V3 de Alexander Holm (un Yamaha C5), bajo la
  licencia Creative Commons Atribución 3.0 (CC BY 3.0). Usamos 30 notas en
  tres fuerzas, en mono y en MP3. El piano brillante son las mismas notas
  con los agudos más fuertes.

PIANO VERTICAL (audio/piano/upright/), que también es el honky-tonk y el

VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition de Versilian Studios, regalado a
todos bajo CC0 1.0 Universal (dominio público). El piano de fieltro son
las mismas notas, suavizadas, como si una tira de fieltro colgara entre
los martillos y las cuerdas.

ARPA, MARIMBA y CUERDAS SUAVES (audio/piano/harp/, marimba/ y strings/):
de VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition (VSCO 2 CE) de Versilian
Studios, grabado por Sam Gossner y Simon Dalzell, y cortado por Elan
Hickler (Soundemote). Regalado a todos bajo CC0 1.0 Universal (dominio
público): no hace falta pedir permiso; los nombramos porque estamos
agradecidos.
  - Arpa: 21 notas, de mi 1 a fa 7, mezzo-forte.
  - Marimba: 10 notas, de fa 2 a do 7, fuerte.
  - Cuerdas suaves: 16 notas, de do 2 a re 6, tocadas suave y con
    vibrato: violonchelos abajo, violas en el medio y violines arriba.
Lo que cambiamos: cada nota está en mono y en MP3, como los pianos;
quitamos el silencio del principio; la acortamos y la apagamos poco a poco
al final; quitamos el retumbo por debajo de cada nota y el soplido de las
cuerdas graves del arpa, que se grabaron muy suave; igualamos el volumen
de las notas de cada instrumento. Medimos la afinación de cada nota: el
arpa estaba un poco baja, así que afinamos cada nota a la = 440. En la
página, una nota larga de cuerdas se repite sin cortes entre los 2,4 y los
5,4 segundos.
Las cuerdas de cinta (un teclado de cinta de los 70, para el rock
progresivo) son las cuerdas suaves, con un sonido más brumoso, un vaivén
lento y una cinta que se acaba a los ocho segundos.

CLAVECÍN, ÓRGANO DE IGLESIA, VIBRÁFONO, GLOCKENSPIEL, CAMPANAS, KALIMBA y
PIANO ELÉCTRICO DE LOS 80 (audio/piano/harpsichord/, pipeorgan/,
vibraphone/, glockenspiel/, bells/, kalimba/ y fmpiano/): de la Versilian
Community Sample Library (VCSL) de Versilian Studios, regalada a todos bajo
CC0 1.0 Universal (dominio público): no hace falta pedir permiso. El órgano
lo grabó Simon Dalzell de Ivy Audio; los nombramos con gratitud.
  - Clavecín: el clavecín inglés (un clavecín Zuckermann armado de un
    kit), 28 notas, de la♯ 1 a mi 6.
  - Órgano de iglesia: el órgano de tubos con todos sus registros fuertes,
    21 notas, de do 1 a do 6.
  - Vibráfono: mazos suaves, 11 notas, de fa 3 a mi 6. Se grabó con el
    motor apagado; la página agrega el latido suave del motor.
  - Glockenspiel: golpeado fuerte, 6 notas, de sol 5 a do 8.
  - Campanas: campanas tubulares, 11 notas, de do 4 a fa 5.
  - Kalimba: la kalimba de Tanzania, 19 de sus lengüetas, de sol♯ 2 a
    do♯ 7.
  - Piano eléctrico de los 80: un sintetizador Yamaha TX81Z de verdad
    tocando su sonido "FM Piano", 18 notas, de mi 1 a do 7.

FLAUTA DE CINTA (audio/piano/flute/): la flauta de VS Chamber Orchestra:
Community Edition de Versilian Studios (CC0), las mismas grabaciones que
toca La Banda: 10 notas, de do 4 a do 7, suave, larga y sin vibrato. La
cinta le da el sonido brumoso, el vaivén lento y los ocho segundos de las
cuerdas de cinta.

Lo que cambiamos en todos estos: cada nota está en mono (antes alineamos los
dos micrófonos) y en MP3, como los pianos; quitamos el silencio del
principio (en las teclas graves del clavecín, también el golpecito de la
tecla justo antes de pulsar la cuerda); la acortamos y la apagamos poco a
poco al final; quitamos el retumbo por debajo de cada nota; igualamos el
volumen de las notas de cada instrumento; en la kalimba suavizamos un poco
el chasquido más agudo de algunas lengüetas, para que suenen igual de
fuerte que las demás. Medimos la afinación de cada nota y la afinamos a
la = 440: las lengüetas de la kalimba están afinadas a la manera
tradicional, hasta unos 60 cents lejos de las notas del piano, y el
glockenspiel estaba un poco alto. La nota de una campana es la que oye el
oído, una octava por debajo de su cuarto parcial. En la página, las notas
largas del órgano y de la flauta se repiten sin cortes mientras mantienes
la tecla.

PIANO ELÉCTRICO CÁLIDO (audio/piano/rhodes/): "jRhodes GM" de Jeff
Learman, su propio piano eléctrico Rhodes Mark I Stage 73 de 1977,
preparado por él para el Discord SFZ General MIDI Bank
(https://github.com/sfzinstruments/Discord-SFZ-GM-Bank). Licencia: CC0;
su archivo dice "License: Creative Commons CC0, Jeff Learman". No hace
falta pedir permiso; lo nombramos con gratitud.
  - 15 notas, de fa 1 a do 7, más o menos cada cinco notas, a una sola
    fuerza.
Lo que cambiamos: cada nota está en MP3, como los pianos. Cada grabación
es corta y termina en un tramo que se repite mientras la nota se apaga;
aquí repetimos ese tramo antes de guardar la nota, y la nota se sigue
apagando al mismo ritmo, hasta que se acaba (8 segundos como mucho).
Quitamos el retumbo por debajo de cada nota, igualamos el volumen y
afinamos cada nota a la = 440. En la página, una nota tocada suave suena
más redonda, y una tocada fuerte suena más brillante y con más garra.

Todos los demás sonidos del piano no son grabaciones: se crean en la
página con ondas sencillas, como la caja de ritmos crea sus kits.
