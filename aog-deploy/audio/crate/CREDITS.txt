THE SCRIPTURE RECORDS — where the voices come from

The words are the King James Version (KJV) of the Bible and, in Spanish, the
Reina-Valera 1909. Both are public domain.

The voices are volunteers reading the Bible for LibriVox (librivox.org), who give
their recordings to everyone as public domain. So nothing here needs permission.
We name them because we are grateful.

  In English (King James Version)
  Be Ye Holy · The Only Way · The Lord's Prayer · Psalms of Praise ·
  Charity Never Faileth · Promises · Faith and the Armour of God
    Darren L. Slider (Leviticus), Jenn Raimundo (Ezekiel), David Lawrence
    (1 Thessalonians, 1 Timothy), Joy Chan (Joshua), Michael Packard (Proverbs),
    and the LibriVox volunteers who read Psalms, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Luke, John,
    Acts, Romans, 1 Corinthians, Ephesians, Philippians, Hebrews and 1 Peter.

Each verse was cut from its chapter, set to the same loudness as the others,
and split in two at the reader's pause, so each half is one pad. The Lord's
Prayer is one take (Luke 11:2–4), cut at the end of each line.

THE SPEECHES (The D-Day Prayer · FDR · Words of Courage)

These are Franklin D. Roosevelt's own words in his own voice, from recordings the
Franklin D. Roosevelt Presidential Library and the National Archives share online
(fdrlibrary.org/utterancesfdr), which the National Archives lists as unrestricted.
Speeches a President gives as President are works of the U.S. government, which
belong to everyone.
  The D-Day Prayer, June 6, 1944 (FDR Library recording afdr290)
  First Inaugural Address, March 4, 1933 (afdr012)
  State of the Union ("four freedoms"), January 6, 1941 (afdr224)
  Address to Congress ("a date which will live in infamy"), December 8, 1941 (afdr244)
Each line was cut where FDR says it and set to the same loudness as the others.

THE SPEECHES (JFK · Ask Not · JFK · We Choose the Moon · Reagan · Challenger ·
Reagan · Tear Down This Wall)

These are John F. Kennedy's and Ronald Reagan's own words in their own voices.
  Inaugural Address, January 20, 1961: the John F. Kennedy Presidential Library's
    recording, which it marks public domain
    (archive.org/details/JohnF.KennedyInauguralAddress)
  Address at Rice University ("we choose to go to the Moon"), September 12, 1962:
    film of the speech marked public domain
    (archive.org/details/president-john-f.-kennedy-09-12-1962)
  Address to the Nation on the Challenger, January 28, 1986: Ronald Reagan
    Presidential Library recording, National Archives ID 7087577, unrestricted
  Address at the Brandenburg Gate, June 12, 1987: Ronald Reagan Presidential
    Library recording, National Archives ID 7087579, unrestricted
Each line was cut where the President says it and set to the same loudness as
the others.
