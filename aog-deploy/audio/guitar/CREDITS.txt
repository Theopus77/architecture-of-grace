THE GUITAR · Architecture of Grace · where the recorded guitars come from

The guitar's sounds play real guitars, recorded one note at a time. Every
recording here is given to everyone under CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain):
  https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
So nothing here needs permission. We name them because we are grateful.

Electric guitars (the sets "green" and "black")
  Black And Green Guitars by Karoryfer Samples
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/karoryfer.black-and-green-guitars
  https://www.karoryfer.com
  A green Gretsch Anniversary and a black Hofner Club, two hollow-body
  electric guitars, recorded and photographed by Brian Wood. Recorded
  straight from the guitar, with no amplifier: the page's own amp and
  pedals shape every electric sound.

Steel-string acoustic guitar (the set "steel"; also the twelve-string sounds)
  A 2017 Martin HD28, recorded by Jeff Learman for the Discord SFZ
  General MIDI Bank
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/Discord-SFZ-GM-Bank
  (The bank's README says each instrument's license is in its own .sfz
  file; this one says "License: Creative Commons CC0".)

Classical guitar (the set "nylon")
  Spanish Classical Guitar, recorded in 2008 by Roberto (zenvoid.org)
  for the FreePats project
  https://github.com/freepats/spanish-classical-guitar
  http://freepats.zenvoid.org/Guitar/acoustic-guitar.html

What we changed
  - We chose notes three half steps apart (for the classical guitar, the
    open strings and the notes between them). Each note is moved to the
    exact pitch on the page.
  - For the electric guitars we chose a softer and a harder pluck, and two
    takes of each low and middle note (the two of the library's four that
    sound most alike). They also have short, muted notes (for chugs and
    gallops) and the sound of a hand letting go of a string.
  - The steel-string and the classical guitar have one pluck and one take
    of each note, as they were recorded. The steel-string's notes were made
    to be looped: we let the loop ring on, fading as the note was fading.
  - Each note starts as its string starts to sound. The quiet moment
    before it is trimmed, and so is the pick or nail pressing the string
    before it lets go, so every note lands on the beat. Each note keeps
    its natural ring for up to 4.6 seconds and ends with a short fade.
  - Each note's tuning is measured, so the page plays it in tune. Within
    each pluck, the notes are evened out along the neck; a soft pluck stays
    softer than a hard one.
  - Each note is mono, 44,100 samples a second, saved as a 128 kbps MP3.
  - How it was done: music-handoff/tools/strings/build_guitar_sets.py.

LA GUITARRA · Architecture of Grace · de dónde vienen las guitarras grabadas

Los sonidos de la guitarra tocan guitarras de verdad, grabadas nota por
nota. Todas estas grabaciones están regaladas a todos bajo CC0 1.0
Universal (dominio público). No hace falta pedir permiso; las nombramos
porque estamos agradecidos.

Guitarras eléctricas ("green" y "black"): Black And Green Guitars de
Karoryfer Samples, una Gretsch Anniversary verde y una Hofner Club negra,
dos guitarras eléctricas de caja hueca, grabadas por Brian Wood
directamente de la guitarra, sin amplificador. El amplificador y los
pedales de la página dan forma a cada sonido eléctrico.

Guitarra acústica de cuerdas de acero ("steel"; también las de doce
cuerdas): una Martin HD28 de 2017, grabada por Jeff Learman para el Discord
SFZ General MIDI Bank (su licencia, CC0, está en su propio archivo .sfz).

Guitarra clásica ("nylon"): Spanish Classical Guitar, grabada en 2008 por
Roberto (zenvoid.org) para el proyecto FreePats.

Lo que cambiamos: elegimos notas separadas por tres semitonos y llevamos
cada una a su altura exacta. En las eléctricas elegimos un toque suave y
uno fuerte, y dos tomas de cada nota grave y media; también tienen notas
cortas y apagadas, y el sonido de la mano al soltar una cuerda. La acústica
de acero y la clásica tienen un toque y una toma por nota, como se
grabaron. Cada nota empieza cuando su cuerda empieza a sonar: quitamos el
silencio de antes y también el roce de la púa o la uña antes de soltar la
cuerda, para que cada nota caiga a tiempo. Cada nota suena hasta 4,6
segundos y termina con un desvanecimiento corto. Igualamos el volumen
a lo largo del mástil. Cada nota es mono, a 44.100 muestras por segundo,
en MP3 de 128 kbps.
