THE DRUM MACHINE · Architecture of Grace · where the recorded kits come from

Twenty of the twenty-five kits are real drums and percussion, recorded.
Kit L is a real drum machine, recorded, and so are most pads of kits A and
B (see below). Kits C and F are drum-machine sounds made on this page
(electro and the 808): no recording of a real 808 with a license we can
use was found. Real drum machines make their sounds electronically, so the
page makes the rest too: the synth tom in kit M, the chord stab in kit L,
and the clap, rim and bell of kits A and B.

Every library below is given to everyone under CC0 1.0 Universal (public
domain): https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
So nothing here needs permission. We name them because we are grateful.

KITS A AND B · THE CLASSIC (audio/drums/classic78/) — October 2026
  Kit A is the machine's own kit, and kit B is kit A retuned. Their kick,
  snare, closed and open hi-hat and tom are now a real Roland CR-78, a
  rhythm machine from 1978, recorded from the machine:
  - kick and snare: wikter, "Roland CR78 Sounds",
    https://freesound.org/people/wikter/packs/40417/ — "Collection of
    Samples registered using a Midiman MAudio Delta44 & a Roland CR78."
  - hi-hats and tom: oceansonmars, "CR78 Drum Kit",
    https://freesound.org/people/oceansonmars/packs/39611/ — "One-shot
    samples from Roland CR-78"
  License of both: Creative Commons 0 (CC0 1.0, public domain)
  https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
  Changes made here: from Freesound's own MP3 copy of each sound, one
  recording for each pad (a sampler's memory holds one), the rumble taken
  away, the snare's first sharp instant held down a little, each pad as
  loud as the kit's page-made sound was (the hi-hats louder: the page-made
  ones could hardly be heard); saved as mono 96 kbps MP3. The
  oceansonmars files are the same on both sides, so one channel loses
  nothing. The CR-78 has no hand clap, and the free recordings have no
  cowbell and only a rimshot cut off after 4 ms, so the clap, rim and bell
  are still made on the page. If a file cannot be fetched, the page makes
  that sound as before.

KIT L · 909 HOUSE (audio/drums/house909/) — added October 2026
  A real Roland TR-909 drum machine, sampled by Janne G:son Berg from his
  own machine, cut up and posted by altemark on Freesound:
  "TR-909 JGB pack", https://freesound.org/people/altemark/packs/1643/
  License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0)
  https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
  The sound pages say: "Sampled by Janne G:son Berg from his old 909. Cut
  up and organized by me. Here his the original readme.txt Janne
  distributed with the wav: Sampled in one session from my (now sold) 909.
  24 bit, 44.1 kHz. Feel free to use the samples for whatever you like."
  Changes made here: from Freesound's own MP3 copy of each sound, a soft,
  a normal and an accent hit for each pad, each a neighbouring knob
  setting of the machine (bd08 to bd12, sn32 to sn35, ch07 to ch10, oh02
  to oh05, clp04 to clp07, lt09 to lt12, ride01 to ride04); the rumble
  under the hats and ride taken away; cut and faded; each pad as loud as
  the kit's page-made sound was (the hats and ride a little louder, so
  they can be heard); saved as mono 96 kbps MP3. The 909 has no chord, so
  the STAB pad is still made on the page.

THE LIBRARIES

  Big Rusty Drums, Karoryfer Samples
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/karoryfer.big-rusty-drums
  A big early-1980s kit made by Zygmunt Szpaderski in Poland: a 24" kick,
  a 14x8 snare, toms, Sabian hi-hats and cymbals.

  Virtuosity Drums, Versilian Studios and Karoryfer Samples
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/virtuosity_drums
  The house kit of Virtuosity Musical Instruments, a drum shop in
  Boston, set up the way a kit is in a jazz club, played by Austin McMahon
  with sticks, recorded with close, overhead, room and "lo-fi"
  microphones. Its timbales come with it.

  Swirly Drums, Karoryfer Samples
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/karoryfer.swirly-drums
  A whole kit played with brushes, with long stirs round the snare, and
  hand drums (a djembe and a cajón played with a foot pedal).

  Gogodze Phu Vol II, Karoryfer Samples
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/karoryfer.gogodze-phu-vol-ii
  A kick, snare, three toms and a hi-hat, tuned to sound like a 1973
  record, with an old "retro" microphone.

  Versilian Community Sample Library (VCSL), Versilian Studios
  https://github.com/sgossner/VCSL
  Hand percussion and band drums: claps, shakers, tambourines, a
  woodblock, triangles, a güiro, claves, agogô bells, cowbells, a cabasa,
  congas, bongos, a concert bass drum, hand cymbals, a glockenspiel, a
  marching snare, a snare roll and a tenor drum.

  VS Chamber Orchestra 2: Community Edition, Versilian Studios
  https://github.com/sgossner/VSCO-2-CE
  A double bass, plucked, and an upright piano.

  jRhodes GM, Jeff Learman (in the Discord SFZ GM Bank)
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/Discord-SFZ-GM-Bank
  A 1977 Rhodes Mark I electric piano.

  The "a" of Hadzi-Fia, Karoryfer Samples (legato_vocal_tutorial)
  https://github.com/sfzinstruments/legato_vocal_tutorial
  One singer holding the vowel "a".

Where no free recording of a sound exists, the nearest real instrument
plays, and the pad says what it is: kit D's finger snap and kit G's snap
are now a hand clap, kit H's maraca is a shaker, and kit N's talking drum
is a djembe. Kit J's stomp is a cajón struck by a foot pedal.

What we changed:
  - For each sound we chose a soft, a normal and a hard hit, each its own
    recording, and a second take of the sounds a beat uses most.
  - The microphones of each hit are mixed together. Some were recorded
    with two microphones set apart, one on each side (cymbals, hi-hats,
    the room, and most hand percussion). Those sounds now keep both
    sides, left and right, as they were recorded. Adding the two sides
    into one made them sound hollow. Sounds recorded with one microphone
    stay in one channel.
  - Each kit has its own EQ, compression and tuning. Some have a room or
    an echo added (made here, not recorded), and many sounds are cut
    shorter.
  - The quiet moment before each hit is trimmed. Each sound is cut to a
    length that fits it, with a gentle fade at the end.
  - The levels are evened out. A rebuilt kit's pads sit as loud as its
    page-made pads did; a new kit's kick and snare sit as loud as kit A's.
  - Each sound is 44,100 samples a second. A one-channel sound is saved
    as a 96 kbps MP3; a two-sided sound as a 192 kbps MP3 (96 for each
    side). Kit T (groove metal) is still all one channel for now.
  - How each kit is built: music-handoff/tools/drumkits in the site's
    source.

LA CAJA DE RITMOS · de dónde vienen los kits grabados

Veinte de los veinticinco kits son batería y percusión de verdad, grabadas.
El kit L es una caja de ritmos de verdad, grabada, y también casi todos
los pads de los kits A y B (ver abajo). Los kits C y F son sonidos de caja
de ritmos hechos en la página (electro y la 808): no encontramos una
grabación de una 808 de verdad con una licencia que podamos usar. Las
cajas de ritmos de verdad hacen sus sonidos de forma electrónica, así que
la página hace el resto: el tom sintetizado del kit M, el acorde del kit L
y el aplauso, el aro y la campana de los kits A y B.

KITS A Y B · EL CLÁSICO (audio/drums/classic78/), desde octubre de 2026:
el kit A es el kit propio de la máquina, y el kit B es el kit A
reafinado. Su bombo, caja, charles cerrado y abierto y tom ahora son una
Roland CR-78 de verdad, una caja de ritmos de 1978, grabada de la
máquina: el bombo y la caja por wikter ("Roland CR78 Sounds",
https://freesound.org/people/wikter/packs/40417/), los charles y el tom
por oceansonmars ("CR78 Drum Kit",
https://freesound.org/people/oceansonmars/packs/39611/), los dos bajo
Creative Commons 0 (CC0 1.0, dominio público). Cada pad suena tan fuerte
como su sonido hecho en la página (los charles, más fuerte: los hechos en
la página casi no se oían). La CR-78 no tiene aplauso, y las
grabaciones libres no tienen cencerro y el aro dura solo 4 ms, así que el
aplauso, el aro y la campana se siguen haciendo en la página. Si un
archivo no llega, la página hace ese sonido como antes.

KIT L · HOUSE 909 (audio/drums/house909/), desde octubre de 2026: una caja
de ritmos Roland TR-909 de verdad, grabada por Janne G:son Berg de su
propia máquina y publicada por altemark en Freesound: "TR-909 JGB pack"
(https://freesound.org/people/altemark/packs/1643/), bajo Creative Commons

Cada pad suena tan fuerte como su sonido hecho en la página (los charles y

El bombo del kit T (groove metal) se hizo de nuevo (octubre de 2026):
afinado un poco más alto y con el cuerpo del tambor, para que el altavoz
de un teléfono oiga un tambor, no un clic.

Cuando no hay una grabación libre de un sonido, suena el instrumento de
verdad más cercano y el pad dice cuál es: el chasquido de dedos de los
kits D y G ahora es una palmada, la maraca del kit H es un shaker y el
tambor parlante del kit N es un djembe. El pisotón del kit J es un cajón
tocado con un pedal.

Lo que cambiamos: para cada sonido elegimos un golpe suave, uno normal y
uno fuerte, cada uno su propia grabación, y una segunda toma de los
sonidos que más se usan. Mezclamos los micrófonos de cada golpe. Algunos
se grabaron con dos micrófonos separados, uno a cada lado (platillos,
hi-hats, la sala y casi toda la percusión de mano). Esos sonidos ahora
conservan sus dos lados, izquierdo y derecho, como se grabaron. Juntar los
dos lados en uno los hacía sonar huecos. Los sonidos grabados con un solo
micrófono siguen en un canal. Cada kit tiene su propia ecualización, compresión y afinación;
algunos tienen una sala o un eco añadidos (hechos aquí, no grabados).
Quitamos el silencio del principio, cortamos cada sonido a un largo que le
queda bien y lo apagamos suavemente al final. Igualamos los volúmenes: los
pads de un kit reconstruido suenan tan fuerte como sonaban hechos en la
página, y el bombo y la caja de un kit nuevo, tan fuerte como en el kit A.
Un sonido de un canal se guarda en MP3 de 96 kbps; uno de dos lados, en MP3
de 192 kbps (96 por lado). El kit T (groove metal) sigue, por ahora, todo
en un canal.
