That 12 is the constant of proportionality. It is called k. Every row is the same equation: y = 12x. A straight line through (0, 0) is what proportional looks like.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

If both numbers go up together, it is proportional.

Going up together is not enough: every row has to divide to the same k, and the graph has to start at (0, 0). A $5 fee plus $12 an hour goes up together and is not proportional.

Same k in every row, and it starts at zero.

⚠️ People often think…

For percent, you move the decimal two places.

Two places, but the direction depends on which way you are going: 25% becomes 0.25 to calculate with it, and 0.25 becomes 25% to name it.

To calculate, left. To name it, right.

⚠️ People often think…

For percent change, you divide by the new number.

Divide the change by the ORIGINAL number, because the original is what changed. $40 to $50 is 10 ÷ 40 = 25% up, not 10 ÷ 50.

Change over original. Always.

⚠️ People often think…

25% off a $40 shirt means the answer is 25% of 40.

25% of 40 is $10, which is the discount, not the price. The price is $30. Read whether the question wants the part taken off or the amount you pay.

Discount, or price? Underline which one.

Helpful Hints

🪜 From a ratio to a percent — the ladder

Ratio → rate → unit rate → the same unit rate every time (proportional) → call it k → y = kx → a percent is just k written out of 100.

Compare, divide, repeat, name it, write it.

🤝 The whole topic in one idea

Everything here is one multiplication: y = k × x. Pay = 12 × hours. Tax = 0.06 × price. Tip = 0.20 × bill. Find the k, and the rest is one step.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • A straight line is NOT enough. Proportional means straight AND through (0, 0).
  • “Goes up by the same amount” is a line. Proportional needs the same RATIO — check y ÷ x.
  • 8% is 0.08, not 0.8. Two places left.
  • Read what the question wants: the tax or the total? The discount or the sale price?
  • Percent change divides by the ORIGINAL, never the new number.
  • 20% off then 10% off is not 30% off. The second one is taken from the new price.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A table — “is this proportional? what is the constant?” (divide every row).
  • A graph with a point marked — “what does (1, 12) mean?” (12 dollars for 1 hour: the unit rate).
  • A receipt — price, then discount, then tax. Do them in that order, one at a time.
  • Before and after — “what is the percent change?” (change ÷ original).
  • Two fractions — “what is the unit rate?” (divide the first by the second; flip and multiply).

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Find a unit rate, even when both numbers are fractions?
  • Tell whether a table, a graph, an equation or a story is proportional — and say why?
  • Write y = kx for a situation and say what k means in words?
  • Find a tip, a tax, a discount and the total, without mixing them up?
  • Find a percent increase or decrease, dividing by the original?
  • Work backward from a sale price to the original price?
