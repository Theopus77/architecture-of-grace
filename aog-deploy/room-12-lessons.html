UNIT 1Unit overview

Knowing & Accepting Yourself

Identity, Feelings, Mistakes, and the Inside Voice
AUGUST · SEPTEMBER · OCTOBER
Before children can extend grace to others, they must first understand who they are, that their feelings carry meaning, and that mistakes are not who they are. These seven lessons build the foundational vocabulary of self-knowledge.
BEFORE YOU TEACH

How to Use the Closed-Loop Routing Tables

A one-page “Score → Intervention Crosswalk” opens every unit of Book 1 · K–2.

1 · Screen. Run the Grace Compass check-in. It scores three domains — A (Regulation & Well-Being), B (Self-Compassion & Growth), C (Social Competency & Repair).

2 · Route to the lowest domain. A student’s lowest-scoring domain is where support is needed most. Find that domain on the unit’s crosswalk; the rows beneath it are this unit’s targeted lessons, novel scenes, and anchor charts.

3 · Run the Teacher Move. The last column gives a one-line, low-prep way to deliver the row — usually a scaffold so students fill content, not structure.

4 · Mind the red flag. A red ★ (elevated) or ★★ (highest-sensitivity) badge means safety steps apply: counselor co-facilitation, posted crisis resources, and — for ★★ — pre/post consultation. Never route a flagged row without them.

Doing well? If all three domains score 75+, use the ◆ enrichment row — an integration capstone — instead of a support row.

Important: deliver lessons in order. The crosswalk tells you where a student’s need is met within the unit — it is not a license to reorder the curriculum. The same routing logic runs the Grace Compass digital screener, so print and screen always agree.

UNIT 1 · LESSON 1Lesson 1

Who Am I?

Build this lesson’s anchor chart live — the interactive board →This lesson’s worksheet is interactive — open it: type in it, turn it in, Print my copy / Print it blank →
20–25 min · Week 1 · First Lesson of the Year
OBJECTIVE

Students name at least two things about themselves and understand every person in the class is unique.

CASEL FOCUS

Self-Awareness · identifying personal qualities and positive self-concept

VOCABULARY

who I am · special · unique · strength · different

MATERIALS

Drawing paper · crayons · large identity web poster · Sammy puppet

ILLINOIS SEL ALIGNMENT GRADES K–2 · EARLY ELEMENTARY

Goal 1 — Self-Awareness & Self-Management

1B.1a Identify one’s likes and dislikes, needs and wants, strengths and challenges.

Goal 2 — Social Awareness & Interpersonal Skills

2B.1a Describe the ways that people are similar and different.

Aligned to the Illinois Social/Emotional Learning Standards (ISBE).

SAFETY CHECK

Before class: Confirm Sammy is in his permanent spot. Practice the Kind Coach voice once. This is the first lesson — the tone you set today shapes the rest of the year.

SAMMY · KIND COACH VOICE

“I want to know all about YOU! Can you tell me one thing that makes you special?”

▶ DIRECT INSTRUCTION

Scripted teaching sequence · 5 STEPS · read aloud or in your own words

01Introduce Sammy3 MIN

Take three minutes to introduce Sammy before any content. Let students meet the puppet and feel the tone. Sammy says hello to each student by name. The classroom learns to anticipate Sammy as a regular presence.

IN-LESSON SCAN

After Sammy greetings: scan the room. Any child who did not respond may need a gentle individual check-in. Note for Teacher Reflection — do not address publicly.

02Build the Identity Web7 MIN

Draw a large web on the board — center circle says "ME." Five branches: Things I Love · Things I’m Good At · Things That Make Me Happy · Something Special About My Family · Something People Might Not Know About Me. Build one branch at a time. Model with your own answers first.

03Individual Webs8 MIN

Students draw their own mini identity web on paper. Circulate with warmth — every web is valid, every answer is right.

IN-LESSON SCAN

While circulating: any student staring at blank paper for more than 2 minutes — crouch down: "Tell me one thing you love, I’ll write it for you." Do not press publicly.

04Share Circle5 MIN

Each student holds up their paper and shares ONE thing of their choosing. They never have to share more than they want to. Sammy responds warmly to each share.

05Establish the Compact2 MIN

After the share circle: begin the Classroom Compact. "What do we need to feel safe in this space?" Collect 3–5 answers, post permanently. Post it beside the Identity Web and keep it up all year.

KINDERGARTEN

GRADES 1–2

Teacher circulates and writes dictated words while student draws. Pointing to a drawing is enough. Verbal sharing is optional.

Students write simple labels independently. Encourage specifics: not "I like animals" but "I like my dog Max." Share one favorite branch.

EXIT TICKET

“Thumb up if you feel like the class knows one true thing about you today.”

Sammy: thumbs-up to every hand. No commentary. Sammy validates participation, not content.

TEACHER REFLECTION

Which students struggled to name anything — defaulting to silence? This is early data about self-perception that will matter all year. Note quietly.

SAFETY CHECK

POST-LESSON CLOSE-OUT · Any child who did not engage at all — flag for a gentle individual check-in before Lesson 2.

Did anything arise today requiring counselor follow-up? ☐ No ☐ Yes — contact counselor today

FAMILY CONNECTION

A Note Home

Today we began the year by celebrating who your child is. Ask at home: "What is one thing you would put on YOUR identity web?" Listen without correcting. Every answer is right.

UNIT 1 · LESSON 2Lesson 2

My Feelings Are Friends

Build this lesson’s anchor chart live — the interactive board →
20–25 min · Week 2
OBJECTIVE

Students identify and name at least four basic emotions and understand that all feelings are okay — every feeling carries a message.

CASEL FOCUS

Self-Awareness · recognizing and naming emotions

VOCABULARY

happy · sad · angry · scared · proud · worried · feelings are messengers

MATERIALS

Feeling face cards · Sammy puppet · feelings anchor chart paper · open floor space

ILLINOIS SEL ALIGNMENT GRADES K–2 · EARLY ELEMENTARY

Goal 1 — Self-Awareness & Self-Management

1A.1a Recognize and accurately label emotions and how they are linked to behavior.

1A.1b Demonstrate control of impulsive behavior.

Aligned to the Illinois Social/Emotional Learning Standards (ISBE).

OPENING REVIEW · 1–2 MIN
Show the identity web from Lesson 1. "Last lesson we learned we are all special. Today Sammy has a question for us." Sammy holds up a worried face: "Is it okay to feel sad? What about angry?" Take 2–3 student responses.
SAMMY · KIND COACH VOICE

“I am feeling something right now. Can you help me figure out what I am feeling?”

▶ DIRECT INSTRUCTION

Scripted teaching sequence · 5 STEPS · read aloud or in your own words

01Name the Feelings5 MIN

Hold up feeling face cards one by one. As each is named, Sammy mirrors the face. "Sammy is feeling SAD. What might be happening for Sammy?" Take 2–3 short answers per feeling. Do not rush.

IN-LESSON SCAN

Watch for any child who physically mimics Sammy’s distressed pose and does not come out of it. These children may be showing you something real. Note privately.

02Feelings Are Messengers4 MIN

Deliver the core teaching: "Feelings are not problems. Every feeling has a message. Sad means something we love is hurt. Angry means something feels unfair. Scared means something feels unsafe. Feelings help us know what we need."

03Body Movement — Show Me5 MIN

Move to open floor space. Teacher calls out feelings; students show the feeling with their whole body — face, posture, hands. "Show me proud." "Show me worried." "Show me brave." End with "show me calm."

IN-LESSON SCAN

During movement: any child frozen, covering face, or refusing to participate may be experiencing something real. Do not press. Note for reflection.

04Build the Anchor Chart7 MIN

Build the Feelings Anchor Chart together. As students name each feeling, Sammy adds the face and one short message. The chart becomes the permanent reference for the year.

05Closing Question2 MIN

Sammy asks: "What is the most important thing about feelings?" Sample from class. Land on: "All feelings are okay. They tell us something."

KINDERGARTEN

GRADES 1–2

Stay with four core feelings: happy, sad, angry, scared. Use exaggerated body movement. The chart can use only the faces — no words needed.

Add proud, worried, lonely. Students write one sentence describing what each feeling tells us. Pair-share before whole-group.

EXIT TICKET

“Show me on your face: one feeling you felt today. Sammy mirrors the emotion back to the child.”

Sammy: thumbs-up to every hand. No commentary. Sammy validates participation, not content.

TEACHER REFLECTION

Which students showed the broadest emotional vocabulary? Which could only name "happy" or "mad"? Note for differentiated support.

SAFETY CHECK

POST-LESSON CLOSE-OUT · Any child who became unusually distressed during the movement activity — note for a brief individual check-in before Lesson 3.

Did anything arise today requiring counselor follow-up? ☐ No ☐ Yes — contact counselor today

FAMILY CONNECTION

A Note Home

Today we learned that feelings are messengers — not problems. Try this at home: when a big feeling comes up, ask "What is this feeling trying to tell us?" rather than trying to fix it right away.

UNIT 1 · LESSON 3Lesson 3

Everyone Makes Mistakes

Build this lesson’s anchor chart live — the interactive board →
20–25 min · Week 3
OBJECTIVE

Students articulate that mistakes are normal and universal, and that a mistake does not mean they are bad.

CASEL FOCUS

Self-Awareness · growth mindset; separating actions from identity

VOCABULARY

mistake · oops · learning · try again · normal · everyone

MATERIALS

Mentor text: Beautiful Oops! by Barney Saltzberg · Sammy puppet · sticky notes · Mistake Wall space

ILLINOIS SEL ALIGNMENT GRADES K–2 · EARLY ELEMENTARY

Goal 1 — Self-Awareness & Self-Management

1A.1a Recognize and accurately label emotions and how they are linked to behavior.

Goal 3 — Responsible Decision-Making

3B.1b Make positive choices when interacting with classmates.

Aligned to the Illinois Social/Emotional Learning Standards (ISBE).

OPENING REVIEW · 1–2 MIN
Show the Feelings Anchor Chart. "Last lesson: feelings are messengers. Today Sammy makes a mistake." Sammy knocks something over dramatically. "Is Sammy bad — or did he just make a mistake?"
SAMMY · KIND COACH VOICE

“Wait — did I make a mistake… or AM I a mistake? Are those the same thing?”

▶ DIRECT INSTRUCTION

Scripted teaching sequence · 5 STEPS · read aloud or in your own words

01Sammy’s Question3 MIN

Deliver the distinction: An Oops is something you DID. "I am bad" is about WHO YOU ARE. They are not the same. "Has anyone here ever made a mistake? Does that mean everyone here is bad?" Wait for the laugh.

Sammy: “I made a mistake. But I am still ME. I am not bad — I just had an Oops!”
02Read-Aloud · Beautiful Oops!7 MIN

Read Beautiful Oops! by Barney Saltzberg with enthusiasm. After each page: "What happened? Did that turn out to be a bad thing or something surprising?"

IN-LESSON SCAN

During the read-aloud: watch for children whose body language shifts at the word "mistake." Clenching or looking down can indicate shame at home. Do not address publicly. Note for the Teacher Reflection.

03Mistake Wall6 MIN

Distribute sticky notes. Each student draws or dictates ONE mistake. Post on the Mistake Wall. "Look at ALL these mistakes — every one made by the amazing people in this room. Mistakes are NORMAL."

04Class Chant6 MIN

Sammy picks up what was knocked over: "Oops. I made a mistake. I will fix it." Students practice as a chant with body movements: OOPS → FIX IT → TRY AGAIN. Repeat three times with growing energy.

05Land the Distinction3 MIN
End with the core line: "A mistake is not who you are — it is something that happened. Oops + Fix It + Try Again = GROWTH. You are a learner. Learners make mistakes."
KINDERGARTEN

GRADES 1–2

The mentor text is the primary anchor. The Mistake Wall creates a powerful normalizing experience. Focus on the chant with movement.

Students write their mistake AND what they learned on the sticky note. Grade 2: name one Oops they made recently and what they did to fix it.

EXIT TICKET

“Raise your hand if you know that making a mistake does not make you bad.”

Sammy: thumbs-up to every hand. No commentary. Sammy validates participation, not content.

TEACHER REFLECTION

Which students seemed to genuinely believe a mistake means they are bad? Note for Unit 2 — these students will need extra support during the Real Sorry and Self-Compassion lessons.

SAFETY CHECK

POST-LESSON CLOSE-OUT · Review the Mistake Wall. Any note describing something witnessed rather than done warrants a quiet follow-up conversation — not in front of peers.

Did anything arise today requiring counselor follow-up? ☐ No ☐ Yes — contact counselor today

FAMILY CONNECTION

A Note Home

Today we learned that mistakes are normal — everyone makes them. At home, try sharing one of YOUR recent mistakes (something small). Modeling makes it safe for children to do the same.

UNIT 1 · LESSON 4Lesson 4

My Inside Voice — Kind or Unkind?

Build this lesson’s anchor chart live — the interactive board →
20–25 min · Week 4
OBJECTIVE

Students identify the difference between a Kind Coach and a Grumpy Gus inner voice, and practice replacing unkind self-talk.

CASEL FOCUS

Self-Awareness · recognizing self-talk; Self-Management: regulating internal narrative

VOCABULARY

inside voice · Kind Coach · Grumpy Gus · self-talk · kind · unkind

MATERIALS

Sammy puppet (with TWO voices ready) · Kind Coach / Grumpy Gus anchor chart paper · red and green markers

ILLINOIS SEL ALIGNMENT GRADES K–2 · EARLY ELEMENTARY

Goal 1 — Self-Awareness & Self-Management

1A.1a Recognize and accurately label emotions and how they are linked to behavior.

1A.1b Demonstrate control of impulsive behavior.

Aligned to the Illinois Social/Emotional Learning Standards (ISBE).

OPENING REVIEW · 1–2 MIN
Show the Mistake Wall. "Last lesson: mistakes are normal. Today: when we make a mistake, what does our inside voice say?" Two student responses.
SAMMY · KIND COACH VOICE

“I have TWO voices that talk inside my head. One is kind. One is grumpy. Today we are going to meet them both.”

▶ DIRECT INSTRUCTION

Scripted teaching sequence · 5 STEPS · read aloud or in your own words

01Meet Kind Coach4 MIN
Sammy speaks in his Kind Coach voice (warm, gentle): "When I make a mistake, my Kind Coach says — ‘Oops! What can I learn? Let’s try again.’ Kind Coach is honest AND kind. Kind Coach helps me grow."
02Meet Grumpy Gus4 MIN
Sammy switches to Grumpy Gus voice (sharper, theatrical, never frightening): "Grumpy Gus says — ‘You ALWAYS mess up! You’re BAD! You’ll NEVER get it right!’ Grumpy Gus attacks WHO YOU ARE — not just what happened. Grumpy Gus makes us want to hide."
03Sort the Statements8 MIN

Read 6–8 statements. Class sorts each into Kind Coach (green) or Grumpy Gus (red). Examples: "I made a mistake — I can fix it." (Kind) / "I always ruin everything." (Grumpy) / "What can I learn from this?" (Kind) / "Nobody likes me." (Grumpy). Add to anchor chart as you go.

IN-LESSON SCAN

During the sort: any child who consistently sides with Grumpy Gus statements ("but they’re true though") may be carrying heavy self-talk. Note for follow-up. Do not correct in front of peers.

04Practice Switching5 MIN

Teacher reads a Grumpy Gus statement. Class together flips it to Kind Coach. "I always mess up" becomes "I made a mistake — I can try again." Repeat three times with different statements.

05The Three Questions3 MIN
Land the closer: "When something goes wrong, the Kind Coach asks three questions. ONE: What happened? TWO: What can I learn? THREE: What is one next step?" Repeat together.
KINDERGARTEN

GRADES 1–2

Use exaggerated voice contrast. Sort with hand signals — thumbs up for Kind Coach, thumbs down for Grumpy Gus. The takeaway: Grumpy Gus is the unkind voice; Kind Coach is the helpful one.

Students write one Grumpy Gus statement they’ve heard themselves say (anonymous, on a slip), then write the Kind Coach version. Pair-share before whole-group.

EXIT TICKET

“Show me with your fingers: how many voices live inside your head? (Hold up two.) Which one are you going to listen to?”

Sammy: thumbs-up to every hand. No commentary. Sammy validates participation, not content.

TEACHER REFLECTION

Which students could not generate a Kind Coach reframe? Which seemed surprised the kind voice was even an option? These students need extra support before Unit 2.

SAFETY CHECK

POST-LESSON CLOSE-OUT · Any student whose Grumpy Gus statements felt unusually harsh or specific to their identity — note for counselor awareness. Do not press publicly.

Did anything arise today requiring counselor follow-up? ☐ No ☐ Yes — contact counselor today

FAMILY CONNECTION

A Note Home

Today we learned about two inside voices: Kind Coach and Grumpy Gus. At home, when you hear your child being hard on themselves, ask gently: "Was that your Kind Coach or your Grumpy Gus?"

UNIT 1 · LESSON 5Lesson 5

Oops vs. I Am Bad

Build this lesson’s anchor chart live — the interactive board →
20–25 min · Week 5
OBJECTIVE

Students distinguish between an Oops (a behavior) and "I am bad" (an identity statement), and articulate that the second is never true.

CASEL FOCUS

Self-Awareness · separating action from identity (foundational distinction of the curriculum)

VOCABULARY

Oops · behavior · identity · I AM · I DID · shame · growth

MATERIALS

Sammy puppet · Oops vs. I Am Bad anchor chart · scenario sorting strips

ILLINOIS SEL ALIGNMENT GRADES K–2 · EARLY ELEMENTARY

Goal 1 — Self-Awareness & Self-Management

1A.1a Recognize and accurately label emotions and how they are linked to behavior.

1B.1a Identify one’s likes and dislikes, needs and wants, strengths and challenges.

Aligned to the Illinois Social/Emotional Learning Standards (ISBE).

OPENING REVIEW · 1–2 MIN
Show the Kind Coach / Grumpy Gus chart. "Last lesson: two inside voices. Today: when Grumpy Gus talks, sometimes he tries to trick us. He confuses two things that are NOT the same."
SAMMY · KIND COACH VOICE

“Grumpy Gus told me two things today. He said ‘You made a bad CHOICE.’ And he said ‘You ARE bad.’ Are those the same thing?”

▶ DIRECT INSTRUCTION

Scripted teaching sequence · 5 STEPS · read aloud or in your own words

01The Big Distinction4 MIN
Deliver the foundational distinction: "An Oops is about what you DID. ‘I am bad’ is about who you ARE. One can be fixed. The other is never true — because no one is bad. People do things. People are not their things."
02Two Columns5 MIN

Build the anchor chart side by side. Left column: OOPS — "What I DID. One moment. Can be fixed. Sounds like: I made a bad CHOICE." Right column: "I AM BAD" — DANGER — "Who I AM. Never true. Sounds like: I am a bad PERSON." Color the danger side red.

03Sort the Statements7 MIN

Read scenario strips one at a time. Class decides: Oops or "I Am Bad"? Examples: "I forgot my homework." (Oops) / "I am stupid." (I Am Bad — never true) / "I bumped into someone." (Oops) / "Nobody likes me." (I Am Bad — never true). Discuss why.

IN-LESSON SCAN

A student who calls "I AM BAD" statements TRUE about themselves — even hypothetically — may be carrying internalized shame. Note for counselor awareness. Do NOT correct publicly.

04The Test Question5 MIN
Introduce the lifetime tool: "When Grumpy Gus says something hard, ASK: ‘Is this about my BEHAVIOR or my IDENTITY?’ If it’s about behavior — you can fix it. If it’s about identity — it’s a lie."
05Closing3 MIN

Sammy: "I will mess up sometimes. That is okay. I will fix it. I will try again. I am NOT my mistakes." Class repeats together as a closing chant.

KINDERGARTEN

GRADES 1–2

Use the simplest distinction: "What I DID" vs "Who I AM." Use thumbs to vote on each scenario. The point lands even if the words feel big.

Students write one Grumpy Gus statement they’ve heard themselves think, then identify whether it was a behavior comment or an identity comment. Discuss: how did each one make them feel?

EXIT TICKET

“"Is this about my BEHAVIOR or my IDENTITY?" — say it together with me, three times.”

Sammy: thumbs-up to every hand. No commentary. Sammy validates participation, not content.

TEACHER REFLECTION

This is the most important distinction in the curriculum. Which students could state it back? Which seemed to genuinely understand "I am not my mistakes"? Which still seemed unsure?

SAFETY CHECK

POST-LESSON CLOSE-OUT · Any student who voiced a strong identity statement about themselves ("I AM bad") — even if they later corrected it — note for counselor awareness.

Did anything arise today requiring counselor follow-up? ☐ No ☐ Yes — contact counselor today

FAMILY CONNECTION

A Note Home

Today we practiced separating what we DID from who we ARE. At home, when redirecting your child, try replacing "you are…" with "what you DID was…" — e.g., not "you are messy" but "what you did was leave the mess."

UNIT 1 · LESSON 6Lesson 6

I Can Change and Grow

This lesson’s worksheet is interactive — open it: type in it, turn it in, Print my copy / Print it blank →
20–25 min · Week 6
OBJECTIVE

Students articulate that they have grown since the start of the year and identify one specific way they have changed.

CASEL FOCUS

Self-Awareness · recognizing growth; identifying capacity for change

VOCABULARY

grow · change · before · now · practice · better at · still learning

MATERIALS

Sammy puppet · Then-and-Now drawing paper · crayons · optional photos from start of year

ILLINOIS SEL ALIGNMENT GRADES K–2 · EARLY ELEMENTARY

Goal 1 — Self-Awareness & Self-Management

1B.1a Identify one’s likes and dislikes, needs and wants, strengths and challenges.

1C.1b Identify goals for academic success and classroom behavior.

Aligned to the Illinois Social/Emotional Learning Standards (ISBE).

OPENING REVIEW · 1–2 MIN
Show the Mistake Wall and the Oops vs. I Am Bad chart. "We have learned a lot together. Today we are going to look back and see how much we have grown."
SAMMY · KIND COACH VOICE

“When I started this year, I did not know how to do a lot of things. Now I can do some of them! I am still learning others. That is what GROWING is.”

▶ DIRECT INSTRUCTION

Scripted teaching sequence · 4 STEPS · read aloud or in your own words

01What Is Growing?4 MIN
Define growing simply: "Growing means — you can do something now that you could not do before. You learned. You practiced. You got better at it. You are still YOU — you just have more skills, more words, more ways to handle hard things."
02Sammy’s Story5 MIN

Sammy shares a small growth story: "When I started this year, I used to say ‘I AM bad’ when I made a mistake. Now I say ‘I made an OOPS — I can fix it.’ I am still learning! But I have GROWN."

Sammy: “What is something you used to find hard — that is easier now?”
03Then-and-Now Drawing12 MIN

Students fold paper in half. On the BEFORE side: draw something that used to feel hard or scary. On the NOW side: draw the same thing — but with how they handle it now. Optional: add a Kind Coach speech bubble showing what they say to themselves now. See the polished Drawing Activity reference.

IN-LESSON SCAN

Students who cannot identify any growth may be feeling stuck. Crouch down warmly: "Even noticing one tiny thing counts. Want me to help?" Do not pressure.

04Share Circle4 MIN

Voluntary share. Each student who wants to holds up their drawing and describes ONE thing that has changed. Sammy responds warmly to each share. Land: "Growing is small — and real."

KINDERGARTEN

GRADES 1–2

Stay with simple before/now contrast. Stick figures and one-word labels are perfect. The act of noticing one growth moment is the lesson.

Students add a sentence below each drawing: "Before, I…" / "Now, I…" Discuss: what helped you change? Was it practice? A safe adult? Time?

EXIT TICKET

Your Hurt Is Real

20–25 min · Week 15 · Unit 3 Begins
OBJECTIVE

Students articulate that real hurt deserves to be named, and that forgiveness begins with acknowledging the hurt — not skipping over it.

CASEL FOCUS

Social Awareness · validation of emotional experience; Self-Awareness: naming hurt

VOCABULARY

hurt · real · name it · acknowledge · validate · feelings count

MATERIALS

Sammy puppet · chart paper · scenario cards (Unit 3 Lesson 1 cards)

ILLINOIS SEL ALIGNMENT GRADES K–2 · EARLY ELEMENTARY

Goal 1 — Self-Awareness & Self-Management

1A.1a Recognize and accurately label emotions and how they are linked to behavior.

Goal 2 — Social Awareness & Interpersonal Skills

2A.1b Use listening skills to identify the feelings and perspectives of others.

Aligned to the Illinois Social/Emotional Learning Standards (ISBE).

SAFETY CHECK

STANDING SAFETY NOTE — ALL UNIT 3 LESSONS: This unit never asks students to forgive anyone who is currently harming them. If a student’s responses suggest an ongoing harmful situation, pause the lesson and follow the Disclosure Protocol immediately.

OPENING REVIEW · 1–2 MIN
Reference the storage box of future-self letters. "Last unit: we learned to put down what WE were carrying. This unit: we learn what to do when someone has HURT us. We start by saying: your hurt is real."
SAMMY · KIND COACH VOICE

“When something hurts — sometimes people say ‘it’s no big deal’ or ‘get over it.’ But the hurt is REAL. Today we say so.”

▶ DIRECT INSTRUCTION

Scripted teaching sequence · 4 STEPS · read aloud or in your own words

01Naming Real Hurt5 MIN
Frame the unit: "When someone hurts you — with words, with actions, with leaving you out — your hurt is REAL. We do not skip over it. We do not pretend. We name it: ‘This hurt me. It still hurts.’ Naming the hurt is the FIRST step — not forgiveness."
02Sammy’s Story6 MIN

Sammy shares (with appropriate distance): "Last week, a friend said something mean about me to other friends. I heard about it. It hurt. People kept telling me ‘just forget it.’ But I could not. I needed to NAME it first. ‘What they said hurt me.’ Once I said that out loud — I could start thinking about what to do."

Sammy: “My hurt is real. Saying it out loud helps.”
03Words for Real Hurt6 MIN

Build a small chart together: words children can use to name real hurt. Examples: "That hurt me." / "I felt left out." / "Those words made me feel small." / "I did not deserve that." Discuss: who can you say these words TO? (a trusted adult, a journal, a friend, yourself.)

04Closing4 MIN
Land the through-line: "Forgiveness is NOT skipping over the hurt. Forgiveness BEGINS with naming the hurt. If you cannot say ‘it hurt’ — you cannot let it go. Today we practiced the first step."
KINDERGARTEN

GRADES 1–2

Use the simplest line: "It hurt. That is real." Practice saying it together. The validation is the lesson. Do not push toward forgiveness yet.

Students name (anonymously, on a slip) one small hurt they remember. They write the words: "What they did was… It made me feel…" Discuss: how does it feel to NAME it?

EXIT TICKET

“"My hurt is real" — say it together, three times. Sammy bows to each saying.”

Sammy: thumbs-up to every hand. No commentary. Sammy validates participation, not content.

TEACHER REFLECTION

Which students could not name any hurt — even hypothetically? Which named hurts that suggest current ongoing harm? Each is meaningful. Note for counselor coordination.

SAFETY CHECK

POST-LESSON CLOSE-OUT · Any student whose named hurt suggests ongoing harm — follow the Disclosure Protocol. Do NOT coach toward forgiveness when someone is currently being hurt.

Why Do People Hurt People?

20–25 min · Week 16
OBJECTIVE

Students explore the gap between intent and impact, and articulate that understanding WHY someone hurt them does not mean what they did was okay.

CASEL FOCUS

Social Awareness · perspective-taking; intent vs. impact

VOCABULARY

why · reason · intent · impact · understanding · not the same as okay

MATERIALS

Sammy puppet · chart paper · scenario cards (Unit 3 Lesson 2 cards)

ILLINOIS SEL ALIGNMENT GRADES K–2 · EARLY ELEMENTARY

Goal 2 — Social Awareness & Interpersonal Skills

2A.1a Recognize that others may experience situations differently from oneself.

2A.1b Use listening skills to identify the feelings and perspectives of others.

Aligned to the Illinois Social/Emotional Learning Standards (ISBE).

OPENING REVIEW · 1–2 MIN
Show the "Words for Real Hurt" chart from Lesson 1. "Last lesson: name the hurt. Today: WHY do people hurt people in the first place? And does WHY matter?"
SAMMY · KIND COACH VOICE

“Sometimes when someone hurts me, I want to know WHY. Did they mean to? Did they have a hard day? Did they not know? Does the WHY matter?”

▶ DIRECT INSTRUCTION

Scripted teaching sequence · 4 STEPS · read aloud or in your own words

01Why People Hurt People5 MIN
Build a chart of reasons (not excuses): "Sometimes people hurt people because… they are tired. They are hurting themselves. They did not see what they did. They were trying to fit in. They did not know better. They were scared. They were copying someone. None of these make hurt OKAY — but understanding helps."
02Intent vs. Impact6 MIN
Introduce the distinction: "Sometimes people MEAN to hurt us. Sometimes they DON’T mean to. Either way — the hurt is REAL. We can understand the WHY — AND still say the impact was real. Both are true."
03Two Stories7 MIN

Tell two short stories using scenario cards: one where the person meant to hurt, one where they did not. Discuss: was the hurt the same? (yes.) Did the WHY make a difference in how to respond? (yes — a little.) Did the WHY make it okay? (no.)

04The Two-Part Truth4 MIN
Land the lesson: "Understanding WHY someone hurt me is NOT the same as saying it was okay. I can hold BOTH. ‘They did not mean to AND it still hurt.’ ‘They were having a hard day AND that does not make it okay.’ Both. Both. Both."
Sammy: “I can understand AND still feel hurt.”
KINDERGARTEN

GRADES 1–2

Stay with one story. The takeaway: "Even if they did not mean to — the hurt is still real." Do not push for sophisticated reasoning yet.

Discuss: when someone hurts you, is it more important to KNOW WHY, or to know IT HURT? (Both matter. Different times.) Practice saying both truths together.

EXIT TICKET

“"I can understand AND still feel hurt" — say it together. Hold up two fingers — both truths.”

Sammy: thumbs-up to every hand. No commentary. Sammy validates participation, not content.

TEACHER REFLECTION

Which students collapsed into one truth (only the WHY matters) or the other (only the HURT matters)? Holding both is the developmental work. Note for differentiated support.

SAFETY CHECK

POST-LESSON CLOSE-OUT · Any student who insisted "they did not mean to" about an ongoing harmful situation — may be making excuses for someone who is currently hurting them. Counselor follow-up.

Did anything arise today requiring counselor follow-up? ☐ No ☐ Yes — contact counselor today

FAMILY CONNECTION

A Note Home

Today we practiced holding two truths: "they may not have meant to" AND "the hurt was real." At home, when your child reports a hurt, validate first: "That hurt. That is real." Understanding the why can come AFTER.

Forgiving Is Not the Same as Trusting

Build this lesson’s anchor chart live — the interactive board →
25–30 min · Week 17 · ★★ HIGHEST RISK · Counselor On-Site
OBJECTIVE

Students distinguish between forgiveness (an inner choice for their own peace) and trust (a between-people thing that must be earned), and articulate that forgiving someone does not mean staying close to them.

CASEL FOCUS

Social Awareness · relationship judgment; Relationship Skills: discernment about closeness

VOCABULARY

forgive · trust · earn · inside · between people · not the same · never owed

MATERIALS

Sammy puppet · Forgiveness vs. Trust anchor chart · scenario cards (Unit 3 Lesson 3 cards) · COUNSELOR PHYSICALLY ON-SITE FULL LESSON

ILLINOIS SEL ALIGNMENT GRADES K–2 · EARLY ELEMENTARY

Goal 2 — Social Awareness & Interpersonal Skills

2D.1a Identify problems and conflicts commonly experienced by peers.

Goal 3 — Responsible Decision-Making

3A.1b Identify social norms and safety considerations that guide behavior.

Aligned to the Illinois Social/Emotional Learning Standards (ISBE).

SAFETY CHECK

★★ HIGHEST RISK — COUNSELOR PHYSICALLY ON-SITE THE FULL LESSON. NO EXCEPTIONS.

This lesson explicitly affirms students do NOT have to be close to people who have hurt them. Some students may be in situations where this affirmation comes as new information — and may be relieved or distressed.

CRITICAL: forgiveness is NEVER required when someone is still hurting them. State this explicitly multiple times.

Crisis resources visible: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline · Crisis Text Line: HOME to 741741.

OPENING REVIEW · 1–2 MIN
Show the two-truths chart from Lesson 2. "Last lesson: understand the why AND name the hurt. Today: even if I forgive someone — do I have to be close to them again?"
SAMMY · KIND COACH VOICE

“Someone hurt me. I am thinking about forgiving them — for ME. But I do not feel safe being close to them anymore. Is that okay?”

▶ DIRECT INSTRUCTION

Scripted teaching sequence · 4 STEPS · read aloud or in your own words

01Two Different Things5 MIN
Frame the central distinction: "Forgiveness and trust are two DIFFERENT things. Forgiveness is INSIDE — it is for YOUR peace. Trust is BETWEEN people — it is about safety. You can give forgiveness alone. You cannot give trust alone — the other person has to EARN it back."
02Build the Two Columns7 MIN

Build the anchor chart together. FORGIVENESS column: Inside · For your own peace · Can happen alone · Needs nothing from the other person · A gift you give yourself. TRUST column: Between people · For the safety of the relationship · Needs both people to be ready · Earned through real change · Never owed — you don’t have to give it.

03The Critical Affirmation6 MIN
State explicitly, more than once: "You can FORGIVE someone and STILL choose not to be close to them. That is not mean. That is WISE. Forgiveness is for your peace. Trust is earned — NEVER owed. AND — forgiveness is NEVER required when someone is still hurting you."
04Closing4 MIN
Sammy: "I can forgive someone for my OWN peace — and still keep my distance. My safety matters. My peace matters. Both can be true."
Sammy: “Forgiveness is for me. Trust must be earned.”
KINDERGARTEN

GRADES 1–2

Stay with the simplest version: "I can forgive someone — AND not have to play with them anymore." The distinction lands even if the words are big.

Discuss scenarios: a friend who keeps being mean, a sibling who broke a trust, a peer who teased then apologized. For each: should you forgive? Should you trust again? Discuss WHY.

EXIT TICKET

“"Forgiveness is for me. Trust must be earned." — say it together, twice. The counselor and teacher both bow.”

Sammy: thumbs-up to every hand. No commentary. Sammy validates participation, not content.

TEACHER REFLECTION

CRITICAL: any student who insisted they MUST forgive AND trust someone who is still hurting them — brief the counselor immediately. They may be receiving harmful messages elsewhere.

SAFETY CHECK

POST-LESSON CLOSE-OUT · Counselor coordinates same-day debrief with teacher. Identify all students whose responses suggested ongoing harmful situations. Disclosure Protocol for any direct disclosures.

Forgiveness Takes Time

Build this lesson’s anchor chart live — the interactive board →
20–25 min · Week 18
OBJECTIVE

Students articulate that forgiveness is not a straight line — setbacks are normal and not failure.

CASEL FOCUS

Self-Management · patience with self; Self-Awareness: noticing emotional progress

VOCABULARY

path · not a straight line · setback · practice · okay where you are

MATERIALS

Sammy puppet · The Forgiveness Path anchor chart · sticky dots (one per student)

ILLINOIS SEL ALIGNMENT GRADES K–2 · EARLY ELEMENTARY

Goal 1 — Self-Awareness & Self-Management

1A.1a Recognize and accurately label emotions and how they are linked to behavior.

Goal 2 — Social Awareness & Interpersonal Skills

2D.1b Identify approaches to resolving conflicts constructively.

Aligned to the Illinois Social/Emotional Learning Standards (ISBE).

OPENING REVIEW · 1–2 MIN
Show the Forgiveness vs. Trust chart. "Last lesson: forgiveness is for our peace. Today: forgiveness is a PATH. And the path is not a straight line."
SAMMY · KIND COACH VOICE

“I thought I had forgiven someone. Then yesterday — I felt mad about it again. Did I do it WRONG?”

▶ DIRECT INSTRUCTION

Scripted teaching sequence · 4 STEPS · read aloud or in your own words

01The Path Is Not Straight5 MIN
Frame the lesson: "Forgiveness is a PATH — not a straight line. Some days are easier. Some days the hurt comes back. That is NOT failure. That is the path. Wherever you are on the path — it is OKAY."
02Walk the Six Stations8 MIN

Walk through the six stations on the anchor chart: 1. STILL HURTING. 2. STARTING TO UNDERSTAND. 3. SOME DAYS ARE BETTER. 4. CHOOSING TO PUT IT DOWN TODAY. 5. THE HURT IS GETTING SMALLER. 6. I AM OKAY. At each: "What might it feel like here? What might it sound like to YOU?"

03Sticky-Dot Activity7 MIN

Distribute one sticky dot per student. "Place your dot on the station where YOU are today — about a hurt you are working on. This is anonymous — nobody has to know which dot is yours. Wherever you are — it is OKAY." Quiet music. No commentary required.

IN-LESSON SCAN

Watch for any student who places at Station 1 with intensity, who refuses to place at all, or who places multiple dots. These are meaningful signals. Note privately.

04The Practice Is the Path4 MIN
Land the closing: "Moving backward on the path is NOT failure. It IS the practice. You put it down again. Each time — you get a little better at it. Wherever you are today — you are exactly where you need to be."
KINDERGARTEN

GRADES 1–2

Use only three stations: "Still Hurting" · "Some Days Are Better" · "I Am Okay." The dot placement is the lesson. Words can be minimal.

Discuss after placement: "Did anyone place where they did NOT expect to? What does that tell you?" Optional journal entry: "Today I am at…"

EXIT TICKET

“"Wherever I am on the path — it is okay" — say it together. Touch the dot you placed (without showing others).”

Sammy: thumbs-up to every hand. No commentary. Sammy validates participation, not content.

TEACHER REFLECTION

Where did the dots cluster? Did dots cluster at "Still Hurting"? At "I Am Okay"? Did anyone place dots in scattered places? The pattern reveals the unit’s emotional weight.

SAFETY CHECK

POST-LESSON CLOSE-OUT · A student who placed at Station 1 with significant emotional intensity — brief the counselor for awareness. Repeated placement at Station 1 across lessons may indicate ongoing harm.

Did anything arise today requiring counselor follow-up? ☐ No ☐ Yes — contact counselor today

FAMILY CONNECTION

Students audit their own grace practice over the year and contribute to a class Grace Garden — a visible record of grace given and received.

CASEL FOCUS

Self-Awareness · reflective practice; Social Awareness: community recognition

VOCABULARY

audit · noticing · garden · growth · what counts

MATERIALS

Sammy puppet · Grace Garden display space · paper flowers (one per student) · pens

ILLINOIS SEL ALIGNMENT GRADES K–2 · EARLY ELEMENTARY

Goal 3 — Responsible Decision-Making

3C.1a Identify and perform roles that contribute to one’s classroom.

3C.1b Identify and perform roles that contribute to one’s family.

Aligned to the Illinois Social/Emotional Learning Standards (ISBE).

OPENING REVIEW · 1–2 MIN
Show the small acts register. "Last lesson: grace with hard people. Today: we audit — we notice — our own grace this year. And we plant a garden."
SAMMY · KIND COACH VOICE

“I want to look at the year and ask: where did I GIVE grace? Where did I RECEIVE it? That is what an audit is.”

▶ DIRECT INSTRUCTION

Scripted teaching sequence · 3 STEPS · read aloud or in your own words

01My Grace Audit10 MIN

Distribute paper flowers. Each flower has THREE petals. Petal 1: One time I GAVE grace this year. Petal 2: One time I RECEIVED grace this year. Petal 3: One time I gave myself grace. Students fill in each petal (write or draw).

IN-LESSON SCAN

A student who cannot fill in any petal — may not be noticing the grace around them. Crouch down warmly: "Want help thinking of one?" Do not pressure.

02The Grace Garden8 MIN

One at a time, students place their flower in the Grace Garden display. They can name one petal aloud (optional) or place silently. Sammy adds his flower too. The Garden becomes the visible record of the year’s grace.

03What the Garden Shows6 MIN
Walk around the Garden together. "What do we see? Many small flowers — many small graces. Some big, some tiny. ALL count. This Garden grew because YOU practiced. The Garden is YOU."
KINDERGARTEN

GRADES 1–2

Petals can be drawings only. Sharing aloud is fully optional. The act of placing the flower IS the celebration.

Encourage specifics on each petal. Optional reflection: which petal was hardest to fill in? Why?

EXIT TICKET

“Touch your hand to your flower and say: "I gave grace this year. I received grace this year. I am part of the Garden."”

Sammy: thumbs-up to every hand. No commentary. Sammy validates participation, not content.

TEACHER REFLECTION

Which petal was hardest for the most students? Often the third (giving themselves grace) is. That tells you where Unit 2’s self-compassion work needs reinforcement.

SAFETY CHECK

POST-LESSON CLOSE-OUT · No specific risk. Standard close-out.

Community Charter & Year-End Celebration

40–45 min · Week 28 · ★ ELEVATED · Counselor on Standby
OBJECTIVE

Students consolidate the year by opening their future-self letters from December, co-creating a Community Charter, and celebrating one year of growth.

CASEL FOCUS

Responsible Decision-Making · consolidation; Relationship Skills: community

VOCABULARY

charter · community · celebrate · who we became · bridge

MATERIALS

Sealed future-self letters (FROM U2 L7 — MUST be retrieved) · charter poster board · pens · celebration materials · Sammy puppet · COUNSELOR ON STANDBY

ILLINOIS SEL ALIGNMENT GRADES K–2 · EARLY ELEMENTARY

Goal 3 — Responsible Decision-Making

3C.1a Identify and perform roles that contribute to one’s classroom.

Goal 2 — Social Awareness & Interpersonal Skills

2C.1b Demonstrate appropriate social and classroom behavior.

Aligned to the Illinois Social/Emotional Learning Standards (ISBE).

SAFETY CHECK

★ ELEVATED RISK — COUNSELOR ON STANDBY (reachable, available same-day).

The future-self letters from December are emotionally weighted. Students may have changed dramatically; some may not have. Some letters may now feel painful to read.

Reserve the right to allow a student to NOT open their letter publicly. They may keep it sealed.

Crisis resources visible: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline · Crisis Text Line: HOME to 741741.

OPENING REVIEW · 1–2 MIN
Walk past every chart in the classroom — every chart from every unit. "Look at what we built. Look at who we became. Look at the year."
SAMMY · KIND COACH VOICE

“In December I sealed a letter to MYSELF — to the May me. Today I open it. So do you.”

▶ DIRECT INSTRUCTION

Scripted teaching sequence · 4 STEPS · read aloud or in your own words

01Open the Letters15 MIN

Return each sealed envelope to its writer. Three full minutes of silence for reading. "Who did you write TO in December? Who are you NOW in May? What changed?" Optional brief voluntary share — no pressure. Sammy reads his too.

IN-LESSON SCAN

A student who looks distressed reading their letter — may have hoped for change that did not come. Acknowledge privately: "I see this is heavy. We can talk after." Do NOT redirect publicly.

02Co-Create the Community Charter15 MIN

Distribute the charter poster. Together, write the Community Charter for next year’s class — the lessons WE learned that we want to PASS DOWN. Each student contributes one line. Examples: "Feelings are messengers." "I am not my mistakes." "Forgiveness is for me." "Grace is a choice."

03Celebration8 MIN

Celebration moment. Each student says ONE thing they are proud of from the year (their own growth). Sammy responds warmly to each. Class chant: the OOPS → FIX IT → TRY AGAIN chant from Lesson 3 of Unit 1, with the year’s wisdom now layered in.

04Closing of the Year5 MIN
Final words: "We started the year by saying ‘Who am I?’ We end the year knowing: I am MORE than my mistakes. I am part of a community. I give grace. I receive grace. I AM — and I am STILL BECOMING. Goodbye for now — but you will carry this work into next year and the year after."
Sammy: “I will see you next year. I am proud of who you have become.”
KINDERGARTEN

GRADES 1–2

Reading letters can be silent and private. Sharing is fully optional. The Community Charter can be drawings only. The CELEBRATION is what matters most.

Engage all four steps. The Charter should have at least one line per student. Encourage specifics in the celebration share.

EXIT TICKET

“Each student says: "I am proud of myself for ____." Sammy bows to each.”

Sammy: thumbs-up to every hand. No commentary. Sammy validates participation, not content.

TEACHER REFLECTION

The deepest reflection of the year. Which students walked into August unable to name themselves — and walked out claiming who they are? Which students did not? The pattern shapes counselor coordination for next year.

SAFETY CHECK

POST-LESSON CLOSE-OUT · Counselor reachable same-day. Year-end dismissal can surface heavy feelings. The Charter and celebration also surface students who have grown deeply — honor that as much as the heavy.

A Note Home

Today we ended the year by opening the letters we sealed in December. Ask your child: "What did your future-self letter say? What did your now-self think reading it?" This is the most important conversation of the year.
