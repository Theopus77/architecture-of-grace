Room 36 · Grades 6–8 · For teachers, counselors and reviewers

Every check-in and survey

Eight short tools, A to H, in one place. What each one is for, when it is used, and who sees it.

What stays private

  • What a student writes stays with the student. No tool here collects a journal, a letter or a worksheet.
  • On-screen answers stay on the device used, unless your school turned on its own Google Sheet. Paper stays in the school. Nothing goes to Architecture of Grace.
  • A check-in score is a teacher's starting point. It is never a diagnosis, a screening result, a label or a placement.
  • The new tools, B to H, ask about skills and about the program. None asks about health, home life, beliefs or money.
  • Families see every question before the first use. A student may skip any question, or the whole tool. Letters and consent

Instrument A · Students · Already on the site

The Grace Compass check-in

The site's own check-in. Students take it on screen. The paper copy is for families and reviewers.

How to give it
  1. Get written family consent before the first check-in, with these statements attached. Letters and consent
  2. On the Educator Dashboard, hand out the class link and choose Quick. Open the Dashboard
  3. Use Quick in Room 36. Thorough adds three written questions, and in Room 36 a student's own words stay with the student.
  4. In September Week 1, give it in another class period, so U1·L1 keeps its time.
  5. Read the statements aloud for anyone who wants that. Taking part is a choice.
  6. Look at the class first. Then find each student's lowest domain on the unit's crosswalk, and use that row inside the lesson you are teaching. Keep the lessons in order.
  7. Statements 18 and 19 are about safety. A low answer on either one means: talk with the student privately, and soon.
  8. If anything suggests a child is being hurt, follow the safety steps. Every school employee in Illinois is a mandated reporter: DCFS Hotline 1-800-25-ABUSE (1-800-252-2873). Safety steps

Room 36 · Instrument A · For teachers and reviewers

How the check-in is scored

  1. Each answer counts 0 to 3: Never 0, Sometimes 1, Often 2, Almost always 3.
  2. Statements 3, 4, 8, 10 and 15 count the other way: 3 minus the answer.
  3. Each domain adds its six statements (0 to 18) and shows the sum as 0 to 100. Statement 19 is not scored.
  4. 75 to 100: Keep noticing. 50 to 74: Worth a conversation. Under 50: Adult follow-up.
  5. A band tells the adult what to do next. It says nothing final about the child.
  6. The lowest domain points to a support row in the unit's crosswalk. All three at 75 or more: the ◆ enrichment row. Lessons stay in order.
  7. Statements 18 and 19 are about safety. One of the two lowest answers on either one means: talk with the student, privately and soon.
  8. Thorough mode counts 0 to 5 with the same statements. The bands are the same.

Domains A, B and C are the check-in's three areas. They are not Instruments A, B and C.

The bridge check

At the end of Units 1, 2 and 3, the exit ticket stays in the journal. This card asks two things, with ticks only.

How to give it
  1. Hand it out after the exit ticket. Say: “Tick one idea. Then tick if you would like a quiet word. Your journal stays with you.”
  2. Students fold it and hand it in. A blank card is fine.
  3. Read the cards the same day. Talk with each student who asked, before the next unit starts.
  4. When a student chose the counselor, bring the counselor in.
  5. Shred the cards once you have followed up.

Instrument C · Students · New in this edition

The self-assessment, September and June

Sixteen “I can” statements, four for each unit. The same sheet in September and in June, so students can see what changed.

How to give it
  1. September: at the start of U1·L1, before any teaching. Skip the optional scenario cards that day to make room.
  2. Say: “This is not a test. There are no wrong answers. You will fill in the same sheet in June.”
  3. Students tick one box a line, quietly. Read lines aloud for anyone who wants that. A student may skip any line.
  4. Collect the sheets and seal them in one envelope until June.
  5. June: Step 1 of U4·L7, before the celebration. Students fill in a new sheet, then get their September sheet back to compare.
  6. Count the class tally for both months, with no names. Then students take both sheets home.
  7. In a validation pilot, students write a code, not a name, and you copy each code's numbers into your school's own Sheet before the sheets go home.

D

Instrument D · An observer · New in this edition

GAPA: skills in action

An adult watches a small group solve a scenario and rates five skills. Only in a validation pilot, for a sample of students.

How to give it
  1. Use it only if your district joined the validation pilot, and only with family consent that names it. Some states have extra rules for adults rating a student's social and emotional skills. Check yours.
  2. Choose one class, or a random sample. Give each student a code. The code list stays in the school's private pilot roster.
  3. Make groups of 3 or 4. Read the card aloud. The group has 10 minutes to agree on what to do, then acts out the first thing they would say.
  4. At minute 5, give the one prompt on the observer's page. Nothing else.
  5. Rate each sampled student, 0 to 3, on the five skills, right after the group finishes. A student who passes gets P, not 0.
See the sheet

Room 36 · Instrument D · For the observer

GAPA: the five skills and the scale

  1. Pause first — Says “wait”, asks a question, or names what they want to happen before the group decides.
  2. See the other side — Says how a person in the story might feel or see it.
  3. Name the impact, offer a repair — Says what the action did to someone and suggests a way to make it right.
  4. Speak fairly about a mistake — Talks about a mistake, their own or a character's, without put-downs: “It was a mistake, not who you are.”
  5. Choose a kind, specific step with a limit — Proposes one specific kind act and a clear limit: “I won't forward it, and I'll check on Sam.”

The scale

  • 0 · Not seen in this task
  • 1 · After the prompt
  • 2 · On their own, once
  • 3 · On their own, clearly, and helps the group do it too
  • P · Passed (that is fine)

The one prompt, at minute 5

“Before you decide: what might each person be feeling, and what do you want to happen?”

Rate right after the group finishes. Codes, not names. Do not teach or hint, except for the one prompt.

Room 36 · Instrument D · Cut apart

GAPA: the scenario cards

Scenario 1 · The photo

Your group is finishing a science poster tomorrow. Tonight, someone in a group chat posts an embarrassing photo of Sam, who is in your group. People are adding laughing faces. Sam has not seen it yet.

Decide together: what do you do tonight, and what do you say to Sam tomorrow?

You have 10 minutes. Then act out the first thing you would say.

Scenario 2 · The lost points

Your group's project lost points because Ari forgot their part. In front of everyone, Ari says, “I'm so stupid. I ruin everything.” Dev is angry and says Ari should do the whole next part alone.

Decide together: what do you say to Ari and to Dev, and how do you fix the plan?

You have 10 minutes. Then act out the first thing you would say.

In September, give half the groups Scenario 1 and half Scenario 2. In June, swap.

E

Instrument E · Teachers · New in this edition

The fidelity log

One line a week. It tells the school what students actually got, so the results mean something.

How to give it
  1. Fill in a line each week a lesson is taught, shortened or moved.
  2. Shortened means the 25-minute path. The calendar
  3. Moved is fine. Write why: a fire drill, the counselor away, a hard week.
  4. A ★★ lesson waits until the counselor can be in the room.
  5. As designed means: in order, the chart up, the worksheet or cards used, and the counselor there when the lesson says so.
  6. At the end of each unit, count the lessons taught as designed. A pilot aims for 80% or more.

Room 36 · Instrument F

My Year-End Card

The four pillars

  • I AM — who I am, not the Mask
  • I CAN BE KIND TO MYSELF — I catch the Critic
  • I CAN FORGIVE — I let go, with limits
  • I CAN GIVE GRACE — from strength, not from fear

My tools: I tick the ones I can use

  • The Mask and the Mirror: what I show, and what is really there.
  • Guilt, not shame: “I did something wrong” is not “I am bad.”
  • The Critic's three tricks: catastrophizing, globalizing, personalizing.
  • The Compassionate Witness: honest, fair, kind.
  • The Friend Test: I say to myself what I would say to a friend.
  • Impact vs. intent: I repair what it did, whatever I meant.
  • Forgiveness is not reconciliation: two separate choices.
  • The Letter of Release: written for me, never sent.
  • The Pause: stop, ask what I want to happen, think what it will cause, choose the smallest response that works, or step away.
  • The Three-Strike Rule: first time, the benefit of the doubt; second, notice the pattern; third, the pattern is real.
  • A limit: “When you ___, I ___. Going forward, I need ___.”
  • Micro-moments: small, specific acts of grace.
  • Grace is not access: I can wish someone well and still keep my distance.
  • Difficult vs. unsafe: a difficult person can get grace; with an unsafe person, safety comes first.

This card is yours. It is never handed in. Need help right now? Call or text 988. Or text HOME to 741741.

If your district joins the validation pilot

  • The district gives permission and sets the rules for the data.
  • Item-level answers live in your school's own Google Sheet, by code, not by name. Nothing is collected on a server of ours.
  • A result is the change from September to June on the check-in's three domains, on the self-assessment, and on GAPA for the sample; 80% or more of lessons taught as designed; and what teachers, families and students say.
  • None of these tools has been checked by outside experts yet. That review comes next, and its findings will be published whichever way they point. Evidence and roadmap
