Away is a push, toward is a pull

A box sitting still. Nothing is touching it, so nothing happens.

Away from you is a push. Toward you is a pull. Nothing moves by itself — if something moved, something pushed it or pulled it.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

A ball rolling on the floor stops by itself.

Something stopped it. The floor rubs against the ball and pushes back a little the whole way. Take the rubbing away, like on ice, and it keeps going much longer.

Something always stops it. Look for what.

⚠️ People often think…

You can only push or pull something you are touching.

A magnet pulls a paper clip from across the table. Wind pushes a leaf with no hands at all. Some pushes and pulls reach without touching.

Magnets and wind push without touching.

Push or pull — which one

1 Watch one

A kid moves a wagon. Push or pull?

  1. Find the kid, and find the wagon.
  2. Ask: is the wagon going away from the kid, or toward the kid?
  3. The handle is behind the wagon and the wagon follows. It comes toward the kid.
  4. Toward means pull. ✓

Helpful Hints

🪜 Push, pull, go — the ladder

A push goes away, a pull comes toward → a push or pull makes a thing start, stop or turn → a big push goes far, a small push goes a little → ramps, wind and magnets push and pull too → find the push in everything that moves.

Away, toward, start, stop, turn, big, small.

🤝 The whole idea

Nothing moves by itself. When you see something move, something pushed it or pulled it — a hand, the wind, a ramp, a magnet, a bump.

Find the push.

⚠️ Tricky ones

  • Opening a door is a PULL from one side and a PUSH from the other.
  • A bump is a push. A magnet is a pull. Wind is a push.
  • A ball doesn’t stop by itself — the floor or your hand pushes it to a stop.
  • Higher ramp = faster ball. Lower ramp = slower ball.
  • Change only ONE thing when you test.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A picture of a kid and a wagon — “push or pull?” (which way is it going?).
  • Two ramps, one taller — “which ball goes faster?” (the tall one).
  • A ball rolling toward a block — “what happens?” (the ball pushes the block, the block moves).
  • “How can you make it go farther?” (a bigger push or a higher ramp).
