Identity Audit

Who am I when I'm performing — and who am I when I'm not?

What do I show? What do I hide? What do I emphasize?

Without judging yourself — where is the gap between these two columns biggest?

For the family

Today we began our identity unit by examining the gap between the persona students perform socially and who they actually are. You can open this at home by modeling vulnerability — share a moment when you felt like you were "performing" rather than being real.

The Choice-Agency Map

Some things I cannot control. My response is always mine.

Type something and press Enter. It drops into that ring. The rings sit inside each other on purpose — the closer to the middle, the more of it is yours.

Outer ring — Outside my control
Things I cannot change, fix, or escape right now.
Middle ring — I can influence
Things I can affect, but not fully control.
Inner circle — I fully choose
Starting with my own response — this is where my power lives.
For the family

Today students learned about the space between what happens and how we respond — and that space is where our agency lives. At home, you can use the language: "You can't control that. What can you control?"

A Letter to My Future Self

To the version of me on the last day of this school year.

This one stays on paper

You write it by hand, fold it, and seal it in an envelope.

Every other sheet in here can be typed. Not this one. The letter gets sealed today and handed back to you in June, and the sealing is the part that makes it work — an envelope you cannot open is a promise a text box cannot make.

Print the letter below, write it in your own handwriting, and seal it. Your teacher keeps the sealed envelopes and does not open them.

Right now I am learning that…
One thing I want you to remember is…
I hope that by June…
Dear future me,

Fourteen lines, a signature, and a date — printed the way the lesson uses it.

From, ______________________________ Date: ______________

For the family

Your child wrote a letter to their future self today. It will be returned at year-end. Ask them: "What is the most important thing you know about yourself now that you didn't know in September?"

Internal Critic Profile

The critic is not the truth about you. It is a voice with three tricks — and it uses the same three on every human brain.

What mine actually says

Not the category — the actual sentence. Write it the way you hear it.

It says…

It says…

Knowing the critic's moves gives you a fighting chance against it.

Catastrophizing

“Everything is ruined. This is the worst thing that has ever happened.”

Turns one bad moment into a permanent condition.

Globalizing

“I always fail. I never get it right. I am just bad at this.”

Turns one thing into every thing — always, never, everyone.

Personalizing

“Everything that went wrong is my fault.”

Hands you the blame for what was never yours to carry.

Honest AND fair AND kind, without punishment — not a cheerleader, not the critic in disguise. What would that voice say about the same moment? It does not have to feel true yet. Just write it.

Rewriting the Script

A script is a story we treat as true without ever examining it.

1

A sentence I sometimes believe about myself is…

2

This script may have first come from… (a person, a moment, an experience)

3

Evidence that this script is NOT the full story of who I am…

4

What would someone who saw the whole of me — with kindness — say about this script?

5

An alternative script, in one sentence. It does not have to feel true yet.
