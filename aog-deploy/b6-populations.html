Every population hits a ceiling

A few deer move into an empty forest. The population climbs fast.

That line is the carrying capacity: how many the place can actually feed. It is not a fixed number — a drought lowers it, a wet year raises it, and the population follows.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Removing the predators is good for the prey.

It ends badly for the prey. With nothing eating them, the deer climb past the food supply, strip the young trees, and then starve in far greater numbers than the wolves ever took.

Predators hold prey below the starving point.

⚠️ People often think…

Carrying capacity is a fixed number for a place.

It moves with the conditions. A drought lowers it, a wet year raises it, a fire resets it. The same forest carries different numbers in different years.

The ceiling moves. Watch the food and the water.

⚠️ People often think…

Competition only happens between different species.

The hardest competition is usually within a species, because members of it need exactly the same things. Two oak seedlings under the same tree are fighting for the same patch of light.

Same needs means harder competition, not easier.

⚠️ People often think…

A burned or flooded ecosystem is ruined for good.

Succession rebuilds it, in order: the first tough species move in, change the soil, and make room for the next ones. It takes years, not never.

Disturbed is not destroyed.

Helpful Hints

🤝 The three symbioses — score them like a game

Mutualism: win–win (bee & flower). Commensalism: win–nothing (barnacle riding a whale). Parasitism: win–lose (tick on a dog).

Win-win · Win-meh · Win-lose

📈 Carrying capacity in one line

The largest population an environment can SUPPORT long-term. Set by limiting factors: food, water, space, shelter. Populations climb, then level off near it.

The environment sets the guest limit.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Predators are GOOD for ecosystems — remove them and prey overpopulate, overgraze, and crash.
  • Carrying capacity is not fixed — drought lowers it, a wet year raises it.
  • Competition happens WITHIN a species too — two oak seedlings fight for the same light.
  • A disturbed ecosystem isn’t ruined forever — succession rebuilds it over time.
  • Biodiversity isn’t about cuteness — more species = more backups = more resilience.

🌋 Succession — two flavors

  • Primary: starts from BARE ROCK (new volcanic island). Lichens and mosses build the first soil. Very slow.
  • Secondary: starts from SOIL that survived (after fire, flood, abandoned farm). Much faster.
  • Key question: “Is there soil left?” Soil = secondary.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A population graph over time — explain rises, falls, and the flat line (carrying capacity) using resources.
  • “Name the relationship” — decide who benefits, who’s harmed, who’s unaffected.
  • A disruption question — one change, then trace effects through the web with evidence.

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Score all three symbioses with an example each?
  • Read a population graph and point at carrying capacity?
  • Tell primary from secondary succession with the soil question?
  • Explain what a keystone species is and what happens when it’s removed?
  • Give three ecosystem services humans depend on?
TermMeaning in 10 words or less
CompetitionOrganisms struggling for the same limited resource.
PredationOne organism hunting and eating another.
MutualismBoth species benefit (win–win).
CommensalismOne benefits; the other is unaffected.
ParasitismOne benefits by harming the other.
Limiting factorAnything that caps population growth.
Carrying capacityLargest population the environment can support.
Invasive speciesOutsider species that spreads and causes harm.
SuccessionThe predictable rebuilding of an ecosystem over time.
Pioneer speciesFirst to move in (lichens, mosses); makes soil.
ResilienceAn ecosystem’s ability to bounce back.
BiodiversityThe variety of species in an ecosystem.
Keystone speciesA species whose removal collapses the system.
