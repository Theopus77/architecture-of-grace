A Board With Wheels

History

Long ago, surfers in California had a problem. Some days the ocean had no waves. So people put roller-skate wheels under a wooden board. They rolled on the sidewalk instead.

In this unit, you will learn how the skateboard began. You will meet the parts of a board. You will learn the job of each part, from the deck to the tiny bearings.

By the end, you can tell the story of the first boards. You can point to each part and say what it does. You can check a board with a grown-up before a ride.

The big question

How did people make a board that rolls, and what does each part do?

The story

A Day With No Waves

The ocean is flat and still. What can a surfer do now?

Mia stands on a beach in California. She holds her dad's hand. They came to watch the surfers. But the ocean is flat today. There are no big waves. The water just laps at the sand.

The surfers sit on the beach. Their long boards lie in the sand. One surfer sighs. "No waves today," he says. Mia looks at the quiet water. She wonders, "What does a surfer do now?"

Then Mia hears a rumble. A boy rolls by on the path. He stands on a small wooden board. It has little wheels under it. He leans, and the board turns. He looks like he is riding a wave!

Mia's dad smiles. "Long ago, surfers did that too," he says. "When the waves were flat, they surfed the sidewalk." Mia watches the boy roll away. She wants to learn how that board began.

Talk about itIf you could not do your favorite thing today, what would you make or try instead?
Section 1

When the Waves Were Flat

1.1

Surfers in California

Main ideaSurfers in California wanted to surf even when the ocean had no waves.

California is a state by the ocean. In the 1950s, many people there loved to . To surf, you stand on a long board. A wave pushes you toward the beach.

Surfers need waves. Some days the ocean is flat. A flat ocean has no big waves. On flat days, the surfers had nothing to ride. They had to wait on the sand.

Waiting was hard for them. They wanted to feel that ride every day. So some of them asked a question. Could they ride a board on land?

On which day could the surfers ride? What could they do on the other day?

Wheels Under a Board

Main ideaPeople made the first skateboards by putting roller-skate wheels under a wooden board.

Long ago, kids had . Roller skates have wheels on the bottom. Some people took the wheels off old skates. They fixed the wheels under a flat piece of wood.

Now the wood could roll! A person could stand on it. That board with wheels was an early . Many people made their own at home.

Who made the very first one? Nobody knows for sure. People tell many stories about it. But no one person is known as the inventor.

Riding Like a Wave

1.3

Sidewalk Surfing

Main ideaPeople called the new ride sidewalk surfing because it felt like surfing on land.

Riders stood on the board like a surfboard. They leaned one way, and the board turned. They leaned the other way, and it turned back. It felt like riding a wave.

So people gave it a name. They called it . A is the path next to a street where people walk.

This was in the late 1950s and early 1960s. Many early riders rode with bare feet, like surfers. Today riders wear flat shoes to grip the board.

Think it through
Lean and Turn

Picture a rider rolling slowly on a flat path. She bends her knees. She leans her body a little to one side. The board curves that way. She leans back to the middle. The board goes straight again.

Stand up and pretend to ride. Which way do you lean to make a turn?

1.4

Bumpy Old Wheels

Main ideaEarly skateboard wheels were hard and bumpy, so small stones could stop them.

The first wheels were made of metal or clay. They were very hard. They did not grip the ground well. They made a loud, rattling sound.

Hard wheels feel every bump. A tiny could stop a wheel fast. Then the rider might tip off the board. Riding was shaky and slow.

Later, people made wheels from softer stuff. Softer wheels roll over small bumps. The ride got smoother and quieter.

Think it through
The Little Stone

Picture a board with metal wheels rolling down a path. Rattle, rattle, rattle. Ahead sits one small stone. The front wheel hits it. The wheel stops. The board stops too.

What happened when the hard wheel hit the stone? Why?

Why: Softer wheels roll over small bumps instead of stopping.

At first, people built their own boards. Then companies started making skateboards. Around 1959, you could buy one in a .

Soon, lots of kids were riding. By the mid-1960s, there were skateboard teams and contests. A is a game where people try to do their best.

Then and Now

Main ideaSkateboards changed a lot, but riders still lean, roll and balance.

An old board was a plank of wood. It had hard metal or clay wheels. Riders often had bare feet and no helmets.

A board today has a curved deck and soft wheels. Riders wear flat shoes. Skaters today wear a that fits and is buckled.

Some things did not change. You still stand on a board. You still lean to turn. You still need , which means staying steady so you do not tip.

Parts
Big questionWhat does each part of a skateboard do?
The story

The Board on the Table

A skateboard lies upside down. Can the class name every part?

Mr. Diaz walks into class. He carries a skateboard under his arm. He sets it on a table upside down. The four wheels point up at the ceiling. The class leans in close.

"Let's find every part," says Mr. Diaz. Jada points to the flat wood. "That is where you stand!" Omar spins one wheel with his finger. It whirs around and around.

Lin finds the metal parts that hold the wheels. Sam turns the board over. The top feels rough under his hand. "Like sandpaper!" he says. Mr. Diaz nods and smiles.

"Every part has a job," says Mr. Diaz. "If one part is missing, the board will not ride well." The class wants to learn each job. They start at the top.

Section 1

The Top of the Board

2.1

The Deck

Main ideaThe deck is the board you stand on.

The big flat part is the . You stand on the deck. Your feet go on top of it. The deck holds you up while you roll.

Most decks are made of wood from maple trees. Thin sheets of wood are glued together. Most decks have seven thin sheets. The sheets make the deck strong but light.

A deck is not perfectly flat. It dips down a little in the middle. The dip helps your feet stay on the board.

Think it through
Stand on the Deck

Look at a skateboard lying flat on soft grass. It cannot roll on the grass. A boy steps on the middle of the deck. He sets one foot down, then the other. He bends his knees. He stands still and steady.

Pretend the floor is a deck. Where would your feet go?

Words to know
deck
the board of a skateboard that you stand on
maple
a kind of tree with hard, strong wood
Check yourself

1. What part of the skateboard do you stand on?

Why: The deck is the flat board you stand on.

2. What are most decks made of?

Why: Most decks are made of thin sheets of maple wood glued together, often seven.

3. Why does the deck dip a little in the middle?

Why: The small dip in the middle helps your feet stay on the board.
2.2

Nose and Tail

Main ideaThe nose is the front end of the deck, and the tail is the back end.

A deck has two ends. The front end is the . The back end is the . Both ends curve up a little.

The tail is very useful. When you step down on the tail, the nose lifts up. Skaters use the tail to turn and to start tricks.

The nose and tail can look almost the same. Often the nose is a little longer and curves up a little more. Some skaters mark the nose so they can tell.

Think it through
Press the Tail

Picture a board resting on a carpet. It is not rolling. A girl puts her foot on the tail. She presses down gently. The tail goes down and touches the carpet. The nose rises up into the air.

What happens to the nose when you press the tail? Show it with your hand.

Words to know
nose
the front end of a skateboard deck
tail
the back end of a skateboard deck
Check yourself

1. What is the front end of the deck called?

Why: The front end is the nose. The back end is the tail.

2. What happens when you press down on the tail?

Why: Stepping on the tail pushes it down, so the nose lifts up.

3. Which end is often a little longer?

Why: The nose is often a little longer than the tail.
2.3

Rough Grip Tape

Main ideaGrip tape is rough like sandpaper so your shoes do not slip.

Rub your hand on top of a deck. It feels rough and scratchy. That is . It is a sticky sheet that covers the top of the deck.

Grip tape is rough like . Your shoes rub on it and hold tight. So your feet do not slide off when the board moves.

Grip tape works best when it is dry. Water makes it slippery. Skate on dry days, in dry places.

Think it through
Slippery or Grippy?

Which board would you want to stand on? Why?

2. What is grip tape for?

Why: Your shoes hold tight on the rough grip tape, so your feet do not slide.

3. Where is the grip tape?

Why: Grip tape covers the top of the deck, where your feet go.
Section 2

Under the Board

2.4

The Trucks

Main ideaTrucks are the metal parts that hold the wheels and help the board turn.

Turn a skateboard upside down. You will see two metal parts. These are the . One truck is near the nose. One truck is near the tail.

Each truck holds two wheels. Small hold each truck onto the deck. Riders put their feet over the bolts to stand steady.

Trucks also help you turn. When you lean, the trucks tilt. Then the wheels point a new way, and the board turns.

Think it through
Lean to Turn

Look at a board upside down on a table. A teacher presses on one side of the deck. Watch the truck. It tilts a little. The wheels turn to point a new way. When she lets go, they point straight again.

Lean your body to one side. Which way would the board turn?

Words to know
trucks
the metal parts under the deck that hold the wheels and help the board turn
bolts
small metal pins with nuts that hold the trucks onto the deck
Check yourself

1. How many trucks are on a skateboard?

Why: There is one truck near the nose and one near the tail. That makes two.

2. What holds a truck onto the deck?

Why: Small bolts hold each truck onto the deck.

3. What do trucks do when you lean?

Why: When you lean, the trucks tilt. The wheels point a new way and the board turns.
2.5

Four Wheels

Main ideaA skateboard rolls on four wheels, two on each truck.

A skateboard has four . Two wheels are on the front truck. Two wheels are on the back truck. Four wheels keep the board steady.

Wheels today are made of a soft, strong plastic. They roll smooth and quiet. They hold on to the ground well. Old metal wheels could not do that.

Some wheels are softer. Soft wheels roll well on rough sidewalks. Some wheels are harder. Hard wheels slide more easily at a skatepark.

Section 3

Every Part Has a Job

2.6

Bearings Help Wheels Spin

Main ideaBearings are small rings inside the wheels that help them spin.

Look inside the middle of a wheel. You will see a small metal ring. That is a . Each wheel has two bearings, one on each side.

Inside a bearing are tiny steel balls. The balls roll around and around. They help the wheel spin fast and easy. Without bearings, the wheel would rub and stick.

Bearings do not like water or sand. Water can make them rust. Sand can make them grind. Keep your board dry and clean.

Think it through
Spin the Wheel

Picture a board upside down. A girl flicks one wheel with her finger. It spins and spins. It keeps going for a long time. It makes a soft whirring sound. The bearings inside help it turn.

Why does the wheel keep spinning after she lets go?

Words to know
bearing
a small metal ring with tiny balls inside that helps a wheel spin
Check yourself

1. Where are the bearings?

Why: Bearings sit inside the middle of each wheel, one on each side.

2. What is inside a bearing?

Why: Tiny steel balls roll inside the bearing and help the wheel spin.

3. What can water do to bearings?

Why: Water can make bearings rust, so keep your board dry.
2.7

Counting the Parts

Main ideaEach part has a job, and a board needs all of them to ride safely.

Let’s count the parts. One deck holds you up. Two trucks hold the wheels and help you turn. Four wheels roll. Eight bearings help the wheels spin.

Each part has a . If a wheel is loose, the board may wobble. If a bolt falls out, a truck can come loose. A board needs every part.

Before you ride, check your board with a grown-up. Spin each wheel. Look at each bolt. Then put on your helmet and buckle it.
