A missing word is not a stop sign

A sentence runs along. Then a word you do not have — a wall — and the sentence stops dead.

That has a name: circumlocution — describing your way around a word you do not have. Three frames do most of it: es una cosa que…, se usa para…, es como un… pero… It keeps the conversation alive, and the rubric calls that a communication strategy.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Reading a script word for word is the safe way to give a talk.

Reading is not speaking, and most rubrics say so. A script makes you sound flat, and one lost line loses the whole page. Notes are the shape of the talk: the thesis, three key words per support, the close. Write only the first sentence and the last sentence in full.

A script is every word. Notes are five words a line.

⚠️ People often think…

When one word is missing, say that one word in English and keep going.

The English word ends the Spanish, and the rubric has a whole row for what you do instead. Describe it: es una cosa que se usa para unir papeles. Or ask in Spanish: ¿cómo se dice…? Both stay in Spanish and both earn credit for repair.

One English word costs more than a long description.

⚠️ People often think…

Write the sentence in English first, then swap each word for a Spanish one.

Word for word breaks in the places this unit already named. I like the classes is not yo gusto las clases — it is me gustan las clases. I see my sister needs the personal a: veo a mi hermana. I am nervous is estoy, not soy. Start from a Spanish sentence you can already say, and grow it.

Start from Spanish you own, not from English you translate.

⚠️ People often think…

A bigger word scores better than a clear one.

The first row of the rubric is comprehensibility: could a listener follow you? A big word you cannot pronounce or conjugate costs you that row. The vocabulary row asks for precise, not fancy — trade a vague word like cosa or bueno for an exact one you control.

Clear first, then precise. Never fancy first.

Talk around it, then hold it together

1 Watch one

You need the word for a corkscrew. You do not have sacacorchos. What do you say?

  1. Do not stop. Start with what it is.
  2. Es una cosa que se usa en la cocina.
  3. Now what it does: abre botellas de vino.
  4. Now what it is like: es como un tornillo con mango.
  5. The listener says: ah, un sacacorchos. You never left Spanish. ✓
2 Do one with me

Three connectors are missing from this defense. Write each one in Spanish.

  1. Adds a reason: Me entendieron ____ describí la palabra.
  2. Turns to the other side: ____, todavía me equivoco con ser y estar.
  3. Closes the talk: ____, hablar español es seguir adelante.

Helpful Hints

🧭 The unit in one line

Three modes — take it in, talk with someone, present to a group → plan a talk: one thesis, three supports, a close → hold it together with connectors → keep going in Spanish when a word is missing → pick the tense the job asks for → proofread → give it from notes → be scored on the rubric.

Tesis, tres apoyos, cierre.

🔑 The one idea

Being understood beats being perfect. A rubric reads comprehensibility first, and a student who says ‘es una cosa que se usa para escribir’ instead of stopping has scored higher than one who went silent looking for ‘bolígrafo’.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • Reading a script word for word. That is not speaking, and most rubrics say so. Use notes — a few words per line.
  • Switching to English the moment a word is missing. Say ‘es una cosa que…’ or ‘¿cómo se dice…?’ and stay in Spanish.
  • A thesis that is only a topic. ‘Mi familia’ is a topic. ‘Mi familia me enseñó a trabajar’ is a thesis — it says something you can defend.
  • Narrating in the present because it is easier. If it already happened, the preterite and the imperfect are the job.
  • Forgetting agreement in the one place a reader always looks: the noun and its adjective. ‘Las clases difíciles’, not ‘las clases difícil’.
  • Dropping the personal a. ‘Veo a mi hermano’, not ‘veo mi hermano’ — a person who is the object needs the a.
  • Saying ‘estás equivocado’ when you disagree. Say ‘no estoy de acuerdo’ or ‘me parece que…’ — it is a disagreement, not an attack.
