Lesson 1 · Skill

Meet the kit

A drum kit is many drums and cymbals played by one person. Start with the three you hear most.

  1. Tap any drum
  2. Tap the kick, the big drum at the bottom
  3. Tap the snare
  4. Tap the closed hi-hat

Try this: Say each name out loud as you tap it: kick, snare, hi-hat.

Think about it

Which drum sounds the deepest? Which sounds the brightest?

Your turn

Draw the kit and label the kick, the snare and the hi-hat.

Lesson 2 · Skill

Loud and soft

Where you tap changes the sound. The middle plays loud. The edge plays soft.

  1. Tap right in the middle of a drum: a loud hit
  2. Tap near the edge of a drum: a soft hit
  3. Tap the snare softly, near its edge: a ghost note

Try this: Play the snare loud, soft, loud, soft. Can you hear the difference?

Think about it

Why would a drummer want to play softly sometimes?

Your turn

Write a pattern of four hits. Mark each one L for loud or S for soft.

Lesson 3 · Skill

Every kit has a style

Each kit is a real recording of a different drum set. Some are for rock, some for jazz, some for Latin music.

  1. Pick another kit in Drum kit
  2. Try three different kits

Try this: Play the same three taps on each kit. Which one do you like best?

Think about it

How does the kit change the feeling of the music?

Your turn

Name your favorite kit. Write three words for how it sounds.

Lesson 4 · Skill

Crash and ride

Two big cymbals. The crash is for big moments. The ride keeps a steady beat.

  1. Tap the crash cymbal
  2. Tap the ride cymbal
  3. Tap the ride 8 times in a row, steady

Try this: Tap the ride like a clock: tick, tick, tick, tick. Keep it even.

Think about it

When in a song would you hit the crash?

Your turn

Write a place in a song you know where a crash would sound great.

Lesson 5 · Skill

The hi-hat: open and closed

The hi-hat is two cymbals. Closed, it ticks. Open, it rings. A closed tap stops the ring, like on a real kit.

  1. Tap the closed hi-hat
  2. Tap the open hi-hat and let it ring
  3. Tap the open hat, then the closed hat to stop it

Try this: Play tick, tick, tick, open, then close it. Do it again.

Think about it

Why does a closed hat stop an open one?

Your turn

Write a hi-hat pattern with x for closed and o for open.

Lesson 6 · Skill

The hi-hat pedal

A drummer closes the hi-hat with a foot pedal. It makes a soft chick and stops an open hat.

  1. Pick a kit with a hi-hat pedal: Kit R, U or X
  2. Tap the hi-hat pedal
  3. Tap the open hat, then stop it with the pedal

Try this: Tap the pedal on every other beat while your other hand plays the ride.

Think about it

A drummer uses both hands and both feet. Which part does each foot play?

Your turn

Write which fingers you use for the pedal and for the ride.

Lesson 7 · Skill

The toms

Toms are the round drums that sing a note. Small toms sound high. The floor tom sounds low.

  1. Tap a tom
  2. Pick Kit S · prog rock, many toms
  3. On Kit S, tap the high tom, the middle tom, then the floor tom

Try this: Go high, middle, low, then low, middle, high.

Think about it

Why is a bigger drum lower?

Your turn

Draw the three toms from smallest to biggest.

Lesson 8 · Skill

A rock beat with your fingers

The rock beat: kick, snare, kick, snare, with the hi-hat ticking in between. Use both hands.

  1. Tap the hi-hat and the kick at the same time, with two fingers
  2. Play kick, snare, kick, snare, with hi-hat taps in between

Try this: Go slowly. Count 1, 2, 3, 4: kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4.

Think about it

Why do so many songs use this beat?

Your turn

Write the beat: K for kick, S for snare, H for hi-hat.

Lesson 9 · Skill

The beat player

Under the kit is a beat that plays by itself on the kit you picked. It starts only when you press Play.

  1. Pick a beat in Beat
  2. Press ▶ Play the beat
  3. Press ■ Stop the beat

Try this: Watch the drums light up as the beat plays. Which ones does it use?

Think about it

Pick a beat from a style you have never heard. What did you notice?

Your turn

Write the names of three beats you liked.

Lesson 10 · Skill

Fast and slow

Speed is how many beats play in one minute. The same beat feels new when it is faster or slower.

  1. Change the Speed
  2. Make it fast: 140 or more
  3. Make it slow: 70 or less
  4. Press ↺ to get the beat's own speed back

Try this: Tap your foot to the beat at 70, then at 140.

Think about it

Which speed feels calm? Which feels exciting?

Your turn

Find the speed you like best for one beat. Write it down.

Lesson 11 · Skill

Swing

Swing makes every second note a little late. Straight sounds even. Swing sounds bouncy.

  1. Set Swing to 60 % or more
  2. Play a beat with swing on
  3. Bring Swing back to 50 %: straight

Try this: Play the same beat straight, then with swing. Say "ta-ta" and then "taa-ta".

Think about it

What kinds of music swing?

Your turn

Write the swing number that sounds best to you, and why.

Lesson 12 · Skill

Play along: the backbeat

Now you join the band. Turn the beat down, then add your own hits on top.

  1. Change the Beat volume
  2. Tap 8 hits while the beat plays
  3. Tap the snare on 2 and 4 with the beat, four times

Try this: Count 1, 2, 3, 4 out loud. Tap the snare on 2 and 4.

Think about it

How do you know where 1 is in the beat?

Your turn

Write which beat was easiest to play along with.

Lesson 13 · Skill

Fills and the crash on 1

A fill is a short run on the toms at the end of a part. It often lands on a crash, right on 1.

  1. Play four tom hits in a row, quickly
  2. Play a tom fill, then a crash right after
  3. Tap the crash on 1, while the beat plays

Try this: Count 1, 2, 3, 4. Play the fill on 4. Crash on the next 1.

Think about it

Why does a crash on 1 feel like a new start?

Your turn

Make up a fill. Write the toms you use, in order.

Lesson 14 · Skill

Record a take

A take is a recording of you playing. You can listen back, keep it or delete it.

  1. Press ● Record a take
  2. Play, then stop recording

Try this: Listen to your take. What would you play the same next time? What would you change?

Think about it

Why do musicians record themselves?

Your turn

Write one thing you liked in your take.

Lesson 15 · Skill

The whole screen

The kit can fill the whole screen, so every drum is bigger under your fingers.

  1. Open the whole-screen kit: turn your phone sideways, or press ⤢
  2. Play a drum on the whole-screen kit
  3. Press ✕ Close in ☰ Menu

Try this: Hold the phone or tablet with both hands. Play with your thumbs.

Think about it

Is it easier to play big or small? Why?

Your turn

Write which fingers play which drums.

Lesson 16 · A song to make

Song: Rock out

A big rock groove on a big kit, with a crash to start each part.

  1. Play a beat from Rock on Kit J, Q, S or T
  2. Play along: 16 hits with the rock beat
  3. Tap the crash on 1 with the rock beat

Try this: Play along for four bars, then a tom fill, then crash on 1.

Think about it

What makes rock drums sound big?

Your turn

Name your rock song. Write which beat and kit you used.

Lesson 17 · A song to make

Song: Funk groove

Funk is tight and bouncy. Soft ghost notes on the snare fill the gaps.

  1. Play a beat from Funk and soul on Kit W · studio funk
  2. Tap 4 ghost notes on the snare with the funk beat
  3. Play along: 16 hits with the funk beat

Try this: Keep the loud snare on 2 and 4. Put soft ghost notes between them.

Think about it

How do soft notes make a groove feel better?

Your turn

Write where your ghost notes go in the bar.

Lesson 18 · A song to make

Song: Jazz with brushes

Jazz drummers swing on the ride and close the hi-hat on 2 and 4. Brushes make it soft and warm.

  1. Play a beat from Jazz and swing on Kit K or Kit V, the brush kits
  2. Tap the ride 8 times with the jazz beat
  3. Tap the hi-hat on 2 and 4 with the jazz beat, four times

Try this: Ride pattern: ding, ding-a, ding, ding-a. Say it while you tap.

Think about it

How are brushes different from sticks?

Your turn

Write the ride pattern with words or marks.

Lesson 19 · A song to make

Song: Latin groove

Latin music has many drums talking together: congas, bongos, clave and more.

  1. Play a beat from Latin on Kit H · Latin percussion
  2. Tap the conga or the bongo 8 times with the beat
  3. Tap the clave 8 times with the beat

Try this: The clave pattern: 1, 2, 3 ... 1, 2. Tap it over the beat.

Think about it

Why do Latin bands have so many drummers?

Your turn

Write the names of the Latin drums you played.

Lesson 20 · A song to make

Song: March

A marching band walks to the drums. The snare keeps everyone in step.

  1. Play Military march, March or Parade on Kit O · marching band
  2. Tap the snare roll with the march
  3. Tap the snare 8 times with the march

Try this: March in place while you tap: left, right, left, right.

Think about it

Where have you heard a marching band?

Your turn

Write a short snare part for your own march.

Lesson 21 · A song to make

Song: Your own groove

Pick any kit and any beat. Play your own part on top and record it.

  1. Start ● Record a take while a beat plays
  2. Play 16 hits while it records
  3. Play, then stop recording

Try this: Plan it first: a groove, a fill, a crash, then the groove again.

Think about it

What would you change if you played it again?

Your turn

Write your groove's name, its kit, its beat and its speed.
