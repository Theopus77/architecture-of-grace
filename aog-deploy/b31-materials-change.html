Does it come back?

A bar of chocolate. Hard, in one piece, its own shape.

Test it, then ask whether it comes back. Melting and freezing come back — the stuff is the same, just a new shape. Cooking and burning do not. A new shape is not a new material.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Sand is a liquid, because you can pour it.

Pouring is not the test — shape is. Every single grain of sand keeps its own hard shape. A liquid takes the shape of its container; the grains never do.

Shape is the test, not pouring.

⚠️ People often think…

Melted chocolate poured into a mold is a new material.

Taste it — still chocolate. Only the shape changed, and cooling it brings the hard bar right back. A change you can undo did not make anything new.

A new shape is not a new material.

Comes back, or does not

1 Watch one

Ice melts into water. Is that a new material?

  1. Ask what it was: water, frozen hard.
  2. Ask what it is now: water, as a liquid.
  3. Now the real test — put it back in the freezer. Does it come back?
  4. It does. Same water the whole time, just colder or warmer. ✓

Helpful Hints

🪜 Materials — the ladder

A material is what a thing is made of → a solid keeps its shape, a liquid takes the shape of its container → every material has properties: hard, bendy, shiny, soaks up water, sticks to a magnet → pick the material whose properties fit the job → small pieces build big things and can be built again → heat and cold change things: melt and freeze come back, cook and burn don’t.

Shape, properties, pieces, heat and cold.

🤝 The whole idea

Everything around you is made of some material, and every material can be tested. Bend it, wet it, heat it, cool it — and ask one question: can I get the old thing back?

Test it. Then ask if it comes back.

⚠️ Tricky ones

  • Sand pours, but it is NOT a liquid — every grain is a tiny solid. Shape is the test, not pouring.
  • Melted chocolate in a new shape is STILL chocolate. A new shape is not a new material.
  • Ice, water and steam are all the same water — just hotter or colder.
  • Cooling a cooked egg does NOT make it raw. Some changes never come back.
  • Take a tower apart and you still have every block. Nothing is lost.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • Pictures of a rock, milk, a block, juice — “circle the solids.”
  • “Which material would you pick for a raincoat, and why?”
  • A picture of ice, water and steam with arrows — “what makes each change?” (heat or cold).
  • “A cake is baked. Can it change back? Why not?”
  • “The same blocks make a tower and then a house. What changed?”

✅ Can you…

  • Name four properties you can test?
  • Pick the right material for a job and say why?
  • Say what heat does to ice and what cold does to water?
  • Name two changes that come back and two that don’t?
