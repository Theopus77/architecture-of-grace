Grades K–2 — the body and the room

Before anybody cooks anything, four things have to be true: hands get washed properly, a kitchen has three kinds of danger and each one has a move, food is everyday or sometimes rather than good or bad, and a job is not finished until the room looks like nobody was here.

Clean Hands
Germs are too small to see, so your eyes cannot tell you when your hands are clean — which is why the rule is twenty seconds. The five steps, the spots everybody misses, and why sanitizer is the spare and not the swap.
Open
Hot, Cold and Sharp
Three kinds of danger and a move for each. Hot has no color, so you ask before you touch. Sharp things travel point-down and are never caught when they fall. And a blunt knife cuts more people than a sharp one.
Open
Everyday Food and Sometimes Food
The parts of a plate, a range of colors, and water. No food is called bad — everyday and sometimes describe how often a food turns up, and your stomach knows you are full before your plate does.
Open
A Job Done to the End
Get it out, do it, put it back. The third part is the one people drop, and dropping it hands the job to somebody else. The test is whether the room looks like nobody was here.

Measure It Right

The Needle and the Button

Open
Money and Choices

Plan It, Cook It, Clean It Up
Most of cooking happens before and after the cooking. Cupboard before list, work backwards from dinner, start what waits — and do the clearing up while the food cooks, because waiting time is working time.

The engines

Every activity here is one of these six kinds, and all six work now. Each unit page has study cards and a quiz; its Workshop tab has four more.

Study cards, hints and a practice quiz
Open · every unit page
Twenty-four cards at three levels, a hints sheet with the charts a lab needs, and a quiz that names the mistake behind every wrong answer.
Label the diagram

Put it in order
Open · the Workshop tab
Threading a machine, dicing an onion, sewing on a button, braising a cut, or a timeline that puts everything hot on the table at once.
Sort into bins
Open · the Workshop tab
Safe or unsafe. Dry heat or moist. Which stitch for which job. Which cut is this. Top thread, bobbin or needle. Fixed or variable.
Table to graph
Open · the Workshop tab

Claim, evidence, reasoning
Open · the Workshop tab
Write the argument out: can this chicken be served, what caused the loops, why are the muffins tough, which apartment costs less over a year.
For the teacher

<b>This page is a map.</b> It saves nothing about a student and sends nothing anywhere. Each door opens a unit page; a finished activity sends its one summary row to your Sheet, under the name the student typed, and nothing else ever leaves the device.

<b>Hand out one band.</b> Add the band to the address and the page opens there: <b>/facs#6-8</b>. Every unit has a short address too — <b>Show on the board</b> prints it large enough for the back row.

<b>The page is not the lab.</b> Every unit teaches the procedure and checks it on the screen; the knife, the machine and the stove still need your supervision, your school's rules and your own demonstration. Nothing here is medical, nutritional or financial advice.
