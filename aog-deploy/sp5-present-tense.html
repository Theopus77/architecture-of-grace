The Present Tense

Every Spanish sentence needs a verb, and a Spanish verb changes its ending to say who is doing the action. This unit holds the three verb families, the six endings for each one, the four verbs you cannot avoid — ir, ser, tener, hacer — and the words for a school day: study cards to learn them, hints to hold them, a quiz to test them, and a workshop to use them.

The end of the verb says who

An English sentence in two boxes. The arrow shows who is acting, and it comes from the little word in front.

That tail has a name: the ending. It already says who is doing the action, which is why Spanish can drop the pronoun. Hablo is a whole sentence — ‘I speak’, with no yo in sight.

What people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

The verb can stay as the dictionary gives it: Yo hablar español.

An infinitive names the action and nothing else. It never says who. Cut the last two letters off hablar to get habl-, then add the ending for the person: Hablo español.

An infinitive left in a sentence is an unfinished verb.

⚠️ People often think…

‘I am eating’ needs a Spanish word for am, so it is Yo soy comiendo.

One Spanish form covers all three English ways. I eat, I am eating and I do eat are all Como. There is no word for am or do to look for here, so nothing has to be invented.

One Spanish word does the work of three English ones.

⚠️ People often think…

Every Spanish sentence needs a pronoun in front of the verb, so you write yo, tú or él each time.

The ending already names the person, so the pronoun is usually extra weight. Keep it for three jobs: to contrast — Yo estudio, pero él trabaja; to clear up habla, which covers él, ella and usted at once; and to put weight on the person.

Say the pronoun only when it does a job the ending cannot.

⚠️ People often think…

Usted means you, so it takes the tú ending: usted hablas.

Usted takes the él and ella ending: usted habla. It is polite, not plural, and the verb treats it as one other person. To a principal you say ¿Habla usted inglés?

Usted is polite, not plural. It rides with él and ella.

Stem plus ending

1 Watch one

hablar — give the yo, tú and él forms.

  1. Last two letters: -ar. That is the family.
  2. Cut those two off. The stem is habl-.
  3. yo takes -o. habl + o = hablo.
  4. tú takes -as. habl + as = hablas.
  5. él, ella, usted take -a. habl + a = habla.
  6. hablo, hablas, habla. The stem never moved — only the tail. ✓

Helpful Hints

🧭 The unit in one line

Find the infinitive → see its family (-ar, -er, -ir) → cut off the last two letters to get the stem → add the ending for the person → say it, usually without the pronoun.

Cut two. Add one. -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an.

🔑 The one idea

The ending does the work. In English the little word in front tells you who acts — I, you, we. In Spanish the tail of the verb tells you, so the little word can go. Hablo already means ‘I speak’, with no yo in sight.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • Do not leave the infinitive in the sentence. ‘Yo hablar español’ is wrong. Cut hablar down to hablo.
  • Do not add a word for ‘am’ or ‘do’. ‘I am eating’ is Como. ‘I do eat’ is Como. One Spanish word does all three jobs.
  • -er and -ir verbs are almost twins. They differ in only two places: nosotros (-emos / -imos) and vosotros (-éis / -ís).
  • The vosotros forms carry an accent — habláis, coméis, vivís. Leave it off and the word is misspelled.
  • No goes right before the verb: ‘No trabajo hoy.’ Not after it, and not at the end.
  • Usted takes the same ending as él and ella — usted habla, not usted hablas. It is polite, not plural.

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Say the six -ar endings from memory, in order?
  • Name the two places where -er and -ir differ?
  • Give the yo form of hacer, poner, salir, traer, conocer, saber, dar and ver?
  • Write out ir, ser and tener in all six persons?
  • Build a near-future sentence with ir + a + infinitive?
  • Explain why ‘Hablo español’ needs no yo — and name a case where you would keep it?
