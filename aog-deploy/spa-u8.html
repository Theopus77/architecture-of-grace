First, Then, Last: Telling a Day

Sequencing

Every day has an order. You wake up, you eat, you go to school, you play and you sleep. In this unit you learn to tell that order in Spanish with small signpost words: primero (first), luego (then), después (after that) and finalmente (finally).

You also learn to name the parts of the day, the days of the week and the time on the hour, like Es la una and Son las tres. You use everyday verbs such as como (I eat), voy (I go), juego (I play) and leo (I read).

Then you become a writer. You join ideas with y, pero and también. You build a short story with a beginning, a middle and an end, or draw it as a comic strip. Finally, you check your verbs and describing words and read your story aloud.

The big question

How do we tell a day or a story in Spanish so that a listener can follow every step?

Daily Routine
Big questionHow can a few small Spanish words turn a list of actions into a day someone can follow?
The story

A Day with Sofía in San Juan

Sofía lives in San Juan, Puerto Rico, and she tells her whole day in order.

Sofía is ten years old. She lives in San Juan, the capital of Puerto Rico. In Puerto Rico, people speak Spanish and English. Sofía speaks both. Today she tells us her day in Spanish, one step at a time.

"Primero, desayuno." (First, I eat breakfast.) Sofía eats bread and fruit in the kitchen. It is la mañana (the morning). "Luego, voy a la escuela." (Then, I go to school.) She walks with her little brother. The sun is already warm.

At school, Sofía reads and writes. "Leo un libro." (I read a book.) At lunch she says, "Como arroz con habichuelas." (I eat rice and beans.) After lunch she plays outside with her friends. "Juego con mis amigos." (I play with my friends.)

"Después, voy a casa." (After that, I go home.) It is la tarde (the afternoon). She does her homework at the table. Outside, she hears a tiny frog sing: "co-quí, co-quí." The coquí is a small frog famous in Puerto Rico.

It is la noche (the night) now. Sofía looks at the clock. "Son las nueve." (It is nine o'clock.) "Finalmente, duermo." (Finally, I sleep.) Her day had a first step, middle steps and a last step. That order makes her story easy to follow.

Talk about itSofía used primero, luego, después and finalmente. Which of those words would you use to tell the first thing you do each morning?
Section 1

Words That Put Things in Order

15.1

Primero and Luego

Main ideaPrimero means first and luego means then; they start the first and next steps of a day.

Picture your morning. You wake up. You eat. You go to school. In Spanish, you can show the order with small words. The first step starts with (first). "Primero, desayuno." means "First, I eat breakfast."

The next step starts with (then, later). "Luego, voy a la escuela." means "Then, I go to school." Put the order word at the start of the sentence. Put a comma after it, just like in English.

A common mistake is to say "primer" alone for "first" in a list of steps. When you mean "first, I do this," say primero. Another mistake is to forget the comma. Say it: Primero, como. Luego, leo.

Think it through
Two Steps in the Morning

Mateo tells his morning. "Primero, desayuno." (First, I eat breakfast.) "Luego, voy a la escuela." (Then, I go to school.) Primero comes before luego. The order words go at the start of each sentence.

Tell two things you do in the morning. Start one with primero and the other with luego.

Words to know
primero
first — starts the first step: Primero, desayuno (First, I eat breakfast)
luego
then, later — starts the next step: Luego, voy a la escuela
Check yourself

1. What does primero mean?

Why: Primero means first. It starts the first step in a list.

2. Which sentence means "Then, I go to school"?

Why: Luego means then, and voy means I go. The order word goes first, with a comma.

3. Mateo says: "Luego, leo. Primero, como." What does he do first?

Why: Primero, como means First, I eat. Primero marks the first step, even if he says it second.
15.2

Después and Finalmente

Main ideaDespués means after that and finalmente means finally; finalmente marks the last step.

Your day keeps going after school. To add another step, use (after that, afterward). "Después, voy a casa." means "After that, I go home." Look at the accent mark on the last e: des-PUÉS.

To tell the very last step, use (finally). "Finalmente, duermo." means "Finally, I sleep." It sounds a lot like the English word finally. Words that look alike in two languages help you guess.

A common mistake is to drop the accent in después. Without it, the word is spelled wrong. Another mistake is to use finalmente in the middle. Save it for the end. Try it: Después, leo. Finalmente, duermo.

Think it through
The End of the Day

Lucía tells her evening. "Después, voy a casa." (After that, I go home.) "Después, como." (After that, I eat.) "Finalmente, duermo." (Finally, I sleep.) You can use después more than once, but finalmente only once, at the end.

Tell the last two things you do at night. Use después and finalmente.

Words to know
después

finalmente
finally — marks the last step: Finalmente, duermo (Finally, I sleep)
Check yourself

1. Which word is spelled correctly?

Why: The word is después, with the accent on the last e.

2. What does "Finalmente, duermo" mean?

Why: Finalmente means finally, and duermo means I sleep.

3. Where does finalmente belong in a list of steps?

Why: Finalmente marks the last step, so it goes at the end.
15.3

Putting Steps in Order

Main ideaUse primero, luego, después and finalmente in that order to tell four steps clearly.

Now use all four words together. Think of making a sandwich. First, you get bread. Then, you add cheese. After that, you close it. Finally, you eat it. Spanish uses the same kind of chain.

Here is Sofía’s morning: "Primero, desayuno. Luego, voy a la escuela. Después, leo. Finalmente, como." Each word is a signpost. It tells the listener where we are in the day: start, next, later, end.

The usual order is primero, luego, después, finalmente. Luego and después can swap places. Both mean a later step. But primero always starts, and finalmente always ends. A mixed-up order confuses the listener.

Think it through
Mixed-Up Steps

These steps are mixed up: "Finalmente, duermo." "Primero, desayuno." "Después, voy a casa." "Luego, voy a la escuela." Read the order words. Primero comes first. Luego and después come in the middle. Finalmente comes last.

Put the four sentences in the right order and read them aloud.

Words to know
el orden
the order — the way steps follow each other: primero, luego, después, finalmente
el paso
the step — one action in a list, like Primero, desayuno
Check yourself

1. Which order word should come last?

Why: Finalmente means finally, so it marks the last step.

2. Which list is in the usual order?

Why: The usual chain is first, then, after that, finally: primero, luego, después, finalmente.

3. Which two words can swap places in the middle?

Why: Luego and después both mean a later step, so either can come in the middle.
Section 2

Times of Day and Days of the Week

15.4

Morning, Afternoon and Night

Main ideaLa mañana is the morning, la tarde is the afternoon and la noche is the night.

Think of three parts of your day. You eat breakfast in (the morning). You finish school in (the afternoon, and early evening). You go to bed in (the night).

All three words are feminine, so they use la. To say "in the morning," Spanish says por la mañana. "Leo por la noche" means "I read at night." English says in or at, but Spanish uses por here.

Watch out: mañana without la means tomorrow. "La mañana" is the morning. "Mañana" alone is tomorrow. Also, la tarde can mean the afternoon and the early evening, until it gets dark.

Think it through
Three Parts of a Day

Picture a window. In the first picture, the sun comes up: la mañana. In the second, the sun is high and school is over: la tarde. In the third, the sky is dark with stars: la noche.

Say one thing you do por la mañana, one por la tarde and one por la noche.

Words to know
la mañana
the morning — Desayuno por la mañana (I eat breakfast in the morning)
la tarde
the afternoon and early evening — Voy a casa por la tarde
la noche
the night — Duermo por la noche (I sleep at night)
Check yourself

1. What does la noche mean?

Why: La noche is the night, when it is dark.

2. How do you say "I read in the afternoon"?

Why: Spanish uses por la tarde for in the afternoon, and tarde is feminine, so it takes la.

3. What does mañana mean when it has no la in front?

Why: Mañana alone means tomorrow. La mañana means the morning.
15.5

The Seven Days

Main ideaThe days are lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado and domingo, written with small letters.

Look at a Spanish calendar. The week usually starts on lunes (Monday), not Sunday. Then come martes (Tuesday), miércoles (Wednesday), jueves (Thursday) and viernes (Friday).

The weekend is (Saturday) and domingo (Sunday). Notice the accents: miércoles and sábado. In Spanish, the days are written with small letters. English uses capital letters, so English speakers often get this wrong.

To say "on Monday," Spanish uses el: "El lunes, voy a la escuela." Do not add en. Saying "en lunes" is a common mistake that comes from English "on." For every Monday, say los lunes.

15.6

My Week, Day by Day

Main ideaPut a day at the start of a sentence to tell what you do on that day.

Now join days and actions. Think of your own week. "El lunes, voy a la escuela." (On Monday, I go to school.) "El sábado, juego fútbol." (On Saturday, I play soccer.) The day tells when.

Add a part of the day to be even clearer. "El domingo por la mañana, como con mi abuela." means "On Sunday morning, I eat with my grandmother." The day comes first, then the part of the day.

Use los to show a habit. "Los martes, leo en la biblioteca" means "On Tuesdays, I read at the library." El martes is one Tuesday. Los martes is every Tuesday. The day word does not change.

Think it through

Tell what you do on two different days. Start each sentence with el or los and a day.

el domingo

los martes
on Tuesdays, every Tuesday — los shows a habit; the word martes stays the same

Section 3

Routine Verbs and the Clock

15.7

Verbs for My Day

Main ideaComo, voy, juego and leo tell what I do; the -o ending means I.

Think of four things you do every day. You eat, you go places, you play and you read. In Spanish: (I eat), (I go), (I play) and (I read). Each one already means "I."

Look at the endings. Com-o, jueg-o, le-o. The -o at the end tells us the speaker is yo (I). That is why you do not need to say yo. "Como" alone is a full sentence: I eat. Voy is special, but it also means I.

A common mistake is to use the dictionary form. "Yo comer" is wrong. The right way is "Yo como" or just "Como." Another mistake: juego has a u inside it. The dictionary form is jugar, but I play is juego.

Think it through
From Dictionary Form to I

comer (to eat) → como (I eat). leer (to read) → leo (I read). jugar (to play) → juego (I play). ir (to go) → voy (I go). The first two follow the pattern: drop the ending, add -o. Juego and voy change more, so learn them by heart.

Say three sentences about your day using como, voy and leo.

Words to know
como
I eat — from comer: Como arroz (I eat rice)
voy
I go — from ir: Voy a la escuela (I go to school)
juego
I play — from jugar: Juego con mis amigos
leo
I read — from leer: Leo un libro (I read a book)
Check yourself

1. How do you say "I read a book"?

Why: Leo means I read. Leer is the dictionary form and cannot stand for I.

2. What does "Voy a casa" mean?

Why: Voy means I go, and a casa means home.

3. Which is the right form for "I play"?

Why: Jugar changes u to ue, so I play is juego.
15.8

It Is One O'Clock

Main ideaAsk ¿Qué hora es? and answer Es la una for one o'clock.

Your friend asks, "" (What time is it?). Look at the clock. If the short hand is on 1, say "Es la una." (It is one o’clock.) Notice es, not son, because one is only one.

Why la? In Spanish, the word hora (hour) is feminine and hidden in the sentence. La una means la una hora, the first hour. So times always use la or las, never el.

A common mistake is "Son la una." One is singular, so use es. Another mistake is "Es uno." The number must be una, the feminine form, to match hora. Say it: ¿Qué hora es? Es la una.

Think it through
Lunch at One

Ana looks at the clock at school. The short hand is on 1 and the long hand is on 12. Her friend asks, "¿Qué hora es?" Ana says, "Es la una." (It is one o'clock.) Then she adds, "Como a la una." (I eat at one.)

Ask a partner ¿Qué hora es? and answer for one o'clock.

Es la una.

Check yourself

1. It is 1:00. What do you say?

Why: One is singular, so use es, and hora is feminine, so use la una.

2. What does ¿Qué hora es? mean?

Why: Hora means hour or time, so ¿Qué hora es? asks the time.

3. Why do Spanish times use la or las?

Why: Times stand for la hora or las horas, and hora is a feminine word.
15.9

Two, Three and More O'Clock

Main ideaFor two o'clock and later, say Son las and the number: Son las tres.

Now the clock says 3:00. More than one hour has passed, so Spanish uses the plural. "" means "It is three o’clock." Son is the plural form of es, and las is the plural of la.

The pattern works for every hour from two to twelve. Son las dos. Son las cinco. Son las doce. To say at a time, use a: "Voy a la escuela a las ocho." (I go to school at eight.)

Add the part of the day to be clear. "Son las tres de la tarde" means 3:00 in the afternoon. "Son las nueve de la noche" means 9:00 at night. Common mistake: "Es las tres." Use son with plural hours.

Writing a Short Story

Writing
Big questionWhat makes a short story in Spanish easy for a reader to follow from beginning to end?
The story

The Red Backpack Comes Home

Marco loses his red backpack on the bus, and a kind stranger helps it find its way back.

Marco has a red backpack. In Spanish he calls it la mochila roja (the red backpack). It holds his books, his pencils and a photo of his dog. On Monday, Marco rides the bus to school with his sister, Elena.

Marco is sleepy. He puts the backpack on the seat. The bus stops at the school, and Marco jumps off fast. Then he stops. "¡Mi mochila!" (My backpack!) The backpack is still on the bus. The bus is already gone.

Marco feels sad. "Estoy triste," he says. (I am sad.) Elena says, "Vamos a la oficina." (Let's go to the office.) The secretary calls the bus company. "Una mochila roja, pero pequeña," Marco says. (A red backpack, but small.)

On the bus, a woman sees the backpack. She opens the front pocket. She finds a card with Marco's name and school. She gives the backpack to the driver. The driver brings it back to the school in the afternoon.

At three o'clock, the secretary calls Marco. There it is: la mochila roja. Everything is inside, and the photo of his dog too. Marco smiles. "¡Gracias!" Now he tells the story in order: a beginning, a middle and a happy end.

Section 1

Words That Join Ideas

16.1

Joining with Y

Main ideaY means and; it joins two words or two short sentences into one.

Picture two short sentences: "Como arroz." "Como frijoles." They sound choppy. Join them with y (and): "Como arroz y frijoles." (I eat rice and beans.) Now it is one smooth sentence.

Y can join two actions too. "Leo un libro y juego con mi perro." (I read a book and I play with my dog.) Y is one letter, and it is said like the ee in see. It does not need a comma before it.

A common mistake is to repeat y too much: "Como y leo y juego y duermo." Use commas for a list: "Como, leo, juego y duermo." Put y only before the last item, the same way English uses and.

Think it through
Two Become One

Marco writes: "Tengo una mochila. Tengo un perro." (I have a backpack. I have a dog.) He joins them: "Tengo una mochila y un perro." (I have a backpack and a dog.) The sentence is shorter and smoother.

Join these two sentences with y: Leo un libro. Como una manzana.

Words to know
y (and)
and — joins words or ideas: Como arroz y frijoles (I eat rice and beans)
la oración
the sentence — a whole thought with someone and an action
Check yourself

16.2

Changing Direction with Pero

Main ideaPero means but; it joins two ideas that go in different directions.

Picture this: Marco loves the bus, but he is sleepy. The second idea surprises us. In Spanish, (but) joins ideas like that. "Me gusta el bus, pero estoy cansado." (I like the bus, but I am tired.)

Put a comma before pero, just like before but in English. "La mochila es roja, pero es pequeña." (The backpack is red, but it is small.) Pero tells the reader: get ready, something different is coming.

Do not mix up pero and perro. Pero with one r means but. Perro with rr means dog. Say them out loud. The rr in perro is rolled. A missing r changes the meaning of your whole sentence.

Think it through
A Surprise in the Middle

Elena writes: "Voy a la escuela. No tengo mi mochila." Those two ideas do not match, so she joins them with pero: "Voy a la escuela, pero no tengo mi mochila." (I go to school, but I do not have my backpack.)

Finish this sentence with pero: Me gusta la pizza, pero…

Words to know
pero
but — joins two ideas that differ: Me gusta leer, pero no me gusta correr
el perro
the dog — spelled with rr; do not mix it up with pero (but)
Check yourself

1. Which word means but?

Why: Pero with one r means but. Perro with rr means dog.

2. Which sentence uses pero correctly?

Why: Pero joins two ideas that differ: I like the sun, but it is hot.

3. What does "La mochila es roja, pero es pequeña" mean?

Why: Roja is red, pero is but, and pequeña is small.
16.3

Adding More with También

Main ideaTambién means also or too; it adds one more matching idea.

Picture Elena and Marco at lunch. Marco says, "Como arroz." Elena says, "Yo ." (Me too.) También means also or too. It adds something that matches what came before.

You can put también after the verb: "Leo un libro. Leo una revista también." Or before the verb: "También leo una revista." (I also read a magazine.) Both are correct. Notice the accent on the last syllable: tam-BIÉN.

A common mistake is using también to show a difference. That is the job of pero. También is for more of the same kind. "Juego fútbol. También juego béisbol." Both ideas are sports, so they match.

Think it through

Tell one thing you do, then add a second matching thing with también.

Section 2

Building a Story

16.4

Beginning, Middle and End

Main ideaA good short story has a beginning that sets the scene, a middle with a problem and an end.

Think of Marco’s story. At the beginning, we meet Marco and his red backpack. In the middle, there is a problem: the backpack stays on the bus. At the end, the problem is solved and he gets it back.

In Spanish, these parts are (the beginning), el medio (the middle) and (the end). The beginning tells who and where. The middle tells what goes wrong. The end tells how it turns out.

Order words help. Start the beginning with primero. Use luego or después in the middle. Start the end with finalmente. A reader who sees finalmente knows the story is closing.

Think it through
Three Parts of Marco's Story

Principio: "Marco tiene una mochila roja." (Marco has a red backpack.) Medio: "Después, la mochila está en el bus." (After that, the backpack is on the bus.) Final: "Finalmente, Marco tiene su mochila." (Finally, Marco has his backpack.)

Tell a tiny story about a lost toy in three sentences: a beginning, a middle and an end.

Words to know
el principio
the beginning — tells who is in the story and where
el final
the end — tells how the problem turns out
el problema
the problem — what goes wrong in the middle; a masculine word even though it ends in -a
Check yourself

Why: The middle has the problem. The end tells how it turns out.

2. What does el final mean?

16.5

Four to Six Sentences

Main ideaA short story in Spanish can be four to six sentences, each one a clear step.

Picture a story as a set of stairs. Each sentence is one step. Four to six steps are enough for a short story. Too few steps, and the reader misses something. Too many, and the reader gets lost.

Here is a six-sentence story. "Es sábado. Primero, voy al parque. Luego, juego con mi perro. Pero mi perro corre. Después, busco a mi perro. Finalmente, está en casa." Each sentence moves the story forward.

Keep each sentence short and complete. Each one needs someone and an action. "Mi perro corre" (My dog runs) is complete. "Mi perro grande" (My big dog) is not a sentence. It has no action.

Think it through

"Es lunes. Primero, voy a la escuela. Luego, leo un cuento. Pero no tengo mi lápiz. Después, mi amiga me da un lápiz. Finalmente, escribo mi cuento." Count the sentences. There are six. Find the problem: no tengo mi lápiz (I do not have my pencil).

Write a four-sentence story about a day at the park. Use at least two order words.

Why: Mi perro corre has someone (my dog) and an action (runs).

3. What does "Escribo un cuento" mean?

Why: Escribo means I write, and un cuento is a story.
16.6

A Comic Strip in Spanish

Main ideaA comic strip tells a story in boxes; each box can hold one short Spanish sentence.

Picture a comic strip with four boxes. Each box is one step. In Spanish, a comic strip is often called . Each box is a panel, called la viñeta. The words people say go in a speech bubble.

Box 1: Marco on the bus. "Tengo sueño." (I am sleepy.) Box 2: the school. "¡Mi mochila!" Box 3: the office. "Estoy triste." Box 4: the backpack returns. "¡Gracias!" Four boxes tell a whole story.

Keep bubble words short. Use ¡ and ! for exclaiming, and ¿ and ? for asking. The upside-down mark goes at the start. A common mistake is to write only the end mark, like in English.

Think it through
Four Boxes

Box 1: a girl sees rain. "¡Llueve!" (It is raining!) Box 2: she looks for her umbrella. "¿Dónde está mi paraguas?" (Where is my umbrella?) Box 3: her dog has it. Box 4: she laughs. "¡Gracias, perro!"

Plan a four-box comic about your morning. Write one short Spanish sentence for each box.

Words to know
la historieta
the comic strip — a story told in boxes with pictures
la viñeta
the panel — one box of a comic strip
Check yourself

1. What is la historieta?

Why: La historieta is a comic strip, a story told in boxes.

2. Which bubble is punctuated correctly?

Why: Spanish exclamations open with ¡ and close with !.

3. What is one box of a comic strip called?

Why: La viñeta is one panel, or box, of a comic strip.
Section 3

Checking Your Story

16.7

Check Every Verb Ending

Main ideaThe verb ending must match who does the action: -o for yo, -as or -es for tú, -a or -e for él and ella.

You finished your story. Now check it like a detective. Look at every verb. Ask: who does this? If it is you, the ending is usually -o: , como, escribo. If it is Marco, it is usually -a or -e: habla, come, escribe.

Here is a sentence with a mistake: "Marco como arroz." Who eats? Marco, not me. So the verb must be come: "Marco come arroz." (Marco eats rice.) The -o ending only works for yo.

English speakers make this mistake because English verbs barely change: I eat, he eats. In Spanish, the ending carries the meaning. Check each verb, one by one, before you share your story.

Think it through
Detective Check

Elena reads Marco's story: "Mi hermana camino a la escuela." She asks, "Who walks?" Mi hermana (my sister) is ella. So camino is wrong. The fix: "Mi hermana camina a la escuela." (My sister walks to school.)

Fix this sentence: Marco juego con su perro.

Words to know
hablo
I speak — from hablar; the -o ending means yo
habla
he or she speaks — from hablar; the -a ending means él or ella
Check yourself

1. Fix the verb: "Marco ___ arroz."

Why: Marco is él, so comer becomes come.

2. Which ending usually means yo (I)?

Why: In the present tense, most yo forms end in -o: hablo, como, escribo.

3. Which sentence is correct?

Why: Mi hermana is ella, so caminar becomes camina.
16.8

Check Nouns and Describing Words

Main ideaA describing word matches its noun: la mochila roja, el libro rojo, los libros rojos.

Look again at Marco’s backpack: la mochila roja. Mochila is feminine, so the describing word ends in -a: roja. A book is masculine: el libro rojo. The describing word changes to match the noun.

Plural nouns need plural describing words. "Los libros rojos" (the red books). "Las mochilas rojas" (the red backpacks). Add -s to both. In Spanish, the describing word usually comes after the noun, not before like in English.

Common mistakes: "la mochila rojo" (wrong ending) and "la roja mochila" (English order). Some describing words end in -e, like (big). They stay the same for both: el perro grande, la casa grande.

Think it through
Match It Up

el libro rojo (the red book) → la mochila roja (the red backpack) → los libros rojos (the red books) → las mochilas rojas (the red backpacks). Look at the last letters. The noun and the describing word end the same way.

Describe your own backpack or bag in Spanish. Check that the endings match.

Words to know
rojo
red — rojo, roja, rojos, rojas to match the noun: la mochila roja
grande
big — the same for masculine and feminine: el perro grande, la casa grande
Check yourself

1. Which is correct for "the red backpack"?

Why: Mochila is feminine, so it takes la and roja, and the describing word goes after the noun.

2. Which is correct for "the big dogs"?

Why: Perros is plural, so grande becomes grandes, and los matches too.

3. Which describing word stays the same for el and la?

Why: Words ending in -e, like grande, are the same for masculine and feminine.
16.9

Read It Aloud

Main ideaReading your story aloud helps you hear missing words, wrong endings and choppy sentences.

Picture yourself reading your story to a friend. Your ears catch things your eyes miss. If a sentence sounds strange, stop and check it. Maybe a word is missing, or an ending is wrong.

Read slowly. Say every vowel clearly: a, e, i, o, u. Spanish vowels have one sound each. Stress the syllable with the accent mark: , también, miércoles. The accent tells your voice where to lean.

Listen for choppy parts. "Como. Leo. Juego." sounds like a robot. Join ideas with y, pero or también. Then ask a friend: "¿Te gusta mi cuento?" (Do you like my story?) Their answer helps you improve.

Think it through
Fixing a Robot Story

Robot version: "Es domingo. Como. Leo. Juego. Duermo." Better version: "Es domingo. Primero, como y leo. Luego, juego con mi perro. Finalmente, duermo." Same actions, but the order words and y make it flow.

Read your own story aloud. Where can you join two sentences with y, pero or también?

Words to know
leer en voz alta
to read aloud — Leo mi cuento en voz alta (I read my story aloud)
después
after that — say it with the stress on the last syllable, where the accent is
Check yourself

1. Why read your story aloud?

Why: Hearing the story helps you catch missing words and wrong endings.

2. In después, which syllable gets the stress?

Why: The accent mark on é shows the stress: des-PUÉS.

3. Which version flows best?

Why: Order words and y join the actions into smooth sentences.
