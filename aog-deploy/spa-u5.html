A Whole Thought in Spanish

Sentences

A single word can name a thing, like perro (dog). But to tell someone what happened at a soccer game, or to ask a friend a question at a sleepover, you need a whole thought. In this unit you build whole sentences in Spanish: someone, and something they do.

You will learn the people words yo, tú, él, ella, nosotros and ellos. You will see how a verb ending like -o or -amos tells you who is acting, so Spanish speakers can often skip the word yo. You will use hablar, bailar, cantar and caminar right away.

Then you will say no, ask yes-or-no questions with a rising voice and the upside-down mark ¿, and answer in full sentences. You will also get a first taste of me gusta (I like). By the end, you can tell a short story and play a guessing game in Spanish.

The big question

What does it take to say a whole thought in Spanish, and to turn it into a no or a question?

The story

The Game in Five Sentences

Sofía tells a whole soccer game with five short Spanish sentences.

It is Saturday morning at the park. Sofía's team, the Tigers, plays soccer against the Hawks. Her grandpa sits on the grass. He speaks Spanish, and Sofía wants to tell him the game in Spanish too. She has a small notebook and a pencil.

Sofía writes her first sentence: "Yo camino al campo" (I walk to the field). Then she writes: "Mi amigo Leo canta" (My friend Leo sings). Leo always sings before a game. Each sentence has a person and an action. That is all a sentence needs.

The game starts. Sofía writes fast: "Nosotros jugamos" (We play). The Tigers score a goal! Sofía writes: "Ellos bailan" (They dance). The whole team dances in a circle. Grandpa laughs and claps.

After the game, Sofía reads her notebook to Grandpa. For the last line she writes only "Hablo con mi abuelo" (I talk with my grandpa). She does not write yo. Grandpa smiles. "The -o at the end already says I," he tells her. "Your Spanish is smart."

Talk about itSofía left out the word yo in her last sentence. How could Grandpa still know who was talking?
Section 1

Someone and an Action

9.1

Every Sentence Has Two Parts

Main ideaA sentence needs someone (the subject) and something that person does (the verb).

Picture Leo at the soccer game. He opens his mouth and sings. In Spanish we say "Leo canta" (Leo sings). This short sentence has two parts. Leo is the person. Canta is what he does.

The person or thing doing the action is called the (subject). The action word is called the (verb). In "Ana baila" (Ana dances), Ana is the subject. Baila is the verb. A sentence needs both parts to be a whole thought.

"Leo" alone is not a sentence. It does not tell us what Leo does. "Canta" alone asks a question: who sings? Put them together and you have a whole thought: "Leo canta." Now we know who and what.

Think it through
Find the Two Parts

Look at three sentences from the soccer game. "Leo canta." (Leo sings.) "Ana baila." (Ana dances.) "Sofía camina." (Sofía walks.) In each one, the first word is the person. The second word is the action.

In "Sofía camina," which word is the subject and which word is the verb?

Words to know
el sujeto
the subject — the person or thing that does the action in a sentence
el verbo
the verb — the action word in a sentence, like canta (sings) or baila (dances)
Check yourself

1. In the sentence "Ana baila," which word is the verb?

Why: Baila (dances) is the action, so it is the verb. Ana is the subject, the person who dances.

2. Which of these is a whole sentence in Spanish?

Why: "Leo canta" has a subject (Leo) and a verb (canta). The others are missing one of the two parts.

3. What does the subject of a sentence tell you?

Why: The subject is the person or thing doing the action, like Leo in "Leo canta."
9.2

Yo, Tú, Él, Ella

Main ideaYo means I, tú means you (a friend), él means he and ella means she.

Sometimes we use a short word instead of a name. In English we say I, you, he and she. These are called subject pronouns. A pronoun takes the place of a name. Spanish has its own set of these words.

9.3

Nosotros and Ellos

Main ideaNosotros means we, and ellos means they when you talk about a group.

At the game, Sofía talks about her whole team. She is part of the group, so she says (we). "Nosotros jugamos" means we play. If the whole group is girls, she says nosotras.

Now Sofía talks about the other team. She is not part of that group. She says (they). "Ellos corren" means they run. For a group of only girls, Spanish uses ellas.

English speakers often forget that Spanish has a boy and girl form here. Use ellos for a group of boys or a mixed group of boys and girls. Use ellas only when every person in the group is a girl or woman.

Section 2

The Ending Tells Who

9.4

Hablar Step by Step

Main ideaTo use an -ar verb, drop -ar and add an ending: -o for yo, -as for tú, -a for él or ella.

Sofía wants to say she talks with Grandpa. The verb is (to speak or to talk). This whole form, with -ar on the end, is the name of the verb. It means to talk, not who talks.

To use it, we conjugate the verb. That means we change the ending to match the person. First, drop -ar. That leaves habl-. Then add an ending. Yo hablo (I talk). Tú hablas (you talk). Él habla, ella habla (he talks, she talks).

A common mistake is to keep the -ar: "yo hablar." That sounds like "I to talk." Another mistake is using -o for everyone: "ella hablo." The -o ending only belongs to yo.

Think it through
Walk Through Hablar

Start: hablar. Step 1: drop -ar → habl-. Step 2: add the ending. yo + -o → hablo. tú + -as → hablas. él + -a → habla. ella + -a → habla. Sofía says: "Yo hablo con mi abuelo." (I talk with my grandpa.)

Follow the same steps with cantar (to sing). What is the form for tú?

Words to know
hablar
to speak or talk — a regular -ar verb: hablo, hablas, habla
conjugar
to conjugate — to change a verb's ending so it matches who does the action
Check yourself

1. Which form goes with yo?

Why: For yo, drop -ar and add -o: hablo.

2. Pick the right sentence.

Why: For ella, drop -ar and add -a: habla. The -o ending is only for yo.

3. What does conjugate mean?

Why: To conjugate is to change the ending so the verb fits the subject: hablo, hablas, habla.
9.5

Endings for Groups

Main ideaFor groups, -ar verbs take -amos for nosotros and -an for ellos or ellas.

Sofía’s team talks in the huddle. She says "Nosotros hablamos" (We talk). The ending is . Drop -ar from hablar, and add -amos: hablamos. Everyone in the huddle is included.

Then she looks at the other team. "Ellos hablan" (They talk). The ending is . It is just -a with an n added. That n is a little sign that means more than one person.

Now you have five endings. Yo hablo. Tú hablas. Él and ella habla. Nosotros hablamos. Ellos and ellas hablan. Say them out loud like a chant. Your ear will start to catch a wrong ending.

Think it through

-an

9.6

Dropping Yo

Main ideaSpanish speakers often leave out yo, tú or nosotros because the verb ending already tells who.

Look again at Sofía’s last line: "Hablo con mi abuelo" (I talk with my grandpa). She did not write yo. The -o ending already says I. Only yo uses -o with these verbs, so nobody gets confused.

This is normal in Spanish. "Cantas muy bien" means you sing very well. The -as ending shows it is tú. "Bailamos" means we dance. The -amos ending shows it is nosotros.

In English you cannot drop I. "Talk with my grandpa" sounds like an order. That is why English speakers put yo in every sentence. It is not wrong, but it can sound extra strong, like saying "I myself talk."

Think it through
Who Is It?

Grandpa reads three lines with no pronoun. "Camino al parque." (I walk to the park.) "Cantas muy bien." (You sing very well.) "Bailamos en el campo." (We dance on the field.) Each ending gives the answer.

"Hablamos con el abuelo." Which pronoun is hiding in this sentence?

Words to know
hablo
I talk — the -o ending means yo, so the word yo can be left out
cantas
you sing — the -as ending means tú: Cantas muy bien (You sing very well)
Check yourself

1. In "Bailo con mi hermana," who dances?

Why: The -o ending belongs to yo, so bailo means I dance.

2. Why can Spanish leave out yo in "Hablo inglés"?

Why: The ending carries the person. Only yo uses -o in these verbs, so the meaning stays clear.

3. What does "Cantamos en la fiesta" mean?

Why: The -amos ending means nosotros, so the sentence means we sing.
Section 3

Verbs to Use Right Away

9.7

Bailar and Cantar

Main ideaBailar (to dance) and cantar (to sing) follow the same pattern as hablar.

After the goal, the Tigers dance. Sofía writes "Ellos bailan" (They dance). The verb is (to dance). It ends in -ar, so it works just like hablar: bailo, bailas, baila, bailamos, bailan.

Leo sings before every game. The verb is (to sing). Drop -ar and add endings: canto, cantas, canta, cantamos, cantan. "Leo canta" means Leo sings.

These are called regular verbs. Regular means they follow the pattern with no surprises. Once you know hablar, you already know many verbs. Learn one pattern, and you can use it again and again.

9.8

Caminar Around the City

Main ideaCaminar (to walk) is one more regular -ar verb you can use to tell where people go.

Sofía walks to the field. She writes "Camino al campo" (I walk to the field). The verb is (to walk). Drop -ar, add -o: camino. The -o tells us it is Sofía herself.

In Chicago, many families walk to the corner store or the park. You might hear "Caminamos al parque" (We walk to the park). Al means to the. It is a joined form of a plus el.

Here is the whole set: camino, caminas, camina, caminamos, caminan. Notice something? Every form keeps camin- at the front. Only the ending changes. The front part is called the (stem).

9.9

Where the Words Go

Main ideaSpanish usually puts the subject, then the verb, then the rest, but it can move words more freely than English.

Most Spanish sentences look like English ones. Subject first, then verb, then the rest. "Ana canta una canción" (Ana sings a song). "Leo camina al parque" (Leo walks to the park).

One difference is describing words. In English we say "the big dog." In Spanish the describing word usually comes after: (the big dog). An English speaker might say "el grande perro." That sounds odd in Spanish.

Another difference is no. It goes right before the verb: "Leo no canta" (Leo does not sing). Spanish has no word for does. You will learn more about no in the next chapter.

Think it through
Fix the Order

A student writes about the game: "Una rápida chica corre." The teacher helps. First the thing: una chica (a girl). Then the describing word: rápida (fast). Then the verb. "Una chica rápida corre." (A fast girl runs.)

Put these words in Spanish order: grande / canta / el perro. What sentence do you get?

Words to know
el perro grande
the big dog — in Spanish the describing word usually comes after the noun
la canción
the song — Ana canta una canción (Ana sings a song)
Check yourself

Saying No and Asking Yes-or-No

Negation and Questions
Big questionHow do you turn a Spanish sentence into a no or a yes-or-no question?
The story

Twenty Questions at Midnight

At a sleepover, four friends play a guessing game using only sí and no.

It is Friday night at Marisol's apartment. Four friends lie in sleeping bags on the living room floor. Nobody is sleepy. Marisol's older cousin Tomás has an idea. "Let's play Twenty Questions," he says. "But in Spanish."

Tomás thinks of a famous animal. The girls can ask only yes-or-no questions. Marisol goes first. "¿Es grande?" (Is it big?) Her voice goes up at the end. Tomás answers, "Sí, es grande." (Yes, it is big.)

Ana asks, "¿Canta?" (Does it sing?) Tomás laughs. "No, no canta." (No, it does not sing.) Lucía writes each question on a notepad. She starts every question with an upside-down mark: ¿. Tomás says that is how Spanish writers warn you a question is coming.

After twelve questions, Marisol shouts, "¿Es un elefante?" (Is it an elephant?) "¡Sí!" says Tomás. Everyone cheers. Then Lucía says, "Me gusta este juego" (I like this game). Ana adds, "No me gusta dormir" (I don't like to sleep). Nobody sleeps until one o'clock.

Talk about itTomás answered "No, no canta." Why do you think he said no two times?
Section 1

Saying No

10.1

No Goes Before the Verb

Main ideaTo make a Spanish sentence negative, put no right in front of the verb.

Ana’s new friend asks what languages she speaks. Ana speaks English and Spanish, but not French. She says, " hablo francés" (I don’t speak French). She just put no in front of hablo.

Making a sentence mean not is called negation. In Spanish it takes one step. Find the verb. Put no right before it. "Leo canta" (Leo sings) becomes "Leo no canta" (Leo does not sing).

English uses extra words: do not, does not, don’t. Spanish has no word for do here. A common mistake is to put no after the verb: "Leo canta no." Another is to look for a word for don’t. Just use no before the verb.

Think it through
One Word Changes It

Yes sentence: "Ana baila." (Ana dances.) Find the verb: baila. Put no in front. No sentence: "Ana no baila." (Ana does not dance.) Yes: "Hablamos inglés." No: "No hablamos inglés." (We don't speak English.)

Make this sentence negative: "Tomás canta en la fiesta."

Words to know
no
no or not — goes right before the verb to make a sentence negative: No hablo francés
el francés
French (the language) — No hablo francés (I don't speak French)

10.2

No, No: The Double No

Main ideaIn an answer, the first no answers the question and the second no makes the verb negative.

At the sleepover, Ana asks, "¿Canta?" Tomás says, "No, no canta." It looks like he said no twice by accident. He did not. Each no has its own job in the sentence.

The first no is the answer, like English no. It comes before a comma. The second no makes the verb negative, like English not. "No, no canta" means "No, it does not sing."

English speakers sometimes drop the second no and say "No, canta." That actually means "No, it sings!" The comma and the second no matter. Say both, and pause at the comma.

Think it through
Two Jobs for No

Marisol: "Tomás, ¿hablas francés?" (Tomás, do you speak French?) Tomás: "No, no hablo francés." First no = the answer. Second no = not, before the verb hablo.

Ana asks you, "¿Bailas?" Answer that you do not dance, using the double no.

Words to know
No, no…

la coma
the comma — separates the answer no from the rest of the sentence
Check yourself

2. What does "No, canta" mean?

3. In "No, no bailo," what is the job of the second no?

Why: The second no sits before the verb bailo and means not.
10.3

Sí and Si

Main ideaSí with an accent mark means yes; si without it means if.

When Tomás says yes, Lucía writes on her notepad. She puts an accent mark on the i. Without the mark, si means if. It is a different word with a different meaning.

A yes answer in a full sentence looks like this: "Sí, es grande" (Yes, it is big). Put sí first, then a comma, then the sentence. No extra word is needed.

Many heritage speakers say sí every day but forget the accent when they write. Your ear cannot hear it, but a reader can see it. Sí is yes. Si is if, as in "si llueve" (if it rains).

Section 2

Asking Yes-or-No

10.4

Let Your Voice Go Up

Main ideaYou can turn a Spanish sentence into a yes-or-no question by raising your voice at the end.

Say "Es grande" in a flat voice. It is a statement: it is big. Now say it again, but let your voice rise at the end: "¿Es grande?" Now it asks: is it big? The words did not change.

In English we often add do or does to ask. "Do you dance?" Spanish does not need that. "Bailas" means you dance. "¿Bailas?" with a rising voice means do you dance?

This rising sound is called (intonation). It is the music of your voice. Listening for it helps you know if someone is telling you something or asking you something.

Think it through
Same Words, New Music

Statement, flat voice: "Cantas." (You sing.) Question, voice goes up: "¿Cantas?" (Do you sing?) Statement: "Hablas inglés." (You speak English.) Question: "¿Hablas inglés?" (Do you speak English?)

Say "Caminas a la escuela" two ways: once as a statement and once as a question.

Words to know
la entonación
intonation — how your voice goes up or down; up at the end can make a question
la pregunta
the question — Tomás hace una pregunta (Tomás asks a question)
Check yourself

1. How can you turn "Bailas" into a question when you speak?

2. What does "¿Hablas inglés?" mean?

Why: Spanish just uses ¿Cantas? The rising voice and question marks do the work.
10.5

The Upside-Down Question Mark

Main ideaWritten Spanish questions start with ¿ and end with ?, and exclamations start with ¡ and end with !.

Lucía writes "¿Es grande?" The first mark is upside down: ¿. It is called the (question mark). Spanish puts one at the start and one at the end.

Why? In a long sentence, you learn early that a question is coming. Then you can raise your voice in the right place when you read aloud. Exclamations work the same way: "¡Sí!" (Yes!) and "¡Qué bien!" (How great!)

A very common mistake is to write only the end mark: "Es grande?" That is missing a piece. Another is to put ¿ at the start of the whole line instead of where the question starts: "Tomás, ¿hablas francés?"

Think it through
Mark It Right

10.6

Answer in a Full Sentence

Main ideaA full answer repeats the verb with the ending for the person who answers.

Your teacher asks, "¿Hablas español?" (Do you speak Spanish?) A short answer is "Sí." A full answer is "Sí, hablo español." Full answers help you practice verbs.

Watch the ending change. The question uses hablas because it is about tú. Your answer is about yo, so it uses hablo. A common mistake is to copy the question: "Sí, hablas español." That means yes, you speak Spanish.

It works for groups too. "¿Bailan ustedes?" (Do you all dance?) "Sí, bailamos." (Yes, we dance.) Ustedes means you all. The answer uses nosotros, so the ending is -amos.

Think it through
Flip the Ending

Question to you: ¿Cantas? → Answer about me: Sí, canto. Question to you: ¿Caminas a la escuela? → Answer: No, no camino a la escuela. Question to you all: ¿Hablan inglés? → Answer: Sí, hablamos inglés.

Answer "¿Bailas en las fiestas?" in a full sentence about yourself.

Words to know
la respuesta
the answer — una respuesta completa (a full answer)
ustedes
you all — more than one person you talk to; takes the -an ending: ¿Bailan ustedes?
Check yourself

1. Your friend asks, "¿Cantas?" Which is a correct full answer about you?

Why: The answer is about yo, so the ending changes from -as to -o: canto.

2. The teacher asks the class, "¿Hablan español?" Which answer fits the class?

Why: The class answers as a group (nosotros), so the ending is -amos: hablamos.

3. Which is a full negative answer to "¿Caminas a la escuela?"

Why: Answer no, then no before the verb, with the yo ending: no camino.
Section 3

A First Taste of Me Gusta

10.7

Me Gusta: I Like

Main ideaMe gusta plus a thing or an action means I like it: me gusta el fútbol, me gusta bailar.

At the sleepover, Lucía says, " este juego" (I like this game). Me gusta is how you say I like. It is a very useful phrase. It does not follow the hablo pattern, so learn it as a chunk.

Put a thing after it with el or la: "Me gusta el fútbol" (I like soccer). "Me gusta la pizza" (I like pizza). Or put an action after it, still ending in -ar: "Me gusta bailar" (I like to dance).

A common mistake is "Yo gusto el fútbol." It feels like English "I like," but in Spanish it is wrong. Stick with the chunk: me gusta. Leave the verb in its -ar form after it: me gusta cantar, not me gusta canto.

Read the numbers

Example survey of 24 students; each student could say yes to more than one

Words to know
me gusta
I like — me gusta el fútbol (I like soccer), me gusta bailar (I like to dance)
el fútbol
soccer — in most of the Spanish-speaking world, fútbol means soccer
Check yourself

1. How do you say "I like to sing"?

Why: Use the chunk me gusta, and keep the action in its -ar form: me gusta cantar.

2. What does "Me gusta el fútbol" mean?

Why: Me gusta means I like, and el fútbol means soccer.

3. Why is "Yo gusto la pizza" a mistake?

Why: Spanish does not say yo gusto for I like. The correct chunk is me gusta la pizza.
10.8

No Me Gusta

Main ideaTo say you don't like something, put no in front: no me gusta.

Ana does not want to sleep. She says, " dormir" (I don’t like to sleep). She put no in front of me gusta. It is the same idea as "no hablo." No goes first.

Watch the order: no, then me, then gusta. A common mistake is "Me no gusta." English speakers put not near the verb, so no ends up in the middle. In Spanish, no comes before the whole chunk.

You can answer with the double no too. "¿Te gusta el brócoli?" (Do you like broccoli?) "No, no me gusta." (No, I don’t like it.) The first no answers. The second one makes it negative.

Read the numbers

Words to know
no me gusta
I don't like — no me gusta dormir (I don't like to sleep)
dormir
to sleep — an -ir verb; after me gusta it stays in this form
Check yourself

1. Which sentence means "I don't like to dance"?

Why: No goes in front of the whole chunk: no me gusta.

2. What does "No me gusta dormir" mean?

Why: No me gusta means I don't like, and dormir means to sleep.

3. "¿Te gusta el brócoli?" Which answer means you do not like it?

Why: The first no answers the question, and the second no makes me gusta negative.
10.9

¿Te Gusta? Asking a Friend

Main ideaTo ask a friend if they like something, say ¿Te gusta…? and they answer with me gusta.

Lucía turns to Marisol. "¿ el juego?" (Do you like the game?) Te gusta means you like. With a rising voice and question marks, it asks do you like?

Here is the swap to remember. The question uses te, because it is about you. The answer uses me, because it is about me. "¿Te gusta cantar?" "Sí, me gusta cantar."

A common mistake is to answer "Sí, te gusta." That means yes, you like it, which is about the other person. Switch te to me when you answer about yourself. It is like switching hablas to hablo.

Think it through
Sleeping Bag Talk

Lucía: "¿Te gusta el juego?" (Do you like the game?) Marisol: "¡Sí, me gusta!" (Yes, I like it!) Lucía: "¿Te gusta dormir?" (Do you like to sleep?) Marisol: "No, no me gusta." (No, I don't like it.)

Ask a partner if they like to sing. Then answer their question about you.

Words to know
te gusta
you like (to a friend) — ¿Te gusta bailar? (Do you like to dance?)
el juego
the game — Me gusta este juego (I like this game)
Check yourself

1. How do you ask a friend "Do you like to dance?"

Why: Te gusta means you like; keep bailar in the -ar form, and use ¿ and ?.

2. Your friend asks, "¿Te gusta la pizza?" You like it. What do you say?

Why: Switch te to me when you answer about yourself: Sí, me gusta.

3. What does "¿Te gusta el juego?" mean?

Why: Te gusta means you like, and the question marks make it ask.
10.10

Playing Twenty Questions

Main ideaWith sí, no, rising questions and me gusta, you can play a whole guessing game in Spanish.

Now you have all the tools from the sleepover. You can ask with a rising voice and ¿ ?. You can answer sí or no. You can say no before a verb. You can talk about what you like.

Try the game. One person thinks of an animal. Others ask: "¿Es grande?" "¿Es pequeño?" (Is it small?) "¿Canta?" The answers must be full: "Sí, es pequeño." "No, no canta."

Keep count on your fingers. Twenty questions is the limit. When you are ready, guess with a question: "¿Es un gato?" (Is it a cat?) End with "¡Me gusta este juego!" if you had fun.

Think it through
A Round of the Game

Ana thinks of an animal. Lucía: "¿Es grande?" Ana: "No, no es grande." Marisol: "¿Es pequeño?" Ana: "Sí, es pequeño." Tomás: "¿Canta?" Ana: "Sí, canta." Lucía: "¿Es un pájaro?" (Is it a bird?) Ana: "¡Sí!"

Think of an animal. Write three yes-or-no questions a friend could ask to guess it.

Words to know
pequeño
small or little — ¿Es pequeño? (Is it small?); pequeña for a feminine noun
grande
big — the same form for masculine and feminine: el elefante grande, la casa grande
Check yourself

Why: The first no answers; no before es makes it negative: it is not big.

2. Which is a correctly written guess?

Why: The question starts with ¿ and ends with ?, around the whole question.

3. The animal is small and sings. Which question gets a sí?

Why: The animal sings, so the answer to ¿Canta? is Sí, canta.
