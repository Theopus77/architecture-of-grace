Two threads meet inside the cloth

Two layers of cloth, cut through. The needle brings the top thread down. The bobbin waits below the plate with the bottom thread.

That is a lockstitch: two threads knotted together inside the cloth. The knot sits in the middle when both threads pull evenly. Loose loops underneath mean the TOP thread never got its tension — not that the bobbin is broken.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Loose loops under the fabric mean the bobbin is the problem.

The mess shows up on the side OPPOSITE the fault. A nest of loops underneath is spare top thread that nothing held and nothing lifted — it missed the tension discs or the take-up lever. Raise the presser foot and rethread the top.

Loops below, look above. Loops above, look below.

⚠️ People often think…

You can thread the top with the presser foot down. The thread still reaches the needle.

With the foot down the two tension discs are shut, so the thread lies across them instead of dropping between them. It reaches the needle holding nothing back, and every stitch comes out slack underneath.

Foot up opens the discs. Thread with it up, every time.

⚠️ People often think…

Watch the needle to keep the seam straight.

The needle sews in one fixed place and cannot be steered. The width of the seam is set by where the cut EDGE of the fabric runs, so watch the edge against the 5/8 line on the throat plate.

Eyes on the edge and the line, not on the needle.

⚠️ People often think…

Sewing over a pin is fine as long as you go slowly.

Speed is not what decides it. The needle comes down on hardened steel and one of the two breaks, and it is never the pin. A piece of a broken needle can fly. Take the pins out as they reach the foot.

The pin wins. Pull each pin before it reaches the foot.

Helpful Hints

🧭 The unit in one line

Know the parts → wind a bobbin → thread the top in order → bring the bobbin thread up → foot down, backstitch, sew on the 5/8 line → pivot at the corner → backstitch again → and when something goes wrong, read the underside of the seam.

Off, up, up — spool, guide, tension, take-up, guide, needle.

🔑 The one idea

A machine stitch is two threads locked together inside the cloth: the top thread from the spool, the bottom thread from the bobbin. Almost everything you learn here — tension, the take-up lever, threading order, the look of a bad seam — is about keeping that lock in the middle.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • Threading with the presser foot DOWN. The tension discs are closed, the thread never drops in, and you get a nest of loops underneath.
  • Skipping the take-up lever. It is the one step almost everybody misses, and nothing pulls the stitch tight without it.
  • Watching the needle instead of the fabric edge. The needle sews where it sews; the width of the seam is set by where the EDGE runs.
  • Pulling the fabric to help it along. The feed dogs move the cloth. Pulling bends the needle and snaps it.
  • Sewing over pins. The needle hits the pin and one of them breaks. It is never the pin.
  • Blaming the bobbin for loops UNDER the fabric. Loops underneath are a top-thread fault; loops on TOP are the bobbin.
  • Changing a needle with the machine plugged in. Unplug it first, every time.
