Social Studies · Activity 6

Map of a Growing Nation

This unit happened somewhere. One region glows at a time — name it, and your answer stays on the map. By the end, the map you built is the map the whole argument happened on.

Show me one first

This one is nobody's answer — it is only here to show the moves before you touch yours. Watching it is instruction, never a trial, and it counts toward nothing.

The map glows on one block of land. Before reading any choice, say WHERE it is: middle of the country, top to bottom.
Now match direction to deal: France sold the MIDDLE. Mexico lost the SOUTHWEST. Britain shared the NORTHWEST.
A middle-of-the-map glow with an 1803 price tag is the Louisiana Purchase, every time.

Now number 1 is yours. The hint button is there the whole time, and using it is part of the lesson.

Place 1 of 8 One glow, one name.

Need help?

Word bank: the original thirteen states · the Louisiana Purchase · Florida · Texas · the Oregon Country · the Mexican Cession · the Mississippi River · the Erie Canal · the Missouri Compromise line · Washington, D.C. · Fort Sumter · Philadelphia. Write the right name after each clue.

  1. 1Clue: bought from France in 1803 — it doubled the country — name the place.
  2. 2Clue: the Atlantic coast states where the country began — name the place.
  3. 3Clue: divided peacefully with Britain in 1846 — name the place.
  4. 4Clue: won from Mexico in 1848 — California and the Southwest — name the place.
  5. 5Clue: an independent republic that joined the Union in 1845 — name the place.
  6. 6Clue: the great river highway down the middle of the country — name the place.
  7. 7Clue: the hand-dug waterway linking the Great Lakes to New York, 1825 — name the place.
  8. 8Clue: the capital city, built new for the new government — name the place.

Geography is not the background of history — it is one of its causes. Rivers decided where cities grew; new land kept re-asking the country its hardest question.
