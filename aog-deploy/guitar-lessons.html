Lesson 1 · Skill

The six strings

Tap a string at the far left, before the frets. It rings open. From thick to thin: E A D G B E.

  1. Play the low E string: the thickest one
  2. Play the A string
  3. Play the D string
  4. Play the G string
  5. Play the B string
  6. Play the high E string: the thinnest one

Try this: Say each name out loud as you play it: E, A, D, G, B, E.

Think about it

Which string sounds lowest? Which sounds highest? Why might that be?

Your turn

Make up a way to remember E A D G B E. For example: Eddie Ate Dynamite, Good Bye Eddie.

Lesson 2 · Skill

Frets make notes

Press a string on a fret and the note goes up. Each fret is one small step.

  1. Tap a string on any fret
  2. Press Higher ▶
  3. Play a note on fret 5 or higher
  4. Press ◀ Lower

Try this: Play the low E string on frets 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5. Hear it climb.

Think about it

The low E on fret 5 sounds just like another open string. Which one?

Your turn

Play a short tune on one string. Write the fret numbers in order.

Lesson 3 · Skill

Chord pads

A chord is a few notes together. Each pad strums one. Every pad fits the key.

  1. Tap a chord pad
  2. Play all six chord pads
  3. In the row of chords by the neck, play one of the last four
  4. Press ✋ Mute the strings

Try this: Tap pads 1, 4, 5, 1. That is how many songs go.

Think about it

Look at the neck when you tap a pad. What do the pale dots show?

Your turn

Pick the pad that sounds most like home to you. Write its name.

Lesson 4 · Skill

First chords: G, C and D

Three chords. Thousands of songs use just these.

  1. Set the Key to G
  2. Play a G chord
  3. Play a C chord
  4. Play a D chord

Try this: Play G, C, G, D. Then try it slower, with a steady count of four.

Think about it

Look at where the fingers go for G and for C. What is the same? What changes?

Your turn

Draw the G chord on six lines. Put a dot where each finger goes.

Lesson 5 · Skill

E, A, and two minor chords

A minor chord sounds softer and more thoughtful. Em and Am are easy ones to start with.

  1. Play an E chord
  2. Play an A chord
  3. Play an E minor chord (Em)
  4. Play an A minor chord (Am)

Try this: Play E, then Em. Only one note changes. Can you hear which?

Think about it

Tip: the key of G has Em and Am. The key of A has A and E. How did you find them?

Your turn

Write a four-chord pattern using only E, A, Em and Am.

Lesson 6 · Skill

Strum

Tap a chord, then swipe across the strip on the right. Every string you cross rings.

  1. Swipe down across the strings on the right
  2. Swipe back up across them
  3. Strum all six strings in one swipe

Try this: Strum down, down, up, down. Keep your hand moving like a pendulum.

Think about it

A fast swipe and a slow swipe sound different. How?

Your turn

Write a strum pattern with arrows, like ↓ ↓ ↑ ↓.

Lesson 7 · Skill

Chord patterns

A pattern is a row of chords, one for each bar. The page plays it for you.

  1. Pick a pattern in Start from a chord pattern
  2. Press ▶ Play the chords
  3. Move the Tempo slider
  4. Press ■ Stop

Try this: Watch the neck while it plays. The orange dots are the notes ringing now.

Think about it

What does the small number under each chord tell you?

Your turn

Name a pattern you liked and the tempo that felt best.

Lesson 8 · Skill

Ways to strum and pick

The same chords can be strummed or picked one string at a time. Pick a style in How the chords play.

  1. Play the chords with Down strums
  2. Play the chords with Folk
  3. Play the chords with Fingerpicking
  4. Play the chords with Travis picking

Try this: Keep one pattern and change only the style. Which one feels calm? Which one feels busy?

Think about it

In Travis picking, the thumb keeps the beat. Can you hear the low notes go back and forth?

Your turn

Pick a style for a sleepy song and one for a dancing song.

Lesson 9 · Skill

Keys, moods and the chord wheel

The key sets which chords fit. Major sounds bright. Minor sounds moody. The wheel shows every chord.

  1. Change the Key
  2. Press Minor · moody
  3. Tap a chord on the chord wheel
  4. Turn the wheel with one of its buttons
  5. Under Any chord, pick a kind and press ▶ Play this chord

Try this: Neighbours on the wheel sound good together. Play three chords side by side.

Think about it

When you turned the wheel, what happened to the six light chords?

Your turn

Choose a key and a mood for a song about the sea.

Lesson 10 · Skill

Amp and pedals

An electric guitar plays through an amp. Pedals change its sound: echo, crunch, wobble and more.

  1. Pick an electric guitar in Instrument
  2. Change a knob or a setting on the amp
  3. Turn a pedal on or off
  4. Move the Sound dial between 1987 and 2026

Try this: Turn on one pedal, play a chord, then turn it off. Name what it added.

Think about it

Which pedal would you use for a dreamy song? Which for a loud one?

Your turn

Make a sound you love. Write the amp and the pedals you used.

Lesson 11 · Skill

Power chords

A power chord is just the root and its fifth. It stays clear through a loud, heavy amp.

  1. Pick a Metal sound in Instrument
  2. With that sound, tap a chord: you hear a power chord
  3. Play the chords with Chug

Try this: Look at the neck: a power chord uses only two or three strings.

Think about it

Why might heavy music leave out the third of the chord?

Your turn

Write the three strings and frets of one power chord you played.

Lesson 12 · Skill

Hold + strum, like a real guitar

On the whole screen, one hand holds the frets and the other strums. A style can play the frets you hold, too.

  1. Turn your phone sideways, or press ⤢ Play on the whole screen
  2. In ☰ Menu, pick ✋ Hold + strum
  3. Hold a fret with one hand. Strum the strip with the other
  4. While the chords play, hold frets: the style plays them

Try this: Hold the shape the pale dots show, then strum. Change one finger and strum again.

Think about it

What happens when you lift your fretting finger?

Your turn

Find a shape of your own that sounds good. Draw it.

Lesson 13 · Skill

Solo mode and scales

Solo mode lights a scale on the neck. Every lit note fits, so you can play freely.

  1. Press Solo, above the neck
  2. Play eight lit notes
  3. Pick another Scale
  4. Press Chords to go back

Try this: Start and end your solo on a ringed note. That is the home note.

Think about it

How did the new scale change the mood?

Your turn

Write the frets of a five-note idea you liked.

Lesson 14 · Skill

Bends, slides and licks

Lead players make notes sing: they bend them and slide between them. Licks are short ideas to copy.

  1. In Solo mode, push a note across its string to bend it
  2. Slide a finger along a string to another fret
  3. Pick a lick and press ▶ Play this lick
  4. Press ▶ Play the band

Try this: Copy a lick slowly with the speed slider, then play it over the band.

Think about it

A bend sounds like a voice. What feeling can it show?

Your turn

Make up a lick with one bend and one slide. Write its frets.

Lesson 15 · Skill

Play with your drum beat

Make a beat on the drum machine and send it here. The guitar plays in time with it.

  1. Turn on Play with my drum beat
  2. Press ▶ Play the chords with your drum beat on

Try this: Try a funky drum beat with the Funk style.

Think about it

Why does the tempo now come from the drum beat?

Your turn

Name your drum beat and the guitar style that fits it best.

Lesson 16 · Skill

Record a take and share it

A take keeps everything you play. Send it to the Mixing Desk or the turntables.

  1. Press ● Record
  2. Play, then press ■ Stop to keep the take
  3. On your take, press Send to the Mixing Desk
  4. Press Send to the turntables

Try this: Record two takes of the same chords. Keep the one you like best.

Think about it

What changed between your first take and your second?

Your turn

Plan a 30-second take: what do you play first, and what comes next?

Lesson 18 · A song to make

Song: Blues shuffle

Twelve bars of blues, with a swinging shuffle on the low strings.

  1. Pick the pattern Blues · 12 bars

  3. Record a take while it plays

Try this: Try it in the key of A or E. Then solo over it with the Blues scale.
