Stars grew, stripes stayed

The flag has stripes — count them. Thirteen, for the first thirteen states.

Fifty STARS for the states today. Thirteen STRIPES for the first states. The stars grew as the country grew; the stripes stayed the same. A symbol lets you see a whole country at once.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

There are fifty stripes on the flag, one for each state.

The STARS count the states — fifty of those. The stripes stayed at thirteen, for the very first states. If someone added a stripe every time, the flag would be nothing but stripes.

Stars count states. Stripes stay thirteen.

⚠️ People often think…

The Pledge and the national anthem are the same thing.

One is SAID and one is SUNG. The Pledge is a promise in words; the anthem is a song about a flag still flying after a long night. Different words, different job.

The Pledge is said. The anthem is sung.

Worth knowing cold

Symbols you can see, things you say

Four to know. Notice which ones you look at and which ones you do with your voice.

  1. The flag Fifty stars, thirteen stripes. You look at it. Every state today has a star; the stripes remember the beginning.
  2. The Pledge A promise you SAY. Words, standing up, hand on your heart.
  3. The anthem A song you SING. Different from the Pledge — one is said and one is sung.
  4. The eagle and the monuments A bald eagle, the Statue of Liberty, the Lincoln Memorial. Each one is a picture of an idea you cannot photograph.

Symbols you can see, stories you can tell

1 Watch one

A new state joins. What gets added to the flag?

  1. Ask which part counts the states: the stars.
  2. Ask what the stripes count: the FIRST thirteen states, way back at the start.
  3. A new state today does not change what happened at the start.
  4. So: one more star, and the stripes stay at thirteen. ✓

Helpful Hints

🪜 Symbols and stories — the ladder

A symbol is a small thing that stands for something big → the flag: 50 stars for 50 states, 13 stripes for the first 13 → the eagle, the Statue of Liberty and the Liberty Bell stand for our country and for freedom → the Pledge is a promise: freedom and fairness for all → the holidays remember stories: the country’s birthday in 1776, the Pilgrims’ feast, Washington, Lincoln, Dr. King.

Symbols you can see, stories you can tell.

🤝 The whole idea

A country is too big to see all at once. Its symbols let you see it, and its stories tell you why it matters.

See it in the symbol. Hear it in the story.

⚠️ Tricky ones

  • 50 STARS for the states today. 13 STRIPES for the first states. The stripes never changed; the stars grew.
  • The Pledge is a promise you SAY. The anthem is a song you SING.
  • Washington was the FIRST president. Lincoln came later — and he was from Illinois.
  • Memorial Day (May) remembers soldiers who died. Veterans Day (November) thanks the ones still living.
  • The bald eagle is the COUNTRY’s bird. The cardinal is ILLINOIS’s bird.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A picture of the flag — “color the stripes and say how many stars.”
  • Four pictures — “which one is the Statue of Liberty?”
  • “What do we celebrate on the Fourth of July?” (the country’s birthday).
  • “Who was the first president?” / “Which president was from Illinois?”
  • “What does the Pledge promise?” (liberty and justice for all).
