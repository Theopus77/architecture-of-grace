Abraham, Sarah and Their Family

Bereshit Stories

This unit is about one family. The Torah says God told Abraham and Sarah to leave home. They went to a new land. Their family grew. Isaac and Rebekah came next. Then Jacob, Leah and Rachel. Then Joseph and his brothers.

Jewish tradition calls Abraham and Sarah the first of its family. Jewish families read these stories every year. In this unit we learn what the Torah says. We learn what the stories mean to the people who read them. We do not decide if they are true. We listen and we learn.

By the end, you will be able to tell how Abraham and Sarah welcomed guests. You will know Jacob's dream and his new name. You will know how Joseph forgave his brothers. And you will know how the twelve tribes got their names.

The big question

What do the Torah's stories of Abraham's family say about promises, kindness and forgiving?

Big questionWhat does the Torah say Abraham and Sarah did when they left home, and how did they treat people?
The story

Three Travelers in the Hot Sun

It is the hottest part of the day, and an old man runs to greet three strangers.

The sun is high. The sand is hot. An old man named Abraham sits at the door of his tent. He is resting in the shade. The Torah says he looks up. Three travelers are standing there.

Abraham does not wait. The Torah says he runs to them. He bows low. "Please stay," he says. "Rest under the tree. Let me bring water for your feet. Let me bring you bread."

Abraham hurries to the tent. "Sarah," he says, "quick, make bread!" Then he runs to the animals. He picks a calf for the meal. He brings milk and cheese too. He stands by the travelers while they eat.

The travelers have news. They say, "Next year, Sarah will have a son." Sarah is listening at the tent door. She laughs. She is very old. Can this be true? Jewish tradition tells this story every year.

Talk about itAbraham ran to help strangers on a hot day. What do you do when a guest comes to your home?
Section 1

Leaving Home

3.1

Go to a New Land

Main ideaThe Torah says God told Abraham and Sarah to leave home and go to a new land, and they went.

Abraham and Sarah lived in a place far away. Their first names were Abram and Sarai. The Torah says God spoke to Abram. God said, "Leave your land. Go to a land I will show you."

Abram did not know where he was going. He went anyway. Sarai went with him. So did his nephew Lot. They took their tents and their sheep. They walked a long way to a new .

The new land was called Canaan. Jewish tradition says this is the land of Israel. Abraham and Sarah lived there in tents. They moved from place to place.

Now the LORD said unto Abram: 'Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father's house, unto the land that I will show thee.
Genesis (Bereshit) 12:1, Jewish Publication Society translation, 1917

"Get thee out" is an old way to say "go." What three things does the story say Abram had to leave?

3.2

A Promise of a Big Family

Main ideaThe Torah says God promised Abraham and Sarah a very big family.

Abraham and Sarah had no children. They were getting old. The Torah says God made them a . God said, "Your family will be big. It will be as many as the stars."

The story says God told Abram to look up at the night sky. Try to count the stars! You cannot. That is how big the family would be.

God gave them new names, the story says. Abram became Abraham. Sarai became Sarah. Jewish tradition says the new names were part of the promise.

The Open Tent

3.3

Three Visitors

Main ideaThe Torah says Abraham ran to feed three strangers who came to his tent.

One hot day, three travelers came by. Abraham did not know them. The Torah says he ran to meet them. He bowed. He asked them to rest and eat.

Abraham and Sarah made a big meal. Bread, milk, cheese and meat. Abraham did not sit down. He stood by his while they ate.

The guests had a message. Sarah would have a baby boy. Sarah laughed. She was very old. Jewish tradition says her laugh became the baby’s name.

3.4

Kindness to Strangers

Main ideaJewish tradition says Abraham's tent was open on all sides so every guest could come in.

A is a person you do not know. Abraham did not know the three men. He helped them anyway. Jewish tradition says this is a big lesson.

An old Jewish story says Abraham’s tent was open on all four sides. A guest could walk in from any way. No one had to go around. The story is a . A midrash is a story that adds to the Torah.

Jewish families still welcome guests. On Shabbat, many families invite people to eat. They call it welcoming guests. It is a way to be like Abraham and Sarah.

Isaac Is Born

Main ideaThe Torah says Sarah had a son named Isaac, and his name means "he laughs."

The Torah says the promise came true. Sarah had a baby boy. They named him . In Hebrew, his name is Yitzchak. It means "he laughs."

Sarah said, "God has made laughter for me." Everyone who heard the news would laugh with her. Abraham and Sarah were very old. The story says Abraham was one hundred.

Jewish families read this story on Rosh Hashanah. That is the Jewish New Year. They hear how Isaac was born.

3.6

Rebekah at the Well

Main ideaThe Torah says Rebekah gave water to a stranger and his camels, and she became Isaac's wife.

Isaac grew up. Abraham sent a helper to find Isaac a wife. The helper took ten . He stopped at a well. A well is a deep hole with water in it.

A girl named Rebekah came to the well. The helper asked for a drink. Rebekah gave him water. Then she said, "I will get water for your camels too." Ten camels drink a lot!

The helper knew she was kind. Rebekah went with him. She married Isaac. Jewish tradition says Rebekah was kind like Abraham.

The story

A Ladder to the Sky

A young man sleeps on a stone and dreams of a ladder that goes all the way up.

It is night. A young man named Jacob is far from home. He is alone. He has no bed. The Torah says he puts a stone under his head. He lies down on the ground. He falls asleep.

Jacob dreams. In the dream, there is a ladder. Its bottom is on the earth. Its top reaches the sky. Angels are going up the ladder. Angels are coming down. Up and down, up and down.

In the dream, the story says, God speaks to Jacob. "I am with you. I will keep you safe. I will bring you back to this land." It is the same promise God made to Abraham.

Jacob wakes up. He is amazed. "God was here," he says, "and I did not know it!" He sets up the stone. He names the place Beth-el. That means "house of God."

Talk about itJacob was alone and far from home. How do you think the dream made him feel?
Section 1

Two Brothers

4.1

Jacob and Esau

Main ideaThe Torah says Isaac and Rebekah had twin sons, Jacob and Esau, who did not get along.

Isaac and Rebekah had . Twins are two babies born together. The first was Esau. He was red and hairy. The second was Jacob. He was holding Esau’s heel.

The boys grew up very different. Esau liked to hunt outside. Jacob liked to stay near the tents. The Torah says the brothers had a big fight over a blessing.

Esau was angry. Jacob had to leave home. He went far away to his uncle Laban. He did not see his brother for a long, long time.

Jacob woke up. He named the place Beth-el. Beth-el means "house of God." Jewish tradition says the dream showed Jacob he was not alone.

Rachel and Leah

Main ideaThe Torah says Jacob worked many years for Laban and married both Leah and Rachel.

Jacob came to his uncle Laban. Laban had two daughters. Leah was the older one. was the younger one. Jacob met Rachel at a well. He loved her.

Jacob said, "I will work for you seven years. Then let me marry Rachel." Laban said yes. But at the wedding, Laban gave him instead. Jacob was tricked!

Jacob worked seven more years. Then he married Rachel too. In those days, a man could have more than one wife. Both Leah and Rachel are called mothers of the family.

Twelve Sons and Dinah

Main ideaThe Torah says Jacob had twelve sons and a daughter, and the twelve tribes are named for his sons.

Jacob had a very big family. The Torah says he had twelve sons. He also had a daughter. Her name was Dinah. Leah and Rachel were mothers of the family.

Some sons were Reuben, Judah, Levi and Joseph. Joseph was Rachel’s son. Rachel’s other son was Benjamin. He was the youngest of all.

Later, the family grew into a people. Jewish tradition says the people had twelve . A tribe is a very big family group. The tribes were named for Jacob’s sons.

Joseph

The Coat and the Dreams

Main ideaThe Torah says Jacob gave Joseph a special coat, and Joseph's dreams made his brothers jealous.

Jacob loved Joseph very much. The Torah says he gave Joseph a special . His brothers did not get one. They were . To be jealous is to want what someone else has.

One day the brothers were far away with the sheep. Jacob sent Joseph to check on them. The brothers saw him coming in his coat.

4.8

Brothers Together Again

Main ideaThe Torah says Joseph forgave his brothers, and the whole family moved to Egypt.

The hungry years came. Jacob’s family had no food. The brothers went to Egypt to buy some. They stood in front of Joseph. They did not know him. He knew them.

Joseph tested them. Then he could not hold back. He cried. "I am Joseph!" he said. "Is my father still alive?" The brothers were scared. But Joseph said, "Do not be afraid."

Joseph forgave his brothers. To forgive is to stop being angry at someone. The whole family moved to Egypt. Jacob saw Joseph again. Jewish tradition says forgiving is one of the big lessons of this story.
