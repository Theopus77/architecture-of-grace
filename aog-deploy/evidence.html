Architecture of Grace · Program integrity

Evidence & Roadmap

What this program is built on, what has not been proven yet, and the specific path from one to the other — written down so a district can hold us to it.

Who this is for. Curriculum directors, school psychologists, review committees, and anyone deciding whether to put this in front of children.

What is research-grounded

Architecture of Grace is built on a published research base, and the books cite it in full. The load-bearing foundations:

Self-compassion — Kristin Neff
The three-element model (self-kindness, common humanity, mindfulness) structures the self-compassion units in every band, explicitly distinguished from self-esteem. The 6–8 book draws on Neff (2003a, 2003b, 2011) and Neff & Germer's Mindful Self-Compassion work.
Forgiveness as a teachable skill — Enright & Fitzgibbons; Worthington
Enright and Fitzgibbons' forgiveness-therapy research defines what forgiveness is not — reconciliation, condoning, forgetting — and that definition is the spine of every forgiveness unit. Worthington's REACH model supplies the process framing. The curriculum's most repeated safety line — forgiveness is never required in situations involving ongoing harm — comes directly out of this literature's boundaries.
Shame vs. guilt — Brown; Tangney and colleagues
The distinction between "I am bad" and "I did something bad," and why shame produces hiding rather than repair, grounds the mistake-and-repair strand from K–2 upward.
Trauma-informed practice and safe messaging
The delivery model — teacher regulation first, disclosure protocols in every lesson, counselor-in-room requirements for the highest-sensitivity lessons — was built to trauma-informed guidelines, and the AFSP Safe Messaging Guidelines are applied by name to every highest-risk lesson.
The honest line, which this site holds everywhere: grounded in research is not the same as evidence of effectiveness. Neff's findings are evidence about self-compassion; they are not evidence about this curriculum. That evidence does not exist yet, and this page is the plan for getting it.

What has not been done

Stated plainly, so no one has to go looking for it:

  • No efficacy study has been conducted. No cohort of students using Architecture of Grace has been compared with a cohort not using it, by anyone. Any claim that the program "works," in the outcome-study sense, would currently be unsupported — so we do not make it.
  • The self-reflection instrument has not been through external reliability or validity testing. It was designed against published constructs, but no independent psychometrician has evaluated whether its items measure what they intend to measure, or measure it consistently across ages, languages, and populations.
  • Because of both facts, every score the instrument produces is a flag for a conversation — never a diagnosis, never a screening result in the clinical sense, and never a basis for a placement or eligibility decision. The curriculum, not the number, is the intervention. This limitation is printed where the scores appear, not only here.

The validation roadmap

STAGE 1

Pilot classrooms

The entire curriculum is published open — every band K–12, the lessons, the workbooks and the materials — specifically so a school can pilot with no cost and no procurement cycle. A pilot classroom contributes three things, each already supported by the program's own tooling:

  • Fidelity logs — what was delivered, what was deferred, and under what staffing (see the flagged-lesson planning sheet). Deferral records are data, not embarrassment.
  • Item-level instrument data — with district permission and under the district's own data governance; the data lives in the school's own Google Sheet either way.
  • Teacher and family feedback — what held up in a real room and what did not.
STAGE 2

External psychometric review of the instrument

With pilot item-level data in hand, the self-reflection instrument goes to independent psychometric review — reliability, construct validity, and performance across grade bands and demographic groups — conducted by reviewers with no stake in the result. Findings will be published here whichever way they point, and the instrument revised or retired accordingly.

STAGE 3

Outcome evaluation

Only after the instrument survives Stage 2 does an efficacy study make sense — a validated measure is a precondition for one, not a product of one. Design and partners for that study will be named here when they actually exist, and not before.

The commitment: Architecture of Grace will not describe itself as "evidence-based," "proven," or "validated" before the evidence exists — not in marketing, not in a grant application, not in a sales conversation. If you ever find language on this site that gets ahead of this page, that is a bug; report it and it will be corrected.

No pilot sites, review partners, or timelines are announced here yet, because announcing names before agreements exist is the same overclaim in a different suit.

Clinical review

The safety architecture — disclosure protocols, counselor-in-room requirements, crisis-resource posting, and the scheduling rules around breaks and substitutes — was built to published guidelines, including the AFSP Safe Messaging Guidelines, which every band applies by name to its flagged lessons — the ★ and ★★ chapters that carry the safety protocols. The books also require each adopting school's own psychologist or counselor to review the most sensitive units and confirm clinical readiness in writing before delivery.

External clinician review is invited. If you are a school psychologist, counselor, clinical social worker, or child/adolescent clinician willing to review any part of the safety protocols or lesson content, the full materials will be provided for that purpose, and substantive critique will be acted on. Continuity — the one-person question

Architecture of Grace is the work of one practicing educator. A district is right to ask what happens to its adoption if that one person stops — so the program is built to survive him, structurally, not just intentionally:

  • The site is static files. There is no server, no database, no account system. A school or district may mirror the site on its own infrastructure, and it runs identically there.
  • All student data lives in school-owned Google Sheets. The sync backend is a script inside your own Google account, writing to your own Sheet. It keeps working — collecting, storing, exporting — regardless of whether this website is ever reachable again.
  • Printed books keep working. The curriculum is delivered from books and printed materials; nothing in a lesson depends on the site being up.
  • The open curriculum stays open. Every band, K–12. Its publication is a standing commitment, not a promotion — nothing readable today becomes paid tomorrow.

The plain statement: a district's adoption does not depend on a company's uptime, and no future change of ownership can reach into a school's Sheets, its printed books, or its mirrored copy of the site.
