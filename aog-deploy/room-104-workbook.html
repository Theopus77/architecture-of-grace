Unit 1 · The Curated Self

The performance tax. The social battery. The integrity anchor. The authenticity gradient. Preference falsification and the Dissent Toolkit. Signal vs. noise. This unit practiced measuring the gap between the self you perform and the self that is true — and what that gap costs.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 The performance tax is —
B. PERFORMANCE TAX = gap width × time maintained. The curated self is filtered, selected, performed for an audience. The authentic self is unfiltered, private, costly to reveal. The wider the gap and the longer you hold it, the more it costs.
2 Your social battery charges when —
A. The battery concept is not about shy vs. outgoing. It is about what charges you and what drains you. Deep 1:1 conversations charge many people; sustained surface-level performance drains them. Knowing yours stops you from misreading other people’s.
3 A rule is imposed from outside and holds because of consequences. An integrity anchor —
C. An anchor holds quietly. It does not announce itself. It does not require others to change. It simply tells you where your own line is. The Anchor Test: does this hold when nobody would know?
4 On the Authenticity Gradient, choosing what to share — not the whole truth, but not a lie — is —
A. SELECTIVE sharing is adaptation — you read the context and choose. CURATED is actively managing an image. INAUTHENTIC is a false identity with a high performance tax. The question is whether you are adapting or deleting.
5 Preference falsification means —
D. When everyone falsifies, the group believes in a consensus nobody actually holds — false consensus. The Dissent Toolkit gives you moves that do not require confrontation: the honest question, the private disclosure, the public “I see it differently.”
6 The signal is —
B. The SIGNAL is real information about something true. The NOISE is anxiety, social comparison, catastrophizing, external validation. The Signal-vs-Noise Pause: pause, breathe, ask the Internal Coach what is actually true here.
7 The Identity Shield is built from —
A. The Unit 1 reflection puts every chart on the wall at once and asks you to synthesize: what did you actually build? The Shield holds core values, authentic strengths, non-negotiables and the adult you are becoming — and the journal holds one carry-forward sentence into Unit 2.
8 Which one is a Dissent Toolkit move?
C. Quiet dissent is social courage without confrontation. The honest question, the private disclosure, and the public disagreement stated plainly all break the false consensus without requiring a fight.

Unit 2 · Resilience Through Self-Kindness

Perfectionism vs. healthy striving. The Not Yet lens. Accountability vs. self-punishment. The three chapters. The Restoration Test. The Compassionate Witness and the Friend Test. The Backpack Audit. This unit practiced being honest, fair, and kind to yourself — at the same time.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 High standards are motivated by growth and recover from failure. Perfectionism is —
D. HIGH STANDARDS: motivated by growth, adjusts and continues. PERFECTIONISM: motivated by fear, avoids starting, mistake = worth. PARALYSIS: nothing attempted — safety through non-trying. Perfectionism is a defense, not a motivator.
2 “I failed. I cannot do this.” The Not Yet lens says —
B. FIXED framing closes the door. NOT YET framing treats failure as data: diagnose what happened, name the gap, decide what comes next. Effort and strategy, not verdicts.
3 Accountability is honest acknowledgment, genuine repair, and a commitment to a different choice. Self-punishment is —
A. Self-punishment repeats the condemnation and produces no repair. Accountability produces a living amend — sustained changed behavior — rather than another apology. Guilt points at the act; shame attacks the self.
4 Chapter 1 is what happened. Chapter 2 is what it meant. Chapter 3 is —
D. Chapter 1 — the facts — is not yours to revise. Chapter 2 — the story you told yourself — may need examining. Chapter 3 is the only chapter you are actually writing.
5 The Restoration Test asks —
C. Genuine self-care passes the Restoration Test. Avoidance temporarily numbs and leaves you less capable — the cost is deferred, not canceled. Same activity can be either; the test is what it leaves behind.
6 The Friend Test compares —
A. Left bubble: what I would say to my best friend — honest, fair, kind, without punishment. Right bubble: what I actually say to myself. The gap between them is the whole lesson. The Compassionate Witness closes it.
7 The Compassionate Witness is —
B. Not softer than the Critic — more accurate. The Witness tells the truth about what happened, is fair about what it means, and is kind about what comes next. All three, or it is not the Witness.
8 In the Backpack Audit, a stone is —
D. The audit is an inventory, not a purge. Each stone gets examined — honestly, fairly, kindly — and then you make a deliberate choice: put it down before Unit 3, or carry it forward on purpose.

Unit 3 · Conflict & Context

Conflict as data. Forgiveness vs. reconciliation. Boundary, wall, open door. Calling in vs. calling out. Contextual empathy. The Grace Challenge. This unit practiced handling other people’s harm without either excusing it or being ruled by it.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 Conflict as data means conflict signals —
B. Conflict is information, not evidence of failure. Fight, flight, freeze, and fawn are the four patterns; each one is a response to a signal. Reading the signal is the reframe.
2 Forgiveness is an internal act. Reconciliation is —
A. FORGIVENESS: available without the other person; does not require them to change. RECONCILIATION: requires the other person, changed behavior, and safety. You can forgive without reconciling. You can reconcile without fully forgiving. Two decisions.
3 “What I need to stay in relationship with you — clear, specific, from self-respect.” That is —
C. A BOUNDARY is what you need to stay in relationship. A WALL is total closure from fear — it protects but isolates. An OPEN DOOR has no conditions and no self-protection — not a boundary, a fawn pattern.
4 Calling in is private, direct, and assumes good faith. Calling out is —
A. Calling in preserves the relationship and repairs a single incident. Calling out names an established pattern publicly. The question that decides: is this a reachable person and a specific harm, or an entrenched pattern that private repair cannot address?
5 Contextual empathy is —
D. Empathy is not agreement and not sympathy. The fuller picture of a person explains their behavior; it does not excuse it or erase consequences. Humanizing is not the same as absolving.
6 Grace, on the Unit 3 chart, is —
B. Grace is the active extension — chosen, deliberate, beyond what was earned. It is not passivity, not ignoring harm, not required everywhere. The Grace Challenge makes it a daily practice you can track.
7 Fawn, as a conflict pattern, looks like —
A. Fight, flight, freeze — and fawn: appeasing to stay safe. Fawn is the pattern most often mistaken for kindness. The open door on the boundary chart is usually fawn in disguise.
8 The Grace Audit at the end of Unit 3 asks —
C. Unit 3 closes by putting every chart back on the wall and asking what shifted — not how much you forgave, but what the distinction between forgiveness and reconciliation let you carry differently into Unit 4.

Unit 4 · Community & Legacy

Grace culture vs. performance culture. Charitable interpretation. Bearing witness. Grace under pressure and the Grace Recovery Protocol. The community Grace Audit. The orchard frame. This unit practiced grace at the scale of a room — and asked what you are leaving in it.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 In a grace culture, the default assumption is —
D. GRACE CULTURE: default assumption is good faith; repair is expected; mistakes produce learning. PERFORMANCE CULTURE: default is evaluation; mistakes generate punishment; only some people speak. Both are contagious.
2 Charitable interpretation applies when —
B. The generous reading is for ambiguity and single data points. When a pattern is established and the evidence is clear, you recalibrate — that is not being uncharitable, it is being accurate. Charitable interpretation is not naivety.
3 Bearing witness is —
A. BEING WITH tolerates discomfort and stays. DOING FOR solves, advises, fills the silence, avoids the discomfort. The four skills of witnessed presence are practiced in silence as much as speech.
4 The Grace Recovery Protocol is —
D. Notice the collapse, pause one breath, choose the smallest grace move. Then the repair sentence: “I wasn’t at my best there. I want to try that again.” Sustained grace means the pressure was anticipated and the anchor named in advance.
5 A community Grace Audit looks at —
C. Default assumption in the room. How repair happens. What mistakes produce. Who is included in decisions. The audit maps the assets and deficits — then you pick one leverage point you can sustain, not the whole room.
6 The orchard frame says —
A. Legacy is not what you receive. It is what you leave. The Grace Legacy Statement names who you are becoming, what that becoming asks of you, and what will be different in the rooms you enter.
7 Performing empathy differs from bearing witness because performing empathy —
B. Performed empathy has an audience — even if the audience is yourself. Witness has none. The witness sentence is short, and it hands the moment back to the person who is in it.
8 “I had no choice” is —
D. When values are abandoned under pressure, the story that follows is “I had no choice.” The Protocol answers that story: notice it, pause, choose the smallest grace move — then the repair sentence. Sustained grace prepares the response before the pressure arrives.

Year-End · Integration

The permanent charts, carried out the door: the performance tax, the integrity anchor, the Internal Coach, forgiveness as an internal act, reconciliation as a relational act. Room 104 learned to look up — from the phone, from the performance, from the story — and see what was actually there.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 Which charts are PERMANENT — up all year?
B. Five charts never come down. They are the spine: what performance costs, where your line is, whose voice to listen to, and the two separate decisions about harm.
2 The Internal Coach is —
A. The Coach is the voice you hear after the Signal-vs-Noise Pause. It is the same voice as the Compassionate Witness — the Friend Test turned inward. Unit 1 introduces it; Unit 2 trains it.
3 The two separate decisions when someone harms you are —
C. This is the Room 104 distinction that outlasts the year. Forgiveness never requires access. Reconciliation is earned. Boundaries are how the second decision is enforced.
4 A living amend is —
A. Accountability without self-punishment produces a living amend: not another apology, but a pattern that is different. It is the Unit 2 tool that Room 207 builds on in Book 5.
5 The year’s last exit ticket asks —
D. “The most important shift in me since August. The tool I will still be using at 25. What this class built together. The sentence I am carrying forward.” The Class Legacy Chart is those sentences, side by side.
