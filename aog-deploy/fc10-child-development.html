A milestone is a range, not a date

One line, one tick, one child standing exactly on it. That is how a milestone gets pictured.

A milestone is a range of months, not a date. Children move through the four domains — the body, thinking, words, and feelings and people — at their own rates. A child can be early in one domain and later in another with nothing wrong.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

A child who is not walking at twelve months is behind.

Most children walk alone somewhere between about nine and fifteen months. Twelve months sits in the middle of that window, not at the end of it. A milestone age is the age by which most children show a skill, and most is not all.

Ranges, not deadlines.

⚠️ People often think…

A two-year-old screaming on the floor is misbehaving.

A two-year-old wants a great deal, can do some of it, and can say almost none of it. That gap comes out as a tantrum. It is normal, it is loudest between about one and three, and it is not manipulation. Stay calm, stay nearby, keep everyone safe, and talk about it after.

A tantrum at two is a stage, not a choice.

⚠️ People often think…

Two-year-olds who play side by side and never share are being selfish.

That is parallel play — the same toys, side by side, separate games — and it is typical around two. One shared game with roles is cooperative play, and it is typical around four. Egocentrism, seeing the world from one point of view, is a stage of thinking and not a fault in the child.

Parallel at two, cooperative at four.

⚠️ People often think…

If a child is slower than her cousin, someone should tell the family what is wrong.

Rates differ, and one cousin is not a measure of anything. Describe what you actually see, in plain words, and keep talking, reading and playing with the child. Whether something is wrong is never a caregiver’s call — that question belongs with the child’s doctor, who knows the whole child.

Describe what you see. The question belongs with the child’s doctor.

Tomás is two. His block tower falls and he drops to the floor screaming. What is happening, and what do you do?

  5. Stay near, stay calm, name the feeling. Talk about it after, not during.

Helpful Hints

🧭 The unit in one line

A child grows in four domains at once — body, thinking, words, feelings and people → the order of the steps is steady, the timing is a window → play is how the learning happens → the adult’s job is to answer, to guide rather than punish, and to keep the child safe.

Body, thinking, words, people. Ranges, not deadlines.

🤝 The one idea

A young child is not a small adult waiting to start. She is already doing the work of her age — and almost everything an adult calls a problem at two or three is that work, done loudly. Notice the child in front of you, and answer.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • A milestone age is a window of months, not a due date. A child who arrives later is usually just that child.
  • You are not the one who decides whether something is wrong. If a family is worried, the question belongs with the child’s doctor.
  • Egocentrism is a stage of thinking, not selfishness. A three-year-old is not being rude when she cannot picture your view.
  • Parallel play is not a failure to share, and a tantrum at two is not manipulation. Both are normal for the age.
  • Toilet learning is a readiness, not a deadline. Pushing a child who is not ready usually costs months.
  • Never shake a baby, not once, not gently. If you feel your patience going, put her down on her back in the crib and step away.
  • Supervision near water means within arm’s reach with your eyes on the child. A phone in your hand is not supervision.

  • “Name the four domains, and show all four in one minute of play.”
  • A behavior at an age — “is this typical, and why?”
  • Two responses to a tantrum — “which is guidance and which is punishment?”
  • “Put these milestones in the order they usually appear.”
  • “You are babysitting tonight. What must you have before the parents leave?”
