Lesson 1 · Skill

Meet the band

Every note here is a real player, recorded. Tap a key for one note, or a pad for a whole chord.

  1. Play any key on the keys
  2. Hold a chord pad
  3. Pick another instrument in the Instrument menu

Try this: Hold a pad and listen until the sound fades away.

Think about it

Which instrument did you pick? What does it sound like to you?

Your turn

Name three instruments you would like to hear in your band.

Lesson 2 · Skill

The brass family

Brass players buzz their lips into a metal mouthpiece. The sound is bright and bold.

  1. Play the trumpet
  2. Play the trombone
  3. Play the French horn
  4. Play the brass section, all four

Try this: Play the same chord pad on each one. Which sounds warmest?

Think about it

The brass section has four players. Which one plays the lowest note?

Your turn

Draw a trumpet. Mark where the air goes in and where the sound comes out.

Lesson 3 · Skill

The woodwind family

Woodwinds blow across a hole or through a reed. Their sound is soft and airy, or sweet and reedy.

  1. Play the flute
  2. Play the clarinet
  3. Play the oboe
  4. Play the woodwinds, all four

Try this: Play a slow tune on the flute, then the same tune on the oboe.

Think about it

The flute has no reed. Can you hear a difference from the clarinet?

Your turn

Pick a woodwind for a bird in a story. Write why.

Lesson 4 · Skill

The string family

Strings sing when a bow moves across them. Small ones sing high; big ones sing low.

  1. Play the violins
  2. Play the cellos
  3. Play the double bass
  4. Play the string section, all four

Try this: Hold one pad with the string section. Listen for the low bass under the violins.

Think about it

Which is bigger, a violin or a cello? Which sounds lower?

Your turn

Put the four strings in order from smallest to biggest.

Lesson 5 · Skill

The percussion family

Percussion is struck: drums, bars and bells. Some play notes; some keep the beat.

  1. Play the timpani
  2. Play the marimba, the xylophone or the glockenspiel
  3. Pick a chord pattern and press ▶ Play the chords with the percussion section

Try this: Play the glockenspiel up high. It sounds like tiny bells.

Think about it

Which percussion sounds play notes? Which only keep the beat?

Your turn

Find three things at home you can tap to make a beat.

Lesson 6 · Skill

High and low: tuba and piccolo

The tuba is the lowest voice in the band. The piccolo is the highest.

  1. Play the tuba's lowest notes, at the left end of the keys
  2. Play the piccolo's highest notes, at the right end of the keys
  3. Press ◀ Lower
  4. Press Higher ▶

Try this: Play the same key on both. Then move the keys to find each one's lowest note.

Think about it

Why does a big instrument sound lower than a small one?

Your turn

Name an animal for the tuba and one for the piccolo.

Lesson 7 · Skill

Plucked and bowed

A bow makes a string sing long. A finger plucks it short: that is pizzicato.

  1. Play violins, violas, cellos or double bass with the bow
  2. Play a plucked string sound (it says “plucked”)
  3. Play the harp

Try this: Play the same chord with the violins, then with the plucked violins.

Think about it

Does a plucked note keep going, or fade away?

Your turn

Pluck a rubber band. Is it long or short? High or low?

Lesson 8 · Skill

Vibrato and mutes

Vibrato is a gentle wobble in a long note. A mute is a cap in the bell that makes the sound softer.

  1. Play the trumpet, trombone, flute, oboe or bassoon
  2. Pick one “with vibrato” and hold a key for two seconds
  3. Play a muted sound (it says “mute”)

Try this: Hold one note on the flute, then on the flute with vibrato. Hear the wobble?

Think about it

Singers use vibrato too. Can you think of a song where you hear it?

Your turn

Hum one long note. Now hum it with a small wobble.

Lesson 9 · Skill

Short and long notes

Pick a pattern and the band plays it. Long notes are smooth. Stabs are short and punchy.

  1. Pick a chord pattern
  2. Press ▶ Play the chords
  3. Play it with Long notes
  4. Play it with Stabs · short and punchy
  5. Press ■ Stop

Try this: Keep it playing and change How the band plays. It changes right away.

Think about it

Which feels calm, long or short notes? Which feels like dancing?

Your turn

Clap a long sound, then four short ones. Write them as lines and dots.

Lesson 10 · Skill

Soft, loud and the swell

Where you tap a key sets how loud it is. A swell grows from soft to loud.

  1. Tap a key near its top: a soft note
  2. Tap a key near its bottom: a loud note
  3. Play it with Swell · soft to loud

Try this: Play the swell with the strings, then with the brass. Which feels bigger?

Think about it

Where in a film might you hear a big swell?

Your turn

Say a word softly, then louder and louder. That is a swell.

Lesson 11 · Skill

Chord pads and keys

Six pads, six chords that fit together. Major sounds bright. Minor sounds moody.

  1. Play all six chord pads
  2. Press Minor · moody
  3. Change the Key

Try this: On a computer, keys 1 to 6 play the pads. Make up an order and play it again.

Think about it

Play pad 1 in Major, then in Minor. How does each one feel?

Your turn

Write the four pads you like best, in order.

Lesson 12 · Skill

A tune on the keys

One key, one note. Pick a scale and the keys show which notes fit.

  1. Play five different keys
  2. Pick a scale in the Scale menu
  3. Play three notes that have the scale's dot

Try this: Play only the dotted keys, going up, then coming down.

Think about it

Is your tune happy, calm or sad? What makes it sound that way?

Your turn

Write your tune as note names, like C D E C.

Lesson 13 · Skill

Your own chord pattern

Make my own lets you choose the chords. Tap pads or the wheel in the order you want.

  1. Press Make my own
  2. Tap four chords to build your pattern
  3. Tap a chord on the chord wheel
  4. Play your own pattern

Try this: Start and end your pattern on pad 1. It feels like coming home.

Think about it

Which chord in your pattern surprised you?

Your turn

Write your pattern as pad numbers, like 1 4 5 1.

Lesson 14 · Skill

Tempo and the band's rhythms

Tempo is how fast. How the band plays sets the rhythm: a march, a waltz and more.

  1. Move the Tempo slider
  2. Play it with March · oom-pah
  3. Play it with Waltz · three beats
  4. Play it with Off-beat · ska and reggae
  5. Play it with Broken · one note at a time

Try this: A waltz counts 1 2 3. Count along out loud.

Think about it

Which rhythm would you walk to? Which would you sway to?

Your turn

Pick a rhythm and a tempo for a rainy day. Write them down.

Lesson 15 · Skill

Jazz groups and the big band

Jazz players each have a style. Small groups play close together; a big band is loud and swings.

  1. Play a jazz player: a trumpet or a sax with a style name
  2. Play a pattern with the Bebop quintet
  3. Play a pattern with the Cool jazz group
  4. Play a pattern with the Big band · swing

Try this: Pick Blues · 12 bars and play it with the big band and Stabs.

Think about it

Bebop or cool jazz: which one feels relaxed? Which feels busy?

Your turn

Name a jazz player you would like to hear. Ask someone at home for one.

Lesson 16 · Skill

Play along with your drum beat

Make a beat on the drum machine and press Send to the turntables. Then the band can play along with it.

  1. Press Play with my drum beat
  2. Press ▶ Play the chords with your drum beat on

Try this: Try a march beat with the marching band, or a swing beat with the big band.

Think about it

The tempo comes from your drum beat. Why does the band follow the drums?

Your turn

Write which beat and which instrument sound best together.

Lesson 17 · Skill

Record a take and send it

● Record keeps what the band plays. Your take can go to the Mixing Desk to become part of a song.

  1. Press ● Record
  2. Play, then press ■ Stop recording
  3. Listen to your take
  4. Press Send to the Mixing Desk on your take

Try this: Record the chords first. Then record a tune on the keys as a second take.

Think about it

What did you like about your take? What would you change?

Your turn

Give your take a name and write it here.

Lesson 18 · A song to make

Song: A fanfare

A fanfare is brass saying “Hooray, here they come!”

  1. Pick the pattern Three chords · 1 4 5 1
  2. Play the brass section with Fanfare
  3. Record a take of your fanfare

Try this: Add the timpani: play it once more with the percussion section.

Think about it

Who would you play a fanfare for?

Your turn

Write the moment your fanfare is for, in one line.

Lesson 19 · A song to make

Song: A march

A marching band plays oom-pah so everyone steps together.

  1. Play the Marching band with March · oom-pah
  2. March at a tempo of 110 or more
  3. Record a take of your march

Try this: March on the spot while it plays: left on oom, right on pah.

Think about it

Where have you seen a marching band?

Your turn

Write the tempo that felt best for marching.

Lesson 20 · A song to make

Song: A lullaby on strings

Soft strings, a slow waltz and a harp tune: music for resting.

  1. Play the string section with Waltz
  2. Slow it down: a tempo of 72 or less
  3. Play a tune of eight notes on the harp

Try this: Turn the Volume down a little. Calm music can be quiet.

Think about it

What makes music feel calm to you?

Your turn

Write who or what your lullaby is for.

Lesson 21 · A song to make

Song: A jazz ballad on sax

A ballad is slow and warm. The tenor sax sings the tune over jazz chords.

  1. Pick a jazz pattern: Jazz · 2 5 1, Jazz turnaround or Circle
  2. Play the tenor sax ballad with Long notes or Swell
  3. Play a tune of eight notes on the tenor sax ballad
  4. Record a take with the tenor sax ballad

Try this: Pick the Tenor sax · ballad. Play the chords, then play your tune over them.

Think about it

Jazz players make up tunes as they play. How did it feel to try?

Your turn

Give your ballad a title.

Lesson 22 · A song to make

Song: A mariachi celebration

Mariachi is music from Mexico: trumpets, violins, harp and a plucked bass, for a party.

  1. Pick the pattern Fiesta · 1 4 5 4
  2. Play Fiesta with the Mariachi
  3. Play the Mariachi with Waltz
  4. With the Mariachi, press Send to the turntables

Try this: Make it faster and shout ¡Ajúa! at the end.

Think about it

What celebration would you play this at?

Your turn

Write the names of the instruments in a mariachi.
