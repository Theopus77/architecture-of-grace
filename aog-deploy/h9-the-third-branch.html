Social Studies · Activity 9

The Third Branch

You have met Congress and the president. This is the branch with no army and no purse — and the last word. First the machine below is live — play it, tap it, watch it move. Then three sets of questions turn what you saw into evidence: the basics, how cases move, and the power with its checks.

Before the questions — watch it move

The machine at a glance

Ten stops — play them like a film, or tap through. The picture moves: chairs light up, a case climbs the ladder, nine justices hand down a ruling, a law is struck down. None of it is a test. Every stop ends at the set of questions where that idea is practiced for real.

Ready to be quizzed?

This page is the lesson: hints on every question, and a miss tells you what that answer would have been right for. The quiz itself — your class's own word-bank paper, all fifteen questions — now lives in one place with every other social studies quiz.

Show me one first

This one is nobody's answer — it is only here to show the moves before you touch yours. Watching it is instruction, never a trial, and it counts toward nothing.

Two drivers read one speed-limit law two different ways, and the argument lands in court. Which branch is at work?
Sort the jobs first. Congress WROTE the law — legislative, done and gone. An officer ENFORCED it — executive, also done.
What remains is the ARGUMENT — what do the words mean? Meaning is a courtroom question.
The judicial branch. Every question on this page yields to the same sort: write, execute, interpret.

Now number 1 is yours. The hint button is there the whole time, and using it is part of the lesson.

Question 1 of 8 Write · execute · interpret.

Need help?

Answer in your own words — this is the quiz, practiced. At the bottom, write the one sentence: who writes the laws, who carries them out, who decides what they mean.

  4. 4How many justices sit on the Supreme Court today?
  5. 5Who leads the Supreme Court?
  6. 6A seat opens on the Supreme Court. How is it filled?
  7. 7How long does a federal judge serve?

Notice what every wrong answer had in common: it put a job in the wrong branch's hands. Sort the three jobs — write, execute, interpret — and the quiz becomes a sorting exercise you have already done twenty-four times.
