A job has three parts, and people stop after two

BEFORE. A clear table and everything you need beside it — the paint, the paper, the water, the cloth. Getting things out is part one, and it is the part nobody minds.

Get it out, do it, put it back. Three parts, and the third one is not a favor you do afterwards — it is the last third of the job. This is the difference between somebody who helped and somebody who helped and then left a mess for another person to deal with, and from the other person's side those two things do not feel the same at all. The test is easy and it never changes: when you walk away, does the room look like nobody was here? If it does, the job is finished. If it does not, you are still working.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

The job is finished when the main part is done. Tidying up afterwards is extra.

Putting away is not extra. It is the third part of a three-part job, and leaving it out does not mean the work is done — it means the work has been handed to somebody else. Here is the honest way to check: after you walk off, does anybody have to do anything because you were there? If somebody has to wipe a table, close a lid, carry a plate or find where the scissors went, then what you actually did was move the job, not finish it. A cook who leaves the kitchen wrecked has not cooked dinner; they have cooked dinner and made a second job.

If somebody else has to finish it, you moved the job — you did not do it.

⚠️ People often think…

If you do a job badly enough, you will stop being asked to do it.

It works. That is the awkward part — it really does work, and it is worth saying so out loud instead of pretending it does not. But look at what it costs. The thing you actually lose is not the chore; it is being trusted, and trust is what you spend on the things you DO want. The person who is trusted with the dishes is the person who gets handed the knife, the hammer, the camera, the money for the shop, the key. Every one of those is somebody deciding whether you finish things. You cannot be careless about one job and trusted with a better one, because they are the same question asked twice.

You do not lose the chore. You lose the next thing you wanted.

Finish a job properly

1 Watch one

You have finished your breakfast. What is the whole job?

  1. Part one already happened: somebody got the bowl, the cereal and the milk out.
  2. Part two was the eating. That is the bit everybody thinks is the job.
  3. Part three: bowl and spoon to the sink, milk back in the fridge, cereal back in the cupboard.
  4. Then the check: wipe any drips, push the chair in, and look back at the table.
  5. Does it look like nobody was here? ✓ Then breakfast is finished. Forty seconds, all of it.

Helpful Hints

🧭 The unit in one line

A job has three parts — get it out, do it, put it back → the third is the one people drop, and dropping it hands the job to somebody else rather than finishing it → the last step of all is a two-second look → and the test is whether the room looks like nobody was here.

Get it out, do it, put it back. Then look.

🔑 The one idea

The reason this room exists is that almost nobody is taught that the third part IS the job. They are taught that the third part is being good, or being helpful, or being nice — which makes it optional, and something you can be praised for. It is not any of those. It is the last third of a thing you agreed to do. Once you see it that way the arguing mostly stops, because there is nothing left to argue about: you either finished or you did not, and the room will tell you.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • The tidying up is not extra. It is the third part of a three-part job.
  • If somebody else has to do something because you were there, you moved the job — you did not do it.
  • Doing a job badly on purpose DOES work. What it costs is being trusted with the things you actually want.
  • Putting an empty box back in the cupboard looks tidier than recycling it and is worse — it hides the job.
  • Tools go back CLEAN. A brush left in gray water is a broken brush for the next person.
  • Two people should agree who does which half BEFORE starting, or one half gets done twice and one not at all.
  • A job done later is bigger than the same job now, because things dry, spread, harden and get walked on.

🎯 How you will be asked

  • “Name the three parts of a job.”
  • “Is this finished, or handed on?” — with a situation given.
  • “What is the very last step, after everything is put away?”
  • “Where does this live?” — with a thing left out.
  • “What does doing a job badly on purpose actually cost you?”

  • Name the three parts of a job without looking?

  • Look at a room and say whether the last person finished or handed it on?
  • Say where five things in your house live?
