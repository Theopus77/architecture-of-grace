Two verbs where English has one

This is Ana. In English one small word — is — covers everything about her.

English has one verb ‘to be’. Spanish has two, and they answer different questions. Ser answers who or what someone is. Estar answers how they are right now, or where they are.

What people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Ser is for what is permanent and estar is for what is temporary.

That rule is the one most students are taught, and it is the one that fails them. Madrid está en España, and Madrid has not moved in a thousand years. Place always takes estar. The test that holds: identity and origin take ser; condition and place take estar.

Identity and origin, or condition and place.

⚠️ People often think…

Yo soy cansado is how you say that you are tired.

Tired, sick, happy, sad, nervous and angry are conditions — how a person is at this moment. Every one of them takes estar. Estoy cansado. Ella está enferma. Soy cansado says tiredness is part of who you are.

How you feel is estar, every time.

⚠️ People often think…

Ellos son jugando says that they are playing right now.

A word ending in -ando or -iendo never pairs with ser. What is happening right now is estar work, and only estar work. Ellos están jugando. Estoy hablando. Ella está comiendo.

-ando and -iendo belong to estar alone.

⚠️ People often think…

Es aburrido and está aburrido are two ways to say the same thing.

They are two different sentences. Es aburrido says he is boring — people get bored near him. Está aburrido says he is bored right now. Listo and verde flip the same way: es lista is clever, está lista is ready.

The verb carries the meaning, not the adjective.

Helpful Hints

🧭 The unit in one line

English has one verb ‘to be’. Spanish has two. Ser says what a thing IS — name, origin, job, material, time, and what a person is like. Estar says where it is and how it is — place, feeling, condition, and what is happening right now.

Ser = what. Estar = where and how.

🔑 The one idea

Do not translate. Ask what the sentence is doing. If it names or describes what something IS, use ser. If it puts something in a place, or tells how it feels or how it is at this moment, use estar. The English word ‘is’ will not help you choose — only the job of the sentence will.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • Place is ALWAYS estar, even when it never moves: Madrid está en España.
  • Tired, sick, happy, sad, nervous, angry — these are conditions. Estoy cansado, never soy cansado.
  • -ando and -iendo only ever pair with estar. Está hablando, never es hablando.
  • A job takes ser with no un or una: Soy estudiante. Mi padre es cocinero.
  • The describing word comes AFTER the noun and matches it: la casa blanca, las casas blancas.
  • Es aburrido and está aburrido are not the same sentence. One says he is boring; the other says he is bored.
  • ‘Permanent versus temporary’ is only a rough rule. Location breaks it every time.

🧩 The three pairs that flip

  • es aburrido = he is boring · está aburrido = he is bored
  • es lista = she is clever · está lista = she is ready
  • es verde = it is green in color · está verde = it is not ripe yet
  • The pattern: ser gives you the lasting trait, estar gives you the state of the moment.

🎯 How you will be asked

  • A blank in a sentence — “ser or estar, and why?”
  • “Give the six present forms of ser. Now give the six of estar.”
  • “What is the difference between es aburrido and está aburrido?”
  • A sentence with the wrong verb or the wrong ending — “fix it and explain the fix.”
  • “Describe a person in five sentences, using both verbs.”

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Say the six forms of ser, then the six of estar, without looking?
  • Name four jobs that belong to ser and three that belong to estar?
  • Build a progressive: hablar → está hablando, comer → está comiendo?
  • Make alto agree with a girl, with two boys, and with three girls?
  • Explain all three flipping pairs in your own words?
  • Introduce yourself in five sentences using both verbs correctly?
