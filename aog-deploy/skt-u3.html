Push, Roll, Stop

Moving

A skateboard sits still until you push it. Then it rolls. Soon you need to slow down, turn and stop. In this unit you learn all four: push, roll, turn and stop.

You will learn which foot does what. Your front foot stays on the board. Your back foot pushes, and later it brushes the ground to slow you down. You will lean on your toes or heels to turn.

You will also learn why it works. A push is a force. Friction is rubbing that slows you. Gravity pulls you down a slope. Always ride with a buckled helmet and a grown-up nearby.

The big question

How do pushes, friction and gravity help us start, turn and stop a skateboard?

When it happened
Step 1

Buckle your helmet and put on wrist guards and pads

Step 2

Find smooth, dry ground away from cars, with a grown-up

Step 3

Stand on the board on grass or carpet to find your front foot

Step 4

Front foot near the front bolts, back foot pushes once

Step 5

Back foot goes on the board, knees bend, and you roll

Step 6

Press toes or heels a little to turn

Step 7

Brush your back foot on the ground to slow down

Step 8

Roll slowly, then step off with your back foot first

Push and Roll

Moving
Big questionHow does one small push make a skateboard roll so far?
The story

One Small Push

Maya gives one small push. The board keeps going and going.

It is a sunny morning at the playground. The blacktop is smooth and dry. There are no cars here. Maya buckles her blue helmet. Her dad stands close by. He holds her new skateboard.

Maya puts her front foot on the board. She points her toes to the front. Her back foot stays on the ground. Her dad says, "Just a small push." Maya taps the ground with her back foot. It is a tiny push.

The board starts to roll! Maya lifts her back foot onto the board. She bends her knees. The wheels hum on the smooth ground. She rolls past the slide. She rolls past the swings. She did not push again.

The board slows down near the grass. Maya steps off. She looks back. "I only pushed one time!" she says. Her dad smiles. "Smooth ground helps you roll far," he says. Maya wants to try again.

Talk about itMaya pushed only once. Why do you think she rolled so far?
Section 1

Ready to Ride

5.1

Safe Before You Roll

Main ideaPut on a buckled helmet and find a smooth, safe place before you ride.

Before you ride, put on a . It should sit low on your forehead. Buckle the strap under your chin. A helmet keeps your head safe if you fall.

Wrist guards and knee pads help too. Wear flat shoes with good grip. Ride on smooth, dry ground. Stay far away from cars and streets.

Kids your age ride with a grown-up close by. The grown-up helps you choose a safe spot. A grown-up can hold your hand on your first rolls.

5.2

Front Foot, Back Foot

Main ideaYour front foot goes near the front of the board, and your back foot does the pushing.

Stand on the board on grass or carpet first. Then it cannot roll away. One foot goes in front. That is your . The other foot is your back foot.

Some people ride with the left foot in front. That is called . Some ride with the right foot in front. That is called goofy. Both ways are good.

How can you find your front foot? Try both ways on the grass. One way will feel more steady. Use the way that feels right to you.

Section 2

The Push

5.3

One Foot Pushes

Main ideaYour front foot stays on the board while your back foot pushes the ground.

Put your front foot on the board near the front . Point your toes to the front. Bend that knee a little. Your back foot stands on the ground next to the board.

Next, press your back foot on the ground. Push back, like a step. Your front foot stays on the board the whole time. The board moves forward.

Does the board slide away from you? Keep more weight on your front foot. Do you wobble? Bend your knees more. Look ahead, not down at your feet.

5.4

A Push Is a Force

Main ideaA push is a force, and a force can start a board moving.

A board sits still on the ground. It will not go by itself. Then your foot pushes the ground. Now the board moves. Your push started it.

A push is a . A force is a push or a pull. Forces make things start to move. Forces can make things stop, too.

Your foot pushes back on the ground. The ground pushes you forward. That is why you go ahead when you push back.

5.5

Foot Back On, Roll

Main ideaAfter a push, put your back foot on the board and roll with bent knees.

First, push one time. Then lift your back foot. Put it on the back of the board, near the back bolts. Your feet are now on the board.

Next, turn your front foot a little sideways. Now both feet point across the board. Bend your knees. Hold your arms out a little. Now you roll!

Put your back foot on gently. A hard stomp can tip the board. Keep your eyes ahead. A grown-up can walk beside you the first times.

Think it through

Act out the steps on carpet. What do you do after the push?

Section 3

How Far You Go

5.6

Small Pushes Are Fine

Main ideaSmall pushes make a slow, steady ride, which is good for learning.

A small push makes a slow roll. A big push makes a fast roll. When you are learning, slow is good. Slow gives you time to think.

Try one small push. Roll and balance. When the board slows, push again. Many small pushes can take you far.

Feeling a little scared is normal. It tells you to go slow. Small pushes help you feel safe. Bigger pushes can come later.

5.7

Smooth or Bumpy Ground

Main ideaSmooth ground lets a board roll far, and bumpy ground slows it down.

Wheels roll best on smooth ground. A smooth playground or a flat floor is good. The board can roll a long way on one push.

Bumpy ground slows you down. Rough spots rub on the wheels. A crack or a small rock can stop a wheel fast. Then you can tip forward.

Before you ride, look at the ground. Kick away little rocks and sticks. Ride where it is smooth and dry. Walk around big cracks.

Slow Down, Turn, Stop

Moving
Big questionHow can a rider slow down, turn and stop safely?
The story

The Little Slope

The board speeds up on a slope. Jada learns how to slow it down.

Jada and her aunt go to the park. There is a long, smooth path with no cars. One part of the path tips down a tiny bit. It is a very gentle slope. Jada checks her helmet strap.

Jada rolls onto the slope. She did not push, but the board goes faster. The wind blows on her face. "Whoa!" she says. Her aunt walks fast beside her. "Drag your back foot," says her aunt.

Jada keeps her front foot on the board. She bends her front knee. She lowers her back foot. Her shoe brushes the ground. Shhh, shhh. The board slows down. Then it stops.

Jada laughs. "My shoe was like a brake!" Her aunt nods. "The ground rubs your shoe. That slows you." Jada walks back up. This time she slows down early, before she gets fast.

Talk about itWhy did the board go faster on the slope when Jada did not push?
Section 1

Slowing Down

6.1

Drag a Foot Gently

Main ideaBrush your back foot lightly on the ground to slow down while your front foot stays on.

First, roll slowly on flat ground. Turn your front foot so the toes point ahead. Bend your front knee. Keep most of your weight on that front foot.

Next, lower your back foot to the ground beside the board. Let the flat bottom of your shoe brush the ground. Press a little. The board slows down.

Press a little more to slow down more. Use the whole flat shoe, not just the heel. A heel alone can bounce. Never drag a foot hard all at once. This way of slowing is called a .

2. Which part of the shoe should touch the ground?

6.2

Rubbing Slows You

Main ideaFriction is the rubbing that slows things down.

Rub your hands together fast. Do they feel warm? Your hands rub and push on each other. That rubbing is called .

Friction slows moving things down. When your shoe brushes the ground, the ground rubs it. The rubbing slows your board. Your shoe works like a brake.

Rough ground makes more friction than smooth ground. Grass makes a lot. That is why a board stops fast on grass. Friction helps you stop.

Leaning to Turn

6.3

Lean on Your Toes

Main ideaPress your toes down and the board turns toward your toe side.

Stand on the board on grass first. Press down with your toes. The board tips toward your toes. The side your toes point to is the .

Under the board are two metal parts called . They hold the wheels. When the board tips, the trucks turn the wheels.

Now roll slowly on smooth ground. Press your toes a little. Bend your knees. Look where you want to go. The board curves to your toe side.

6.4

Lean on Your Heels

Main ideaPress your heels down and the board turns toward your heel side.

Your heels are at the back of your feet. The side of the board behind your heels is the . Stand on the board on grass. Press your heels down.

The board tips toward your heels. Now try it while you roll slowly. Press your heels a little. Bend your knees. The board curves to your heel side.

Heel turns can feel strange. You cannot see behind you well. Turn your head and shoulders to look where you are going. Small leans make small turns.

Section 3

Hills and Stopping

6.5

Slopes Make You Faster

Main ideaGravity pulls you down a slope, so the board speeds up without a push.

A ball on a slide rolls down. Nobody pushes it. A pull brings it down. That pull is called . Gravity pulls everything toward the ground.

A is ground that tips down. On a slope, gravity pulls your board down. The board goes faster and faster. You do not need to push.

Learn on flat ground first. Later, a grown-up can find a tiny slope with no cars. Never ride down a street or a big hill. Practice slowing down before you try any slope.

6.6

Stop Before Too Fast

Main ideaStart slowing down early, before the board gets too fast.

Slow is easy to stop. Fast is hard to stop. So start your foot brake early. Do not wait until you are going fast.

Pick a stop spot before you roll, like a tree or a line. Start to slow down well before it. Then you can stop right there.

Feeling fast and scared? Bend your knees and get low. Brush your back foot gently. A grown-up can stand at your stop spot to help.

6.7

Step Off Safely

Main ideaSlow down first, then step off with your back foot and walk it out.

First, slow down until you roll like a walk. Next, step your back foot onto the ground. Then step your front foot off. Take a few walking steps.

Sometimes you tip. That is part of learning. Bend your knees and get low. Low falls are small falls. Try to roll onto your side.

Do not catch yourself with stiff, straight arms. That can hurt your wrists. Wrist guards and knee pads help. Get up slowly and check that you feel okay.

3. You start to fall. What helps?
