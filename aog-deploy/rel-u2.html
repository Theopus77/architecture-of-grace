Stories and Books

People all over the world keep old stories. Grandparents tell them. Children learn them. Then the children tell them again.

In this unit you will hear stories from many groups. You will see special books and how people care for them. You will learn to say, "This is a story that ___ tell."

Old Stories from Many Lands

Stories
Big questionWhy do people keep telling the same old stories?
The story

A Story from Grandpa

Grandpa has a story that is older than anyone he knows.

It is a rainy Saturday. Leo sits on the rug. Grandpa sits in his big chair. He pats the seat next to him. Leo hops up fast. Leo hops up.

"Sit down," Grandpa says. "This is a story my grandpa told me. His grandpa told it to him. It is very, very old. Now I will tell it to you."

Leo leans close. The rain taps on the window. Grandpa's voice gets soft and slow. Leo listens to every single word. He does not want to miss any of it.

When the story ends, Leo asks, "Can I tell it to my friend Sam?" Grandpa smiles. "Yes. That is how old stories stay alive."

Talk about itWho tells you stories at home or at school? Is there one you like to hear again?
Section 1

Stories We Pass Along

3.1

A Story Told Again

Main ideaSome stories are told again and again for a very long time.

A family can keep a story. So can a big group of people. They tell it to children. The children grow up and tell it too.

Some of these stories come from religions. A is a way people believe and live together. Each group keeps its own stories.

Think it through
A Story Chain

Picture five people in a line. Great-grandma tells a story to Grandma. Grandma tells Mom. Mom tells Leo. Leo tells baby June.

How many people heard the same story? Who will June tell it to?

Words to know
religion
a way a group of people believe, pray and live together
Check yourself

1. What is an old story?

Why: Old stories are passed from one person to the next for many years.

2. Who told Leo's grandpa the story?

Why: Grandpa said his grandpa told him the story.

3. What is a religion?

Why: A religion is a way people believe, pray and live together.
3.2

Saying Whose Story It Is

Main ideaWe say which group tells a story.

We can say, "This is a story that Hindus tell." Or, "This is a story that Sikhs tell." Those words tell us whose story it is.

Saying it this way is kind and true. It shows respect. It helps us learn about our friends and neighbors.

Think it through
Fill the Blank

Mia hears a story about a boat. Her teacher says Jews, Christians and Muslims tell it. Mia starts to share: "This is a story that ___ tell."

What words can Mia put in the blank?

Words to know
respect
being kind and careful with what matters to someone else
Check yourself

1. Which words tell whose story it is?

Why: Naming the group tells whose story it is, kindly.

2. Why do we say which group tells a story?

Why: Saying who tells a story is true and shows respect.

3. What does respect mean?

Why: Respect means being careful with what matters to someone else.

Main ideaBuddhists tell of a prince who saw that people grow old and get sick.

Buddhists tell of a prince named Siddhartha. He lived in a palace long ago. He did not know about sadness.

One day he rode outside. He saw an old man and a sick man. Then he saw a , a man who lived simply. Buddhists say he later became the Buddha.

Lamps for Rama

Main ideaHindus tell how lamps welcomed Rama home.

Hindus tell a story about Prince Rama. He was sent far away. He stayed away for fourteen years.

At last, Rama came home. The people lit rows of little lamps. Many Hindus remember this at , the festival of lights.

3.6

Guru Nanak Shares Food

Main ideaSikhs tell how Guru Nanak shared food with people who were hungry.

Sikhs tell a story about young Guru Nanak. His father gave him money. "Make a good trade," his father said.

Nanak met hungry people. He used the money to buy them food. Sikhs call this the true trade. Today Sikhs share free meals called .

From the source
A Sikh Teaching
Those who work hard for what they eat, and share some of what they have, know the true way.
Guru Granth Sahib, p. 1245 (Guru Nanak), in plain words (paraphrased)

What two things does this teaching ask people to do?

Words to know
Guru
a teacher; Sikhs have ten human Gurus
langar
a free meal Sikhs share with anyone who comes
Check yourself

Why: This is a story that Sikhs tell.

Books People Keep Safe

Sacred Books
Big questionHow do people show that a book is special to them?
The story

The Glass Case

The librarian has four books that never touch the floor.

Ms. Ortiz is the school librarian. Today she walks to a glass case. The class follows her very quietly. Everyone wants to see.

Inside are four books. "People care for these books in special ways," she says. "Their families never leave them on the floor."

One is a scroll on two wooden rollers. One is wrapped in soft cloth. One sits on a little stand. One is a big book with a gold cover.

"Can we touch them?" asks Amir. Ms. Ortiz smiles. "First, let us learn how people hold them. Then we will use our careful hands."

Talk about itDo you have a book or a thing that you keep extra safe? How do you care for it?

Special Books

Four Special Books

Main ideaMany religions have a book that they keep with great care.

Jews read the . Christians read the Bible. Muslims read the Qur’an. Sikhs read the Guru Granth Sahib.

Each book is special to its people. Other people can find it interesting. We can learn about all of them.

Read and Sung Aloud

4.5

Words Out Loud

Main ideaPeople read and sing their holy books out loud.

Many Muslims learn to the Qur’an in Arabic. To recite is to say words from memory, often like a song.

In a synagogue, the Torah is chanted. Sikhs sing hymns from the Guru Granth Sahib. Christians read the Bible aloud in church.

From the source
From the Book of Deuteronomy
And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children.
Deuteronomy 6:6-7, King James Bible (1611)

Who does this passage say should learn the words?

Words to know
recite
to say words out loud, often from memory
Check yourself

1. What does recite mean?

Why: To recite is to say words aloud, often from memory.

2. How do Sikhs share the Guru Granth Sahib?

Why: Sikhs sing hymns from the Guru Granth Sahib.

3. In the Deuteronomy passage, who should be taught?

Why: The verse says to teach the words to children.

A holy book may be very special to one family. Another family may not read it at home. Some families have no holy book.

Every family is welcome in the . The library keeps copies of many books. Anyone can learn about them.
