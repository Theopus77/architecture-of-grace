Heat moves one way

A hot mug and a cold hand. Two temperatures, not touching yet.

Heat always moves from hot to cold. Your cold hands do not give cold to the cocoa — the cocoa gives heat to your hands. There is no such thing as cold moving.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

A heavy thing always has more energy than a light one.

Speed counts too. A bowling ball barely rolling can carry less energy than a marble flicked hard across the floor. Ask about both the mass and how fast it is going.

Heavy and fast both count.

⚠️ People often think…

When a battery dies, the energy is used up and gone.

It turned into other things — light, sound, and mostly heat that spread out into the room. Energy changes form; it never disappears. It just ends up somewhere you cannot use.

It changes form, mostly into heat.

Helpful Hints

🪜 From the sun to your lamp — the ladder

Sunlight → plants store it → we eat it, or it becomes coal and oil, or it drives wind and rain → power plants and batteries → electricity in wires → light, motion, sound and heat at the other end. Heat leaks out at every arrow.

Sun, store, carry, use, leak.

🤝 The whole topic in one idea

Energy is never made or lost — it just moves from thing to thing and changes form. When something moves, lights, sounds or warms, ask where the energy came from and where it went next.

Where from, what form, where next.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Heat moves HOT to COLD. Your cold hands don’t give cold to the cocoa; the cocoa gives heat to your hands.
  • Faster = more energy. A slow bowling ball can have less energy than a fast marble.
  • A circuit needs a closed LOOP. One loose wire and nothing lights.
  • Energy isn’t ‘used up’ — it turns into heat and spreads out.
  • Coal, oil and gas are NON-renewable. Sun, wind and water are renewable.
  • Dark things absorb light and get hot; white things bounce it.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A device — “what form goes in, what form comes out?” (toaster: electrical in, heat out).
  • A ramp table — “higher start, what happens to the cup?” (slides farther — more energy).
  • A circuit drawing — “will the bulb light?” (is the loop closed?).
  • A list of sources — “renewable or not?” (does it come back?).

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Name six forms of energy with an example each?
  • Trace the energy through a flashlight or a fan?
  • Explain why a higher marble knocks a cup farther?
  • Say what a circuit needs to light a bulb?
  • Sort five energy sources into renewable and not?
