Word Parts: Prefixes, Suffixes, Roots

In K–2 you pulled a word apart into sounds. Now you pull it apart into meanings. A root carries what the word is about, a prefix goes on the front and changes it, and a suffix goes on the end and usually changes the word's job. Learn twelve of these pieces and you can work out the meaning of words nobody has ever taught you — which is the whole point. Five tabs — cards for the pieces, hints with the prefix and suffix chart, a quiz that asks you to build and break words, and a workshop where you sort real prefixes from lookalikes, build a word in order, label the parts and say why word parts beat memorizing.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

It starts with re–, so re– is the prefix.

Only if a real word part is left behind. Take re– off rebuild and build is left, so it is a prefix. Take re– off red and you are left with d, which is nothing — red just happens to start with those letters. Same test for pretty, dish and uncle.

Take it off. If nothing real is left, it was not a prefix.

⚠️ People often think…

un– always means “not”, so untie means not tie.

un– has two jobs. On a word that describes — happy, kind, fair — it means not. On an action you can reverse — tie, lock, pack — it means undo it. The ROOT tells you which one you have, so read the whole word before you decide.

The root decides which job the prefix is doing.

Root, then front, then end

1 Watch one

Work out what transportable means, without being told.

  1. Find the root first: port, which means carry.
  2. Check the front: trans–, which means across.
  3. Check the end: –able, which means it can be done.
  4. Put the three together: something that can be carried across — something you can move from place to place.

Helpful Hints

🧭 The unit in one line

Find the root first — it carries the meaning → check the front for a prefix that changes it → check the end for a suffix that changes the word's job → then put the three answers together.

Root = what it is about. Prefix = changes it. Suffix = changes its job.

🔑 The one idea

Twelve word parts will unlock hundreds of words. That is why this is worth more than learning hundreds of words one at a time — the parts keep working on words you have never seen.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • un- does not always mean “not.” Unhappy means not happy, but untie means the opposite of tie — you undo it. Look at what the whole word does.
  • Letters that look like a prefix often are not one. Red and ready start with r-e and have no prefix, because d and ady are not roots.
  • Take the prefix off and a real root is left. If what is left is not a word part you know, there was no prefix.
  • A suffix usually changes the word's JOB, not its topic. Care, careful and carefully are all about caring — but one is a thing, one describes, and one tells how.
