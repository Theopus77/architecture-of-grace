Okinawa and Korea

Origins

Karate and taekwondo are often mixed up on signs and in movies. They have separate histories. Karate grew on Okinawa, in the old Ryukyu Kingdom, with ideas from China. Taekwondo was built in South Korea after 1945, with a new national name.

Both arts moved from small rooms into schools, armies, universities and world federations. Wars, empires and the Olympic Games shaped them. In this unit you will follow each art from its first teachers to its first Olympic medals.

By the end, you will be able to explain where each art came from and name the people who carried it. You will also compare how karate and taekwondo got their names, ranks and rule books.

The big question

How did karate and taekwondo each become an art with a national name, a school plan and a world federation?

The story

An Empty Floor in Tokyo

In 1922 a schoolteacher from Okinawa shows Tokyo an art most people there have never seen.

It is the spring of 1922 in Tokyo, Japan. A national sports exhibition fills a hall. It is organized by Japan's Ministry of Education. Teachers from many parts of the country have come to show their subjects.

One visitor comes from far to the south. His name is Funakoshi Gichin. He is a schoolteacher from Shuri, on the island of Okinawa. He is in his early fifties. He has come to show an art his island calls karate.

Few people in Tokyo know the word. Okinawa had been its own kingdom, called Ryukyu, until 1879. Its arts grew there, with ideas carried by ships from China. Now Funakoshi stands before a Tokyo crowd with an empty floor in front of him.

People notice. Kanō Jigorō, the founder of judo, invites Funakoshi to show karate at his Kodokan school. Funakoshi was expected to go home. Instead he stays in Tokyo, teaching and writing, for the rest of his life.

An Island Kingdom

11.1

The Ryukyu Kingdom

Main ideaKarate began in the Ryukyu Kingdom, an island nation that became part of Japan only in 1879.

The Islands are a long chain in the sea. They stretch south from Japan toward Taiwan. The biggest island is Okinawa. For centuries these islands had their own kings, their own court and their own languages.

The kings lived in a castle at , on Okinawa. Ryukyu sent ships to China with gifts for the Chinese emperor. Chinese ships and envoys came back. Ryukyu also traded with Japan, Korea and ports in Southeast Asia.

In 1609 soldiers from Satsuma, a domain in southern Japan, invaded Ryukyu. After that, Satsuma controlled much of the kingdom. In 1879 Japan ended the kingdom and made it Okinawa Prefecture. The last king was sent to Tokyo.

11.2

Hand and China Hand

Main ideaOkinawans had a local art called te, and they also learned from Chinese teachers and traders.

Okinawans had an old unarmed art often called . Te simply means hand. We have few written records of how it was taught. Most lessons passed quietly from one teacher to a few students.

Ryukyu had close ties with Fuzhou, a port city in China’s Fujian province. Okinawan students, officials and traders spent time there. Some of them studied Chinese boxing arts. A village of Chinese families, called Kume, also lived near Naha on Okinawa.

Okinawans came to write the art with two Chinese characters. They meant Tang hand, or . Tang was an old name for China. The characters could be read tōde or karate. The name itself showed where some of the ideas came from.

From Island to Capital

11.4

Karate Goes to School

Main ideaIn the early 1900s, karate entered Okinawa's schools as a subject for building fit students.

After 1879 Okinawa followed Japan’s school system. Japan wanted strong, healthy students and future soldiers. In the early 1900s, Itosu Ankō helped bring karate into Okinawan schools as . This meant school lessons for the body.

Teaching a whole class was new. Old teachers had taught one or two students at a time. Itosu made simpler lessons that many children could learn in rows. He named a set of beginner Pinan. A kata is a fixed pattern practiced alone, like a written piece of music.

In 1908 Itosu wrote a letter with ten points about karate. It was meant for school officials. It said karate was for health and for building character, not for hurting people. Historians still read this letter as an early plan for karate in schools.

11.6

China Hand Becomes Empty Hand

Main ideaIn the 1930s, karate's written name changed from China hand to empty hand.

In the 1930s, Japan and China were moving toward war. The name China hand now sounded wrong to many people in Japan. Teachers looked for a new way to write karate.

They kept the sound karate but changed one character. The new characters meant . Funakoshi used the new writing in his 1935 book Karate-dō Kyōhan. He explained empty as a hand without a weapon, and also as a mind free of bad thoughts.

In 1936 senior karate teachers met in Naha, on Okinawa. They agreed to use the empty hand writing. They also added , which means way. Karate became karate-dō, the way of the empty hand.

Section 3

Many Clubs, One Word

11.7

Universities and Styles

Main ideaIn mainland Japan, university clubs and named styles gave karate new rooms and ranks.

In 1924 Keio University in Tokyo started a karate club. It is often named as the first university karate club in Japan. Funakoshi taught there. Other universities soon started clubs too.

Karate took ideas from judo. Clubs began to use a white uniform and colored belts. They also used ranks, the black belt levels that Kanō had created for judo. This made karate look like other Japanese school arts.

Different teachers named their own . A style is one teacher’s line of lessons with its own name. Funakoshi’s became known as Shotokan, from his pen name Shōtō. Miyagi Chōjun’s was called Gōjū-ryū. Each style kept its own kata and its own ranks.

11.8

After 1945

Main ideaAfter World War II, American service members learned karate in Okinawa and Japan and opened schools at home.

World War II ended in 1945. The Battle of Okinawa that spring killed a huge number of people, many of them Okinawan civilians. After the war the United States ruled Okinawa. It returned Okinawa to Japan only in 1972.

Large American military bases stood on Okinawa. Some trained with Okinawan and Japanese karate teachers. When they went home, a few opened karate schools in the United States. Karate schools spread across American towns in the 1950s and 1960s.

In American , names began to blur. A sign might say karate over a room that taught a Korean or Chinese art. Many Americans used karate as a word for any art from East Asia. The teachers behind the signs came from many different lines.

11.9

Karate at the Olympics

Main ideaKarate was an Olympic sport once, at the Tokyo Games held in 2021.

Karate grew into a world sport with its own . A federation is a group that sets rules and runs contests. The World Karate Federation runs the Olympic kind of karate. It chose shared rules so that many styles could meet.

Karate joined the Olympic program for the Tokyo 2020 Games. The Games were delayed by the COVID-19 pandemic and held in 2021. Karate had two kinds of events. In kata, judges scored a solo pattern. In kumite, two athletes faced each other under a points system.

The Olympic sport was chosen for Tokyo, not for every Games. Karate was not on the program for Paris 2024. Okinawa built the Okinawa Karate Kaikan, a center for karate history and training, which opened in 2017 in Tomigusuku.

A Korean School Sport

Taekwondo
Big questionHow did South Korea build taekwondo into a national sport and then an Olympic one?
The story

A Plan for Every School

After years of war and division, South Korea sets out to give one art one name and one plan.

It is the 1960s in Seoul, South Korea. The country is still rebuilding after the Korean War, which stopped in 1953. Many small schools teach unarmed arts. Each school is called a kwan, and each has its own name and teacher.

Leaders of the kwan meet in crowded offices. They argue about names, ranks and who should lead. Slowly they agree to join one national association. The art will have one name: taekwondo.

The association writes plans for ranks and lessons. The art is taught in the army, in schools and in clubs. A certificate from the national body now means the same thing in every city.

The goal grows bigger. Leaders want taekwondo known around the world as a Korean sport. That goal would lead, many years later, to an Olympic mat in Sydney, Australia.

Years of Colonial Rule

Main ideaJapan ruled Korea from 1910 to 1945, and some Koreans learned Japanese arts such as karate in those years.

Japan’s rule ended in 1945, when Japan lost World War II. Koreans call that day . The Korean men who had learned karate came home. They would soon open schools of their own.

12.3

The First Kwan

Main ideaAfter 1945, Korean teachers opened schools called kwan that taught karate-based lessons.

One National Name

12.4

Naming Taekwondo

Main ideaIn 1955 the name taekwondo was chosen, meaning roughly the way of foot and fist.

In 1955 a group of teachers and leaders met in Seoul to pick a new name. They chose . General Choi Hong-hi, an army officer who ran a kwan, is often named as the one who proposed it.

The name has three parts. Tae means foot or to kick. Kwon means fist or hand. Do means way, the same word as the Japanese dō. Together it means roughly the way of foot and fist.

The new name sounded like taekkyeon, the older Korean art. That was on purpose, many historians say. Leaders wanted a name that pointed to Korea, not Japan. Not every kwan used the new name at first.

12.5

A National Association

Main ideaIn the 1960s, many kwan joined one national association, and the art spread through the army and schools.

In the early 1960s, South Korea’s government pushed the kwan to unite. They formed one national . It changed its name more than once. By 1965 it was called the Korea Taekwondo Association.

The association set shared ranks and tests. The South Korean army trained soldiers in taekwondo. Korean instructors went abroad too, including to Vietnam, where South Korean troops served in the 1960s.

Not everyone agreed about who should lead. In 1966 Choi Hong-hi founded the International Taekwon-Do Federation in Seoul. Later it moved its headquarters out of South Korea. Two taekwondo bodies, with different rules and forms, still exist today.

In 1972 opened in Seoul. Its name means national hall. It is the headquarters of taekwondo in South Korea. Kukkiwon gives out dan certificates, the papers that record black belt ranks, to students around the world.

In 1973 the World Taekwondo Federation was founded in Seoul. It set rules for contests between countries. In 2017 it shortened its name to . It runs world championships and the Olympic events.

Kukkiwon also set new forms in the early 1970s. They were named Taegeuk, after the red and blue circle on South Korea’s flag. They replaced older forms borrowed from karate. In 2018 South Korea passed a law naming taekwondo its national martial art.

The Olympic Path

12.7

From Demonstration to Medals

Main ideaTaekwondo was shown at the 1988 and 1992 Olympics and became a full medal sport in 2000.

Seoul hosted the Summer Olympics in 1988. Taekwondo appeared there as a . That meant it was shown at the Games, but its medals did not count. It was shown again in Barcelona, Spain, in 1992.

In 2000 the Olympics came to Sydney, Australia. Taekwondo became a full there. Men and women each had four weight classes. That made eight events, with the same number for women and men from the start.

Many countries won medals, not only Korea. This showed how far the sport had spread. Taekwondo has been on the Olympic program at every Summer Games since Sydney.

New Rules for a Watched Sport

Main ideaOlympic taekwondo added electronic scoring and Paralympic events as the sport grew.

Early contests were scored by judges pressing buttons. Fans and coaches often argued about the scores. World Taekwondo brought in , with sensors in the body protectors. The London 2012 Olympics used this system.

Olympic contests use a set , a round clock and a square or eight-sided mat. These rules let athletes from any country meet on fair terms. The rules also reward some scoring moves with more points than others.

Doors opened for more athletes. Para taekwondo, for athletes with impairments of the arms, joined the Paralympic Games in Tokyo. Those Games were held in 2021. Refugee athletes have also competed under a refugee team flag.

Judges and Sensors

Picture a contest in 1995. Judges sit at the corners and press buttons when they see a point. Now picture 2012. Sensors in the body protectors send a signal to a scoreboard. The judges still watch from their chairs.

12.9

More Than Korean Karate

Main ideaTaekwondo began with karate lessons but was rebuilt with a Korean name, forms, rules and national bodies.

Some people call taekwondo Korean karate. There is a true part to this, because taekwondo has karate . The first kwan teachers had trained in Japanese karate. Their early forms and belts came from karate.

But taekwondo leaders changed much after 1955. They chose a Korean name and built national bodies. Kukkiwon wrote new Taegeuk forms. World Taekwondo wrote contest rules that are different from karate’s rules.

Leaders also made taekwondo a and tied taekwondo to older Korean arts such as taekkyeon. Historians still debate how close that tie is. Today taekwondo is taught in more than 200 countries, many of which belong to World Taekwondo.

Two Sides of a Story

One writer says taekwondo is only Korean karate with a new name. Another says it is an ancient Korean art. Records show the first kwan teachers trained in karate. Records also show new Korean forms, rules and bodies after 1955.

Which parts of each claim do the records support?

A Korean School Sport

0 / 8

1. Which art is Korean wrestling?

Why: Ssireum is traditional Korean wrestling.

2. Why did some Korean men know karate in 1945?

Why: During Japanese rule, some Koreans studied in Japan and trained in karate clubs.

3. Put these in order: Kukkiwon opens, the name taekwondo is chosen, Olympic medals in Sydney.

Why: The name came in 1955, Kukkiwon opened in 1972 and Sydney gave medals in 2000.

4. How is Kukkiwon different from World Taekwondo?

Why: Kukkiwon, in Seoul, issues dan certificates. World Taekwondo runs world contests and Olympic events.

5. What does taekwondo mean?

Why: Tae means foot, kwon means fist and do means way.

6. How was taekwondo's Olympic path different from karate's?

Why: Taekwondo has been on every Summer Games since 2000. Karate appeared only at Tokyo, held in 2021.

7. What is a kwan?

Why: Kwan means hall or school. The early taekwondo schools were each a kwan.

8. Why do two taekwondo world bodies exist?

Why: Choi Hong-hi founded the International Taekwon-Do Federation in 1966, apart from the national association.

Why: Itosu's ten points explained karate as health and character training for schools.

Why: Japan ruled Korea from 1910 until 1945.

Why: Their founders had trained in karate, and their early lessons came from it.

8. Which statement compares the two arts correctly?

Why: Karate grew on Okinawa. The name taekwondo was chosen in Seoul in 1955.

9. What does Kukkiwon do?

Why: Kukkiwon, taekwondo's headquarters in Seoul, issues dan certificates.

10. When did taekwondo become a full Olympic medal sport?

Why: Taekwondo was a demonstration sport in 1988 and 1992 and a medal sport in 2000.

Why: A demonstration sport is shown at the Olympics, but its medals are not official.
