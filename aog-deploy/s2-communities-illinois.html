Your street sits inside four things

Start with your street. It sits inside a town — Darien, say.

Town, county, state, country — four rings, each inside the next. When a question names a place, ask which ring it belongs to first, because that decides who runs it and what it is responsible for.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

The biggest city in a state is its capital.

In Illinois the capital is Springfield and the biggest city is Chicago. States often put the capital somewhere central instead of somewhere crowded, so being big and being the seat of government are two separate facts.

Springfield governs. Chicago is biggest.

⚠️ People often think…

Native nations used to live in Illinois.

They ARE here — present tense. Treaties and removal moved many nations, but Native people live in Illinois today, in towns and cities across the state. The tense you use is part of whether the sentence is true.

Say “are”, not “were”.

Worth knowing cold

Illinois, worth knowing cold

Three facts, then four dates in order. Each line stands on its own.

  1. Capital Springfield is the capital. Chicago is the biggest city — two different jobs, two different cities.
  2. Rivers The Mississippi is the WEST border. The Ohio touches the SOUTH tip. The Illinois River runs through the MIDDLE.
  3. First here Native nations were here long before any date below — and they are still here. Say are, not were.
  4. 1673 French explorers arrive.
  5. 1818 Illinois becomes a state.
  6. 1837 Chicago is incorporated as a city.
  7. 1871 The Great Chicago Fire.

🤝 The whole topic in one idea

Illinois is a place — prairie, rivers and a lake — and a story: the peoples who lived here first, the explorers and settlers who came, the city that grew where the water met the rails, and the towns, counties and state that run it today.

A place, a story, and who runs it.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Springfield is the capital. Chicago is the biggest city. Not the same thing.
  • The Mississippi is the WEST border. The Ohio is the SOUTH tip. The Illinois River runs through the MIDDLE.
  • 1673 explorers, 1818 statehood, 1837 Chicago, 1871 the Fire — keep the order.
  • Darien is in DuPage County, not Cook. The county seat is Wheaton, not Darien.
  • Native nations were here first, and they are still here.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A blank Illinois map — “label the lake, the rivers, Chicago and Springfield.”
  • A problem — “town, county or state?” (read the word on the sign).
  • Events — “put these in order” (1673, 1818, 1837, 1860, 1871).
  • “Why did Chicago grow?” (the lake, the canal, the railroads).

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Point to Lake Michigan, the Mississippi, Chicago and Springfield on a map?
  • Say what the town, the county and the state each run?
  • Tell Illinois’s story in order with four dates?
  • Name the three regions and one thing about each?
  • Explain why Chicago grew, in one sentence?
