Lesson 1 · Skill

The four strings

Tap a string at the far left, before the frets. It rings open. From thick to thin: E A D G.

  1. Play the low E string: the thickest one
  2. Play the A string
  3. Play the D string
  4. Play the G string: the thinnest one

Try this: Play E, A, D, G slowly, then G, D, A, E. Feel the low notes in your chest.

Think about it

The bass's four strings have the same names as the guitar's lowest four. Why might that help a band?

Your turn

Make up a way to remember E A D G.

Lesson 2 · Skill

Frets make notes

Press a string on a fret and the note goes up. Each fret is one small step.

  1. Tap a string on any fret
  2. Press Higher ▶
  3. Play a note on fret 5 or higher
  4. Press ◀ Lower

Try this: Play the E string on frets 1 to 5. Hear it climb.

Think about it

The E string on fret 5 sounds just like another open string. Which one?

Your turn

Play a short tune on one string. Write the fret numbers.

Lesson 3 · Skill

Root notes

Every chord has a root: its home note. The bass plays it, and the whole band stands on it.

  1. Tap a chord pad: you hear its root, the chord's home note
  2. Play all six chord pads
  3. In the row of chords by the neck, play one of the last four
  4. Press ✋ Mute the strings

Try this: Tap pads 1, 4, 5, 1. Watch where the ringed dot moves on the neck.

Think about it

Why does a song feel empty when the bass stops?

Your turn

Write the root note of each pad you played.

Lesson 4 · Skill

Octaves

An octave is the same note, higher. On the bass it is two strings up and two frets up.

  1. Play a note, then the same note an octave higher
  2. Play a note on fret 12
  3. Play the chords with Octaves

Try this: Bounce between a note and its octave: low, high, low, high. That is disco.

Think about it

Fret 12 sounds like the open string, only higher. Why do you think that is?

Your turn

Draw where a note and its octave sit on the four strings.

Lesson 5 · Skill

Fifths

The fifth is the root's best friend. Root and fifth make a strong, simple bass line.

  1. Play a note, then its fifth: one string up, two frets up
  2. Play a note, then the fifth below it: same fret, one string lower
  3. Play the chords with Root and fifth

Try this: Play root, fifth, root, fifth with a steady count. That is country bass.

Think about it

The fifth above and the fifth below share a name. How do they sound different?

Your turn

Write a root and fifth line for two chords.

Lesson 6 · Skill

Follow the chords

Pick a chord pattern and press Play. The bass follows each chord, one bar at a time.

  1. Pick a pattern in Start from a chord pattern
  2. Press ▶ Play the chords
  3. Move the Tempo slider
  4. Press ■ Stop

Try this: Watch the neck while it plays. The orange dot is the note ringing now.

Think about it

What does the small number under each chord tell you?

Your turn

Name a pattern you liked and the tempo that felt best.

Lesson 7 · Skill

Steady lines

A steady bass keeps the whole band together. Pick a style in How the bass plays.

  1. Play the chords with Root
  2. Play the chords with Steady
  3. Play the chords with Driving
  4. Play the chords with Waltz

Try this: Keep one pattern and change only the style. Tap your foot to each one.

Think about it

The waltz counts in threes. How does that feel different?

Your turn

Pick a steady style for a calm song and one for a fast song.

Lesson 8 · Skill

Moving lines

Some bass lines walk from chord to chord, with little steps in between.

  1. Play the chords with Walking
  2. Play the chords with Arpeggio
  3. Play the chords with Lead-in

Try this: Listen to the last note of each bar. It leads into the next chord.

Think about it

Why does a walking line feel like it is going somewhere?

Your turn

Write four notes that walk from one chord's root to the next.

Lesson 9 · Skill

Keys, moods and the chord wheel

The key sets which chords fit. Major sounds bright. Minor sounds moody. The wheel shows every chord.

  1. Change the Key
  2. Press Minor · moody
  3. Tap a chord on the chord wheel
  4. Turn the wheel with one of its buttons

Try this: Neighbours on the wheel sound good together. Play three roots side by side.

Think about it

When you turned the wheel, what happened to the six light chords?

Your turn

Choose a key and a mood for a song about a storm.

Lesson 10 · Skill

Slides and the fretless bass

Slide a finger along a string and the note glides. A fretless bass glides with no steps at all.

  1. Slide a finger along a string to another fret
  2. Pick Fretless or Upright bass in Instrument
  3. Play a note with that sound

Try this: On the whole screen, a fretless note is right under your finger. Slide slowly.

Think about it

A slide sounds like a voice. What feeling can it show?

Your turn

Make up a line with one slide in it. Write where it starts and ends.

Lesson 11 · Skill

Bass sounds, amp and pedals

Each bass has its own amp and pedals. Change any of it, and it stays with that sound.

  1. Pick another bass in Instrument
  2. Change a knob or a setting on the amp
  3. Turn a pedal on or off
  4. Move the Sound dial between 1987 and 2026

Try this: Turn on one pedal, play a note, then turn it off. Name what it added.

Think about it

Which bass sound would you pick for a calm song? Which for a loud one?

Your turn

Make a sound you love. Write the bass, the amp and the pedals.

Lesson 12 · Skill

Lock in with the drums

The bass and the kick drum are a team. Make a beat on the drum machine and play with it here.

  1. Turn on Play with my drum beat
  2. Press ▶ Play the chords with your drum beat on

Try this: Listen for the kick. Try Steady, then Driving. Which one sits with it best?

Think about it

Why do bass players listen to the kick drum so closely?

Your turn

Name your drum beat and the bass style that fits it best.

Lesson 13 · Skill

Hold and pluck, like a real bass

On the whole screen, one hand holds a fret and the other plucks. A style can follow the fret you hold, too.

  1. Turn your phone sideways, or press ⤢ Play on the whole screen
  2. In ☰ Menu, pick ✋ Hold + strum
  3. Hold a fret with one hand. Pluck the strip with the other
  4. While the chords play, hold a fret: the line follows it

Try this: Pluck with two fingers in turn, like a real bass player.

Think about it

What happens when you lift your fretting finger?

Your turn

Find a note that sounds good under every chord. Write its fret.

Lesson 14 · Skill

Solo mode

Solo mode lights a scale on the neck. Every lit note fits. A band can play along.

  1. Press Solo, above the neck
  2. Tap eight lit notes: each tap is a slap
  3. Pick another Scale
  4. Press ▶ Play the band

Try this: Start and end on a ringed note. That is the home note.

Think about it

How did the new scale change the mood?

Your turn

Write the frets of a five-note idea you liked.

Lesson 15 · Skill

Slap, pop and bend

Funk bass players slap the low strings with the thumb and pop the high ones.

  1. In Solo mode, flick a string up fast to pop it
  2. In Solo mode, push a note across its string to bend it
  3. Press Chords to go back

Try this: Slap a low note, then pop an octave up: thump, snap.

Think about it

Which sounds more like a drum: the slap or the pop?

Your turn

Write a slap and pop pattern, like T T P T.

Lesson 16 · Skill

Record a take and share it

A take keeps everything you play. Send it to the Mixing Desk or the turntables.

  1. Press ● Record
  2. Play, then press ■ Stop to keep the take
  3. On your take, press Send to the Mixing Desk
  4. Press Send to the turntables

Try this: Record two takes of the same line. Keep the one you like best.

Think about it

What changed between your first take and your second?

Your turn

Plan a 30-second take: what do you play first, and what comes next?
