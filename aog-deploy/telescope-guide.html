iWhat everything is

The eyepiece is on the left. Every control is in the panel on the right, and the knobs are under the eyepiece.

How big? 900 ÷ the eyepiece. 25 mm = 36× · 10 mm = 90× · 6 mm = 150×.

The honest ceiling

The lander is 0.002″ across from here. This telescope can split about 1.3″. That is hundreds of times too small. Bigger magnification does not change it.

★More things in the sky

After Mission 3 the Target menu opens up. Every picture here is the real eyepiece. The small ones are close-ups of the middle.

Saturn, 150×The rings, the dark Cassini gap, the ring shadow on the globe. The point off to the side is Titan.
Mars, 300×Small and orange. A white cap at the top, a dark wedge in the middle. It is not quite round: not quite full.
Venus, 150×A bright crescent, like a tiny Moon. No markings. It is all cloud.
Pleiades, 36×Seven bright sisters. The faint veil hangs from Merope, the lowest one. Use the 25 mm.
Andromeda Galaxy, 36×A soft oval glow with a bright middle, wider than the glass. The small smudge below is M32.
M13, 150×A ball of stars. The middle stays a glow; the edge breaks into single points.
Ring Nebula, 150×A tiny gray smoke ring with a darker hole. At 36× it is only a fuzzy star.
Mizar, 150×Two white stars 14″ apart. Easy. Alcor sits 12′ away; the 25 mm shows both.
The 2.4″ pair + BarlowTwo points with a notch of dark between them. Wider than 1.3″, so this lens can do it.

What is real here? Every sea on the Moon, every belt on Jupiter and every star in these pictures is drawn from where it really is and how a 90 mm lens really shows it. The colors are what the eye sees, not what a camera collects.

!The Sun: only with the filter on the front

NEVER

No filterThe cap stays on. An unfiltered Sun burns the eye.
Solar filter: FrontNow it is safe. Those dark spots are sunspots.
What the screen saysPress Front to put the safe filter on.
Solar filterOnly Front. A filter in the eyepiece can crack.
REAL LIFE: never point a real telescope or binoculars at the Sun without a proper solar filter on the front. Ask an adult.

?Something looks wrong

  • The circle is blackLens cap: Off. (On the Sun, the filter goes on first.)
  • The sky slides out of the circleMount: Locked.
  • Blurry after changing the eyepieceThat always happens. Use Fine.
  • Can’t find itTap the target button again; it re-centers. Or drag the field.
