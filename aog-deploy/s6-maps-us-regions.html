Three tools, three jobs

The KEY. It tells you what the symbols mean — a star for a capital, a dot for a city.

The KEY explains symbols, the SCALE measures distance, the COMPASS gives direction. Three different jobs — and reading a map means checking the title and all three before you look at anything else.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

To find a distance with the scale, add the numbers.

You MULTIPLY. Measure how many inches the trip is, then multiply by what one inch stands for. Three inches at fifty miles an inch is 150 miles — adding would give you 53, which is not a distance at all.

Measure, then multiply.

⚠️ People often think…

Illinois is in the West, because it is west of New York.

Regions are fixed names, not directions from where you happen to be standing. Illinois is MIDWEST — the middle of the country. The West is out past the Plains and the Rockies.

Illinois is Midwest. A region is a name.

Worth knowing cold

The land, worth knowing cold

Four facts about the shape of the country. Each one explains something about how people live there.

  1. Rockies In the WEST. Tall, jagged and young — young mountains have not been worn down yet, which is why they are so sharp.
  2. Appalachians In the EAST. Low, rounded and very old — hundreds of millions of years of weather have smoothed them down.
  3. The Plains BETWEEN the two, flat and deep-soiled — which is why this is farm country and why Illinois looks the way it does.
  4. Five regions Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, West. Illinois is MIDWEST — not West and not Northeast.

1 Watch one

On a map, one inch stands for 50 miles. Two cities are 3 inches apart. How far is it?

  1. Find the scale first: 1 inch = 50 miles.
  2. Measure the gap on the page: 3 inches.
  3. Multiply, do not add: 3 × 50.
  4. 150 miles. Adding would have given 53, which would be a strange-looking answer. ✓

Helpful Hints

🪜 From a page to the whole country — the ladder

A map is a place from above → read the title, key, compass and scale → physical maps show nature, political maps show borders → the country has five regions with different land and water → Illinois is in the Midwest, between the Mississippi and Lake Michigan.

Title, key, compass, scale — then the regions.

🤝 The whole topic in one idea

A map answers a question — if you read its four parts first. The country is five regions, and each one’s land, water and weather explain how people live there.

Read the map; the map explains the people.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • The KEY explains symbols. The SCALE measures distance. The COMPASS gives direction. Three different jobs.
  • Scale: measure the inches, then MULTIPLY. Don’t add.
  • Rockies west (tall, young). Appalachians east (low, old). Plains between.
  • Illinois is MIDWEST — not West, not Northeast.
  • Southeast is down-right; Southwest is down-left. North is up.
  • Physical map for nature questions; political map for people questions; sometimes both.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A map with a strange symbol — “what is it?” (look in the key).
  • A scale and a ruler — “how far?” (inches × the scale).
  • A blank U.S. map — “label the five regions, the Rockies, the Mississippi.”
  • A question — “physical or political map?” (nature or people?).

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Name the four parts of a map and what each does?
  • Use a scale to turn 3 inches into miles?
  • Place the five regions on a blank map?
  • Name two mountain ranges, two oceans and one river?
  • Say where Illinois is using a region, a lake and a river?
