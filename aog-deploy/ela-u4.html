Writing and Telling

Writing and Speaking

You lost a tooth. You found a worm. Your shoe fell in a puddle. Those are stories. They are true. They are yours. In this unit you learn to tell them so a reader can see them.

A story has a beginning, a middle and an end. It has order words. It has details. You will also write what you think and why. You will write a page of true facts. And you will fix your sentences.

Then you will say it out loud. Loud and clear. In order. With details. You will listen to others. You will ask questions. You will share your writing and hear what your friends think.

The big question

How do you tell a true story so that someone else can see it and hear it?

The steps
Day 1

Something small happens to you. A tooth comes out at lunch.

Day 2

You close your eyes and see the moment again. Who? Where? What?

Day 3

You draw three boxes: beginning, middle and end.

Day 4

You write one line under each box, using first, next, then and last.

Day 5

You add details a reader can see: the red apple, the wiggle.

Day 6

You add a feeling word. You felt proud.

Day 7

You fix your sentences: capital letter, spaces, a mark at the end.

Day 8

You practice reading it loud and clear, with your chin up.

Day 9

You sit in the author's chair and read it to the class.

Day 10

You listen to two questions and add the best idea to your story.

Writing
Big questionHow do you turn a small thing that happened into a story a reader can see?
The story

The Lost Tooth, Told Three Ways

Jamal lost a tooth. He told it three times. Each time it got better.

Jamal lost a tooth at lunch. He ran to his teacher. He said, "I lost my tooth!" That was all. His teacher smiled. She said, "Tell me more. What happened first?"

So Jamal tried again. "First I bit my apple. Then my tooth felt loose. Then it fell out. Last I put it in a napkin." Now his story had a beginning, a middle and an end.

His teacher asked, "What did you see? What did you feel?" Jamal thought. "The apple was red and crunchy. My tooth wiggled like a little door. I saw a tiny drop of blood. I felt proud."

Jamal told it one more time. This time he used all of it. First, next, then, last. The red apple. The wiggle. The proud feeling. His teacher said, "Now that is a story."

Talk about itWhich telling did you like best? What did Jamal add each time?
Section 1

Three Parts of a Story

7.1

A Beginning, a Middle, an End

Main ideaEvery story has a beginning, a middle and an end.

Here is a tiny story. Mia found a kitten. She gave it milk. It fell asleep in her lap. That is the whole story. It has three parts.

The tells who and where. Mia found a kitten. The tells what happened. She gave it milk. The tells how it turned out. The kitten fell asleep.

Think of a story like a walk. You start somewhere. You go somewhere. You stop somewhere. Beginning, middle, end. Every story you tell can have all three.

Think it through
The Story in Three Pictures

Picture one: a boy holds a kite in a field. Picture two: the kite is way up in the sky. The boy runs. Picture three: the kite is stuck in a tree. The boy laughs.

Which picture is the beginning? Which is the end?

Words to know
beginning
the first part of a story, where it starts
middle
the part of a story where things happen
end
the last part of a story, where it stops
Check yourself

1. What does the beginning of a story tell?

Why: The beginning tells who is in the story and where they are.

2. In Mia's story, what is the middle?

Why: The middle tells what happened. Mia gave the kitten milk.

3. The kitten fell asleep. Which part is that?

Why: The end tells how the story turned out. The kitten fell asleep.
7.2

Draw It First

Main ideaDrawing your story first helps you know what to write.

Leo wants to write about the beach. He does not know how to start. So he draws. Sand. Waves. A crab. His dad. Now he sees his story. Now he can write it.

A is a picture you make. You can draw before you write. Draw the beginning. Draw the middle. Draw the end. Three boxes. Three pictures.

Then look at each picture. Say what you see. Write that down. Leo wrote: I saw a crab. It ran under a rock. Dad laughed. His pictures gave him the words.

Think it through
Three Boxes

Look at Leo's paper. Box one: a boy and a dad walk on sand. Box two: a small crab by a rock. Box three: the dad laughs and the crab is gone. Under each box there is one line for words.

What words would you write under box two?

Words to know
drawing
a picture you make with a pencil or crayon
Check yourself

1. Why did Leo draw before he wrote?

Why: Leo did not know how to start. His pictures showed him what to write.

2. How many boxes did Leo draw?

Why: He drew three boxes: beginning, middle and end.

3. What do you do after you draw?

Why: Look at each picture. Say what you see. Then write it down.
Section 2

Tell a Small Moment

7.3

Something That Really Happened

Main ideaA small moment is a short, true story about one thing that happened to you.

You do not need a dragon. You do not need a spaceship. You lost a mitten. You found a worm. You rode a bus. Those are stories too. They are true. They are yours.

A is one little thing that really happened. Not your whole day. Just one part. Not the whole trip to the zoo. Just the goat that ate your map.

Pick one small moment. Close your eyes. See it again. Where were you? Who was there? What happened? Then tell it. That is a true story.

Think it through
One Slice of the Day

Ava's day: she woke up, ate toast, rode the bus, did math, had recess, went home. At recess her shoe fell in a puddle. Her sock got cold and wet. She hopped on one foot all the way inside.

Which part of Ava's day would make the best small moment? Why?

Words to know
small moment
one short, true thing that happened to you
true
real; something that really happened
Check yourself

1. What is a small moment?

Why: A small moment is one little thing that really happened to you.

2. Which one is a small moment?

Why: The goat and the map is one small, true thing that happened.

3. Before you tell, what should you do?

Why: Seeing the moment again helps you remember where you were and what happened.
7.4

First, Next, Then, Last

Main ideaOrder words like first, next, then and last show what happened in order.

Read this. I put on my boots. I got in the snow. I got up. I fell down. Wait. That is mixed up. You fell, then got up. The order is wrong.

fix it. First, I put on my boots. Next, I went out in the snow. Then, I fell down. Last, I got up. Now a reader can follow you.

First tells what came first. Next and then tell what came after. Last tells the end. Use them like stepping stones. One step. Then the next.

Think it through
Making a Sandwich

Look at the counter. Two slices of bread. A jar of jam. A knife. Now think of the steps. Open the jar. Spread the jam. Put the bread together. Take a bite.

Say the steps with first, next, then and last.

Words to know
order words
words like first, next, then and last that tell what came when
order
the way things happen, one after another
Check yourself

1. Which word tells what came at the end?

Why: Last tells the end. First tells the start.

2. Why do we use order words?

Why: Order words show what came when. A reader can follow along.

3. Which sentence is in the right order?

Why: You put on boots before you go out. That order makes sense.
7.5

Details a Reader Can See

Main ideaDetails tell what you saw, heard and felt so a reader can see it too.

I had a dog. That is true. But you cannot see the dog. Now read this. I had a small brown dog with one floppy ear. Now you see him.

A is a small piece of what you saw. Colors. Sizes. Sounds. Smells. What did it look like? What did it sound like? How did it feel?

Add one detail at a time. The apple was red. The apple was red and crunchy. It went crack when I bit it. Each detail is a little window. The reader looks through it.

Read the numbers
Counting Details

counted for this lesson

Which sentence helps you see the dog best? What does each new detail add?

Words to know
detail
a small piece of what you saw, heard, smelled or felt
Check yourself

1. What is a detail?

Why: A detail is a small piece, like a color, a size or a sound.

2. Which sentence has more details?

Why: Small and brown are details. They help the reader see the dog.

3. Why add details?

Why: Details are like windows. The reader looks through them and sees what you saw.
Section 3

Other Things to Write

7.6

What I Think and Why

Main ideaAn opinion tells what you think, and a reason tells why.

My favorite season is winter. That is what I think. Not everyone agrees. Some kids love summer. That is fine. What I think is called an .

An opinion needs a . A reason tells why. My favorite season is winter because I love to sled. The word because opens the door to the reason.

Try it. Pick a favorite. A food. A color. A game. Say what you think. Then say because. Then say why. That is an opinion with a reason.

From the source
Sam's Opinion Page
My favorite lunch is pizza. I like pizza because it is warm and cheesy. I also like it because I can eat it with my hands. Pizza day is the best day.
written for this lesson

What is Sam's opinion? Find the two reasons.

Words to know
opinion
what you think or feel about something
reason
why you think it
Check yourself

1. Which one is an opinion?

Why: Best is what someone thinks. Not everyone will agree. That makes it an opinion.

2. What word often opens the door to a reason?

Why: Because tells the reader that a reason is coming.

3. Why does an opinion need a reason?

Why: A reason tells why. It helps a reader understand your opinion.
7.7

A Page of True Facts

Main ideaA fact book page teaches a reader something true about one topic.

Frogs have wet skin. Frogs lay eggs in water. Baby frogs are called tadpoles. These are not opinions. They are true for everyone. They are .

A fact book page is about one . Frogs. Trucks. The moon. It gives facts a reader can learn. Start with the topic. Then write two or three true things.

Where do facts come from? From looking. From books. From people who know. If you are not sure it is true, do not write it. Check first.

From the source
All About Ants
Ants are insects. An ant has six legs. Ants live together in big groups. Some ants carry food that is bigger than they are. Ants are small but strong.
written for this lesson

What is the topic? Name one fact you learned.

Words to know
fact
something that is true for everyone
topic
the one thing a page is about
Check yourself

1. Which one is a fact?

Why: Frogs laying eggs in water is true for everyone. Cutest and gross are opinions.

2. What is a topic?

Why: The topic is what the page is about, like frogs or ants.

3. You are not sure a fact is true. What should you do?

Why: A fact must be true. If you are not sure, check before you write it.
7.8

Fixing a Sentence

Main ideaA sentence starts with a capital letter, has a space between words and ends with a mark.

Look at this. the dogran fast. Something is wrong. Three things are wrong. Can you find them?

First, a starts with a . The, not the. Second, words need spaces. Dog ran, not dogran. Third, a sentence ends with a mark. A period. A question mark. An exclamation point.

Now fix it. The dog ran fast. Capital T. Spaces. A period. Read your writing out loud. Your ears will find what your eyes miss.

Think it through
Spot the Fixes

Here is a line from Ben's paper: my cat sat on themat. Look close. Is the first letter big? Are all the words apart? Is there a mark at the end?

What three things would you fix in Ben's sentence?

Words to know
sentence
a group of words that tells one whole idea
capital letter
a big letter, like T or M, that starts a sentence or a name
Check yourself

1. How does a sentence start?

Why: A sentence starts with a capital letter, like The or My.

2. What is wrong with dogran?

Why: Dog and ran are two words. They need a space between them.

3. Which sentence is fixed right?

Why: It has a capital T, spaces between all words and a period at the end.

Say It, Write It, Share It

Speaking and Listening
Big questionHow do you share what you know so others can hear it, and hear what they share back?
The story

Priya in the Author's Chair

Priya wrote a story. Now she has to read it out loud. Her hands shake a little.

In Priya's class there is a special chair. It is blue. When you sit in it, you are the author. You read your writing. Everyone else listens. Today it is Priya's turn.

Priya sits down. Her paper shakes. She takes a breath. She reads loud and slow. "My little brother lost his shoe at the park. We looked under the slide. We looked in the sand. A dog had it!"

The class laughs at the dog part. Then hands go up. Max asks, "What color was the dog?" Priya says, "Brown, with a white spot." Lily asks, "Did you get the shoe back?" Priya grins. "Yes. It was wet."

Priya stands up. Her hands are not shaking now. Her teacher says, "You read loud and clear. And you answered two good questions." Priya thinks, I might add the wet shoe to my story.

Talk about itWhat did the two questions help Priya think about? What would you ask her?
Section 1

Being a Good Listener

8.1

Listen and Take Turns

Main ideaGood talk means one person speaks while the others listen, and then you switch.

Three kids talk at once. "I saw a—" "My dog—" "Wait, listen—" Nobody hears anything. It is just noise. Now one kid talks. The others look at her. They hear every word.

To means to hear and think about it. Look at the person. Keep your hands still. Wait until they are done. Then it is your turn.

Taking is like passing a ball. One person has it. Then they pass it. Everyone gets a turn. Nobody grabs. That is how a good talk works.

Think it through
The Talking Circle

Look at the rug. Six kids sit in a circle. Rosa holds a small stone. Only the one with the stone talks. She tells about her cat. When she is done, she hands the stone to Deon. Now it is his turn.

Why does the class use a stone? What does it help them do?

Words to know
listen
to hear what someone says and think about it
turn
your time to talk while others listen
Check yourself

1. Three kids talk at once. What happens?

Why: When everyone talks at once, it is just noise. Nobody can hear.

2. What does it mean to listen?

Why: Listening is hearing what someone says and thinking about it.

3. What is taking turns like?

Why: One person has the turn, then passes it. Everyone gets one.
8.2

Ask When You Do Not Know

Main ideaIf you do not understand, ask a question; it helps you and the speaker.

Mr. Cole says, "Put your work in the bin." Kai does not know which bin. He could guess. He could sit and worry. Or he could ask. "Which bin, Mr. Cole?"

A is how you find out. Asking is smart. It is not a bad thing. Everyone gets confused sometimes. A question fixes it fast.

Good questions start with who, what, where, when, why or how. What do you mean? Where does it go? Can you say it again? Ask, and you will know.

Think it through
Two Kids, One Direction

The teacher says, "Get your folder and line up." Nia grabs her folder and goes to the door. Tomas is not sure which folder. He raises his hand. "The red one or the blue one?" The teacher says, "Blue." Now Tomas knows.

What might have happened if Tomas guessed instead of asking?

Words to know
question
something you ask to find out what you do not know
Check yourself

1. Kai does not know which bin. What is the best thing to do?

Why: A question finds out fast. Asking is smart.

2. Which words start good questions?

Why: Who, what, where, when, why and how are question words.

3. Is asking a question a bad thing?

Why: Everyone gets confused sometimes. Asking is how you fix it.
Section 2

Speaking So People Hear

8.3

Loud and Clear

Main ideaSpeak loud enough to reach the back, slow enough to be understood, and look up.

Ella tells her story to the rug. Her voice is soft. She talks fast. Her hair hides her face. The class leans in. They still miss most of it. Then Ella looks up. She speaks slow and . Now they hear it all.

To speak loud does not mean to yell. It means your voice reaches the back of the room. To speak means every word comes out whole. Not mushed together.

Try this. Lift your chin. Look at a face in the back. Say each word. Take a breath at the end of a sentence. Your voice is a gift. Let people have it.

Read the numbers
Who Heard Ella?

counted for this lesson

There are 20 kids. Which way of talking reached them all? Why?

Words to know
loud
strong enough to reach across the room, not yelling
clear
easy to understand; every word comes out whole
Check yourself

1. What does speaking loud mean?

Why: Loud means your voice reaches the back of the room. It is not yelling.

2. What does clear mean?

Why: Clear means each word is easy to hear and understand.

3. Ella's hair hid her face. What should she do?

Why: Looking up sends your voice out to the room.
8.4

Tell It in Order

Main ideaWhen you tell a story or give directions, say the steps in the order they happen.

Ben tells how to get to the gym. "Turn left. Well, first go out the door. Then, um, past the office." The class is lost. He mixed up the .

Ben tries again. "First, go out our door. Next, turn left. Then, walk past the office. Last, the gym is on your right." Now everyone can find it.

Stories work the same way. Say what happened first. Then what came next. Then how it ended. Use first, next, then and last. Your listener will follow you.

Think it through
Washing Hands

Look at the sink. Soap. Water. A towel. Now think of the steps. Turn on the water. Wet your hands. Rub the soap. Rinse. Dry. What if you said dry first, and rub soap last?

Say the hand-washing steps out loud with first, next, then and last.

Words to know
steps
the parts of a story or directions, one after another
directions
steps that tell someone how to do something or where to go
Check yourself

1. Why was the class lost the first time?

Why: Ben said turn left before go out the door. The order was mixed up.

2. What comes last in Ben's directions?

Why: Last, the gym is on your right. That is the end of the directions.

3. What words help you tell steps in order?

Why: First, next, then and last are order words. They show what comes when.
8.5

Describe What You See

Main ideaTo describe a person, place or thing, tell details like size, color, sound and feel.

Guess what I am thinking of. It is a thing. Hard to guess, right? Now listen. It is small. It is round. It is red. It is crunchy. You bite it. An apple!

To means to tell what something is like. Use details. How big? What color? What sound? How does it feel? What does it smell like?

Describe a person: my grandpa is tall with a gray beard and a soft voice. Describe a place: the library is quiet and smells like paper. Details paint the picture.

Think it through
Describe the Bus

Look at the school bus. It is long and yellow. The door folds open with a hiss. Inside it smells like rubber. The seats are green and bumpy. When it starts, it rumbles under your feet.

Which detail is about a sound? Which one is about a smell?

Words to know
describe
to tell what something is like, using details
Check yourself

1. What does describe mean?

Why: To describe is to tell what something is like, with details.

2. Which one describes an apple best?

Why: Small, round, red and crunchy are details. They help you picture it.

3. Which detail tells about a sound?

Why: A soft voice is something you hear. Tall is size. Smell is not sound.
Section 3

Sharing Our Words

8.6

Share and Get Feedback

Main ideaWhen you share your writing, listeners can tell you what they liked and what they wonder.

Priya read her story. Max asked about the dog. Lily asked about the shoe. Those questions were . They told Priya what her readers wanted to know.

Feedback is what someone tells you about your work. Good feedback says two things. What I liked. What I wonder. I liked the dog part. I wonder what color it was.

When you get feedback, listen. Say thank you. Then think. Should I add that? Priya added the wet shoe. Her story got better because she shared it.

feedback
what someone tells you about your work to help it get better
share

Check yourself

Why: Feedback is what a listener tells you to help your work get better.

2. Good feedback says what two things?

Why: Tell what you liked. Then tell what you wonder about.

3. What did Priya do with her feedback?

Why: Priya listened and thought. Then she added the wet shoe to her story.
8.7

Words That Show Feelings

Main ideaFeeling words like proud, scared and excited tell a reader what is inside you.

Jamal said, "I felt proud." Priya said, "My hands shook." Both told a . Proud is a feeling word. Shaking hands show a feeling too. Nervous.

There are many feeling words. Happy. Sad. Mad. Scared. Excited. Shy. Proud. Surprised. Each one tells what is going on inside a person.

Add a feeling to your story. I lost my tooth. I felt excited. Or show it. I jumped up and down. Both let the reader feel it with you.

feeling
what is going on inside you, like happy, sad or scared
proud

Check yourself

2. Priya's hands shook. What feeling does that show?

Why: Shaking hands show a person is nervous.

3. How can you show a feeling without naming it?

Why: I jumped up and down shows excited without saying the word.
8.8

New Words from Books

Main ideaBooks give you new words; say them, and use them when they fit.

Deon read a book about a fox. The fox was . Deon did not know that word. He asked. Sly means tricky and clever. Now he had a new word.

Books are full of new words. When you find one, stop. Ask what it means. Say it out loud. Then find a place to use it. My cat is sly. She hides my sock.

Use a new word where it fits. Sly fits a fox or a tricky cat. It does not fit a rock. The more you use a word, the more it is yours.

Think it through
A Word from the Story

Look at Deon's paper. He wrote: The fox was sly. Then he wrote: My little sister is sly. She hid my crayons and smiled. His teacher put a star next to the second one.

Why did the teacher like the second sentence? Where else could sly fit?

Words to know
sly
tricky and clever
new word

Check yourself

Why: Sly means tricky and clever, like a fox.

2. You find a new word in a book. What do you do first?

Why: Ask what it means. Then say it and use it.

3. Where does the word sly fit?

Why: A cat that hides things is tricky and clever. That is sly.
