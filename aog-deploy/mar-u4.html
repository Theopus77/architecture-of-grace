Wrestling Older Than the League

Origins

Wrestling may be the oldest sport people still play. Painters in Egypt showed it on tomb walls about 4,000 years ago. Storytellers in Mesopotamia put it in the Epic of Gilgamesh. Festivals in Mongolia, Turkey and Iran still hold it today.

Schools and leagues came much later. Weight classes, padded mats and match clocks are all modern. In Illinois, the IHSA crowned its first state wrestling champions in 1937. Girls got their own state finals in 2022.

In this unit you will line up old evidence and new records. You will be able to say what a date measures. You will tell a legend from a record. You will explain how a state association turned an old practice into a school sport.

The story

Two Wrestlers in a Glass Case

A small figure of two wrestlers is far older than any gym clock.

A museum hall is quiet. In one glass case stands a small metal figure. It shows two wrestlers gripping each other. Each one balances a jar on his head. A label says it was found at Khafajah, in today's Iraq.

The label gives a date of about 2600 BCE. That is more than 4,500 years ago. The people who made it were Sumerians. They lived between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers. Nobody wrote down who these two men were.

Down the hall, a school trophy case holds a newer object. It is a plaque from a high school wrestling tournament. It names a weight class and a year. It was made less than a hundred years ago.

Both objects show wrestling. But they come from very different rooms. The old figure comes from a world with no weight classes and no clock. The plaque comes from a school sport with a rule book. This chapter follows wrestling from old walls to the modern mat.

7.1

The Tombs at Beni Hasan

Main ideaTomb paintings at Beni Hasan in Egypt show hundreds of wrestling pairs from about 4,000 years ago.

Beni Hasan is a row of rock tombs in Middle Egypt. The tombs are cut into cliffs on the east bank of the Nile River. Local governors were buried there about 4,000 years ago. Historians call that time the Middle Kingdom.

Painters covered some tomb walls with rows of pairs. One wall shows hundreds of pairs, one after another. Each pair is painted in a different grip. Painters used a darker and a lighter skin color so viewers could tell the two men apart.

The paintings do not tell us the rules. They do not say who won. But they are , or proof, that wrestling mattered in Egypt long ago. Governors chose it for the walls of their own tombs, next to scenes of farming, hunting and soldiers.

Main ideaAncient Greeks trained in a building called a palaestra and added wrestling to the Olympic Games.

In ancient Greece, many cities had a . It was a building with an open courtyard for wrestling. Young men trained there under a teacher. The word comes from pale, the Greek word for wrestling.

Sumo at the Japanese Court

Main ideaSumo has old roots in Japan, from legends to court festivals to paid tournaments.

is Japan’s best-known wrestling. An old book called the Nihon Shoki was finished in 720 CE. It tells a legend of a match between two strong men. One of them, Nomi no Sukune, is honored in sumo as a founder. Historians treat the tale as legend.

Records show sumo at the emperor’s court in the Heian period, which began in 794. Wrestlers came from the provinces for a yearly court festival. The contests were part of court ceremony, with music and officials.

Later, in the Edo period (1603 to 1868), sumo became a paid public show. Wrestlers joined stables called . Tournaments drew crowds in cities like Edo, now Tokyo. The rule that a wrestler loses by leaving the ring comes from this long history.

Main ideaIn Iran, the zurkhaneh is a traditional gym where wrestling and exercise are joined with poetry and drums.

In Iran, a is a traditional gym. The Persian word means house of strength. The training area is a sunken pit, often with eight sides. Men train in it while others watch from around the edge.

A leader called the sits above the pit. He beats a drum and rings a bell. He also sings or recites verses, often from the Shahnameh. The Shahnameh is a long Persian poem of kings and heroes by Ferdowsi, finished around 1010 CE.

The tradition is called koshti pahlevani, or heroes’ wrestling. It honors good character, like humility and helping others. In 2010, UNESCO listed the rituals of Pahlevani and the zurkhaneh as world cultural heritage.

Wrestling in the Modern Olympics

Main ideaWrestling was in the first modern Olympics in 1896, and weight classes arrived soon after.

The first modern were held in Athens, Greece, in 1896. Wrestling was on the program. There was only one wrestling event. There were no , so a small man could face a much larger one. Carl Schuhmann of Germany won.

The 1904 Olympics were held in St. Louis, Missouri. That year added freestyle wrestling, with several weight classes. Almost every wrestler was American. A weight class groups wrestlers who weigh about the same.

The 1896 event followed Greco-Roman rules. In Greco-Roman wrestling, holds below the waist are not allowed. Freestyle allows them. Both styles have been part of most Olympics since.

The Mat, the Scale and the Clock

Main ideaSchool wrestling added a padded mat, a scale and a clock to a very old practice.

Old wrestling happened on sand, grass or packed earth. School wrestling in the United States happens on a padded . A circle painted on the mat marks where the match counts. The padding was added for safety.

Before a school meet, each wrestler steps on a scale at the . The weigh-in places each wrestler in a weight class. This lets students of many sizes compete fairly. It is a rule that Beni Hasan and Naadam never needed.

High school matches in the United States follow the rule book of the National Federation of State High School Associations, called the NFHS. Its matches have three periods of two minutes each. A clock is one of the newest parts of wrestling’s very long history.

The story

A Program Under a State Seal

A tournament program from 1937 marks a beginning, but not the beginning of wrestling.

Picture a thin paper program from the spring of 1937. At the top is the seal of the Illinois High School Association. Below it are the words State Wrestling Tournament. It is the first one the association ever held.

Inside, the pages are lists. Each list is headed by a weight. Under each weight are names of high school boys and the towns of their schools. Next to the lists are blank lines for scores. Someone at the tournament will fill them in with a pencil.

The paper is new in 1937. Wrestling in Illinois is not. More than a hundred years earlier, a young Abraham Lincoln wrestled in the village of New Salem. In 1908, crowds in Chicago watched a famous professional match.

So what does the program mark? It marks the day wrestling became a state school sport with a champion in each weight. That is a real change. It is just not the first time anyone in Illinois wrestled.

Lincoln at New Salem

Main ideaAbraham Lincoln wrestled at New Salem, Illinois, around 1831, more than a century before the first state tournament.

In 1831, young Abraham Lincoln moved to , a small village on the Sangamon River in Illinois. He worked in a store. In frontier villages, wrestling was a common way for young men to test strength and win respect.

Stories say Lincoln wrestled Jack Armstrong, leader of a local group called the Clary’s Grove boys. People who knew Lincoln told different versions later. Some said it ended in a draw. All agree that Lincoln earned the group’s respect.

Many stories about Lincoln’s wrestling were told after he became famous. Historians treat the details carefully. Still, the National Wrestling Hall of Fame honors Lincoln. The New Salem village has been rebuilt as a state historic site near Petersburg.

Main ideaThe year 1937 marks the start of a school contest, not the start of wrestling.

Historians ask what a date measures. The year 1937 measures when the IHSA first crowned wrestling champions. It does not measure when people first wrestled. For that, we look at like the Beni Hasan tomb paintings, about 4,000 years old.

It helps to line dates up. Beni Hasan is about 4,000 years old. Wrestling joined the ancient Olympics in 708 BCE. Lincoln wrestled at New Salem around 1831. The IHSA tournament came in 1937.

Each of these dates is true. Each answers a different question. Mixing them up is a common mistake. A careful reader always asks: first what, and in which ?

Girls Join the State Finals

Main ideaIllinois girls wrestling held its first IHSA state finals in 2022, eighty-five years after the boys.

For most of IHSA history, the state wrestling tournament was for boys only. Some girls wrestled on boys teams over the years. But there was no separate girls state title in Illinois.

That changed in the 2021–22 school year. The IHSA held its first girls wrestling in 2022. Girls now had their own weight classes and their own champions. Other states had started girls tournaments too.

The gap between 1937 and 2022 is eighty-five years. That gap shows that a sport can exist long before everyone is let into its . Dates of new doors are part of history, just like dates of first titles.

Reading a Tournament Record

Main ideaPrograms, newspapers and association lists are sources, and each shows something different.

A is a record made at the time of an event. A 1937 program is one. A newspaper story from the next morning is another. Historians use primary sources to check what happened.

Each source shows something different. A program printed before a meet lists who entered. A newspaper report tells who won and how many people came. An IHSA champions list gives the names that the association made official.

Sometimes sources disagree about a spelling or a score. Then historians compare them. A careful keeps the name, the school, the weight and the year together. Good records let people today find the wrestlers of 1937.
