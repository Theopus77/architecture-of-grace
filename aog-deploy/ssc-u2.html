Our Community

Civics and Economics

Walk down your street. A mail carrier waves. A bus stops. A store opens its door. These people live and work near you. They are your community. This unit is about them.

First, the people. Who lives near you? Who has a job that helps you? What places do you share? And what happens when something breaks and no one can fix it alone?

Then, the money. Why do people work? What do they buy? Why can't you have everything? You will learn the difference between a need and a want, and what it feels like to save for something big.

The big question

How do people in a community help each other get what they need?

Community
Big questionWho are the people in our community, and how do they help each other?
The story

The Day We Fixed the Swing

One swing hangs by a single chain. A whole block shows up to fix it.

Rosa lives on a street in Chicago. Her favorite swing is at the park. One morning she runs to it. The swing hangs by one chain. The other chain is broken. Rosa cannot swing. She walks home sad.

Rosa tells her dad. Her dad tells Mr. Bell next door. Mr. Bell tells the mail carrier, Ms. Kim. Ms. Kim tells everyone on her route. By dinner, the whole block knows.

On Saturday, people come to the park. Mr. Bell brings a wrench. Ms. Kim brings a new chain. A nurse from the corner brings water for everyone. Rosa's class picks up trash. A firefighter on her day off holds the ladder.

By noon the swing has two chains. Rosa sits down. Her dad pushes. She flies up high. Every person did one small job. Together they fixed the swing.

Talk about itRosa could not fix the swing alone. Who helped? What did each person do?

What a Community Is

3.1

People Near Each Other

Main ideaA community is the people who live and work near each other.

Look out your window. You see houses. You see a store. You see people walking. Those people are your . A community is people who live and work near each other.

You live in a community. So does your teacher. So does the person at the store. You do not need to know every name. You share the same streets. You share the same park.

People in a community need each other. The baker sells bread. The bus driver drives the baker to work. You ride the same bus. Everyone helps in some way.

Think it through
Rosa's Street

Look at Rosa's street. Mr. Bell waters his flowers. Ms. Kim carries a bag of mail. Two kids ride bikes. A bus stops at the corner. A man opens the store door. All of these people live or work here.

Who on Rosa's street is part of her community?

Why: The baker sells bread. The bus driver drives. Everyone helps in some way.

Main ideaCommunities can be big cities, small towns or the open country.

Some communities are big. A has tall buildings. It has many streets. Many people live close together. Chicago is a city in Illinois.

Some communities are small. A has a few streets. It has one school. People often know each other by name.

Some people live in the . Houses are far apart. Farms and fields are all around. Neighbors may live a mile away. They are still a community. They still help each other.

3.5

Every Job Matters

Main ideaA librarian and a farmer help too, and every job in a community matters.

A works at the library. She helps you find a book. She reads stories out loud. She shows you how to use a computer. Books are free to borrow because of her.

A grows food. He plants corn in spring. He picks it in fall. In Illinois, farmers grow lots of corn. Your cereal may come from an Illinois farm.

Some jobs are loud. Some are quiet. Some are far from town. Every job matters. Take away the farmer, and there is no food. Take away the librarian, and there are no books.

Main ideaPeople in a community can solve a problem by each doing a small part.

Rosa told her dad. Her dad told a neighbor. Soon many people knew. Each one asked, what can I do? One brought a tool. One brought a chain. One brought water.

This is how a community works. You do not fix a big problem alone. You . That means you work together. Each person does a small part. The big job gets done.

Needs, Wants and Money

Economics
Big questionHow do people get the things they need and the things they want?
The story

Leo's Lemonade Stand

Leo wants a bike. He has one jar, a pitcher of lemonade and a hot Saturday.

Leo sees a red bike in the store window. He wants it. It costs a lot. Leo has two dollars in a jar. His mom says, you can earn more. So Leo makes a plan.

On Saturday, Leo sets up a table by the sidewalk. He makes lemonade. He makes a sign. Fifty cents a cup. The sun is hot. Neighbors stop by. Ms. Kim buys a cup. Mr. Bell buys two. The jar gets heavy.

At the end of the day, Leo counts. He has eleven dollars. He is thirsty and tired. He walks past the ice cream truck. He wants a cone. A cone costs two dollars.

Leo stops. If he buys the cone, the bike is farther away. If he saves, the bike is closer. He thinks hard. He puts the money back in the jar. The bike can wait, but not for long.

Talk about itLeo had to choose: the cone now or the bike later. What would you choose? Why?

Things We Must Have

Main ideaA need is something you must have to live, like food, water, shelter and clothes.

Some things you must have. You must have food. You must have water. You must have a place to sleep. You must have clothes. These are .

A need is something you cannot live without. Without food, you get weak. Without water, you get sick. Without a home, you get cold and wet. A home is .

Think about your day. You ate breakfast. You drank water. You put on clothes. You slept in a bed last night. You met your needs. Every person has the same four needs.

Main ideaA want is something you would like to have but can live without.

Sometimes it is tricky. Shoes are a need. Your feet must stay safe. A second pair with lights that blink? That is a want. You already have shoes.

Main ideaMoney buys goods, which are things, and services, which is work someone does for you.

Leo sold a good. The lemonade was a thing. Leo’s mom sells a service. She cares for sick people. Both earn money. Both help others.

Making Choices

4.5

You Cannot Buy It All

Main ideaMoney runs out, so you must choose what to buy.

Leo has eleven dollars. The bike costs more. The cone costs two dollars. He cannot have the cone and the bike. Money runs out. So Leo must .

Every person chooses. Your family chooses at the store. Milk or juice? New shoes or a new coat? No one can buy everything. There is never enough money for it all.

When you choose one thing, you give up another. Leo picks the bike. He gives up the cone. That is the cost of his choice. Think before you choose.

Many people save. A family saves for a car. A grown-up saves for a trip. Some people keep money in a . A bank keeps money safe until you need it.

Where Food Comes From

4.8

Farm to Store to Table

Main ideaFood starts on a farm, travels to a store, and ends up on your table.

Look at the milk on your table. Where did it come from? Not the store. The store is just one stop. Milk starts on a . A cow makes it. A farmer milks the cow.

Next a truck picks up the milk. It goes to a place that puts it in jugs. Another truck takes the jugs to the store. A worker puts them on a shelf.

Your family buys the milk. You bring it home. You pour it on your cereal. Farm, truck, store, table. Many workers helped. Each one earned money. Each one did a job.

Count the stops from field to bowl. Who did a job at each stop?

a place where people grow food or raise animals

4.9

Food Is Work Too

Main ideaGrowing food is a job, and the food we buy pays the people who grew it.

A farmer in Illinois plants corn in spring. All summer she waters and weeds. In fall she picks it. That is months of work. Then she sells the corn.

Your family pays for food at the store. Some of that money goes to the farmer. Some goes to the truck driver. Some goes to the store worker. Buying food pays for their work.

Not all food comes from a farm. Some people grow tomatoes in a garden. Some catch fish in Lake Michigan. But most food takes many hands. Every meal is a gift of work.
