Maps and Places

Geography

Where are you right now? In a room. In a building. On a street. In a city or a town. In the state of Illinois. In the United States. On planet Earth. A map can show every one of those places.

In this unit you will learn to read a map and to make one. You will learn the four directions. You will find Chicago and Illinois on a globe. Then you will look at the land and water around you: hills, plains, rivers and one very big lake.

You will also see how weather changes what we wear and do. You will see how people change places, and how places change people. And you will learn ways to take care of the place where you live.

The big question

How do maps and places help us understand where we live?

The story

The Treasure Map

The class hid a treasure. Now they must draw a map so a friend can find it.

Room 12 is quiet. Ms. Ortiz holds up a small gold box. "This is our treasure," she says. "Jamal will hide it. Then we will draw a map. The map will show Priya where it is." Priya waits in the hall.

Jamal hides the box under the reading rug. Now the class draws. They draw the room as if they were birds looking down. A big square is the rug. Small squares are the desks. A long shape is the teacher's table.

Sofia draws a star where the box is. Leo draws a little door. Ms. Ortiz writes a key on the side. It says: star means treasure. Square means desk. Now the map is done.

Priya comes in. She looks at the map. She finds the door. She finds the desks. She walks to the big square. She lifts the rug. There is the gold box! The map worked.

Talk about itWhat did the class draw on the map? What did they leave out?
Section 1

A Picture From Above

5.1

Looking Down on a Room

Main ideaA map is a drawing of a place as seen from above.

Think of a bird. It flies over your classroom. It looks down. It sees the tops of the desks. It sees the top of the rug. It does not see the legs of the chairs. It sees the room from above.

A is a drawing of a place from above. It shows where things are. A map of your room shows the desks, the door and the rug. It shows them the way a bird sees them.

A map is not a photo. It is simpler. It leaves out small things. It keeps the big things. That makes it easy to read.

Think it through
Your Desk From Above

Look at your desk. Now stand up. Look down at it. You see the top. You see a book. You see a pencil. You do not see the legs. That is how a map sees things.

What would you draw if you made a map of your desk?

Words to know
map

Check yourself

2. What would a map of a room show?

Why: A map shows where things are. From above, you see the tops of desks and the rug.

3. Why is a map easier to read than a photo?

Why: A map is simpler than a photo. It keeps the big things and leaves out the small things.
5.2

The Map Key

Main ideaA map key tells what each symbol on a map means.

Look at the treasure map. There is a star on it. What does the star mean? You cannot tell. So the class made a . The key says: star means treasure. Now you know.

A is a small picture that stands for something. A square can stand for a desk. A star can stand for treasure. A wavy line can stand for water. Symbols keep the map simple.

The key is a list. It shows each symbol. Next to it, it says what the symbol means. Always read the key first. Then you can read the map.

Think it through
A Key for the Playground

A map of the playground has three symbols. A circle. A triangle. A long line. The key says: circle means swing. Triangle means slide. Long line means fence. Now look at the map. There are two circles by the fence.

What is by the fence on this playground?

Check yourself

Why: Read the key first. Then you know what every symbol on the map means.
5.3

Bigger and Bigger Maps

Main ideaA map can show a room, a school or a whole neighborhood.

A map can show a small place. Your room. A map can show a bigger place. Your whole school. On a school map, each room is a small box. The gym is a big box. The hall is a long line.

A map can show a . That is the streets and homes around your school. On this map, the school is one small box. The streets are lines. The park is a green shape.

The bigger the place, the smaller each thing looks. On the room map, your desk is a square. On the school map, your whole room is a square. On the neighborhood map, the school is a square.

Section 2

Which Way?

5.4

Near, Far, Left and Right

Main ideaWords like near, far, left and right tell where a thing is.

Priya looks at the treasure map. The star is the rug. It is from the door. Near means close. Far means a long way off. These words help her find the box.

Left and right help too. Hold up your left hand. Now your right hand. On the map, the door is on the left. The windows are on the right. Priya walks to the right side of the room.

Use these words when you give directions. "The book is near the sink." "The ball is far from the fence." "Turn left at the door." Then a friend can find the way.

Think it through
Where Is the Cat?

Look at a picture of a yard. A cat sits on the left. It is near a tree. A dog sits on the right. It is far from the tree. A ball sits in the middle. The cat is close to the ball. The dog is not.

Is the dog near the tree or far from it? How do you know?

Words to know
near
close to something
far
a long way from something
Check yourself

1. Near means what?

Why: Near means close. Far means a long way off.

2. On the treasure map, the star was far from what?

Why: The star was near the rug and far from the door.

3. A friend says, "Turn left at the door." What kind of word is left?

Why: Left and right are direction words. They tell which way to go.
5.5

North, South, East and West

Main ideaNorth, south, east and west are the four main directions on a map.

Left and right can change. Turn around, and left is now right! So map makers use four words that never change. . . East. West. On most maps, north is at the top.

Here is a trick. The sun comes up in the east. It goes down in the west. Face the sunrise. East is in front of you. West is behind you. North is on your left. South is on your right.

Maps often have a small cross with the letters N, S, E, W. Find it. Then you know which way is north. Then you can find every other direction too.

Think it through
The Sun in the Morning

It is morning. Ana stands in her yard. The sun is just coming up. She faces it. Now east is in front of her. Her house is behind her. Her left hand points to the park.

Which direction is the park from Ana? Which direction is her house?

Words to know
north
the direction at the top of most maps
south
the direction at the bottom of most maps
Check yourself

1. On most maps, which direction is at the top?

Why: On most maps, north is at the top. South is at the bottom.

2. Where does the sun come up?

Why: The sun comes up in the east. It goes down in the west.

3. Why do map makers use north and south instead of left and right?

Why: Left and right change when you turn around. North, south, east and west stay the same.

The Globe Is a Model of Earth

Main ideaA globe is a round model of Earth that shows land and water.

Earth is very big. It is round like a ball. We cannot see all of it at once. So people made a . A globe is a small model of Earth. You can spin it. You can hold it.

Look at a globe. Most of it is blue. Blue means water. The big blue parts are oceans. The green and brown parts are . There is more water than land on Earth.

A flat map of the world shows the same thing. Blue is water. Green or brown is land. Rivers are thin blue lines. Lakes are blue shapes with land all around.

5.7

Finding Our Home

Main ideaWe live in Chicago, in the state of Illinois, in the United States.

Find the United States on a globe. It is a big country. It is in the top half of the globe. It has an ocean on its left side and an ocean on its right side. That is our .

The United States has fifty . One is Illinois. Find it in the middle of the country. It is tall and thin. A big lake touches its top right corner. That lake is Lake Michigan.

Chicago is a city in Illinois. It sits right on Lake Michigan. On a map, it is a dot near the top of the state. Many people live in that dot. Maybe you do too.

Think it through
Zooming In

Start with the whole globe. Find the United States. Now zoom in. Find Illinois in the middle. Now zoom in more. Find the big lake in the corner. Find the dot on the lake. That dot is Chicago. Zoom in again. Somewhere in that dot is your school.

Which is bigger, Illinois or Chicago? How can you tell from the map?

5.8

A Route to School

Main ideaA route is the path you take to get from one place to another.

Leo walks to school. He leaves his door. He turns right. He walks past the store. He turns left at the park. He crosses the street at the light. Then he is at school. That is his .

A route is the path from one place to another. You can draw it on a map. Start at home. Draw a line. Follow the streets. End at school. Mark the turns.

A good route map tells you where to turn. It shows the things you pass. The store. The park. The light. Those things help you know you are going the right way.

Think it through
Two Ways to the Park

Mia can go two ways to the park. Way one: go straight up her street. It is short. Way two: go past the library, then turn left. It is longer. But it has a sidewalk the whole way. Mia's mom picks way two.

Why might Mia's mom pick the longer route?

Why: A good route map shows the things you pass. Seeing them tells you that you are on the right path.

Places
Big questionHow does a place shape the people who live there, and how do people shape the place?
The story

Out the Car Window

Grandpa's farm is far from Chicago. Watch what changes on the long drive south.

Nia climbs into the back seat. Her family is driving to Grandpa's farm. It is far to the south. Out the window she sees tall buildings. She sees buses and trains. She sees a slice of blue. That is Lake Michigan.

The car gets on the highway. The tall buildings get small. Then they are gone. Now Nia sees houses with yards. Then big stores. Then fewer and fewer houses. The land gets very flat.

Soon there are fields on both sides. Green corn goes on and on. A red barn sits far away. A tractor moves slowly. The sky looks bigger here. Nia can see all the way to the edge of the land.

The car turns onto a dirt road. Dust flies up. Grandpa waves from the porch. "Same state as Chicago," he says. "Just a different place." Nia looks around. No lake. No tall buildings. Only fields and sky.

Talk about itWhat did Nia see in the city that she did not see at the farm? What did she see at the farm that was not in the city?
Section 1

Land and Water

6.1

Hills and Plains

Main ideaLand can be high and bumpy, like a hill, or low and flat, like a plain.

Land is not all the same. Some land goes up. A is a bump of land that rises up. You walk up one side. You walk down the other. A very big, tall hill is a mountain.

Some land is flat. A is a big piece of flat land. You can see a long way across it. There are no bumps to block your view. Nia saw a plain from the car.

Both hills and plains are called landforms. A landform is a shape the land makes. Look out a window. Is the land near you flat? Or does it go up and down?

Think it through
The Sled Hill

It snowed last night. Kids drag sleds to the park. There is one big hill. They climb up. They zoom down. Next to the park is a flat field. Nobody sleds there. The sleds just sit still on the flat snow.

Why do the kids sled on the hill and not on the field?

Words to know
hill
a bump of land that rises up
plain
a big piece of flat land
Check yourself

1. What is a hill?

Why: A hill is a bump of land. You walk up one side and down the other.

2. On a plain, why can you see a long way?

Why: A plain is flat. Nothing rises up to block your view.

3. A hill and a plain are both…

Why: A landform is a shape the land makes. Hills and plains are both landforms.
6.2

Rivers, Lakes and the Ocean

Main ideaWater on Earth comes in rivers, lakes and the huge, salty ocean.

A is water that moves. It flows from high land to low land. It is long and thin. On a map, it is a thin blue line. Boats can ride on a river.

A is water with land all around it. It does not flow away. Lake Michigan is a lake next to Chicago. It is so big you cannot see the other side. But it is still a lake. Its water is fresh, not salty.

The is the biggest water of all. It is salty. It goes on for days and days. Illinois does not touch an ocean. The United States touches two. One is on the left side of the map. One is on the right.

6.3

Flat Illinois

Main ideaMost of Illinois is flat prairie land, and much of it is now farmland.

Long ago, most of Illinois was . A prairie is a plain covered in tall grass. The grass could grow taller than a person. Wind made it wave like water. Illinois is still called the Prairie State.

Today most of that prairie is . Farmers plowed the grass under. They planted corn and soybeans. When Nia looked out the car window, she saw fields, not tall grass.

Illinois has water too. The Mississippi River runs along its west edge. The Illinois River flows through the middle. Lake Michigan touches the top right corner. Chicago sits on that lake.

Think it through
Tall Grass, Then Corn

Picture a field two hundred years ago. Grass grows higher than your head. Wind blows and the grass waves. Now picture the same field today. Rows of corn stand in straight lines. A tractor rolls between them. Same flat land. Different plants.

What did people do to change the field from grass to corn?

Weather and Seasons

6.4

Four Seasons in Illinois

Main ideaIllinois has four seasons, and the weather changes with each one.

is what the sky is doing today. Sunny. Rainy. Windy. Snowy. In Illinois, the weather changes a lot. That is because we have four .

Winter is cold. Snow falls in Chicago. Lake Michigan can get ice near the shore. Spring is wet and cool. Rain falls. Plants wake up. Summer is hot. Corn grows tall. Fall is cool again. Leaves turn red and gold and drop.

The seasons go in a circle. Winter, spring, summer, fall. Then winter again. Every year, the same order. Farmers plant in spring and pick in fall because of this.

6.5

What Weather Makes Us Do

Main ideaThe weather shapes what people wear and what they do.

It is January in Chicago. Nia puts on a coat, a hat and mittens. She walks to school on a shoveled sidewalk. At recess, she stays inside. It is too cold to play out.

It is July. Nia wears shorts and a T-shirt. She runs through a sprinkler. Her family goes to the beach on Lake Michigan. The city opens the pools.

Weather changes what we wear. It changes what we do. It changes work too. Snowplows run in winter. Farmers work the fields in summer. People . That means they change to fit the weather.

Think it through
Two Backpacks

Look at two backpacks by the door. One has mittens, a scarf and a thermos of hot cocoa. The other has a water bottle, sunscreen and a swimsuit. Both belong to the same kid.

Which backpack is for winter? Which is for summer? How do you know?

Words to know
adapt
to change what you do so it fits the place or the weather
Check yourself

1. Why does Nia stay inside at recess in January?

Why: In January, Chicago is very cold. Kids often stay inside at recess.

2. What does adapt mean?

3. Which job is a winter job in Chicago?

Why: Snowplows run in winter, when snow falls. Beaches and sprinklers are for summer.
Section 3

People and Places

6.6

People Change Places

Main ideaPeople change the land by building roads, planting crops and digging ponds.

Grandpa’s farm was not always a farm. Long ago it was tall grass. People cut the grass. They plowed the dirt. They planted corn. They built a barn and a house. They changed the place.

People change places every day. They roads over fields. They dig ponds so cows can drink. They put up houses and stores. In Chicago, people even made a river flow the other way, long ago.

Look around your neighborhood. The sidewalk. The street. The school. The playground. People made all of that. Under it all is the land that was there first.

Think it through
The Empty Lot

There is an empty lot on the corner. It has dirt and weeds. One spring, people come with shovels. They dig out rocks. They bring in good soil. They plant tomatoes and beans. They put up a little fence. Now it is a garden.

How did the people change the lot? What was there before?

Words to know
build
to make something new, like a road or a house
Check yourself

1. What did people do to turn prairie into a farm?

Why: People cut and plowed the tall grass. Then they planted corn and built a barn.

2. Which one is a way people change a place?

Why: Building a road is something people do. Rain and snow are weather, not people.

3. Why do farmers dig ponds?

Why: Farmers dig ponds so cows and other animals have water to drink.
6.7

Places Shape People

Main ideaThe land and water of a place shape the jobs and lives of the people there.

A town by the ocean often has many fishers. The water is right there. Boats go out in the morning. They come back full of fish. The place gave people that job.

A town on the flat prairie often has many farmers. The soil is rich. The land is flat and easy to plow. Grandpa’s town is a farm town. Its stores sell seeds and tractor parts.

Chicago grew big because of Lake Michigan. Boats could bring things in and out. Then trains met the boats. The place made the city. So places shape people, and people shape places.

Think it through
Two Towns

Town A sits by the sea. Its main street has a boat shop, a net shop and a fish market. Town B sits on flat farmland. Its main street has a seed store, a tractor shop and a grain tower. Each town looks different.

Why does Town A have a net shop and Town B have a seed store?

Words to know
fisher
a person whose job is catching fish
Check yourself

1. Why does a town by the ocean have many fishers?

Why: The ocean is right there. So many people in that town catch fish for a living.

2. What kind of town is Grandpa's town?

Why: Grandpa's town is on flat prairie with rich soil. It is a farm town.

3. What helped Chicago grow into a big city?

Why: Boats on Lake Michigan carried things in and out. Later trains met the boats. The city grew.
6.8

Taking Care of a Place

Main ideaPeople can keep a place healthy by picking up litter, saving water and planting trees.

A place takes care of us. It gives us water, food and air. We can take care of it too. Start with . Litter is trash left on the ground. Pick it up. Put it in a can.

Save water. Turn off the tap while you brush your teeth. Lake Michigan gives Chicago its drinking water. Keep trash out of the lake and the rivers. Clean water is for everyone.

Plant trees. Trees give shade in hot summers. They give homes to birds. Their roots hold the dirt in place when it rains. One kid, one tree, one clean park. That is how you care for a place.
