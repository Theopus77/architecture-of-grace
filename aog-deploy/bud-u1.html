The Prince Who Asked Why

The Buddha's Story

Buddhists all over the world tell a story about a prince named Siddhartha. He lived in India long ago. He had a palace and everything he wanted. But he had a big question.

In this unit you will hear his story as Buddhists tell it. You will learn about the Four Sights, a bowl of rice, and a tree. You will be able to retell the story in order and say what the middle way means.

The big question

Why did a prince leave his palace, and what did Buddhists say he found?

Siddhartha Sees the World

The Buddha's Story
Big questionWhat did the prince see outside the palace, and why did it make him ask why?
The story

Outside the Palace Gate

A prince rides out and sees something new.

Buddhists tell this story. Long ago, a prince named Siddhartha lived in a big palace. He had gardens, music and good food. His father wanted him to be happy every day.

One day the prince rode out of the palace gate. His driver was named Channa. On the road, the prince saw a man with white hair and a bent back. He walked slowly with a stick.

"Who is that?" the prince asked. "That is an old man," said Channa. "Everyone grows old one day." The prince was quiet. He had never seen an old person before.

That night the prince did not sleep well. He kept thinking. Why do people grow old? Why do people get hurt? He wanted to know.

Talk about itWhat question would you ask if you saw something for the very first time?
Section 1

Life in the Palace

1.1

A Prince Named Siddhartha

Main ideaBuddhists tell of a prince named Siddhartha who lived long ago in India.

Buddhists tell a story about a boy named Siddhartha. He lived about 2,500 years ago. His home was in the north of India, near Nepal today.

His father was a king. His mother was a queen named Maya. Siddhartha was a prince. He would grow up to be called the Buddha.

Think it through
Picture the Palace

Look at a big palace with tall walls. Inside are green gardens and a pond with flowers. A boy sits by the pond. He has everything he could want.

What would you like to see if you lived in this palace?

Words to know
prince
the son of a king and queen
Check yourself

1. Who was Siddhartha?

Why: The Buddhist story says Siddhartha was a prince, the son of a king.

2. Where did Siddhartha live?

Why: The story takes place in northern India about 2,500 years ago.

3. What was his mother's name?

Why: Buddhists tell that Queen Maya was his mother. Channa was his driver.
1.2

Walls All Around

Main ideaThe story says the king kept sad things away from his son.

The king loved his son. The story says he wanted the prince to see only happy things. So the palace had high walls.

Inside the walls, no one was sick or old where the prince could see. Siddhartha did not know about sadness yet. Buddhists say he had never seen it.

Think it through
Only Happy Things

Look at the prince in his garden. Music plays. Flowers bloom. Every face he sees is smiling. Outside the wall, the town is busy with all kinds of people.

What might the prince not know about, if he only sees his garden?

Words to know
palace
a very big house where a king or queen lives
Check yourself

1. Why did the palace have high walls?

Why: The story says the king wanted his son to see only happy things.

2. Had the prince seen sadness?

Why: Buddhists tell that he had never seen sadness inside the palace.

3. Who built the walls to keep sad things out?

Why: The story says his father, the king, wanted to protect him.
Section 2

Four Things on the Road

1.3

A Sick Man and an Old Man

Main ideaOutside the walls, the prince saw a sick man and an old man.

Buddhists tell that the prince went outside the walls. First he saw a man who was very sick. Then he saw a man who was very old.

The prince was surprised. His driver said that all people can get sick. All people grow old. The prince felt sad for them.

Think it through
On the Road

Look at the dusty road. An old man walks with a stick. Nearby, a sick man rests by a tree. The prince stops his cart to look.

How do you think the prince felt? What face would he make?

Words to know
sick
not feeling well in your body
Check yourself

1. What did the prince see first outside?

Why: In the story he saw a sick man and an old man.

2. What did the driver tell him?

Why: Channa said sickness and old age come to everyone.

3. How did the prince feel?

Why: The story says he felt sad and began to wonder.
1.4

A Funeral and a Calm Man

Main ideaNext he saw a funeral, and then a calm holy man.

Next the prince saw people carrying a man who had died. His family was crying. This was a funeral. The prince felt very sad.

Last, he saw a holy man in a plain robe. The man looked calm and peaceful. Buddhists call these four the Four Sights.

Read the numbers

Buddhist life stories of the Buddha, such as the Jataka's opening story (Nidanakatha)

Which sight came last?

Words to know
calm
quiet and peaceful inside
Four Sights
the sick man, old man, funeral and holy man the prince saw
Check yourself

1. What did the prince see last?

Why: In the Four Sights, the calm holy man comes last.

2. How many sights are there?

Why: Buddhists call them the Four Sights.

3. How did the holy man look?

Why: The story says the holy man was calm, and this gave the prince an idea.
Section 3

Asking Why

1.5

A Big Question

Main ideaThe prince wondered why people suffer.

The prince kept thinking about what he saw. Why do people get sick? Why do they grow old? Why are people sad?

Buddhists say he wanted to find a way to help. He wanted to understand suffering. Suffering means hurting or feeling sad.

Think it through
Wondering

Look at the prince sitting by his window at night. The moon is bright. He is not playing. He is thinking hard about the people he saw.

What is one big question you wonder about?

Words to know
suffer
to hurt or feel sad
Check yourself

1. What did the prince want to know?

Why: The story says the Four Sights made him ask why people suffer.

2. What does suffer mean?

Why: Suffer means to hurt or feel sad.

3. Why did he keep thinking?

Why: Buddhists say he wanted to find a way to help.
1.6

Leaving the Palace

Main ideaBuddhists tell that the prince left the palace to find an answer.

One night the prince made a hard choice. He left the palace. He left his fine clothes and his riches.

Buddhists tell that he wanted to find an answer to his question. He became a traveler looking for wisdom. His search had begun.

Think it through
Two Kinds of Asking

Some questions have quick answers, like "What is for lunch?" Some questions take a long time, like "Why are people sad?" The prince asked a long-time question.

Can you think of a quick question and a long-time question?

Words to know
wisdom
knowing what is true and good
Check yourself

1. What did the prince leave behind?

Why: The story says he left the palace and his riches to search.

2. Why did he leave?

Why: Buddhists tell that he left to answer his question about suffering.

3. What does wisdom mean?

Why: Wisdom means knowing what is true and good.

Under the Bodhi Tree

The Buddha's Story
Big questionHow did Siddhartha find a middle way, and what happened under the tree?
The story

A Bowl of Rice

A girl brings food to a tired man under a tree.

Buddhists tell this story. A thin, tired man sat under a tree. It was Siddhartha. For years he had eaten almost nothing. He was trying to find an answer.

A young woman named Sujata came down the path. She carried a bowl of rice cooked in milk. She saw the tired man. She gave him the bowl with both hands.

Siddhartha ate the rice. He felt strong again. The story says he thought, "Being so hungry did not help me. I need a better way."

He thanked Sujata. Then he walked to a big fig tree. He sat down under it. He decided to sit and think until he understood.

Talk about itWhy do you think the bowl of rice mattered so much?
Section 1

Too Hard, Too Hungry

2.1

Trying Very Hard

Main ideaBuddhists tell that Siddhartha tried being very hungry, and it did not help.

After he left the palace, Siddhartha looked for answers. Buddhists tell that he tried to be very hard on himself. He ate only a few grains a day.

He grew very thin and weak. He could hardly think. The story says he learned that hurting his body did not bring an answer.

Think it through
Too Tired to Think

Look at a student who skipped breakfast. It is hard to listen. The words on the board look blurry. Her tummy growls.

Why is it hard to learn when you are very hungry?

Words to know
weak
not strong
Check yourself

1. What did Siddhartha try first?

Why: Buddhists tell that he tried being very hungry and hard on himself.

2. Did being hungry help him?

Why: The story says it made him weak and did not bring an answer.

3. What does weak mean?

Why: Weak means not strong.
2.2

Sujata's Gift

Main ideaA girl named Sujata gave Siddhartha a bowl of rice.

Buddhists tell that a young woman named Sujata brought him rice cooked in milk. She shared her food with a stranger.

Siddhartha ate it and felt strong. Buddhists remember Sujata’s kindness. Her small gift helped him on his way.

Think it through
A Kind Act

Look at Sujata walking down the path. She holds a warm bowl with both hands. She sees a tired man. She stops and holds out the bowl.

What small gift could you give to help someone?

Words to know
gift
something you give to help or please someone
Check yourself

1. Who brought the rice?

Why: Buddhists tell that Sujata brought the bowl of rice.

2. What was in the bowl?

Why: The story says it was rice cooked in milk.

3. How did the rice help?

Why: After eating, he felt strong enough to keep searching.
Section 2

The Middle Way

2.3

Not Too Much, Not Too Little

Main ideaBuddhists call the path between too much and too little the middle way.

In the palace, the prince had too much. In the forest, he had too little. Neither one helped him find his answer.

Buddhists say he found a middle way. It is not too much and not too little. The Buddha later taught this in his first talk.

Think it through
The Guitar String

Buddhists often tell this picture. A string that is too tight snaps. A string that is too loose makes no sound. A string that is just right makes music.

How is the string like the middle way?

Words to know
middle way
the path between too much and too little
Check yourself

1. What is the middle way?

Why: Buddhists describe the middle way as between too much and too little.

2. Where did the prince have too much?

Why: In the palace he had riches and everything he wanted.

3. In the string picture, which string makes music?

Why: The just-right string stands for the middle way.
2.4

The Middle Way Every Day

Main ideaPeople can think about balance in everyday life.

Buddhists use the middle way to think about many things. Eat enough, but not too much. Play hard, and rest too.

You can see balance in your day. Too much screen time feels bad. Too little sleep feels bad. Just enough helps.

Read the numbers
A Balanced Plate

USDA MyPlate, 2011 (rounded)

Which food takes up the most room on the plate?

Words to know
balance
just enough of each thing
Check yourself

1. Which shows balance?

Why: Balance means just enough of each, like the middle way.

2. What does balance mean?

Why: Balance is just enough of each thing.

3. Who taught the middle way?

Why: Buddhists say the Buddha taught the middle way in his first talk.
Section 3

The Awakened One

2.5

Sitting Under the Tree

Main ideaBuddhists believe Siddhartha understood under the tree and became the Buddha.

Siddhartha sat under a big fig tree. He sat very still and thought for a long time. Buddhists tell that it was a full-moon night.

Buddhists believe that, by morning, he understood why people suffer and how it can end. From then on he was called the Buddha. Buddha means "the awakened one."

Think it through
Waking Up

Think about waking up in the morning. Your eyes open. The room gets clear. Buddhists use "awake" as a picture word. They mean the Buddha's mind saw clearly.

Why might Buddhists use the word awake for this?

Words to know
Buddha
a title meaning "the awakened one"
awakened
woken up; Buddhists use it to mean seeing clearly
Check yourself

1. What does Buddha mean?

Why: Buddha is a title meaning "the awakened one."

2. Where did Siddhartha sit?

Why: Buddhists tell that he sat under a fig tree, later called the Bodhi tree.

3. What do Buddhists believe happened?

Why: Buddhists believe he awakened under the tree.
2.6

The Bodhi Tree Today

Main ideaA tree grown from a cutting of the old tree still grows in Bodh Gaya.

The place is called Bodh Gaya, in India. The tree is called the Bodhi tree. Bodhi means awakening.

Buddhists say the tree there today grew from a cutting of the old one. Visitors come from all over the world. They bow and leave flowers to show respect.

Think it through
A Visit to Bodh Gaya

Look at a big tree with heart-shaped leaves. Bright flags hang nearby. People sit quietly in its shade. Some bring flowers and set them down gently.

Why do you think people bring flowers to this tree?

Words to know
Bodhi tree
the tree where Buddhists believe the Buddha awakened
Check yourself

1. Where is the Bodhi tree?

Why: The Bodhi tree grows at Bodh Gaya in India.

2. What does Bodhi mean?

Why: Bodhi means awakening.

3. What do visitors do there?

Why: Buddhists show respect with bows and flowers.
