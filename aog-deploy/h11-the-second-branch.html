Social Studies · Activity 11

The Second Branch

One office, a four-year leash, and a desk where every argument lands. The office below is live — play it, tap it, try on the hats. Then three sets: the basics, the office and its tools, and the era's presidents testing its edges.

Before the questions — watch it move

The office at a glance

Ten stops — play them like a film, or tap through. The picture moves: four hats light up over one desk, a four-year clock runs, and the era's presidents test the office one by one. None of it is a test. Every stop ends at the set of questions where that idea is practiced for real.

Show me one first

This one is nobody's answer — it is only here to show the moves before you touch yours. Watching it is instruction, never a trial, and it counts toward nothing.

A storm knocks out a harbor. The governor asks Washington for help, a senator promises a law, an agency ships supplies that same night. Which mover is the executive branch at work?
Sort the jobs: the promised law is legislative — a pen, and slow on purpose. The governor is a STATE executive — right kind of job, wrong government.
The agency shipping supplies under existing law — that is the executive: the branch that ACTS, tonight, inside the rules already written.

Now number 1 is yours. The hint button is there the whole time, and using it is part of the lesson.

Question 1 of 8 One office, many hats, every hat leashed.

Need help?

Answer in your own words. At the bottom, list three things the president CANNOT do alone — and name who must agree for each.

  1. 1Which branch carries out and enforces the nation's laws?
  2. 2How long is one presidential term?
  3. 3The Constitution names the president 'commander in chief.' Commander of what?
  4. 4The heads of the executive departments — State, Treasury, War in the early years — meet as the president's advisors. What is that group called?
  5. 5A bill from Congress reaches the president's desk. What are the president's two main choices?
  6. 6The president picks a cabinet secretary, an ambassador, a judge. Who must approve those picks?
  7. 7The president commands the army — so can the president also DECLARE war?
  8. 8Can a president be removed from office between elections?
How confident are you with this skill?

There is no right answer here. It only tells your teacher where to start next time.

Done

The second branch, held in one hand.

Notice the pattern one more time: for everything the office can do, some other branch holds a handle — the Senate on treaties and seats, Congress on money and war, the courts on the meaning of it all. Power multiplied, and leashed at every point.
