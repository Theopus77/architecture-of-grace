Clean Hands

Health and Safety

Your hands touch hundreds of things every day. A door. A crayon. A dog. A sandwich. Tiny germs ride along on your hands. You cannot see them. But they can make you sick.

In this unit you will find out where germs hide and how they travel. You will learn when to wash. You will learn the five steps and how to count twenty seconds without a clock.

By the end you will be able to wash your hands the right way all by yourself. You will know what to do when there is no sink. And you will know how to keep germs to yourself with an elbow.

The big question

How do clean hands keep me and my friends healthy?

How people learned it
Wake up

Brush your teeth for two minutes before breakfast.

Breakfast

Wash before you eat, then sit down.

Bathroom

Wash every time, even when you are in a hurry.

Recess

Come inside, go to the sink, then do anything else.

Lunch

Wet, lather, scrub for two songs, rinse, dry.

A sneeze

Sneeze into your elbow, toss the tissue, wash.

Pet time

Feed the pet, then wash your hands.

Field trip

No sink? Rub sanitizer until dry with a grown-up.

Dinner

Wash before you help, and wash before you eat.

Bedtime

Brush your teeth again and stand the brush up to dry.

Germs You Cannot See

Hygiene
Big questionIf germs are too small to see, how do we know where they are and how do we stop them?
The story

The Glitter Hands

One sprinkle of glitter shows the whole class where hands have been.

Mr. Lopez put a spoon of glitter in Maya's hand. He asked her to rub her hands together. Then he said, "Go on with your morning. Do not wash yet." Maya opened the door. She picked a book. She sharpened a pencil. She gave Sam a high five.

At snack time the class looked around. There was glitter on the door handle. There was glitter on the pencil sharpener. There was glitter on the book. Sam had glitter on his hand. Sam had rubbed his nose. Now his nose sparkled.

"Glitter is easy to see," said Mr. Lopez. "Germs are not. But germs travel the same way. They ride on hands. They land on what we touch." Maya looked at her sparkly fingers. She thought about her snack.

Then Maya washed with soap and warm water. She scrubbed for twenty seconds. The glitter went down the drain. Her hands were clean. "That," said Mr. Lopez, "is what soap does to germs too."

Talk about itWhere did Maya's glitter travel? Where do you think germs travel in our classroom?
Section 1

Too Small to See

1.1

What a Germ Is

Main ideaGerms are tiny living things that can make you sick, and they are too small to see.

Look at your hand. It looks clean. But tiny may be on it right now. A germ is so small that a thousand could sit on the dot of this i. You cannot see them without a special tool called a microscope.

Some germs make people sick. They can give you a cold. They can give you a tummy ache. Germs get inside your body through your mouth, your nose and your eyes.

Clean hands keep germs out. That is why we wash. Washing is a job you can do all by yourself.

Think it through
The Dot on the i

Look at the dot on the letter i in the word 'sick'. Now picture a thousand tiny germs sitting on that dot. They are so small that the dot still looks the same. Now picture your whole hand.

If a thousand germs fit on one dot, how many could fit on your hand? Why can you not see them?

Words to know
germ
a tiny living thing, too small to see, that can make you sick
microscope
a tool that makes tiny things look big enough to see
Check yourself

1. Why can't you see a germ on your hand?

Why: Germs are so tiny that you need a microscope to see them.

2. Which tool lets people see germs?

Why: A microscope makes tiny things look big enough to see.

3. How do germs get inside your body?

Why: Germs enter through the mouth, nose and eyes, so clean hands matter before you touch your face.
1.2

Germs Ride on Hands

Main ideaGerms travel on hands, in coughs and sneezes, and on food.

Germs cannot walk. They cannot fly on their own. They need a ride. Hands are their favorite ride. You touch a door handle. Germs hop on. You touch your mouth. Germs hop off, right inside you.

A cough or a sneeze is a ride too. Tiny drops fly out of your mouth. Germs ride the drops. They land on desks, toys and other people.

Food can carry germs too. If dirty hands touch a sandwich, the germs go on the sandwich. Then you eat them. Clean hands keep food clean.

Think it through
Follow the Germ

Ben sneezes into his hand. He does not wash. He picks up the red crayon. Ava uses the red crayon next. Then Ava eats a cracker with that hand. Follow the germ from Ben's sneeze to Ava's cracker.

Name each stop the germ made. Where could someone have stopped it?

Words to know
spread
to move from one place or person to another
Check yourself

1. What is a germ's favorite way to travel?

Why: Germs ride on hands from the things we touch to our mouths.

2. Ben sneezes into his hand and picks up a crayon. What happened?

Why: Germs spread from a dirty hand to anything it touches.

3. Dirty hands touch a sandwich. Where do the germs go?

Why: Germs on hands move onto food, and then into the person who eats it.
Section 2

When to Wash

1.3

Before You Eat

Main ideaAlways wash your hands before you eat or touch food.

It is lunch time. Your hands have touched a lot today. The bus rail. Your desk. The ball at recess. Now you will pick up a sandwich with those hands. Stop. Wash first.

Wash before every meal and every snack. Wash before you help make food too. Germs from your hands can go on the food. Then they go in your mouth.

Make it a habit. Line up. Wash. Then sit down to eat. Your food stays clean, and so do you.

Think it through
The Lunch Line

Picture the lunch line. Kai has his tray. He just came in from recess. He touched the swing, the slide and the door. He has not washed. His hands are about to touch his apple.

What should Kai do before he picks up the apple? What might happen if he does not?

Words to know
habit
something you do the same way every time without having to think about it
Check yourself

1. When should you wash before lunch?

Why: Germs you cannot see can be on your hands, so wash before every meal and snack.

2. Kai came in from recess. What is the first step before eating?

Why: Recess hands touched many things; washing with soap takes the germs off.

3. Why wash before helping make food?

Why: Hands that touch food can put germs on it, so clean hands keep food clean.
1.4

After the Bathroom and Outside

Main ideaWash after using the bathroom and after playing outside.

The bathroom is full of germs you cannot see. They are on the handle. They are on the seat. After you use the bathroom, wash your hands. Every time. Even if you are in a hurry.

Outside is fun and a little dirty. Dirt, bugs and playground bars all carry germs. When you come inside, go to the sink. Wash before you do anything else.

Here is the rule. Bathroom, then wash. Outside, then wash. No skipping, even when you are in a hurry.

Why: Bathroom germs are on hands and handles, so wash every single time.

1.5

After Pets and Sneezes

Main ideaWash after you touch a pet and after you cough or sneeze.

You pet a dog. The dog is soft and happy. But the dog’s fur has germs. So does its mouth. After you touch any pet, wash your hands. Wash after feeding a pet too.

You sneeze. Drops fly out. Some land on your hand. If you touch a toy now, the germs go on the toy. So wash after you sneeze or cough. Wash after you blow your nose.

Pets, sneezes, tissues. All three mean go wash. Say it to yourself. Pets, sneezes, tissues, wash.

Think it through
Three Kids, Three Reasons

Lena just fed the class hamster. Omar just blew his nose. Rosa just petted the neighbor's cat. All three walk to the reading rug. None of them stopped at the sink.

Which of the three should wash? Why did each one need to?

Words to know
tissue
a soft paper you use to blow your nose, then throw away
Check yourself

1. You just petted a dog. What should you do?

Why: Pet fur and mouths carry germs, so wash after touching any animal.

2. You blow your nose with a tissue. Then what?

Why: The tissue and your hands both have germs; toss the tissue and wash.

3. Which one does NOT need a wash right after?

Why: Reading does not put new germs on your hands; pets and sneezes do.
Section 3

Stopping Germs

1.6

Soap Lifts, Water Carries

Main ideaSoap lifts germs off your skin, and running water carries them down the drain.

Germs stick to skin. Water alone slides right past them. That is why you need . Soap grabs germs and dirt. It lifts them up off your skin.

Then water does its job. Running water carries the loose germs away. Down the drain they go. Soap and water are a team.

Try it. Wet your hands. Add soap. Rub until you see bubbles. Rinse. Feel how clean your hands are.

Think it through
Two Sinks

At sink one, Jada rinses her hands with water only. She counts to three. At sink two, Theo wets his hands, rubs on soap, scrubs until bubbles cover his hands, and then rinses.

Whose hands are cleaner? What did soap do that water alone could not?

Words to know
soap
what lifts germs and dirt off your skin so water can wash them away
rinse
to run clean water over something to wash the soap away
Check yourself

1. What does soap do?

Why: Soap grabs germs and dirt and lifts them off so water can carry them away.

2. Jada used water only for three seconds. What is wrong?

Why: Water alone slides past germs; soap is needed to lift them.

3. What carries the loose germs away?

Why: After soap lifts the germs, rinsing under running water washes them down the drain.
1.7

Cough Into Your Elbow

Main ideaCover a cough or sneeze with your elbow, not your hand.

A cough is coming. Where does it go? Not into the air. Not into your hand. Into your . Bend your arm. Put your mouth in the crook. Cough there.

Why the elbow? Your elbow does not touch door handles. It does not pick up crayons. Your hand does. Germs in your elbow stay put.

If you use a tissue, throw it away right away. Then wash your hands with soap. Cover, toss, wash.

Think it through
Where the Sneeze Went

Picture Nia feeling a sneeze. She bends her arm and sneezes into her elbow. Next to her, Leo sneezes into his open hand. Then Leo picks up the class tablet. Nia picks up her pencil with a clean hand.

Who spread germs to something the class shares? What should Leo do now?

Words to know
elbow
the bend in the middle of your arm
Check yourself

1. Where should a cough go?

Why: The elbow does not touch shared things, so germs stay put.

2. Leo sneezed into his hand and grabbed the tablet. What happened?

Why: Hands touch shared things; germs on the hand moved to the tablet.

3. You used a tissue for a sneeze. What is the last step?

Why: Cover, toss, wash: the tissue goes in the trash and hands get washed.

Twenty Seconds, Five Steps

Handwashing
Big questionHow do you know your hands are really clean?
The story

How Long Is Twenty Seconds?

The class has no clock at the sink, so they find another way to count.

Ms. Bell asked the class to wash for twenty seconds. Diego turned on the water. He rubbed his hands. He counted, "one, two, three, four, five, done!" It took about three seconds. His hands still had marker on them.

"We need a way to count," said Ms. Bell. "There is no clock at the sink." Priya had an idea. "We could sing!" The class tried the birthday song. They sang it once. Ms. Bell watched the clock. "Ten seconds," she said.

"So sing it twice," said Diego. They tried again. Wet, soap, scrub. They sang the birthday song two times. Ms. Bell called out, "Twenty seconds!" Diego looked at his hands. The marker was gone.

From then on, the sink had a song. Anyone walking past the bathroom could hear it. Two rounds. Twenty seconds. Clean hands, every time.

Talk about itWhat song could you sing two times to count twenty seconds?
Section 1

The Five Steps

2.1

Wet and Lather

Main ideaStep one is wet your hands, and step two is lather with soap.

Stand at the sink. Turn on the water. Warm or cold both work. Step one: your hands all over. Both sides. Then turn the water off to save water.

Step two: get soap. One pump or one rub of the bar is enough. Rub your hands together. Rub until you see bubbles. The bubbles are called . Lather means the soap is working.

Wet first, then soap. If you put soap on dry hands, it will not spread. No bubbles means no lather.

Think it through
The Dry-Hand Mistake

Picture Ali at the sink. He grabs soap first. He rubs it on his dry hands. It sticks in one spot and will not spread. He rubs harder. Still no bubbles.

What step did Ali skip? What should he do to fix it?

Words to know
wet
to cover something with water
lather
the bubbles you make when you rub soap with water
Check yourself

1. What is step one of washing hands?

Why: Wet hands first so the soap can spread and make lather.

2. What are the bubbles called?

Why: Lather is the bubbles made by rubbing soap with water.

3. Ali put soap on dry hands. What went wrong?

Why: Soap needs water to spread and make lather, so wet first.
2.2

Scrub Every Part

Main ideaStep three is scrub for twenty seconds: backs, between fingers and under nails.

Step three: . This is the big one. Rub your palms together. Now rub the backs of your hands. Germs hide there too.

Lace your fingers together. Rub between them. Rub around your thumbs. Now scratch your nails on your palm. Germs love to hide under nails.

Scrub for twenty seconds. That is longer than it feels. Most people stop too soon. Keep going.

Read the numbers
Spots People Forget

the four scrub spots, in order

Which spot is easiest to forget? Point to it on your own hand.

Words to know
scrub
to rub hard all over to get something clean
palm
the flat inside part of your hand
Check yourself

1. Where do germs like to hide?

Why: Nails trap dirt and germs, so scratch them on your palm while scrubbing.

2. How long should you scrub?

Why: Twenty seconds of scrubbing is the standard time to get hands clean.

3. Which part do people often skip?

Why: People rub palms and forget the backs of the hands and between fingers.
2.3

Rinse and Dry

Main ideaStep four is rinse under running water, and step five is dry with a clean towel.

Step four: rinse. Turn the water back on. Hold your hands under it. Let the water carry the soap and germs away. Rinse until no bubbles are left.

Step five: dry. Wet hands pick up germs faster than dry ones. Use a clean paper towel. Or use the air dryer. Dry all over.

Then use the towel to turn off the tap. Now your clean hands never touch the dirty handle.

Think it through
Order the Steps

Here are the five steps, but they are mixed up: dry, wet, rinse, lather, scrub. Say them out loud in the right order. Then act them out with pretend water.

What is the right order? What happens if you rinse before you scrub?

Words to know
tap
the handle you turn to start or stop the water
Check yourself

1. What is the last step?

Why: Drying is step five; wet hands pick up germs faster than dry ones.

2. Why use the towel on the tap?

Why: The handle was touched by dirty hands, so the towel keeps your clean hands clean.

3. What is the right order of the five steps?

Why: Wet, lather, scrub, rinse, dry is the standard order.
Section 2

Counting Twenty

2.4

Sing It Twice

Main ideaTwenty seconds is about two rounds of a short song like the birthday song.

Twenty seconds is longer than it feels. Counting fast does not work. A song works better. The birthday song takes about ten seconds. Sing it two times. That is about twenty seconds.

Sing it in your head or out loud. Scrub the whole time. Do not stop when the first song ends. Start it again.

Try other songs too. Pick a short one. Sing it twice. Ask a grown-up to time you once.

Think it through
One Song or Two?

Picture Mara at the sink. She sings the birthday song one time while she scrubs. She stops. She rinses. Beside her, Jun sings it two times before rinsing.

Who scrubbed for about twenty seconds? How many seconds did the other one get?

Words to know
second
a very short bit of time; a slow count of one is about one second
Check yourself

1. How many times do you sing the birthday song?

Why: One round is about ten seconds, so two rounds make about twenty.

2. Mara sang it once and stopped. About how long did she scrub?

Why: One round of the song is about ten seconds, only half the time needed.

3. Why not just count fast to twenty?

Why: Counting fast takes only a few seconds; a song keeps a steady pace.
2.5

A Clean Towel

Main ideaDry your hands on something clean, or the germs come right back.

Your hands are clean. Now do not put germs back on them. A dirty towel has germs. Your shirt has germs. A clean paper towel does not.

At school, take one paper towel. Dry your hands. Throw it in the trash. At home, use a clean cloth towel. Ask a grown-up to change it often.

An air dryer works too. Hold your hands under it until they are all the way dry. Wet hands pick up germs.

Think it through
Three Ways to Dry

Picture three kids. Ana wipes her wet hands on her jeans. Cole uses the same cloth towel that has hung by the sink all week. Ivy takes one clean paper towel.

Whose hands stay clean? What is wrong with the other two ways?

Words to know
clean
free of dirt and germs
Check yourself

1. What should you dry your hands with?

Why: A clean towel keeps germs from going back onto washed hands.

2. Ana wiped her hands on her jeans. What happened?

Why: Clothes carry germs, so drying on them undoes the washing.

3. What goes wrong with a towel used all week?

Why: A towel used for days collects germs; a clean one should replace it often.
Section 3

No Sink and Clean Teeth

2.6

Hand Sanitizer Helps

Main ideaHand sanitizer is a backup when there is no sink, but soap and water are better.

You are on a field trip. No sink. It is snack time. What now? Use . Squirt a small amount in your palm. Rub it all over both hands. Keep rubbing until they feel dry.

Sanitizer kills many germs. But it does not lift dirt. If your hands look dirty, it will not work well. Soap and water are always better.

Sanitizer is not for eating or for playing. It is only for hands. Ask a grown-up to help you use it.

Think it through
Sink or Squirt?

Picture two places. One: the school lunchroom, with a sink right by the door. Two: a picnic table at the park, with no sink anywhere near. Both times it is snack time and your hands are dirty from playing.

Which place is right for sanitizer? Which place should use soap and water?

Words to know
hand sanitizer
a gel you rub on your hands to kill germs when there is no sink
Check yourself

1. When is hand sanitizer a good choice?

Why: Sanitizer is a backup for times with no sink; soap and water are better.

2. Your hands are covered in mud. What works best?

Why: Sanitizer does not lift dirt; soap and water wash it away.

3. How do you use sanitizer?

Why: Rub sanitizer over both hands until they feel dry, with a grown-up's help.
2.7

Brush Twice a Day

Main ideaBrush your teeth two times a day, morning and night, for two minutes.

Germs live in your mouth too. They eat bits of food stuck on your teeth. That can make holes in teeth. Brushing sweeps the germs and food away.

Brush in the morning. Brush before bed. Use a pea-sized bit of toothpaste. Brush the front, the back and the tops of your teeth. Brush for two minutes. Sing a song here too.

Rinse your brush. Stand it up to dry. Get a new brush when the bristles bend out.

Words to know
toothbrush
a small brush with bristles for cleaning teeth
bristles
the short stiff hairs on a brush
Check yourself

1. How many times a day should you brush?

Why: Brush in the morning and before bed, two times a day.

2. How much toothpaste do you use?

Why: A pea-sized bit is enough for a child's brush.

3. When do you get a new toothbrush?

Why: Bent bristles do not clean well, so the brush gets replaced.
