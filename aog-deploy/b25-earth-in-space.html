Spin for a day, orbit for a year

Earth turns all the way around once. That turn takes 24 hours, and it gives you one day and one night.

Spin gives you the day. Orbit gives you the year. They are two separate motions, and the test loves to swap them — so say which one you mean out loud before you answer.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

The sun is the brightest thing in the sky because it is the biggest star.

It is bright because it is CLOSE. Plenty of stars are far bigger and brighter — they just sit unimaginably farther away, so they look like specks.

Close, not biggest.

⚠️ People often think…

At noon your shadow disappears.

It gets SHORTEST, not gone. The sun is high but not straight overhead here, so there is always a stub of shadow — and it points away from the sun, the way shadows always do.

Shortest at noon, and pointing away.

Helpful Hints

🪜 From your shadow to the stars — the ladder

Earth spins → the sun seems to cross the sky and shadows swing → Earth orbits → the daylight hours and the night stars change with the seasons → the sun is a close star; the others are far → gravity pulls everything toward the center → all of it repeats in patterns.

Spin, orbit, near, far, center, repeat.

🤝 The whole topic in one idea

The sky only looks like it moves — it is Earth that spins and orbits, and everything you see up there follows patterns you can measure with a stick, a shadow and a calendar.

We move; the sky repeats.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • The sun is bright because it is CLOSE, not because it is the biggest star.
  • The sun doesn’t move across the sky — Earth spins. Say it that way on the test.
  • Shadows point AWAY from the sun. Shortest at noon, not gone.
  • Spin = day. Orbit = year. Don’t swap them.
  • Gravity pulls toward the CENTER — there is no bottom of the Earth to fall off.
  • The moon doesn’t change shape — we see different amounts of its lit half.

🕰️ Three clocks in the sky — the chart

PatternHow longWhat you seeWhat causes it

Spin for the day, moon for the month, orbit for the year.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A shadow table — “what time is the shortest? which way does the 3 p.m. shadow point?”
  • “Why does the sun look brighter than other stars?” (close, not biggest).
  • A daylight-hours graph across the year — “which month is longest and why?”
  • “Why don’t people in Australia fall off?” (gravity pulls toward the center).

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Explain the sun’s brightness with distance?
  • Describe a shadow at 9, noon and 3?
  • Tell spin from orbit and what each makes?
  • Explain why the stars change with the seasons?
  • Say where gravity pulls and prove it three ways?
