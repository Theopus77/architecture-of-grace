For the teacher

The course is the academic study of religion, not the practice of it: how to read a sacred text, then the major traditions through their own texts, then the questions they share — as units, chapters, sections and numbered lessons, in original text written at each band’s reading level. Beliefs are always attributed (“Muslims believe…”, “the Torah teaches…”), traditions are never ranked, and every lesson ends with three checks answerable from its reading.

Trusted sources to read alongside the course

  • American Academy of Religion, Guidelines for Teaching About Religion in K–12 Public Schools (2010).
  • Harvard University, The Pluralism Project (pluralism.org): the religions of America, described fairly.
  • Pew Research Center, Religion (pewresearch.org/religion): reliable data on belief and practice.
  • Stephen Prothero, God Is Not One: eight religions, each on its own terms.
  • Charles Haynes and Oliver Thomas, Finding Common Ground: a First Amendment guide to religion in public schools.
