Turn it on and play

Tap a key. Tap a chord pad. Pick a pattern and press Play. That is the whole piano.

On the piano
  1. Open the piano.

  3. Tap a chord pad. Three notes play at once.

  5. Let it play for a few seconds. Then press Stop.

Find C: the black keys show the way

The black keys come in groups of two and three. Every C sits just to the left of a group of two.

On the piano
  1. Look at the black keys. Find a group of two, then a group of three.
  2. The white key just left of the group of two is C. Play it.
  3. Find another C. On a phone, use the C at the right end, or press Higher ▶.
  4. Play the two black keys of a pair. Then play the three of a trio.

Seven letters, then they repeat

The white keys are named A B C D E F G, and then the names start over. From C up to the next C is the C major scale.

On the piano
  1. Find C. Play every white key going up, one at a time, until the next C.
  2. Say each name out loud as you play it.
  3. Now play the same eight keys going down.
  4. Move one octave higher and play the scale going up again. On a computer, the keys A S D F G H J K play it.

Lesson 4 of 38

Octaves: same name, twice as fast

Play a C, then the next C up. It sounds like the same note, only higher. The higher string shakes twice as fast. That jump is an octave.

On the piano
  1. Play a C. Count the white keys up to the next C: that is one octave.
  2. Play both C's, one after the other. Listen: same name, higher sound.
  3. Press Higher ▶ and ◀ Lower. The small number on each C tells you which octave you are in.
  4. Go all the way down and play a low note. Then go all the way up and play a high one.

Open the piano

Lesson 5 of 38

Soft and loud: piano and forte

The piano's full name is pianoforte, Italian for soft-loud. Press near the top of a key for a soft note and near the bottom for a loud one.

On the piano
  1. Pick the Grand piano.
  2. Press a key near its top edge. Then press it near its bottom edge. Listen to the difference.
  3. Play the same key four times, each one a little louder.
  4. On a computer, click high or low on a key with the mouse. The letter keys always play medium.

Make a chord with your hand

A chord is three or more notes played together. Play one key, skip one, play one, skip one, play one.

On the piano
  1. Put your thumb on C, your middle finger on E and your little finger on G. Press all three together.
  2. Move your whole hand so your thumb is on F: F, A, C. Then on G: G, B, D.
  3. On a computer, hold A, D and G for C, E and G.

Major and minor: bright and moody

Move the middle note of a chord down one key, and bright turns moody. C E G is C major. C E♭ G is C minor.

On the piano
  1. Hold C, E and G. Listen.

  3. Go back and forth between the two. Find one word for each sound.
  4. Press Minor · moody and tap pad 1. Then press Major · bright and tap it again.

Four ways to play a chord

The same chords can feel calm, steady, flowing or bouncy. Change How the chords play and listen.

On the piano
  1. Pick any chord pattern and press Play the chords.
  2. While it plays, change How the chords play. Try all four.
  3. Broken plays the notes of each chord one at a time. Musicians call that an arpeggio.
  4. Off-beat plays between the counts: 1 AND 2 AND. Clap on each AND.

The pedal: let it ring

On a real piano, the right pedal lifts felt dampers off the strings, so notes keep ringing after you let go. Here it is the Hold notes button, or Shift on a computer.

On the piano
  1. Play a few keys without the pedal. Each note stops when you let go.
  2. Press Hold notes (pedal). Play C, E, G, C, E going up. They all keep ringing.
  3. Press Hold notes again to lift the pedal. The sound stops.
  4. On a computer, hold Shift for the pedal.

Eight sounds: hammers, tines, reeds and pipes

A grand piano hits strings with felt hammers. An electric piano hits metal tines or reeds. An organ blows air through pipes or spins wheels. Same notes, different color. That color is called timbre.

On the piano
  1. Pick a chord pad you like. Keep tapping it as you change the Instrument.
  2. Listen to how each sound starts and how it fades.
  3. Hold a key on the Grand piano, then on an organ. Which one fades? Which one keeps going?
  4. Open Meet the sounds in the Go to menu to read how each one works.
