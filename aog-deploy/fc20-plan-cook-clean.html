Most of cooking happens before and after the cooking

Ask somebody to make dinner and this is what they picture: the cooking. A pan, some heat, twenty minutes. That is the middle of it.

Plan it, cook it, clean it up — and you have seen that shape before, in the K–2 room about finishing a job. This is the same three parts, on the biggest task a house does every single day. Everything you have learned this year turns up inside it: you wash your hands before you start, you know which things are hot and sharp, you build a plate with something in each part of it, you measure by spooning and levelling, and you cost the list before you go. Nothing in this room is new. What is new is that it all has to happen in one hour, in one kitchen, usually with somebody else in it.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

You start with the recipe that takes longest, because it needs the most time.

You start with whatever has to be WAITED for, which is not the same thing. Rice takes twenty minutes but you are not standing over it — you start it and walk away, and it looks after itself while you chop. A salad takes six minutes and needs you for all six. So the order is not longest-first; it is: start the things that cook without you, then do the things that need your hands, and finish with the thing that goes cold fastest. Work backwards from when you want to eat and you will find the whole order falls out on its own.

Start what waits. Then what needs your hands. Work backwards from dinner.

⚠️ People often think…

Clearing up is what happens at the end, once everybody has eaten.

Most of the clearing up can be done while the food is cooking, and the difference is enormous. Rice is sitting there for twenty minutes doing nothing that needs you — that is twenty minutes in which the chopping board, the knife, the peelings and the empty packets can all disappear. Wash as you go and by the time you sit down there are four plates and a pan left, instead of an entire kitchen. This is not about being tidy. It is that a pan washed while the food in it is still warm takes thirty seconds, and the same pan the next morning takes ten minutes and a scraper.

Wash while it cooks. Waiting time is working time.

Plan one meal all the way through

1 Watch one

Rice and beans with a salad, for four people, eating at six o'clock.

  1. Check the plate: rice is the grain, beans are the protein, salad is the half. It balances, so the plan is sound.
  2. Look in the cupboard BEFORE writing the list. Rice is there; beans are there; there is no lettuce.
  3. Work backwards from six. Rice needs twenty minutes and waits by itself, so rice goes on at 5:35.
  4. Beans warm through in ten, so 5:50. Salad needs your hands for six minutes, so 5:52 — and it goes soggy if it waits.
  5. 5:35 rice on · 5:35–5:50 wash the board and the knife · 5:50 beans on · 5:52 salad · 6:00 eat. ✓ And the kitchen is nearly clean already.

Helpful Hints

🧭 The unit in one line

A meal is three parts — plan, cook, clear up — and the cooking is the shortest → check the cupboard before you write the list, and write the list before you go → work backwards from the time you want to eat → start the things that cook without you first → and do most of the clearing up during the waiting time, because waiting time is working time.

Plan, cook, clear up. Start what waits. Wash while it cooks.

🔑 The one idea

Nothing in this room is new, and that is the design. Handwashing, the three dangers, the parts of a plate, the three parts of a job, spooning and levelling, and costing a list — all six earlier rooms turn up here doing actual work, inside one hour, in one kitchen, usually with somebody else in it. What a capstone tests is not whether you know the pieces. It is whether you can run them all at once while a pan is on.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • Longest-first is the wrong rule. Start what has to be WAITED for — the things that cook without you.
  • Clearing up is not what happens at the end. Most of it can happen while the food cooks.
  • Check the cupboard BEFORE the list, or you buy the fourth bag of rice in the house.
  • A pan washed warm takes thirty seconds. The same pan the next morning takes ten minutes and a scraper.
  • Dress a salad at the LAST moment. Dressed early, it is soggy by the time anybody sits down.
  • Do all the chopping before anything goes in a pan. A knife in your hand and a pan going brown behind you is where cuts come from.
  • When something goes wrong: heat OFF first, then think, then an adult. Turning it off costs a second and un-does nothing.
