Four boxes, four equal chances

Each parent carries one of each: a B and a b. Each one passes down only one.

That is a Punnett square. It gives a probability, not a promise: one in four, every single time — no matter how the last one turned out.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Dominant means stronger, or more common.

Dominant only means it shows whenever it is present. Plenty of dominant traits are rare, and plenty of recessive ones are everywhere.

Dominant is about showing, not about winning.

⚠️ People often think…

If you don't show a trait, you don't carry it.

Bb shows the dominant trait and carries the recessive one, quietly, ready to pass on. That is how two brown-eyed parents can have a blue-eyed child.

A recessive trait needs two copies to show. One copy just rides along.

⚠️ People often think…

Things you do to your body get passed to your kids.

Acquired traits are not in your DNA. Muscles built at the gym, a scar, a learned skill — none of it is written into what gets passed on.

If it happened to the body, it does not travel.

⚠️ People often think…

A 1-in-4 square means exactly one of four puppies.

A Punnett square gives probabilities, not promises. Four puppies can come out with none, or with all four — 1 in 4 is the chance for each one separately, like a coin.

Each offspring rolls its own dice.

Helpful Hints

🪆 The size order — smallest to biggest

DNA → gene → chromosome → nucleus → cell. A gene is a section of DNA; chromosomes are packages of DNA; humans carry 23 pairs.

DNA is the alphabet, genes are the words, chromosomes are the chapters.

🅱️ Geno vs. pheno — the two-letter trick

Genotype = the Genes (the letters, like Bb). Phenotype = the Physical (what you see, like brown eyes). Capital = dominant; lowercase = recessive.

Genotype = Genes. Phenotype = Physical.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Dominant does NOT mean stronger or more common — it only shows whenever present.
  • A recessive trait needs TWO copies (bb). Bb shows dominant but CARRIES the recessive.
  • Acquired traits don’t pass on — gym muscles and scars aren’t in your DNA.
  • Punnett squares give PROBABILITIES, not promises.
  • Mutations aren’t all bad — most are neutral, a few even help.

🏗️ The DNA → protein pipeline

DNA holds instructions → ribosomes build PROTEINS → proteins build the trait. Change the DNA (mutation) → maybe change the protein → maybe change the trait.

DNA writes it, protein builds it, the trait wears it.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • At least one Punnett square to fill in — practice Bb × Bb (3:1) and Bb × bb (1:1) until automatic.
  • “Explain why siblings differ” = each parent gives a random half; different halves, different combos.
  • Mutation questions want the pipeline: DNA change → protein change → trait change.

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Order DNA, gene, chromosome, nucleus by size?
  • Fill in a Bb × Bb Punnett square and give the ratio?
  • Tell genotype from phenotype in one sentence?
  • Trace a mutation from DNA to protein to trait?
  • Give one example of selective breeding and one of genetic engineering?
