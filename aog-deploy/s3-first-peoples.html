Name the nation

One word on a card: “Indian”. It covers hundreds of different nations, and tells you nothing about any of them.

There is no one “Indian” people — there are many nations, each with its own name, language and land. Name the nation you mean. And say “are”, not “were”: Native nations are still here.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

The people who lived here lived in tipis.

Tipis belong to the Plains, where people followed buffalo across open grassland. Here in the woodlands people built WIGWAMS and longhouses — homes that stay put, because the food here did. The house matches the land.

Wigwams in the woodlands, tipis on the Plains.

⚠️ People often think…

Cahokia is the old name for Chicago.

Different place, different time. Cahokia was a city of enormous earthen mounds near present-day St. Louis, at its height around a thousand years ago. Chicago is three hundred miles northeast and was incorporated in 1837.

Cahokia: mounds, near St. Louis, 1,000 years ago.

Worth knowing cold

The dates, in order

One date, one event. Notice how much happens before any European arrives.

  1. about 1050 Cahokia is at its height — a city of mounds near what is now St. Louis, larger then than London.
  2. about 1350 Cahokia is largely emptied. People move and reorganize; the mounds are still standing today.
  3. 1673 French explorers arrive. They came to TRADE.
  4. 1818 Illinois becomes a state. American settlers come for LAND — a different purpose entirely.
  5. 1833 A treaty signed under enormous pressure removes many nations from this land.
  6. today Their descendants are citizens of nations that exist right now.

Before, then, now — one land

1 Watch one

Why did the French and the American settlers want such different things?

  1. Ask what the French came for in 1673: furs, to trade.
  2. Trading needs partners, so they mostly wanted people to stay and trade with them.
  3. Ask what the settlers came for after 1818: farmland, to own.
  4. Land cannot be shared the way trade can — which is why the pressure and the treaties followed. ✓

Helpful Hints

🪜 From Cahokia to today — the ladder

Native nations for thousands of years → Cahokia, the mound city (about 1050–1350) → the Illiniwek and Potawatomi farming, hunting, trading → the French arrive to trade (1673) → statehood and settlers (1818) → the Treaty of Chicago forces the Potawatomi west (1833) → Native people in Illinois today.

1050 · 1350 · 1673 · 1818 · 1833 · today.

🤝 The whole topic in one idea

This land had cities, farms, calendars and trade routes long before Illinois. Newcomers traded, then took, and treaties moved nations west — but the nations did not disappear. Native people are part of Illinois today.

Before, then, now — one land.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Say ‘are’, not ‘were’. Native nations are still here.
  • Cahokia is NOT Chicago. Cahokia is a 1,000-year-old mound city near St. Louis.
  • Wigwams here, not tipis — tipis are for the Plains.
  • Many nations, not one ‘Indian’ people. Name the nation.
  • A treaty signed under pressure is not a free choice.
  • The French came to TRADE (1673); American settlers came for LAND (after 1818).

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A picture of a mound — “who built it and how?” (the Mississippians, baskets of earth by hand).
  • An object found at Cahokia — “what does it tell us?” (where it came from = who they traded with).
  • Events to order — 1050, 1350, 1673, 1818, 1833, today.
  • “Was the treaty fair?” — a signature under pressure and broken promises.
