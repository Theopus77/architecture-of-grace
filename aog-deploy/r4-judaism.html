The year 70 changes everything

Before 70 CE there is a Temple in Jerusalem, with priests and offerings, in one place.

In 70 CE the Temple is destroyed and rabbinic Judaism rebuilds around study, prayer and community instead of sacrifice. That is why a rabbi is a TEACHER rather than a priest, and why a synagogue is not a replacement Temple.

What people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

A rabbi is the Jewish equivalent of a priest.

A rabbi is a TEACHER — someone trained to interpret texts and law. Priests served in the Temple, and there has been no Temple since 70 CE. A synagogue is a house of study and prayer, not a small Temple.

Rabbi: teacher. Priest: the Temple.

⚠️ People often think…

Hanukkah is the most important Jewish holiday.

It is a MINOR festival. The High Holy Days — Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur — and Passover carry far more weight. Hanukkah is widely known outside the community largely because of where it falls on the calendar.

High Holy Days and Passover are the major ones.

⚠️ People often think…

Judaism, the Jewish people and the state of Israel are one topic.

Three different things: a religion, a people, and a country founded in 1948. Jews hold every possible view about the state, and many Jews are not religious. Collapsing the three is a category error that makes a summary wrong before it starts.

Religion, people, state. Say which one.

⚠️ People often think…

Judaism is just the religion of the Old Testament.

It is the Hebrew Bible read through two thousand years of rabbinic interpretation — Mishnah, Talmud, codes, commentary and ongoing argument. Describing it from the biblical text alone skips most of what the tradition actually is.

Two thousand years of reading come with it.

Worth knowing cold

Words that get swapped

Each row is a distinction that a careful summary has to keep straight.

  1. Torah / Tanakh / Talmud Five books; the whole Hebrew Bible; and the rabbinic discussion compiled around 500 CE. Three different libraries.
  2. Rabbi / priest A rabbi teaches and interprets. The priesthood belonged to the Temple, which has not stood since 70 CE.
  3. Judaism / Jews / Israel A religion; a people; a modern state. Three different things, and a sentence about one is not a sentence about the others.
  4. Major / minor festivals The High Holy Days and Passover are the major observances. Hanukkah is a MINOR festival that became well known for reasons of calendar, not rank.

How did Judaism survive the loss of the Temple?

  1. Ask what the Temple system required: one location, a priesthood, offerings.
  2. After 70 CE none of those are available anywhere.
  3. Rabbis rebuild around what IS portable: study of the text, prayer, and the community that gathers.
  4. So the tradition could exist anywhere — which is exactly what the next eighteen centuries required. ✓

Helpful Hints

🧭 The story in one line

Covenant and Torah → Temple and priests → Temple destroyed (70 CE) → rabbis, synagogue and study → Mishnah (200) → Talmud (500) → two thousand years of diaspora → modern movements, the Holocaust, Israel (1948).

Torah, Temple, 70, rabbis, Mishnah, Talmud, diaspora, today.

🤝 The one idea

Judaism is something you DO, together, and argue about. Practice comes before creed, community before the individual, and the question before the answer.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • Torah (five books) ≠ Tanakh (whole Hebrew Bible) ≠ Talmud (rabbinic discussion, about 500 CE).
  • A rabbi is a teacher, not a priest. A synagogue is not the Temple.
  • Hanukkah is a MINOR festival. The High Holy Days and Passover are the major ones.
  • Judaism (religion), Jews (people), Israel (state): three different things.
  • Judaism is not ‘the Old Testament religion’. It is the Hebrew Bible as read through two thousand years of rabbinic tradition.
