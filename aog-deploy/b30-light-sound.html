No shake, no sound

A bell hanging still. It is quiet, because nothing is moving.

Sound is something shaking. No shake, no sound — grab a ringing bell and it goes quiet in your hand, every single time. A BIG shake is loud; a FAST shake is high.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

The moon makes its own light.

It bounces the sun’s light, the way a mirror does. Turn the flashlight off in a dark room and the mirror goes dark too — a bouncer has nothing to bounce.

Some things make light. Some only bounce it.

⚠️ People often think…

Loud and high mean the same thing.

They are two different shakes. BIG shake is loud; FAST shake is high. A tuba can be loud and low at once, and a whistle can be quiet and high.

Big shake is loud. Fast shake is high.

Helpful Hints

🪜 Sound and light — the ladder

Every sound starts with something vibrating — shaking fast → a big shake is loud, a fast shake is high → sound goes out through the air and can shake other things → you see a thing only when light reaches it and bounces to your eye → some things let light through, some let a little, some block it and make a shadow → a flashing light or a drum can send a message far away.

Shake to hear. Light to see.

🤝 The whole idea

Sound is a shake you can hear. Light is what you need to see. Both of them travel — that is why you can hear a drum across the gym and see a flashlight across the yard.

Both travel. Both carry a message.

⚠️ Tricky ones

  • No vibration, no sound. Grab a ringing bell and it stops — every time.
  • Loud is a BIG shake. High is a FAST shake. They are not the same thing.
  • The moon and a mirror do NOT make light. They bounce it.
  • In the dark, things are still there. You just can’t see them until light lands on them.
  • Closer to the light = BIGGER shadow, not smaller.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A picture of a drum with rice jumping — “why is the rice moving?” (the drum is vibrating).
  • Pictures of a window, wax paper and a wall — “which one makes a shadow?”
  • “Which one makes its own light?” (the sun or a flashlight — not the moon).
  • “Why can’t you see in a dark room?” (no light to bounce into your eye).
  • “How could you send a message across a field without your voice?” (a light or a drum).
