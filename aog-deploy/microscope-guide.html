The Microscope, in pictures

Seven missions. Each one opens the next. Every picture below is the real microscope.

iWhat everything is

The eyepiece is on the left. Every control is in the panel on the right, and the knobs are under the eyepiece.

SlideWhat is under the lens. Start with the letter e.
LampNo light, no picture.
Stage clipsSeated holds the slide still.
ObjectiveHow big. 4× is the smallest and the safest.
CoarseBig focus moves. Only on 4× and 10×.
FineSmall focus moves. The only knob on 40× and 100×.
DiaphragmMore or less light. Fixes a washed-out picture.
EyepieceTurns the eyepiece. Finds dust on the glass.
Working distanceHow far the lens is from the glass. Red means too close.
Missions (teacher)Locked opens one control at a time. Lock it before a student sits down.

3Change what you see Mission

Stain the onion. Then darkfield a live pond drop — with no stain.

Slide: OnionThen 40× and Fine.
Stain: IodineThe stain buttons appear once a slide can take one.
No stainA ghost. You can barely see the cells.
IodineThe walls go dark and every nucleus turns brown.
Illumination: DarkfieldLoad Pond first. Stain: None.
BrightfieldPale and hard to see.
DarkfieldThe field goes black. Living things glow.

Why no stain on the pond? A stain kills what it colors. Darkfield lets you see them alive.

Scale in. Then Measure, and tap two points on a cell at 40×.

Eyepiece scale: In, then MeasureThe ruler lives in the eyepiece, so it stays still when the slide moves.

5What can’t this microscope see? Mission

Load Virus?, go to 100×, and put the oil on.

100×Fine knob only.
Immersion oil: On the 100×This row appears on 100×.
No oilDim and soft. That is physics, not a broken lens.
Oil onThe cells a virus lives in. No virus.

The honest ceiling. A virus is about 0.10 µm. Light cannot draw anything that small. 1,000× with oil is as far as this microscope goes.

+More slides in the trays

The slide menu has six trays. Pick a slide, start at 4×, and read the one line under the circle at each power. It tells you what to look for.

Blood smear · 40×Red cells with a pale middle. The purple ones are white cells. Stacks of coins are rouleaux.
Leaf cross-section · 40×Tall cells like fence posts, full of green. Find a stoma in the bottom skin.
Fly wing · 4×The whole wing fits. Go to 40× to see the tiny hairs on the sheet.
Fern spore capsules · 10×Each ball is a bag of spores. The dark ridge on top is the spring that throws them.
Cloth · 10×Cotton on one side, polyester on the other. At 40× cotton twists; polyester is a smooth rod.
DNA goo · 40×White threads from mashed fruit. One DNA molecule is 100 times finer than this microscope can show.

Trays: Start here (letter e, stage micrometer) · Plants (onion, elodea, leaf peel, leaf cross-section, leaf hairs, pollen, fern) · Animals and people (cheek, blood, hair, dog hair and tick, butterfly wing, fly wing) · Tiny life (pond water, bacteria, diatoms, Virus?, DNA goo) · Crystals and cloth · Ancient texts (papyrus, scroll, tablet).

?Something looks wrong

  • The circle is blackLamp: On. Then check the lamp before you blame the focus.
  • It is blurry4× or 10×: Coarse. 40× or 100×: Fine only.
  • Cracked glassCoarse on high power did it. Press New slide, start again on 4×.
  • Too bright and washed outTurn the Diaphragm down.
  • Too dark around the edgeTurn the Diaphragm up.
  • A speck that will not move when you dragTurn the Eyepiece knob. If the speck turns with it (compare the two pictures), it is dust on the eyepiece, not on the slide.
  • Dim and soft on 100×Put the oil on.
  • The slide keeps slidingStage clips: Seated.
