What it is, and what it is not

Twelve sessions of ninety minutes, once a week. One closed group of six to ten adults. Every week has the same shape and ends with the same words. It grows out of Books 1–5 of the K–12 curriculum.

It is

  • A structured group for adults, run in clinics and community programs.
  • A good partner to trauma therapy, medication-assisted treatment and recovery programs, once a person is stable.
  • Practice, not treatment. The work happens between you and the page. The group listens; it never pulls a story out of you.

It is not

  • A self-help book to work through alone.
  • A replacement for trauma therapy, medication-assisted treatment, or psychiatric care for active suicidal thinking, psychosis or severe dissociation.
  • A religious program. It is taught without religion and sits well beside a chaplain’s own work.

Who it is for

Adults who

  • carry shame or blame toward themselves that has become how they see themselves — in recovery, after harm they caused, or after a loss they blame themselves for;
  • hold a long resentment toward someone, when making up is not possible, not safe, or not wanted;
  • carry shame that a family, culture or community handed them before they could say no;
  • cannot tell taking responsibility apart from punishing themselves.

Good settings: recovery after about sixty days of stability; trauma recovery after first treatment; the months after leaving incarceration, once housing and work are steady; chaplaincy; grief groups where the grief carries self-blame.

Before anyone joins

  • Eighteen or older, and here by choice.
  • Has at least one way to calm down that has worked in the last thirty days.
  • Thirty days on a steady medication plan, and no hospital stay in the last fourteen days.
  • In trauma therapy? Their therapist agrees in writing that this group fits alongside it.

Being steady comes first. Too early, looking inward can shake someone whose supports are still forming. At the right time, it locks in the gains they have already made.

Not now, and not without care The wrong group at the wrong time can do harm, so facilitators screen at the start and again if anything changes. The group does not go ahead with active suicidal thinking with a plan, intent or a recent attempt; active psychosis or untreated severe mental illness; intoxication or a relapse in the last thirty days; or dissociation strong enough to stop the work.

The four phases

The phases come in this order for a reason. Sessions are never skipped, swapped or shortened.

Sessions 1–3

Foundation

The group, its promises, and four big ideas. Shared words come first, and the inside voice becomes something you can hear.

Sessions 4–6

Interior

Looking inward. Feelings carry messages. The Backpack: what you carry, written down. The Witness: an honest, fair, kind inner voice.

Sessions 7–10

Repair

The heart of it. Telling grief from resentment. Forgiving the younger self. Giving back shame that was never yours. The Living Amend.

Sessions 11–12

Legacy

Forgiveness is not the same as making up. Then the Final Audit: put down, carry differently, or choose to carry.

What the group promises

The group writes these five promises together in Session 1, and they stay on the wall for all twelve.

  1. What is said here, stays here.Except where the law says otherwise: immediate danger to yourself or others, abuse of a child or vulnerable adult, or a court order. These are named on day one. The group never promises more privacy than the law allows.
  2. You may pass at any time, at any prompt, without explanation.Passing is part of the work. It is you choosing for yourself.
  3. We do not give advice unless asked. We listen.
  4. We do not narrate other people’s stories. We speak only to our own.
  5. The work is between you and the page. The room witnesses; it does not extract.
You never have to tell your story Nobody is asked to say what happened. A person can finish all twelve sessions without naming their trauma, their history with substances, or harm they caused. The work happens on the page.

Who leads it

Two facilitators are in the room every session. One person alone cannot run this program, whatever their training.

The lead facilitator · the voice

  • A master’s-level mental-health license (LCSW, LMHC, LMFT, LPC, LPCC, psychologist or equal), or a credentialed addiction counselor with three years of leading groups after licensing.
  • At least twelve hours of trauma-informed training and forty hours of co-leading groups first.
  • Two private hours doing the participant’s own work before Session 1.
  • Clinical supervision every week.

The co-facilitator · the eyes

  • A credentialed peer recovery or peer mental-health specialist, a master’s-level intern supervised by the lead, or a licensed clinician.
  • In the room the whole session, and at the twenty-minute debrief after.
  • Watches for distress and responds without stopping the session.

Faith-based, peer-led and community programs for people returning from incarceration can run it only with a licensed clinical consultant who has read the manual and can be reached the same day, lighter content in the high-care sessions, and referral paths in place before Session 1. Crisis & safety resources

Free, private, any time. Every group also posts its local crisis line and its own emergency contacts.

  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline — call or text 988. 24/7, free, confidential.
  • Crisis Text Line — text HOME to 741741.
  • SAMHSA National Helpline — 1-800-662-4357. Free, confidential treatment referral and information, 24/7.
  • Veterans Crisis Line — dial 988, then press 1 (or text 838255).
  • Immediate danger — call 911 or your local emergency number.
Nothing is sent unless you send it. No account, no sign-in, no tracking. The workbook saves on your own device. Sending a page to your facilitator is always your choice, one page at a time.
