Unit 1 · The Compass of Values

The threshold — leaving and arriving. The Exit Interview. Examined vs. absorbed values. The values-behavior gap. Institutional vs. operational values. The developmental window. The Moral North Star. This unit practiced building a values map that is honest rather than aspirational — and finding the one principle that holds when the rest are under pressure.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 The threshold is —
B. The threshold is liminal space: leaving the identity context in which you built yourself, arriving somewhere that has not met you yet. The Exit Interview is how you take honest inventory before you cross.
2 Absorbed values are —
A. Absorbed values arrived before you had a choice about them. Not necessarily wrong — but not yet yours. Examined values have been held up to the light, tested, and claimed. The values map should be honest, not aspirational.
3 The values-behavior gap is —
C. Everyone has one. Stated values appear on the map; actual values show up when the situation is real and the cost is immediate. The question is not whether you have a gap — it is whether you will look at it honestly.
4 Institutional values are what an organization publicly claims. Operational values are —
A. Often they match. Sometimes they do not. Values fit is the alignment between your examined values and an institution’s operational values — the ones that will actually shape your days there.
5 The prefrontal cortex — complex moral reasoning, long-term consequence, impulse regulation — reaches full maturity at about —
D. At 16–18 it is still developing: capable of post-conventional reasoning, but under construction. That is not an excuse; it is the developmental window. Character is built by what you practice while it is still being built.
6 The Moral North Star is —
B. Not a rule. Not an unexamined inheritance. The North Star sits at the top of the hierarchy and holds when every other value is under pressure — and it costs something to hold in the environments you are entering.
7 The Compass of Integrity integrates —
A. Unit 1 ends with one navigational document you carry across the threshold: North, the principle that holds; South, the gap and what closes it; East, what has already held under pressure; West, what is still forming — and the one sentence you carry forward.
8 The Exit Interview asks three things. Which one is on the list?
C. Who have you been? What was performance, and what was actually built — qualities and capacities, not accomplishments? What identity are you not taking with you? The first line of who you are arriving as is written here.

4 Your Moral North Star: the principle, why it is genuinely yours, and what it will cost to hold in the environment you are entering next.

Unit 2 · The Adult Repair Manual

Apology vs. living amend. Forgiving the younger self — grief, not resentment. The mental health continuum and the clinical threshold. The Sustainable Soul. Forgiveness fatigue. The inheritance of grace. The Final Backpack Audit. This unit practiced the adult forms of accountability, self-forgiveness, and asking for help.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 An apology addresses the incident. A living amend addresses —
D. Apology: a statement of remorse, available in one conversation, does not address the pattern. Living amend: sustained change that addresses the pattern. When the honest response is a pattern change, another apology is not accountability.
2 The younger self is —
B. Not a different person. The same person, earlier, operating with less information and fewer resources. Self-resentment accumulates toward that version; forgiving the younger self releases it without pretending the harm did not happen.
3 “I wish things had been different” is grief. “Someone has to pay for this” is —
A. GRIEF: I wish things had been different. RESENTMENT: someone must pay. Grief can be carried and metabolized. Resentment calcifies. Forgiving the younger self usually means turning resentment back into grief.
4 On the mental health continuum, the clinical threshold is where —
D. Sadness, anxiety, stress, and grief are normal variation — the Pause, the Witness, the Recovery Protocol address them. When duration + interference + intensity cross the threshold, asking for professional help is literacy, not weakness.
5 Short-term restoration restores capacity for next week. The Sustainable Soul asks about —
C. Book 4’s Restoration Test covers the next day. Book 5 asks what restores you across a decade — the portable self-care arc you can carry into any city, job, or relationship.
6 Forgiveness fatigue is —
A. Expected at the Capstone threshold. Real and valid. It is not resistance — resistance says “I will not”; fatigue says “I am tired.” The reframe: the prior work is honored, and this is deeper material, not the same material again.
7 Unnamed grace is —
B. Named grace: the teacher who stayed, the friend who did not leave. Unnamed grace: the systems, strangers, and generations that held you without your knowing. The inheritance of grace is both — and grace is transmitted between generations the same way harm is.
8 The Final Backpack Audit applies the whole of Unit 2 to each stone. That means —
D. It is comprehensive — everything you have been carrying into adulthood — and it is deliberate. Some stones are put down. Some are carried forward on purpose. None are carried by accident.

Unit 3 · Transcendent Forgiveness

The leadership prerequisite. The childhood backpack — placed, not chosen. Complex reconciliation and the space between. Global empathy. The cost of bitterness. Forgiveness as legacy. The Graduation of the Heart. This unit practiced forgiveness beyond what is fair — available only to someone regulated, self-compassionate, and anchored.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 Conventional forgiveness releases resentment when the harm has stopped and the other person has acknowledged, apologized or changed. Transcendent forgiveness —
B. That is the leadership prerequisite: transcendent forgiveness is available, never required, and it needs prior work — regulation, self-compassion, and a Moral North Star — or it is performance.
2 The childhood backpack holds —
A. Book 4’s backpack was about your choices. This one is about what was placed in you before you could evaluate it. Releasing it is not blaming; it is separating what was placed from what you chose to carry.
3 The space between is —
C. Most adult relationships involving significant harm live here. Forgiveness without contact is real forgiveness. Conditional reconciliation with appropriate limits is real reconciliation. The space between is not indecision — it is the honest place.
4 Global empathy is —
A. The same framework as personal contextual empathy, applied to systems and history. Understanding why does not remove structural accountability. Justice and forgiveness are related, not opposed.
5 Bitterness is —
D. Legitimate anger responds to a current harm. Grief mourns a loss. Advocacy works to change something. Bitterness is resentment that stopped responding to anything and became a posture — and it has documented psychological, physical, and relational costs.
6 Forgiveness as legacy means —
B. It gives the forgiver freedom from the ongoing cost of resentment and the capacity to be present. It gives witnesses a model. And it costs something real — legacy is never free.
7 The Graduation of the Heart is —
A. The whole unit is preparation. The last lesson is the crossing: from the weight of what was done to you toward the freedom of what you choose. Every permanent chart from Books 4 and 5 is on the wall for it.
8 Which of these is NOT bitterness?
C. Anger at a current harm is appropriate and useful. The others have calcified past the point of responding to anything. The honest assessment is: what am I holding that is no longer protecting me?

Unit 4 · The Orchard Legacy

Systemic grace. Radical generosity. Counseling vs. canceling. Charitable interpretation at scale. The Mentor Path. The Graduation Charter. This unit practiced grace at the scale of institutions and generations — and asked what you are planting.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 Systemic grace is —
D. Personal grace: goodwill to one person. Community grace: goodwill as a culture’s default. Systemic grace: repair and second chances designed into the structure itself. Reform adjusts a system; transformation changes what it is for.
2 Transactional giving expects return. Performative charity is about the giver’s image. Radical generosity —
B. The gift that costs something. The Radical Generosity Project is not a fundraiser — it is a legacy contribution built from what you specifically can do, for a community you specifically belong to.
3 Counseling — the adult register of calling in — is —
A. Counseling is for a reachable person and a specific harm, where private repair is available and safe. Canceling is public, punitive, exclusionary removal. Proportionality and pattern-vs-incident decide which is honest.
4 Charitable interpretation at scale means —
D. Same framework as Book 4, larger targets. Motivated reasoning and willful blindness are the failure modes on both sides. Social trust depends on people willing to read generously AND accurately.
5 The gift of attention is —
C. Not advice, not instruction, not correction. The person who stayed. The person who named something in you before you saw it. The Mentor Path asks who gave you that — and who you will now give it to.
6 The Graduation Charter is —
A. Collective commitment, public act, witnessed. Each person signs it as their own. It is the class’s answer to the year’s question: what did we build, and what are we taking with us?
7 Reform vs. transformation: reform —
B. Systemic grace can be either. The question is whether the structure needs its practices corrected or its purpose re-examined. Both are legitimate work. Naming which one you are doing is the honest start.
8 The Mentor Path is the final Regulation & Repair touchpoint because —
D. The strand ends where the year ends: with the tools turned outward. You received the gift of attention. You are now positioned to give it — deliberately, to someone specific.

Year-End · Integration

Every permanent chart from Books 4 and 5 on one wall. The Compass of Integrity in one hand, the Graduation Charter signed with the other. Room 207 learned: the room inside you is portable. It comes with you.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 Which permanent charts from Book 4 are on the wall for the Graduation of the Heart?
B. The K–12 arc ends with every permanent chart visible at once — Book 4’s five and Book 5’s own: the Moral North Star and Forgiveness as Legacy. It is one spine.
2 The two Book 5 charts marked PERMANENT are —
A. One tells you what holds when everything is under pressure. The other tells you what forgiveness plants and what it costs. Those are the two you carry out.
3 “The room inside you is portable” means —
C. Room 207 is the last room. The point of five books was never the room. It was the interior architecture — and interior architecture comes with you.
4 The final witness ritual is —
A. Bearing witness, from Book 4, is the last thing the class does for each other — in pairs, turned to the person next to you: full presence, no agenda to fix. You are received as the person you have become.
5 The examined life, in this curriculum, means —
D. Not perfection. Not self-absorption. Examination: knowing what you value, where your behavior diverges from it, and what principle you are unwilling to trade — and paying its price on purpose.
