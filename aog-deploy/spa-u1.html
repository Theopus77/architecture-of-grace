Sounds of Spanish

Sounds

Spanish is a language that you can read out loud. Its five vowels say one sound each. Once you know them, you can say new words right away.

In this unit, you will meet a friend named Ana and a llama named Yoyo. You will learn the vowels. You will learn the special letters h, j, ll, ñ and rr.

By the end, you can say hola and adiós. You can clap the beats in a word. You can read a new Spanish word before you know what it means.

The big question

How do the sounds of Spanish help you read any word out loud?

Five Vowels, Five Sounds

Vowels
Big questionHow can you read a Spanish word out loud the first time you see it?
The story

Ana Says Her Name

A new friend has a name that sounds the same both ways.

A new girl comes to class on Monday. She smiles and waves. She says, "¡Hola! Me llamo Ana." That means "Hi! My name is Ana."

Ana writes her name on the board. A-N-A. She says it slowly: ah-nah. Then she reads it from the end to the start. It is still Ana!

Ana tells us a secret. In Spanish, the letter a says ah every time. So the name sounds the same both ways. Every a in Ana says ah.

At the end of the day, Ana waves again. She says, "¡Adiós!" That means "Goodbye!" We wave back and say it too: ah-dee-OHS.

Talk about itCan you think of another name that reads the same from the end to the start?
Section 1

Meet the Five Vowels

1.1

The Sound of A

Main ideaIn Spanish, the letter a always says ah, like in mamá.

Say (mom). Open your mouth wide. Say ah. Ma-má has two a sounds. Both say ah.

Now say (dad). It has two a sounds too. Each a says ah. In Spanish, a never changes its sound.

In English, a can make many sounds. Think of cat, cake and car. In Spanish, a only says ah.

Think it through
Ah, Ah, Ah

Picture a doctor. The doctor says, "Open wide and say ah." That is the Spanish a. Now say mamá: mah-MAH. Now say papá: pah-PAH. Now say Ana: AH-nah.

Say mamá and papá. Does every a sound the same?

Words to know
mamá
mom — both a letters say ah: mah-MAH
papá
dad — both a letters say ah: pah-PAH
Check yourself

1. What sound does the Spanish a make?

Why: The Spanish a always says ah, in every word.

2. What does mamá mean?

Why: Mamá means mom. Papá means dad.

3. How do you say papá?

Why: Each a says ah, so papá sounds like pah-PAH.
1.2

E, I, O and U

Main ideaThe five vowels say ah, eh, ee, oh, oo, and they never change.

Spanish has five vowels: a, e, i, o, u. Each one has one sound. It says that sound every time.

A says ah. E says eh, like in pet. I says ee, like in see. O says oh. U says oo, like in moon.

Try (bear): OH-soh. Try (grape): OO-vah. The o and the u never change.

Think it through
The Vowel Song

Sing it slowly. A says ah. E says eh. I says ee. O says oh. U says oo. Now sing it fast: ah, eh, ee, oh, oo! Clap one time for each sound.

Sing the five sounds. Which one makes your lips round?

Words to know
el oso
the bear — both o letters say oh: OH-soh
la uva
the grape — the u says oo: OO-vah
Check yourself

1. What sound does the Spanish i make?

Why: The Spanish i always says ee.

2. What does el oso mean?

Why: El oso is the bear. La uva is the grape.

3. What sound does the Spanish u make?

Why: The Spanish u always says oo.
Section 2

Read It Like It Looks

1.3

Say Every Letter

Main ideaSpanish words sound the way they are spelled.

In Spanish, you can read a new word out loud. Just say each letter’s sound. Vowels never trick you.

Look at (the island). The s is easy. I says ee. A says ah. Say it: EES-lah.

In English, many letters are quiet. In Spanish, the vowels always speak. Say every one.

Think it through
Sound It Out

Look at a new word: luna. L says l. U says oo. N says n. A says ah. Put it together: LOO-nah. Luna means moon. You read it before you knew it!

Sound out the word mesa, one letter at a time.

Words to know
la isla
the island — say EES-lah; the i says ee
Check yourself

1. How do you say isla?

Why: The i says ee and the a says ah, so isla is EES-lah.

2. What does la isla mean?

Why: La isla means the island.

3. Why can you read a new Spanish word out loud?

Why: Spanish vowels keep one sound, so words sound like they look.
1.4

Watch Out for English

Main ideaDo not use English vowel sounds when you read Spanish.

English readers make one common mistake. They use English sounds for Spanish vowels.

Take (me). In English, me sounds like mee. In Spanish, the e says eh. So me sounds like meh.

Take uno (one). Say OO-noh, not YOO-noh. Keep each vowel short, clean and the same.

Think it through
Two Ways to Read It

A boy reads the word me. He says mee, like English. His friend says, "Try the Spanish e. It says eh." He tries again: meh. Now it sounds like Spanish!

Read me the Spanish way. Now read no. Does no sound like English no?

Words to know
me
me — say meh; the Spanish e says eh, not ee
Check yourself

1. How do you say the Spanish word me?

Why: The Spanish e says eh, so me sounds like meh.

2. How do you say uno (one)?

Why: The Spanish u says oo, so uno is OO-noh.

3. What mistake do English readers often make?

Why: English vowels change a lot. Spanish vowels do not, so use Spanish sounds.
Section 3

Clap and Greet

1.5

Clapping Syllables

Main ideaClap once for each vowel sound to find the parts of a word.

A syllable is one beat in a word. Most Spanish beats have one vowel sound. Clap one time for each beat.

Try (the butterfly). Ma-ri-po-sa. Clap, clap, clap, clap. It has four beats.

Try oso: o-so. That is two claps. Try Ana: A-na. That is two claps too. Now clap your own name.

Read the numbers
Count the Claps

Counted by saying each word slowly

Which word has the most claps? Clap it to check.

Words to know
la mariposa
the butterfly — four beats: ma-ri-po-sa
Check yourself

1. How many claps does mariposa have?

Why: Ma-ri-po-sa has four beats.

2. What does la mariposa mean?

Why: La mariposa is the butterfly.

3. How many claps does oso have?

Why: O-so has two beats.
1.6

Hola and Adiós

Main ideaSay hola to say hi and adiós to say goodbye.

When you meet someone, say (hi or hello). The h is quiet, so skip it. Say OH-lah.

When you leave, say (goodbye). Say ah-dee-OHS. The mark on the o tells you to say that part loudest.

Hola has two beats: o-la. Adiós has three beats: a-di-ós. Clap them both. Clap them again, fast.

Think it through
At the Door

Ana walks into class. She waves. "¡Hola!" she says. The teacher says, "¡Hola, Ana!" At three o'clock, Ana walks out. "¡Adiós!" she says. Everyone waves back.

What do you say when you walk into class? What do you say when you leave?

Words to know
hola
hi or hello — the h is quiet: OH-lah
adiós
goodbye — say ah-dee-OHS
Check yourself

1. What do you say when you leave?

Why: Adiós means goodbye.

2. How do you say hola?

Why: The h in hola is quiet, so it sounds like OH-lah.

3. What does hola mean?

Why: Hola means hi or hello.

Special Letters: h, ll, ñ, rr

Letters
Big questionWhich Spanish letters make sounds that English letters do not?
The story

Yoyo and Perro at the Farm

A llama and a puppy teach us some special letters.

Our class visits a farm. A tall, fuzzy animal walks up. It is a llama. The farmer says, "Se llama Yoyo." That means "Its name is Yoyo."

In Spanish, llama starts with ll. The ll sounds like y. So we say YAH-mah. Yoyo chews some hay. He looks right at all of us.

Then a puppy runs up. It barks and wags its tail. The farmer laughs. "This is Perro," she says. Perro means dog in Spanish!

The farmer says perro with a rolled rr. It sounds like a little motor: PEH-rrrroh. We all try it. The happy puppy barks back.

Talk about itWhich sound was harder for you, the ll in llama or the rr in perro?
Section 1

The Quiet H and the J

2.1

H Is Quiet

Main ideaIn Spanish, the letter h makes no sound at all.

Say hola (hi). Listen closely. You do not hear the h. You say OH-lah. The h is quiet.

Now try (the ice cream). Skip the h. Start with the e. Say eh-LAH-doh.

English speakers often say the h. In Spanish, skip it. Start with the next sound.

Think it through
The Sleepy H

Picture the letter h asleep in a bed. It never wakes up. In hola, the h is asleep. In helado, the h is asleep. We say OH-lah and eh-LAH-doh. Shh, do not wake the h!

Say helado without the h. Now say hola.

Words to know
el helado
the ice cream — the h is quiet: eh-LAH-doh
Check yourself

1. What sound does the Spanish h make?

Why: The Spanish h is always quiet.

2. How do you say helado?

Why: Skip the quiet h: eh-LAH-doh.

3. What does el helado mean?

Why: El helado means the ice cream.
2.2

J Is a Strong H

Main ideaThe Spanish j sounds like a strong, breathy h.

The h is quiet. But the j makes a sound like a strong h. Push air from your throat.

Try (the juice). Say HOO-goh. Do not say the English j sound, like in jump.

So jugo sounds like it starts with an h. Hola starts with h but sounds quiet. Funny, right?

Think it through
Foggy Window

Hold your hand near your mouth. Say a strong h, like you are fogging a cold window. That is the j. Now say jugo: HOO-goh. Did you feel the air?

Say jugo. Then say hola. Which one has air at the start?

Words to know
el jugo
the juice — the j sounds like a strong h: HOO-goh
Check yourself

1. How do you say jugo?

Why: The Spanish j sounds like a strong h.

2. What does el jugo mean?

Why: El jugo means the juice.

3. Which letter is quiet in Spanish?

Why: The h is quiet. The j says a strong h sound.
Section 2

LL and Ñ

2.3

LL Sounds Like Y

Main ideaThe double l in Spanish sounds like the y in yes.

Two l letters together make one new sound. In most places, that sound is like y.

(the llama) sounds like YAH-mah. (the chicken) sounds like POH-yoh.

Do not say two l sounds. Say one y sound instead. Llama is YAH-mah, not LAH-mah.

Think it through
Yoyo and the Chicken

Yoyo the llama sees a chicken. The farmer points. "Mira, el pollo," she says. That means "Look, the chicken." We say POH-yoh. Yoyo the YAH-mah just keeps chewing.

Say llama and pollo. Where do you hear the y sound?

Words to know
la llama
the llama — the ll says y: YAH-mah
el pollo
the chicken — the ll says y: POH-yoh
Check yourself

1. How do you say pollo?

Why: The ll sounds like y, so pollo is POH-yoh.

2. What does el pollo mean?

Why: El pollo means the chicken.

3. What sound does ll make?

Why: In most places, the Spanish ll sounds like y.
2.4

The Letter Ñ

Main ideaThe letter ñ has a squiggle on top and says ny, like in canyon.

The ñ is a special letter. The squiggle on top is called a tilde. Say it: TEEL-deh.

The ñ says ny. (the boy) is NEE-nyoh. (Spain) is ehs-PAH-nyah.

N and ñ are not the same letter. Always write the little squiggle on top of the ñ.

Think it through
Find the Squiggle

Look at three words on the board: niño, año, España. Each one has a ñ. Put your finger on each squiggle. Now say them: NEE-nyoh, AH-nyoh, ehs-PAH-nyah. Año means year.

Say año. Can you hear the ny sound in the middle?

Words to know
el niño
the boy — the ñ says ny: NEE-nyoh
España
Spain — the country; say ehs-PAH-nyah
Check yourself

1. What sound does ñ make?

Why: The ñ says ny.

2. What does el niño mean?

Why: El niño means the boy.

3. Which word is spelled right?

Why: Spain in Spanish is España, with a ñ.
Section 3

R and RR

2.5

The Rolled RR

Main ideaThe double rr is a rolled sound, like a little motor.

Put the tip of your tongue behind your top teeth. Blow air. Let your tongue buzz. That is the rolled rr.

(the dog) has rr. Say PEH-rrroh. (the car) has rr too. Roll it!

Rolling takes practice. Many kids learn it slowly. Keep trying every day. It gets easier.

Think it through
Motor Mouth

Pretend you are a little car. Your tongue is the motor. Say rrrrrr. Now drive into a word: el ca-rrrro. Now park and pet the dog: el pe-rrrro. Beep beep!

Make your motor sound. Then say perro.

Words to know
el perro
the dog — the rr is rolled: PEH-rrroh
el carro
the car — the rr is rolled: KAH-rrroh
Check yourself

1. What does el perro mean?

Why: El perro means the dog.

2. Which word has a rolled rr?

Why: Carro has rr in the middle, so you roll it.

3. Where does your tongue go for rr?

Why: For rr, the tongue tip buzzes behind the top teeth.
2.6

Pero or Perro?

Main ideaOne r is a quick tap, and rr is a long roll.

One r in the middle of a word is quick. Your tongue taps one time. (but) has one r.

Perro has two r letters. You roll them. So pero means but, and perro means the dog.

One sound can change the whole word. Listen closely. Tap for pero. Roll for perro.

Read the numbers
How Many R Letters?

Counted from the spelling of each word

Which words need a rolled sound?

Words to know
pero
but — one quick tap of the tongue: PEH-roh
Check yourself

1. What does pero mean?

Why: Pero with one r means but. Perro with rr means dog.

2. Which word means dog?

Why: Perro, with a rolled rr, means dog.

3. How do you say one r in the middle of a word?

Why: One r in the middle is a quick tap. The rr is a long roll.
Section 4

Say It Before You Know It

2.7

Read a New Word

Main ideaYou can say a Spanish word before you know what it means.

You know the vowels. You know h, j, ll, ñ and rr. Now you can say almost any Spanish word.

Try (the pineapple). The i says ee. The ñ says ny. The a says ah. Say PEE-nyah.

Say the word first. Then find out what it means. Ask a friend or look at a picture.

Think it through
Word Detective

A new word: jirafa. The j is a strong h. The i says ee. One r is a tap. The a says ah. Say it: hee-RAH-fah. Now guess: it is a tall animal with a long neck!

Say jirafa out loud. What animal is it?

Words to know
la piña
the pineapple — say PEE-nyah
Check yourself

1. How do you say piña?

Why: The i says ee and the ñ says ny: PEE-nyah.

2. How do you say jirafa?

Why: The j is a strong h and the i says ee.

3. What does la piña mean?

Why: La piña means the pineapple.
2.8

The Alphabet Song

Main ideaThe Spanish alphabet has 27 letters, and ñ is one of them.

The Spanish alphabet is (the alphabet). It has the same letters as English, plus ñ.

So it has 27 letters. The ñ comes right after n. Kids sing the letters in a song: a, be, ce, de.

Today, ll and rr are not extra letters. They are pairs of letters with their own sounds.

Read the numbers
Counting Letters

Real Academia Española, the Spanish language academy in Madrid

How many more letters does Spanish have than English? Which one is it?

Words to know
el abecedario
the alphabet — 27 letters, with ñ after n
Check yourself

1. How many letters are in the Spanish alphabet?

Why: Spanish has the 26 English letters plus ñ, so 27.

2. Where does ñ go in the alphabet?

Why: The ñ comes right after n.

3. What does el abecedario mean?

Why: El abecedario means the alphabet.

Write it

Say or write 3 sentences in Spanish. Greet a friend, tell your name, and say goodbye. For example: ¡Hola! Me llamo Ana. ¡Adiós! Use your own name.

  • Start with ¡Hola! and end with ¡Adiós!
  • Put ¡ at the start and ! at the end.
  • Remember the accent mark on the o in adiós.
  • Read it aloud and say every vowel the Spanish way.
