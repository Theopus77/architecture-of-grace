Everyone Invited Home

Promise Kept

This unit has stories of people who needed help. Hagar was alone in the desert. Moses was far from home. Mary heard surprising news. In each story, the Bible says a messenger from God came close and spoke kindly.

Then come stories about Jesus. He fed hungry people and welcomed children. He sent his friends to all nations. At Pentecost, people heard good news in their own languages. Many readers see an invitation here for every nation.

In this unit you will retell these stories in order. You will learn words like messenger, holy and nation. You will say what each story says. Then you will hear how readers explain it. And you will find ways to help others feel welcome.

In the Bible stories, how does God come close and invite everyone home?

The story

The Bush That Did Not Burn Up

Moses is watching sheep. Then he sees something new. A bush is on fire, but it stays green.

This story is in the Bible, in a book called Exodus. Moses lives in the dry hills. He takes care of sheep. Each day he walks with them. He finds them grass and water. It is quiet work.

One day Moses looks up. He sees a bush on a hill. The bush has fire in it! But the leaves stay green. The branches do not turn black. The bush keeps burning, and it does not burn up.

Moses walks closer to look. The story says a voice calls from the bush. "Moses, Moses!" Moses answers, "Here I am." The voice tells him to take off his sandals. This place is special, the voice says.

The story says it was God who spoke from the bush. God had heard his people crying in Egypt. God would help them. And God wanted Moses to help too. Moses listened carefully.

Talk about itMoses walked closer to look at something new. What do you do when you see something new?

5.5

Who Was the Messenger?

Main ideaIn Exodus 3, the Angel is in the bush and God speaks from the bush. Readers ask who was there.

Read Exodus 3 again. Verse 2 says the Angel of the LORD was in the bush. Verse 4 says God called from the bush. So who was there? People have asked this for a very long time.

Hagar’s story is like this too. In Genesis 16:13, she says she has seen the God who sees her. Some Bible scholars, like Michael Heiser, think the Angel was God himself. God came close so people could him.

Other readers think the Angel was a messenger God sent. He spoke God’s words for God. Both groups read the same story closely.

Think it through
Two Verses Side by Side

Look at Exodus 3:2. It says the Angel of the LORD was in the flame, in the bush. Now look at Exodus 3:4. It says God called to Moses from the middle of the bush. It is the same bush.

Who does verse 2 name? Who does verse 4 name? What do you notice?

Big questionIn the Bible stories, who is invited into God's family?
The story

Hello in a New Language

A new student joins the class. He speaks Spanish. Soon the whole class can say hello to him.

It is Monday at a school in Chicago. A new boy stands at the door. His name is Mateo. He just moved from far away. Mateo speaks Spanish. He knows only a few English words.

The teacher smiles. She says, "Mateo, welcome!" Then she turns to the class. "How do we say hello in Spanish?" Mateo says it softly. "Hola." The class tries it together. "Hola, Mateo!"

At lunch, Ava saves Mateo a seat. She points to her apple. She says, "Apple." Mateo points too. He says, "Manzana." They both laugh. Now each one knows a new word.

By Friday, Mateo has three friends. The class knows five Spanish words. Mateo knows ten new English words. Two languages, one class. Everyone belongs.

Talk about itHow did the class help Mateo feel welcome? What could you do for a new friend?

6.5

Babel Turned Around

Main ideaMany readers see Pentecost as Babel turned around: languages bring people together.

Remember Babel? In Genesis 11, God mixed up the people’s languages. They could not understand each other. They spread out over the earth.

Put the two stories side by side. At Babel, many languages sent people apart. At Pentecost, many languages brought people together. Some Bible scholars, like Michael Heiser, see Pentecost as the of Babel.

Other readers say Pentecost is mostly about God’s Spirit. The Spirit came to Jesus’ friends. Both readings look closely at Acts 2.

6.7

Making Room for Others

Main ideaThe stories invite everyone in, and we can welcome people who are new or different.

Look at the stories in this chapter. Jesus fed hungry people. He welcomed children. At Pentecost, every language was heard. In Revelation, every nation is together.

Many Christians read these stories as an invitation. Everyone is in God’s family, they say. Revelation 7:9 paints this picture.

You can make room for others. Learn how to say hello in a new language. Save a seat. Share a toy. Say someone’s name kindly. Small things help a lot.
