When a student tells you something

A student may tell you they are being hurt, or that something is very wrong. Do these, in this order.

  1. Listen.Let the student talk. Give them your full attention.
  2. Stay calm.Keep your face and voice steady. Your calm tells them it was right to tell.
  3. Do not promise to keep it secret.Say early: “I can’t keep this just between us. I will only tell the people who can help.”
  4. Do not investigate.Do not ask leading questions, like “Did he hit you?” Let them say it their own way. If you need to, ask only: “Is there anything else you want me to know?”
  5. Say these words.
    • “Thank you for telling me.”
    • “You did the right thing.”
    • “It is not your fault.”
    • “I am going to get you help.”
  6. Write it down soon after.Write what the student said, in their own words, as close as you can. Add the date, the time and what you saw. Write what was said, not what you think it means. Keep it out of the gradebook and any shared system.
  7. Tell the counselor the same day.If what you heard gives you reasonable cause to believe the child is abused or neglected, you also call the DCFS Hotline yourself. See below.

Illinois: you are a mandated reporter

  • Every school employee in Illinois is a mandated reporter.
  • If you have reasonable cause to believe a child is abused or neglected, you must call the DCFS Hotline:
    1-800-25-ABUSE (1-800-252-2873)
  • Reasonable cause is enough. You do not need proof, and you do not investigate first.
  • Make the call yourself, right away. Do not hand it to someone else. Telling the counselor or the principal does not take the place of your call.
  • If a child is in danger right now, call 911.

If a student may hurt themselves

When a student says, writes or shows that they want to die, want to hurt themselves, or have hurt themselves:

  1. Stay with the student.Never leave them alone, not even for a minute.
  2. Stay calm and kind.Thank them for telling you. Do not promise to keep it secret.
  3. Walk them to the counselor yourself.Do not send them alone. Call the office for another adult to watch your class. If the counselor is out, follow your school’s crisis plan and stay with the student.
  4. If someone is in danger right now, call 911.
  5. Give the student the crisis lines.988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: call or text 988. Crisis Text Line: text HOME to 741741. Keep them posted in the room all year.
  6. Write it down afterward.The student’s own words, the date and the time, as in the steps above.

The counselor takes it from there with your school’s crisis plan. Your part is to stay, to walk with them, and to tell.

Room 36 journals and sheets are never collected or read. If a student chooses to show you something they wrote, act on it the same way.

Afterward, talk with the counselor about how you are, too.

The lessons that need extra care

Each one says what must be in place before, during and after. Open one to see it.

  • ★★Highest: the counselor is in the room for the whole lesson.
  • ★Elevated: the counselor is on-site or reachable the same day.

If the counselor cannot be there as a lesson needs, move the lesson to another day. The counselor-capacity page shows how.

★★ Highest

★★U2·L6Rewriting the ScriptDecember, week 2

Before

  • The counselor is in the room for the whole lesson. Not on call, not in the hallway.
  • Crisis lines posted where every seat can see them: 988 and HOME to 741741.
  • Not before a holiday, weekend or break, and not on a fire-drill day.
  • No substitute. Only the class’s own teacher teaches it.
  • You told the counselor about students you noticed in L1–L5.
  • Families had notice and the consent form. An alternative activity is ready.
  • The sign-off form is signed and filed.
  • Take 60 seconds to settle yourself before students arrive.

During

  • Skip the quick review. Open with the grounding ritual, 4–5 minutes. Do not shorten it.
  • All work is private, on the worksheet. No pairs, no sharing. No adult reads it.
  • Step back from the center of the room. The counselor sits where they can see everyone.
  • If a student leaves, the counselor follows. You stay with the class and say: “Keep working.”
  • Say: “You do not have to share anything.” Never ask where a script came from or who wrote it.
  • Give the exit ticket 4 full minutes.

After

  • Debrief with the counselor the same day, even if no one said anything.
  • Anything about dying, self-harm or being hurt went to the counselor at the time, not first at the debrief.
  • The counselor checks in with any student you named, before Unit 3.
  • Your notes stay private: not in a gradebook, not in a shared system.
  • The crisis lines stay up for the rest of the year.
  • Take care of yourself tonight.

★★U3·L6The Agency of Release — Letter of ReleaseMarch, week 1

Before

  • The counselor is in the room before the first student walks in, and stays the whole lesson.
  • Crisis lines and the counselor’s contact posted where every seat can see them.
  • Not before a holiday, weekend or break, and not on a fire-drill day. No substitute.
  • Letter templates face-down at each seat before students arrive.
  • Enough time to reach the Release Ritual before the bell. If not, do not start the Letter. It cannot be split across two days.
  • Families had notice and the consent form. An alternative activity is ready. The sign-off form is signed and filed.
  • Take 60 seconds to settle yourself.

During

  • Opening ritual: 3 full minutes. Do not shorten it.
  • The Letter is a choice. A student may write, journal about something else, or sit quietly. No one reads it.
  • Say: “If someone is still hurting you, this letter is not for that. Tell me or the counselor today.”
  • Step back. Allow the full 15 minutes. Do not prompt, ask who, ask to share, or promise it will feel better.
  • If a student leaves, the counselor follows and you stay. If a student goes to the counselor, they meet outside the room.
  • Exit ticket: at least 3 full minutes.

After

  • Debrief with the counselor the same day, even if no one said anything.
  • Anything about dying, self-harm or being hurt went to the counselor at the time.
  • The counselor checks in with any student you named, before L7.
  • Crisis lines stay up. Take care of yourself tonight.

★U3·L3Forgiveness vs. ReconciliationFebruary, week 1

Before

  • The counselor is on-site or reachable within 60 seconds, for the whole lesson.
  • Crisis lines still posted. No substitute.
  • Not within 24 hours of a school-wide safety incident or a conflict-heavy event.
  • Card B (The Family Situation): only with the counselor in the room. Skip it if you know a student lives with ongoing harm at home.

During

  • Opening ritual, 2 minutes. Then say first: “Forgiveness is never required in situations involving ongoing harm.”
  • Never ask students for their own forgiveness stories.
  • If a student hints at ongoing harm, or pressure to forgive someone still hurting them: pause the cards, signal the counselor, and follow the steps at the top of this page.

After

  • Brief the counselor the same day, even if no one said anything.
  • Reasonable cause to believe a child is abused or neglected: you call the DCFS Hotline yourself.

Talking about it in the room

How to talk about suicide and harm with students, following the AFSP safe-messaging guidelines.

Do

  • Use plain, calm words. It is all right to say “suicide.”
  • Every time, say where help is: the counselor, 988, and HOME to 741741.
  • Speak of hope: people get through very hard times, with help.
  • Keep students’ writing private. No one has to share.
  • Tell the counselor what you saw and heard.

Avoid

  • Details of how anyone hurt themselves or died.
  • Calling suicide common, a way out, or something that makes sense.
  • “Committed suicide.” Say “died by suicide.”
  • Treating tears or distress as a sign the lesson worked.
  • Guesses or labels about a student’s mind. Say what you saw instead.
