The Recording Studio · Learn a song

Build Runaway, one part at a time

A famous song from 2010, built in our Studio. One note, four chords, a big slow beat, a bass that walks down. You make each part, then put them together.

← The Recording Studio

0Before you start

Runaway is by Kanye West, from 2010. Listen to it first, so your ears know where you are going.

The recording has grown-up words. Listen with a grown-up, or find the instrumental: the music with no voice. This lesson is about the music only.

Go one part at a time. Take a break whenever you like. Every part sounds good on its own.

1Set the song: 87 · E major

Runaway moves slowly: about 87 beats a minute. Its home note is E, and it sounds bright: major.

Press the button. Every room in the Studio will open at 87 · E major, so all your parts line up.

On paper: in each room, set Tempo to 87 and Key to E · Major.

2The famous note

The song starts with one piano note, played again and again. It is a high E.

Find E: the white key just after the two black keys.

Open the Piano

  1. Instrument: Grand piano.
  2. Pick an E high on the keys. Tap it once on every beat. Count out loud: 1, 2, 3, 4.
  3. Keep it even, like a clock. Play it for 8 bars: 32 taps.
  4. When it feels steady, press ● Record, play your 8 bars, then ■ Stop. Press + Add to My Track.
Why one note works: A single note, repeated, makes you wait. The listener leans in. Big songs often start small.

In the Piano

  1. Key E · Major · Tempo 87.
  2. How the chords play: Pulse · four beats.
  3. Press Clear, then Make my own and tap pads 1, 5, 6, 4.
  4. Press Play the chords. Tap the high E on every beat over them.
  5. Record 8 bars (the four chords, two times round) and add it to My Track.
Why the high E fits: Look at the notes. E is inside three of the four chords. Over B it rubs a little and wants to move on. That small pull keeps the song going.

You know these chords already: 1 5 6 4 is the Pop anthem pattern, in a new key. Many players put D♯ at the bottom of the B chord (B/D♯). The bass does that for us in part 5.

4The drums: big and slow

The drums come in hard after the piano. A heavy kick, a loud snare and clap on beats 2 and 4, and a steady hi-hat. Here is a beat in that style, made for this lesson.

A filled step is a hit. The orange steps are the loud ones. Each beat is 4 steps: beats start on steps 1, 5, 9 and 13.

Open the Drum Machine

  1. Tempo 87. Bank A: the drums. Start from an empty loop: press Clear the loop.
  2. Kick first (pad 1). Tap the steps in the grid: 1, 8 and 11.
  3. Snare (pad 2) and Clap (pad 5): steps 5 and 13. Those are beats 2 and 4.
  4. Closed hat (pad 3): every odd step, 1, 3, 5 … 15.
  5. Press ▶ Play. Nod your head on the snare. Change the kit if you want a heavier sound.
  6. Press + Add the loop to My Track.
Why it hits hard: Slow songs leave space between the hits. Each kick and snare has room to ring, so it sounds bigger.

Extra: on bank B · Chords you can tap the same pads 1, 5, 6, 4. The Drum Machine plays chords too.

Open the Bass

  1. All four notes live on one string: the A string. Play fret 7, 6, 4, then the open string.
  2. Turn on Play with my drum beat, and play one note at the start of each bar.
  3. Play it on the same beat as the first kick. Bass and kick together hit hardest.
  4. Record 16 bars and add it to My Track.
Why it walks down: Each step down is the smallest move there is. Notes that fall step by step feel like walking away. That is the feeling of the song.

6The ending: strings

The song ends with a long part for strings, like a cello, and a voice changed by an effect until it sounds like an instrument. Ours keeps the strings.

Open the Band

  1. Pick a strings sound: Cello or String section.
  2. Play the same four chords, 1 5 6 4. Hold each one for the whole bar.
  3. Record 8 bars and add it to My Track.

7Put it together

Now every part is on My Track. Line them up like this. Count in bars.

Bars 1–8Intro: the high E alone.
Bars 9–16The piano chords come in, with the high E on top.
Bars 17–32Drop the drums and the bass in. The big part.
Bars 33–36Take a breath: drums out, piano only.
Bars 37–44Everything back in.
Bars 45–52The ending: strings, with a long fade out.

Open the Mixing Desk

  1. Use Starts at bar to move each part to its place. Use Plays to repeat a loop (the drum loop plays 4 times in bars 17–32).
  2. Volume: the piano loudest. Drums a little lower. Bass in between.
  3. Side: keep the drums and bass in the middle. Move the strings a little to one side.
  4. Give the strings a long Fade out. Then press ▶ Play and listen to the whole song.
Why parts come in one by one: Each new part makes the song grow. When something drops out, the next part feels even bigger.
