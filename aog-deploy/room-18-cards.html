Scenario Cards

Room 18 · Grades 3–5 · two cards a lesson

Each page contains two scenario cards for a single lesson. The page is designed to be printed and cut along the marked cut lines.

Cards are color-coded by unit: Navy = Unit 1, Teal = Unit 2, Purple = Unit 3, Green = Unit 4.

Laminating the cards allows reuse across multiple class periods and school years.

Each card contains: The Situation (read aloud or displayed), Discussion Questions (guide the conversation), and where relevant, a Teacher Note (italicized — not read to students).

Risk-flagged lessons (★ Elevated, ★★ Highest Risk) are marked on the lesson header. See the Teacher Master Document for full Safety Check protocols before delivering these lessons.

Scenario cards are also embedded in their lessons in the Teacher Master Document for reference during delivery.

Knowing & Accepting Yourself

Lesson 1

Who Am I?

1-A

WHO AM I?

The situation

Marcus shared on his identity web that he loves drawing comics. A classmate said, "That's babyish." Marcus feels embarrassed and wishes he had said something else.

Discussion questions
  1. Is Marcus's love of comics part of who he truly is?
  2. Why does a classmate's comment make him want to hide it?
  3. What is the difference between something being part of your identity and something being "cool" according to others?
  4. What would you say to Marcus to help him hold his identity with confidence?
1-B

WHO AM I?

The situation

Amara is filling out her strengths inventory and cannot think of a single thing to write. She keeps crossing things out. "I'm not really good at anything," she tells her partner. Her partner looks surprised.

Discussion questions
  1. Why might Amara struggle to name her own strengths, even though others can see them?
  2. What is the difference between a strength you were born with and one you developed through effort?
  3. If you were Amara's partner, what specific strength might you have already noticed about her in just this one class?
Lesson 2

Emotions Are Information

1-C

EMOTIONS ARE INFORMATION

The situation

During a math test, Jayla's mind goes blank even though she studied. She feels a wave of panic. After the test: "I hate when that happens — my feelings always ruin everything."

Discussion questions
  1. What message was Jayla's panic actually trying to send her?
  2. Is Jayla's emotion the problem — or is it a messenger pointing at a different problem?
  3. If Jayla could name the emotion precisely, how might that help her next time?

EMOTIONS ARE INFORMATION

The situation

Deon gets in the car after school and says "Fine" when asked about his day — but he actually felt lonely, left out, and invisible. He didn't have words for it.

Discussion questions
  1. What emotions was Deon actually feeling? Can you name them more precisely than "fine"?
  2. What message were those emotions trying to carry?
  3. Why might it be hard to share emotions, even when we do have words for them?
Lesson 3

Everyone Makes Mistakes

1-E

EVERYONE MAKES MISTAKES

The situation

Priya forgot her part of a group project. The group had to present without it and got a lower grade. That night, Priya kept repeating: "I always ruin everything. I am so irresponsible. I am a bad friend."

Discussion questions
  1. Which of Priya's self-statements are about the MISTAKE, and which are about her IDENTITY?
  2. What is the difference between "I made an irresponsible choice" and "I am irresponsible"?

Inner Critic: "I always ruin everything. I'm a terrible friend." │ Inner Coach: "I forgot something important. That hurt my team. I need to apologize and figure out how to be more reliable."

1-F

EVERYONE MAKES MISTAKES

During soccer tryouts, Jaylen missed the winning penalty kick in front of everyone. His older brother says: "Messi missed penalty kicks. Jordan missed shots. That doesn't make you or them a bad player."

Discussion questions
  1. Is the missed kick evidence that Jaylen is a bad soccer player — or evidence that he is a soccer player?
  2. Why do we hold onto our worst moments as if they define us?
  3. What would you tell Jaylen to help him put the miss in its proper place?
Lesson 4

My Inner Critic vs. My Inner Coach

1-G

INNER CRITIC vs. INNER COACH

The situation

Sofia just got her science test back — she failed. Her immediate thought: "See? I knew it. I'm just not smart. There's no point trying harder because I'm always going to fail."

Discussion questions
  1. Which voice is speaking — the Inner Critic or the Inner Coach?
  2. Rewrite Sofia's inner monologue as if the Inner Coach were speaking.

Inner Critic: "I'm just not smart. There's no point trying." │ Inner Coach: "That didn't go the way I wanted. What did I not understand? I can figure out how to study differently."

INNER CRITIC vs. INNER COACH

The situation

During a class presentation, Theo's voice cracked and the class giggled. His inner voice said: "Everyone thinks I'm so weird. I'm never doing a presentation again."

Discussion questions

  2. How is the Inner Critic's advice making Theo's future worse?
  3. What would the Inner Coach say instead?

Inner Critic: "Everyone thinks I'm weird. I'm never doing that again." │ Inner Coach: "That was embarrassing. Voices crack — it happens. I got through it, and next time will be easier."

Lesson 1

What Is Forgiveness, Really?

2-A

WHAT IS FORGIVENESS, REALLY?

The situation

After hurting her friend, Sofia cannot forgive herself. "Forgiving myself feels like I'm saying it was okay. And it wasn't okay. I was awful."

Discussion questions
  1. What is Sofia confusing forgiveness with? What does self-forgiveness actually mean?
  2. Does forgiving yourself require that what you did was acceptable?

Myth: "Self-forgiveness means what I did was okay." │ Truth: Forgiving yourself means releasing the weight of it — not excusing the action.

2-B

WHAT IS FORGIVENESS, REALLY?

The situation

Elijah got a two-day suspension eight months ago. He did everything required — apologized, served the suspension, made amends. But he still introduces himself as "the kid who got suspended."

Discussion questions
  1. What myth about forgiveness might Elijah be living out?
  2. Why might someone hold onto a label even after they have done everything repair requires?

Body Check-In: Where do you feel tension when you are being hard on yourself? Chest? Stomach? Shoulders? Jaw?

Lesson 3

Accountability Without Self-Punishment

2-E

ACCOUNTABILITY WITHOUT SELF-PUNISHMENT

The situation

Isabella accidentally broke her neighbor's prized garden gnome while playing. She told her parents and offered to pay for it with her allowance. But weeks later, she is still apologizing every time she sees the neighbor — even though the neighbor has clearly moved on.

Three-Part Map: Broke the gnome ✓ Impact acknowledged ✓ Offered to pay ✓ COMPLETE. The self-punishment can stop here.

YOUR HURT IS REAL AND IT MATTERS

The situation

Why People Hurt People

3-C

WHY PEOPLE HURT PEOPLE

The situation

Unit 4

Grace & Generosity

Lesson 1

What Does Grace Look Like?

4-A

WHAT DOES GRACE LOOK LIKE?

The situation

When Mateo transferred to a new school mid-year, he sat alone at lunch for three weeks. On the twenty-second day, a girl named Ryn moved her tray and said, "You can sit here." When asked what made her do it, Ryn shrugged: "Nobody should have to eat alone that long."

Discussion questions
  1. What makes Ryn's action an act of grace rather than just friendliness?
  2. Mateo had done nothing to earn a place in the group. Does grace require that someone deserve it?

Grace Defined: Giving something generous — kindness, a second chance, inclusion — to someone who has not necessarily earned it. A choice, not a requirement. A strength, not a weakness.

4-B

WHAT DOES GRACE LOOK LIKE?

The situation

During a heated argument at recess, Omar said something cutting to his friend Darius. He meant it to sting — and it did. Afterward, he apologized sincerely. Darius was quiet for a moment, then said: "I know you were angry. We are still friends." He did not make Omar grovel.

Discussion questions
  1. What did Darius choose to give Omar that Omar had not yet earned back?
  2. Grace is sometimes described as choosing the relationship over the wound. What does that mean here?

Grace vs. Doormat: Darius chose to extend grace — he was not forced to. A doormat has no choice. A person extending grace has full choice and chooses generosity deliberately.

Lesson 2

What Does Mercy Look Like?

4-C

WHAT DOES MERCY LOOK LIKE?

The situation

After Priya was caught passing a note with an unkind comment about the substitute teacher, the substitute teacher paused. "I have seen meaner notes in thirty years. I am not going to make this a bigger deal than it is. But think about how it would feel to read your name in someone's note." No referral. No call home. Priya thought about that comment for the rest of the week.

Discussion questions
  1. What consequence did the substitute teacher have the power to deliver — and what did she choose instead?
  2. Why might the substitute's mercy have been more effective than a referral?

Mercy Defined: Choosing not to deliver the full consequence someone might deserve. Research shows unexpected mercy often produces more genuine change than punishment.

4-D

WHAT DOES MERCY LOOK LIKE?

The situation

When Zach's group was assigned to clean up the science lab, he got distracted and left early without finishing. His lab partner Imani stayed and cleaned everything alone — again. When the teacher asked what happened, Imani said "We got it done." Zach said, "You covered for me. I owe you." She said, "Just don't leave me with it again."

Discussion questions
  1. What did Imani choose not to do — and what did that choice cost her?
  2. How is what Imani did different from repeatedly covering for someone who takes advantage?

4-G

SMALL ACTS, BIG DIFFERENCE

The situation

Maya noticed the cafeteria worker named Ms. Linda greets every student who says hi to her — but most kids barely look up. Maya's grace gap: thanking Ms. Linda by name every day for a week.

Discussion questions
  1. Why does Maya's micro-action matter even though it is small?
  2. What is the difference between this and a one-time gesture?
4-H

SMALL ACTS, BIG DIFFERENCE

The situation

James noticed a kid in his class who never raises his hand. James's grace gap: making one specific positive comment about that kid's contribution every day for five days — even something small.

Discussion questions
  1. What might shift for the other kid?
  2. What might shift for James?

GRACE WITH HARD PEOPLE

The situation

Tina has a relative who makes critical comments at every family gathering. Her mom always says 'just ignore her.' But Tina dreads every visit.

Lesson 7

Community Charter & Year-End Celebration

4-M-CHARTER★

COMMUNITY CHARTER — WRITING GUIDE

The situation

Every person in this room contributes one commitment in their own voice. One thing you are choosing to carry into your future from this year. Charter language should be specific and true — not generic. "We will be kind" is not a charter commitment. "We will say something when we see someone left out" is a charter commitment.

Discussion questions
  1. What is ONE specific, true thing you are choosing to carry from this year?
  2. How do you want to finish this sentence: "In this class, we chose to ___"?
  3. What would a student reading this charter three years from now understand about who this class decided to be?
Safety

ELEVATED RISK LESSON — Notify school counselor minimum ONE WEEK in advance. See Teacher Master for full Safety Check.

Teacher note — not read to students. Every student must contribute one line. Every student must sign with a full signature — not initials. Photograph the signed charter. Share with families.

4-N-LETTERS★

FUTURE-SELF LETTERS — RETURN PROTOCOL

The situation

These letters were written in December, sealed, and stored by your teacher. Today — in May — you are the person your December self wrote to. Open the letter. Read it silently. Give it eight full minutes.

Discussion questions
  1. Who did you write to in December? Who are you now in May?
  2. What changed — and what stayed the same?
  3. What would you write to your future self now, at the end of this year?
Safety

ELEVATED RISK LESSON — Notify school counselor minimum ONE WEEK in advance. See Teacher Master for full Safety Check.

Teacher note — not read to students. Return sealed envelopes. Allow 8 full minutes of silent reading before asking any questions. This moment is one of the most powerful in the full curriculum. Do not rush it.
