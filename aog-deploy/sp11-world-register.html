There is no single correct Spanish

One language, drawn the way a book draws it: one small flat box.

Both have names. Variety is the difference between places — autobús, camión, guagua, colectivo. Register is the difference between situations — ¿Qué tal? and ¿Cómo está usted? Neither one is an error. Both follow rules.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

One country speaks the real, pure Spanish, and the others speak a version of it.

No such ranking exists. Every variety has a full grammar and follows regular rules. What moves from place to place is prestige, and prestige comes from power, money and which cities make the television and the textbooks.

Prestige comes from power. Grammar comes from rules.

⚠️ People often think…

Vos is slang, or an old form, and vos tenés is a mistake for tú tienes.

Vos is the ordinary everyday pronoun in Argentina, Uruguay and Paraguay, in much of Central America, and in parts of other countries. The verb changes with it: vos hablás, vos tenés, vos sos. It appears in the newspapers and the novels there.

Vos hablás is as regular as tú hablas.

⚠️ People often think…

Estoy embarazada means I am embarrassed.

Embarazada means pregnant. A student saying estoy embarazada to a class has announced a pregnancy. The word for embarrassed is avergonzado or avergonzada. The two words look alike because both came from Latin, and each language kept a different part of the old meaning.

Looking alike is not meaning the same.

⚠️ People often think…

Once the greeting is polite, the rest of the email can sound however you like.

The reader hears the register as one sound. Estimada señora López: followed by Un abrazo reads as two different writers. Estimada goes with Atentamente; Querida goes with Un abrazo. Pick the pronoun first and let it decide all eight lines.

Start formal, stay formal. Estimada with Atentamente.

Helpful Hints

🧭 The unit in one line

Spanish is official in twenty countries and in Puerto Rico → its words come from Latin, from Arabic, and from Nahuatl, Quechua, Taíno and Guaraní → every country speaks it a little differently, and none of them speaks it wrongly → your job is to pick the right register: tú, usted or vos, Estimado/a or Querido/a, Atentamente or Un abrazo.

20 countries + Puerto Rico. tú · usted · vos. Estimado/a is formal; Querido/a is warm.

🤝 The one idea

Register means how formal your language is. Spanish marks it in the pronoun itself, so you choose a register before you finish your first word. Different is not wrong: guagua, camión, colectivo and autobús are all the right word somewhere.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • Do not say a country speaks “bad Spanish” or “the purest Spanish”. There is no such ranking. Every variety follows rules.
  • Puerto Rico is a territory of the United States, not a country. Spanish and English are both official there.
  • Brazil is not Spanish-speaking. Its language is Portuguese.
  • Embarazada does not mean embarrassed. It means pregnant.
  • In a Spanish letter the greeting takes a colon — Estimada señora López: — not a comma.
  • Vos is not slang and not old-fashioned. It is the ordinary everyday pronoun for millions of speakers.
  • When you do not know which pronoun to use, start with usted. Moving down to tú is easy; moving up is awkward.

📚 Go to the sources

These open another website and need the internet. Look up embarazada in the dictionary and read the definition in Spanish. Then look up voseo in the Diccionario panhispánico de dudas and see how carefully the academies describe it.

  • Diccionario de la lengua española (RAE and ASALE) — the free dictionary the academies write together; each entry marks the countries where a meaning is used
  • Diccionario panhispánico de dudas — the academies’ answers to questions like voseo, ustedes and how to write a letter
  • Asociación de Academias de la Lengua Española — the association itself: see the list of member academies and where each one sits
  • Instituto Cervantes — the public institution of Spain that teaches Spanish abroad and reports each year on the language in the world
