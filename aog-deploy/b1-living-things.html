One of these three is alive

A flame, a mushroom, a rock. One is alive — and it is not the one that moves.

Living things do six things: made of cells, use energy, grow, respond, reproduce, and hold steady inside. Fire moves and uses energy — and fails the other four.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

If it moves, it is alive.

Moving is one sign, and it is not enough. Fire moves, spreads and uses energy, and it has no cells and no way to hold itself steady. Use all six characteristics, not one.

Six out of six, not one out of six.

⚠️ People often think…

A virus is a tiny living creature.

A virus has no cells and cannot copy itself without hijacking one. That is why it is not counted as alive, and why antibiotics do nothing to it.

No cells, no life. It needs a host.

⚠️ People often think…

Mushrooms are plants that grow in the dark.

Fungi are their own kingdom. Plants make their own food from light; fungi absorb food that something else already made.

Plants make food. Fungi absorb it.

⚠️ People often think…

Bacteria are germs that make you sick.

Most bacteria do not. Some help you digest food, some make yogurt and cheese, and some put nitrogen into soil. Only a small share cause disease.

A few make you sick. Most keep you going.

🧠 The Big Six — memorize these cold

Every living thing: is made of Cells, Responds, Grows, Reproduces, Uses energy, and keeps its inside Balanced (homeostasis).

🪜 The taxonomy ladder

Domain → Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species. Smaller group = more alike.

Dear King Philip Came Over For Good Soup

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Viruses are NOT alive — no cells, and they can’t reproduce without a host.
  • Mushrooms are NOT plants — fungi absorb food, they can’t make it.
  • Most bacteria don’t make you sick — many are helpful.
  • Moving ≠ living. Use all six characteristics, not one.
  • Fire isn’t alive — no cells, no homeostasis.

✍️ Scientific names — easy points

  • Two parts: Genus + species — like Homo sapiens.
  • Genus gets a CAPITAL; species stays lowercase; both in italics.
  • Same genus = close relatives.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • “Cite evidence” wants a fact + because.
  • Expect a mystery organism to classify from its traits.
  • Read a chart’s title and headings first.

TermMeaning in 10 words or less
OrganismAny living thing.
CellThe smallest unit of life.
HomeostasisKeeping conditions inside the body steady.
StimulusA change that makes an organism react.
ResponseWhat the organism does about the change.
AutotrophMakes its own food (plants, algae).
HeterotrophMust eat or absorb food (animals, fungi).
UnicellularMade of one cell.
MulticellularMany cells with different jobs.
ClassificationSorting organisms into groups by traits.
GenusGroup of very similar species; first name.
SpeciesOrganisms that can reproduce together; last name.
DomainThe biggest group — there are three.
ProkaryoteCell with no nucleus (bacteria, archaea).
EukaryoteCell with a nucleus.
VirusNonliving particle that copies itself inside host cells.
Vertebrate / InvertebrateBackbone / no backbone.
