Most of the energy never makes it

Start with 1,000 units of energy in the grass.

Energy flows one way and leaves as heat at every step — about 90% of it. Matter cycles and comes back; energy does not. That is why there are always fewer foxes than rabbits.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Energy gets recycled through the ecosystem.

Matter cycles. Energy does not. It enters from the sun, moves up one way, and leaves as heat at every step — which is why the sun has to keep shining.

Matter goes in circles. Energy goes in one door and out as heat.

⚠️ People often think…

The top predator gets the most energy.

It gets the least. Only about 10% passes up at each step, so a top predator is living on what is left after two or three of those cuts.

Higher up the chain, less energy — and fewer animals.

⚠️ People often think…

Decomposers are the optional part of a food web.

They are the part that returns the matter. Without them, nutrients stay locked inside dead bodies and the producers eventually run out.

No decomposers, no way back to the soil.

⚠️ People often think…

Plants get their food from the soil.

Plants MAKE their food by photosynthesis, out of light, air and water. Soil supplies water and minerals — useful, but not food.

Soil is the water bowl, not the plate.

Helpful Hints

🔑 The one rule that unlocks the whole topic

Energy FLOWS one way; matter CYCLES around. Energy enters as sunlight, moves up the food chain, and leaves as heat — gone. Matter (carbon, water, nitrogen) is reused forever.

Energy flows. Matter loops.

🏹 Food chain arrows

The arrow points in the direction the ENERGY moves — from the eaten to the eater. Grass → rabbit → fox means the rabbit gets energy FROM grass.

The arrow means “feeds into,” not “attacks.”

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Energy is NOT recycled — only matter cycles. Energy escapes as heat at every step.
  • The top predator gets the LEAST energy — only ~10% passes up each level.
  • Decomposers are not optional — without them, nutrients stay locked in dead bodies.
  • Plants don’t get food from soil — they MAKE it by photosynthesis; soil gives water and minerals.

👥 Know your roles

  • Producers (plants, algae): make food from sunlight — energy’s entry point.
  • Consumers: herbivores eat plants, carnivores eat animals, omnivores eat both.
  • Decomposers (bacteria, fungi): break down dead matter, returning nutrients to soil — the recyclers.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • Expect a food web: “what happens if the ___ disappears?” Trace every arrow touching it, both directions.
  • 10% rule math: 10,000 units in grass → 1,000 in rabbits → 100 in foxes. Practice dividing by 10.
  • Cycle questions want the loop named step by step — where the carbon/water goes, in order.

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Sort ten things into biotic and abiotic?
  • Draw a food chain with correct arrows?
  • Apply the 10% rule to a 3-level pyramid?
  • Explain why energy must keep entering but matter never runs out?
  • Walk the water cycle and the carbon cycle out loud?
TermMeaning in 10 words or less
EcosystemAll living things + nonliving surroundings in an area.
Biotic factorA living (or once-living) part of an ecosystem.
Abiotic factorA nonliving part — sun, water, soil, temperature.
PopulationAll members of ONE species in one area.
CommunityAll the populations living together in an area.
ProducerMakes its own food; energy’s entry point.
ConsumerGets energy by eating other organisms.
DecomposerBreaks down dead matter; recycles nutrients.
Food webAll the food chains connected together.
Energy pyramidShows energy shrinking ~90% at each level.
EvaporationLiquid water becoming water vapor.
CondensationVapor becoming liquid — clouds form.
PrecipitationRain, snow, sleet — water falling back down.
Nitrogen cycleNitrogen looping air → soil → living things → back.
