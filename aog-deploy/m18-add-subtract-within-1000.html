When a column cannot pay, trade

52 take away 27. But two ones cannot give away seven ones.

When a column cannot pay, trade from the one next door — and that column is one smaller afterward. Forgetting to make it smaller is where most wrong answers come from.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

In 52 − 27, you do 7 − 2 because 2 − 7 “doesn't work.”

It really doesn't work — which is the signal to TRADE, not to flip the digits around. Flipping gives 35. Trading gives 25, and 25 is the right answer.

Can't take it? Go next door and borrow.

⚠️ People often think…

After you trade, the column you took from stays the same.

It is one smaller — you spent it. In 435 − 162, the 4 becomes a 3, so the hundreds are 3 − 1 = 2, not 4 − 1 = 3. Cross it out and write the new digit, every time.

Cross it out. If you didn't write it, you'll forget it.

Helpful Hints

➕ Adding in columns — the four steps

Line up ones under ones, tens under tens, hundreds under hundreds → add the ONES first; if you get 10 or more, write the ones digit and carry 1 ten → add the TENS, counting the carried ten; if you get 10 or more, write the tens digit and carry 1 hundred → add the HUNDREDS → read the answer and check it against an estimate. 347 + 285: 7 + 5 = 12 (write 2, carry 1), 1 + 4 + 8 = 13 (write 3, carry 1), 1 + 3 + 2 = 6. Answer: 632.

Line up, ones first, ten or more means trade.

🔟 The whole topic in one idea

Every place holds ONE digit, 0 to 9. When a place gets too full (10 or more), you trade 10 of them for 1 of the next place up: 10 ones = 1 ten, 10 tens = 1 hundred. When a place is too empty to subtract from, you trade the other way: 1 ten = 10 ones, 1 hundred = 10 tens. The amount never changes — only how it is written.

Ten of these is one of those.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Flipping the digits when subtracting: doing 7 − 2 in 52 − 27 because 2 − 7 “doesn’t work.” It doesn’t — so trade a ten. The answer is 25, not 35.
  • Forgetting the carried 1. The little 1 on top of the tens column is a real ten. Add it.
  • Writing 12 or 13 inside one column. A place holds one digit — trade the rest to the next place.
  • Forgetting to make the top digit one less after a trade. In 435 − 162 the 4 becomes 3, so the hundreds are 3 − 1 = 2, not 4 − 1.
  • Adding 10 as if it were 100 or 1. 456 + 10 = 466 — only the tens digit moves.
  • Skipping the estimate. 347 + 285 is about 650; if you got 532, something is missing.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A column sum or difference — “find the answer.” Ones first. Watch for a column that reaches 10.
  • “Does this problem need a trade?” Look at each column before you work.
  • “Fill in the missing digit” after a trade — what the 5 became, what the 2 became.
  • “Add 10” or “add 100” in your head — which digit moves?
  • “Is this answer reasonable?” Round, add, compare.
  • A story problem — decide first: put together, or take away?
  • “Explain why” — say what a trade is: 10 of one place equals 1 of the next.

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Say what each digit in a three-digit number is worth?
  • Add two three-digit numbers with one trade, and with two?
  • Subtract with a trade without flipping the digits?
  • Tell before you start whether a problem needs a trade?
  • Add or subtract 10 or 100 in your head?
  • Estimate a sum and say whether an answer is reasonable?
  • Check a subtraction by adding?
