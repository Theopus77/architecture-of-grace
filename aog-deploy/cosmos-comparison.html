Primary documents

Ptolemy's Almagest (about 150 CE) is the full mathematical geocentric system. Copernicus's De revolutionibus (1543) puts the Sun at the center. Kepler's Astronomia nova (1609) replaces the circles with ellipses. Galileo's Sidereus nuncius (1610) reports the moons of Jupiter and the phases of Venus.

Institutions

Manuscripts and early prints of these works are held by the Vatican Library, the British Library, the Bodleian, the Bibliothèque nationale de France, and university libraries. This page does not claim access to restricted archives.

The shift

The change was not one day. Copernicus published a Sun-centered geometry. It predicted about as well as Ptolemy until Kepler's ellipses and Newton's gravity. The phases of Venus, reported by Galileo, are hard to square with a simple geocentric scheme.
