Balanced does not mean nothing is pushing

A book sitting still on a table. Nothing is moving, so it looks like nothing is happening.

Balanced means the forces cancel, not that there are none. Two equal forces pushing opposite ways leave the thing still — and the moment one gets bigger, it moves that way.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

A rolling ball stops because it runs out of push.

Push is not fuel in a tank. Friction between the ball and the floor pushes backward the whole way and slows it down. On ice, with almost no friction, it keeps going and going.

Friction stops it. Push is not stored.

⚠️ People often think…

A magnet picks up any small metal thing.

Only iron and steel. A penny, an aluminum can and a gold ring are all metal and none of them stick. Test it before you decide — metal is not the same as magnetic.

Iron and steel only. Test it.

Push, balance, predict, prove

1 Watch one

Two arrows on a box: one pointing left, one pointing right, both the same size. Does it move?

  1. Compare the sizes: both the same.
  2. Compare the directions: opposite.
  3. Equal and opposite means they cancel out.
  4. It does not move. Make one arrow bigger and it moves that way. ✓

Helpful Hints

🪜 From a push to a prediction — the ladder

A force is a push or pull → balanced forces change nothing, unbalanced forces change motion → friction slows, gravity pulls down, magnets pull without touching → motion that repeats is a pattern → a pattern lets you predict → a fair test lets you prove.

Push, balance, slow, pull, pattern, predict, prove.

🤝 The whole topic in one idea

Nothing starts, stops or turns by itself — a force did it. When you see motion change, find the push or pull. When you don’t, the forces are balanced.

Find the force.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Balanced doesn’t mean ‘no forces’. Gravity and the table are both pushing on the still book.
  • A ball doesn’t ‘run out of push’. Friction stops it.
  • Same poles REPEL. Opposite poles ATTRACT. Say it out loud.
  • A magnet only pulls iron and steel — not paper, not plastic, not a penny.
  • A fair test changes ONE thing. Change two and the test proves nothing.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • Two arrows on a box — “does it move?” (same size, opposite ways = no; one bigger = yes, that way).
  • A ramp table — “predict the next row” (find how much each step adds).
  • Two magnets drawn — “push or pull?” (read the letters on the ends).
  • An experiment — “is it fair?” (count what changed).
