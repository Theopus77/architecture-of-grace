Three questions, three kinds of unit

How long is it? Count along the edge: 4 units.

Length is counted in units. Covering is area, counted in SQUARE units. Filling is volume, counted in CUBIC units. Decide which the question wants before you pick a formula — then let the units check you.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

The slanted side of a triangle is its height.

Height meets the base at a right angle. The slanted side is longer, and using it gives an answer that is too big.

No right angle, not the height.

⚠️ People often think…

A 12-inch pizza has r = 12.

A 12-inch pizza is 12 inches ACROSS. That is the diameter, so r = 6. Area uses the radius, never the diameter.

Across is diameter. Halve it first.

⚠️ People often think…

πr² means multiply π by r, then square it.

Square the radius first, then multiply by π. With r = 6: 36, then 36 × 3.14 = 113.04. Doing it the other way gives about 355.

Only the r is squared.

⚠️ People often think…

Surface area is the three faces you can see.

A box has six faces, and the hidden three match the three you see. Three faces is exactly half the answer.

Count six, or double the three.

Helpful Hints

🪜 From a rectangle to everything else — the ladder

Rectangle (base × height) → parallelogram (slide the end: still base × height) → triangle (half of that) → trapezoid (average the bases) → circle (πr²: slices rearranged into a rectangle) → net (add the faces) → prism (base area × height).

Every formula here is a rectangle in disguise.

🤝 The whole topic in one idea

Length is counted in units, covering in SQUARE units, filling in CUBIC units. Decide which one the question wants before you reach for a formula — then let the units check your answer.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • The slanted side is not the height. Height meets the base at a right angle.
  • Forgetting the ½ on triangles and trapezoids.
  • Radius or diameter? Area uses the RADIUS. A 12-inch pizza has r = 6.
  • πr² means π × r × r — square first. It is not (π × r)², and it is not 2πr.
  • Surface area needs all six faces. Three faces is half the answer.
  • In a composite figure, the missing length is usually a subtraction.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A shape with an extra number on it — pick the base and the HEIGHT, ignore the slanted side.
  • A composite figure — cut it, find the missing length, add the pieces (or subtract a hole).
  • A circle given by its diameter — halve it before finding the area.
  • A net — find each face, add them all.
  • A story — wrapping, painting, filling, fencing: name the measure first.
  • A box with fraction edges — multiply the mixed numbers carefully, two at a time.

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Find the area of a parallelogram, a triangle and a trapezoid — and explain the ½?
  • Cut a composite figure into pieces and find its area?
  • Find a circle’s circumference and area from either the radius or the diameter?
  • Sketch a net and use it to find surface area?
  • Find the volume of a prism, even with fraction edges?
  • Tell from a story whether it wants length, area, surface area or volume?
