Lights, Colors and Kindness

Festivals and Values

Hindu families keep many festivals. Two big ones are Diwali and Holi. Diwali is the festival of lights. Holi is the festival of colors.

Festivals come with stories. The stories often teach values. You will learn namaste, ahimsa and seva. All children can talk about these ideas.

By the end, you can say how families get ready for Diwali. You can tell about Holi and Prahlada. You can name ways to be kind.

The big question

How do festivals and stories help people be kind?

Diwali: The Festival of Lights

Diwali
Big questionWhy do so many families light lamps at Diwali?
The story

A Flower by the Door

A girl draws a rangoli flower by the front door and sets a tiny lamp in the middle.

It is a cool autumn evening. Priya kneels by her front door. She has little bowls of colored powder. Red, yellow, green and blue.

She pinches some red powder. She lets it fall in a curvy line. Line by line, a big flower grows. Her grandma helps with the petals.

Then Priya holds a tiny clay lamp. Her dad lights it for her. She sets it in the middle of the flower. The little flame dances.

"Now the house is ready," says Grandma. "Happy Diwali!" Inside, the whole family is getting ready for the festival of lights.

Talk about itPriya made something beautiful to welcome people. How does your family get ready for a special day?

The Festival of Lights

5.1

What Is Diwali?

Main ideaDiwali is a festival of lights in the autumn.

is a festival of lights. It comes in the autumn, in October or November. In many places it lasts five days.

Families clean their homes. They light lamps. They wear new clothes. They visit family and share sweets. It is a very happy time.

Think it through

Look at the calendar on the fridge. Five days in a row have little stars. A boy points to the middle day. "That is the big night of lights," his mom says.

Why Families Celebrate

Different Stories, One Festival

Main ideaHindu families remember different stories at Diwali.

Many Hindus remember Rama and Sita coming home. People lit lamps to welcome them. Other Hindus honor Lakshmi. She is the goddess of good fortune.

In South India, many remember a story of Krishna. The stories are different. The lights are the same.

Lakshmi Puja

Main ideaMany families honor Lakshmi with a puja on Diwali night.

Main ideaSikhs and Jains keep Diwali too, for their own reasons.

Hindus are not the only people who keep Diwali. Sikhs light lamps too. They remember Guru Hargobind being set free.

Jains keep Diwali too. They remember their great teacher Mahavira. Each faith has its own reason. Each lights lamps.

Holi, Namaste and Helping Others

Values
Big questionHow can we be kind to people and to every living thing?
The story

Colors in the Air

Pink and green powder fills the air, and two old friends laugh and hug.

It is spring. The sun is warm again. Children run into the park with bags of colored powder. Pink, green, yellow and blue.

Arjun sees his friend Meera across the grass. Last week they had a fight. They did not talk for days. Arjun feels a little shy.

Meera smiles. She tosses a puff of pink powder at him. Arjun laughs. He tosses green powder back. Soon they are both covered in color.

"Happy Holi!" they shout. They hug. Many families say Holi is a day to make up with friends. Arjun and Meera are friends again.

Talk about itArjun and Meera made up. What helps you make up with a friend?

Section 2

Brave, Kind Prahlada

6.3

The Boy Who Stayed Brave

Main ideaPrahlada stayed brave and kind, even when it was hard.

Prahlada was a prince. He loved Vishnu with all his heart. Many Hindus honor Vishnu as God.

His father was a proud king. He wanted everyone to honor only him. Prahlada said no, kindly. He kept loving Vishnu. He stayed brave.

From the source
Prahlada Stays True
Prahlada's father tries many times to make him stop honoring Vishnu. Each time Prahlada stays calm and true, and each time he is kept safe.
Retold from the Bhagavata Purana, Book 7, chapters 5 to 8 (paraphrased)

How does Prahlada act when his father is angry?

The Bonfire

Main ideaMany people light a bonfire the night before Holi to remember Prahlada.

In a well-known Holi story, Prahlada is kept safe from a great fire. His cruel aunt Holika is not.

So the night before Holi, many people light a big bonfire. They remember that Prahlada was kept safe. They say good wins over bad.

is a greeting. You press your hands together. You bow your head a little. Many Hindus greet people this way.

Main ideaAhimsa means not hurting any living thing.

is an old word. It means not hurting. Not hurting people. Not hurting animals. Not hurting any living thing.

Many Hindus, Jains and Buddhists teach ahimsa. Some families eat no meat because of it. Mahatma Gandhi made ahimsa famous.

The Mahabharata calls not hurting others the highest duty.
