🤝 The whole book in one idea

Two groups of kids are sure they are nothing alike. One boy crosses the line — first by talking with a Soc girl, then by living through the worst week of his life — and learns that everybody is an outsider to somebody, and that the way out is to really see people.

See the person, not the label.

⚠️ Mix-ups to dodge

  • Darry is NOT Dally. Darry is the big brother. Dally is the tough friend from New York.
  • Johnny — not Ponyboy — kills Bob, and he does it to save Ponyboy.
  • Bob has the rings and dies at the park. Randy is his friend who quits fighting.
  • Cherry is not Ponyboy’s girlfriend. She is Bob’s girlfriend who becomes Ponyboy’s friend.
  • The greasers WIN the rumble — and it changes nothing.
  • Soda is the brother; Steve is Soda’s best friend; Two-Bit is the joker.
  • The gold poem is by Robert Frost. Ponyboy recites it; Johnny explains it in his letter.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A character’s action — “what trait does this show?” Name the trait AND point to the action.
  • “How does ___ change?” — give a START moment and an END moment, with chapter numbers.
  • A short passage — “whose point of view?” It is always Ponyboy’s: look for “I.”
  • A symbol — “what does it stand for?” Sunsets = what is shared. Gold = young and good. Hair = belonging.
  • “What is a theme of the novel?” — answer with a full sentence, not one word.

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Name the three Curtis brothers and say one thing about each?
  • Tell a greaser from a Soc — and name one thing both sides share?
  • Put nine events of the story in order?
  • Explain how Ponyboy AND Johnny change, with a start moment and an end moment for each?
  • Say what “stay gold” means in your own words?
  • Write a claim, evidence with a chapter number, and reasoning?
