One parent, or two

One parent. The offspring are copies — same everything.

One parent and identical offspring is asexual reproduction. Two parents and a new mix every time is sexual reproduction. That mix is the whole point: variety is what survives a change.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Offspring are basically copies of their parents.

Only in asexual reproduction. With two parents, every offspring gets a different mix — which is why siblings with the same parents are not the same person.

Two parents, new mix, every single time.

⚠️ People often think…

Pollination and fertilization are the same event.

Pollination is the delivery — pollen arrives. Fertilization is when the two cells actually join. Delivery is not the handshake, and plenty of pollen never gets there.

Delivered is not the same as joined.

⚠️ People often think…

A peacock's tail is just decoration.

That tail is heavy and easy for a predator to spot. It exists because it wins mates, and winning mates is how the trait gets passed on at all.

Costly features are paid for by mating.

⚠️ People often think…

How big something grows is decided entirely by its genes.

Genes set the range; food, water, light and temperature decide where inside that range it lands. Two seeds from the same packet grow to different heights.

Genes set the ceiling. Conditions set the height.

Helpful Hints

👯 The big split: asexual vs. sexual

Asexual: ONE parent → identical copies. Fast, no partner needed. Sexual: TWO parents → offspring get a MIX of traits. Slower, but variety helps survival when conditions change.

One parent = copies. Two parents = combos.

🌸 Flower parts in one line

Stamen = male part, makes pollen. Pistil = female part, holds eggs. Pollination moves pollen to the pistil; fertilization is sperm meeting egg — that’s when a seed begins.

StaMEN = male. Pollination is the delivery; fertilization is the handshake.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • Sexual reproduction does NOT make identical offspring — each gets a different mix.
  • Pollination ≠ fertilization. Delivery isn’t the handshake.
  • Fancy features aren’t decoration — a peacock’s tail exists because it wins mates.
  • Growth isn’t only genes — environment (food, water, light, temperature) changes how big organisms grow.

🐣 Behaviors that boost reproduction

  • Courtship displays: dances, songs, bright colors — advertising for a mate.
  • Nest building and territory defense: a safe place raises survival.
  • Parental care: feeding and guarding young — fewer babies, better odds each.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • MS-LS1-4 wants an ARGUMENT: claim + evidence that a structure or behavior INCREASES reproduction odds.
  • MS-LS1-5 gives you DATA (plant height vs. light) — read the pattern, then say “environment influenced growth.”
  • Watch for “genetic or environmental?” — genes set the potential, environment decides how much happens.

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Give two differences between asexual and sexual reproduction?
  • Name one advantage of EACH type?
  • Define pollination vs. fertilization?
  • Give two animal behaviors that raise reproduction success?
