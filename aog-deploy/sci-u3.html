Living Things and Where They Live

Life Science

A bean sprouts in a cup. A bee lands on a purple flower. A rabbit freezes in tall grass. What do these living things need? Where do they live? In this unit, you will find out.

You will learn to tell living from nonliving. You will see what plants and animals need. You will meet parents and their young. You will visit ponds, forests, prairies and deserts. You will see how living things change the land, and how people can care for it.

The big question

What do living things need, and how does the place they live give it to them?

Week 8

Green pods hang down. Inside are new bean seeds.

What Plants and Animals Need

Living Things
Big questionWhat do plants and animals need to stay alive, and how do they change the places they live?
The story

The Bean in the Cup

A tiny seed sat in a cup of dirt on the windowsill. What would it do?

Mia put a bean seed in a paper cup. She added dirt. She poured in a little water. She set the cup on the sunny windowsill. Then she waited. Day 1, nothing. Day 2, nothing. The dirt just sat there.

On Day 4, Mia saw a crack in the dirt. A pale loop was pushing up. It was the bean! It bent like a tiny green neck. By Day 6, two leaves opened. They turned toward the window. They were reaching for the light.

Mia's friend Leo did a test. He put his bean cup in a dark closet. He gave it water too. His bean came up. But it was thin and yellow. It leaned and flopped. It looked sick. Then Leo moved it to the window. In three days, it turned green.

Mia forgot to water her bean over the weekend. On Monday, the leaves drooped. She gave it water. By lunch, the leaves stood up again. The bean was alive. It needed things, just like Mia did. It needed water. It needed light. And it needed air.

Talk about itLeo's bean turned yellow in the closet. What did it need? How do you know?
Section 1

Alive or Not?

5.1

Living and Nonliving

Main ideaLiving things grow, need food and water, and make more of their kind.

Look around your room. A cat is . A rock is . How can you tell? Living things grow. They need water. They need air. They make new living things like themselves.

A rock does not grow. It does not eat or drink. It never has baby rocks. A toy car moves, but only when you push it. It does not need food. So it is nonliving.

A tree does not walk. But it is living. It grows taller each year. It drinks water with its roots. It makes seeds. Seeds grow into new trees.

Think it through
A Robot Dog and a Real Dog

A robot dog walks. It barks. It wags its tail. Then its battery runs out. It stops. A real dog eats. It drinks. It grows from a puppy. One day it can have puppies of its own.

Which dog is living? What clues tell you?

Words to know
living
alive; it grows, needs water and air, and makes more of its kind
nonliving
not alive; it does not grow, eat or make babies
Check yourself

1. Which one is living?

Why: A tree grows, drinks water with its roots, and makes seeds.

2. Why is a rock nonliving?

Why: Living things grow and need food and water. A rock does neither.

3. A toy car moves. Is it living?

Why: Moving is not enough. A toy car does not grow, eat or make new toy cars.
5.2

Signs of Life

Main ideaYou can find clues that show something is alive.

Scientists look for clues. Does it grow? Does it need water? Does it need air? Does it make babies or seeds? If the answer is yes, it is living.

Some clues are tricky. A river moves. A fire grows bigger. But a river does not eat. A fire does not have baby fires that grow up. They are nonliving.

Mold on old bread is living. It grows. It spreads. It takes water from the bread. Even tiny things can be alive.

Think it through
The Puddle and the Plant

After rain, a puddle sits next to a small plant. The next day, the puddle is smaller. The plant is a little taller. A week later, the puddle is gone. The plant has a new leaf.

Which one is living, the puddle or the plant? What clues tell you?

Words to know
clue
something you notice that helps you find an answer
Check yourself

1. Which clue shows that something is living?

Why: Growing and needing water are signs of life. Wet things and moving things can be nonliving.

2. A fire grows bigger. Is fire living?

Why: Fire gets bigger, but it does not eat, drink or make baby fires. It is nonliving.

3. Mold on bread is...

Why: Mold grows, spreads and takes water. Those are signs of life, even in tiny things.

What Living Things Need

5.3

Plants Need Water, Light and Air

Main ideaA plant needs water, light and air to grow.

A plant cannot walk to a store. It makes its own food. It uses . It uses air. It uses water. Take one away, and the plant gets sick.

drink water from the dirt. Leaves catch light. Leaves also take in air. That is why leaves are flat and wide. They spread out to catch the sun.

Remember Leo’s bean in the closet? It had water and air. It had no light. It turned yellow and weak. Light was the missing thing.

Think it through
Two Cups on a Shelf

Ana has two bean plants. Both sit on the windowsill. She waters Cup A every day. She never waters Cup B. After one week, Cup A is tall and green. Cup B is brown and dry.

What did Cup B need? How did Ana's test show it?

Words to know
sunlight
light from the sun; plants use it to make their food
roots

Check yourself

1. What do roots do?

Why: Roots are under the ground. They take in water for the plant.

2. A plant in a dark closet gets water. What will happen?

Why: Plants need light to make food. Without light, Leo's bean turned yellow and floppy.

3. Why are leaves flat and wide?

Why: Flat, wide leaves spread out. That lets them catch lots of sunlight and air.
5.4

Animals Need Food, Water, Air and Shelter

Main ideaAnimals need food, water, air and a safe place to live.

A plant makes its own food. An animal cannot. A rabbit must find food. It eats clover. A robin pulls up worms. A fox hunts mice.

Animals need water too. A deer drinks from a creek. Animals need air. A fish takes air from the water with its . A whale swims up to breathe.

Animals also need . Shelter is a safe place. A bird builds a nest. A rabbit digs a hole. Shelter keeps animals warm and dry. It hides them from danger.

Think it through
Setting Up a Hamster Cage

You get a pet hamster. You hang a water bottle in the cage. You fill a dish with food. You add a little plastic house. The cage has holes in the top, so air gets in.

Name the four things the hamster needs. Which thing in the cage gives each one?

Words to know
shelter
a safe place to rest and stay warm and dry
gills
parts of a fish that take air out of the water
Check yourself

1. What is shelter?

Why: A nest or a hole is shelter. It keeps an animal safe, warm and dry.

2. How is an animal different from a plant?

Why: Plants make their own food. Animals must eat plants or other animals.

3. A fish gets air from...

Why: Gills pull air out of the water. That is how a fish breathes.
5.5

Who Eats What

Main ideaSome animals eat plants. Some animals eat other animals. Some eat both.

All animals need food. Food gives them to move and grow. But animals eat different things. A cow eats grass. A hawk eats mice. A raccoon eats berries and frogs.

A rabbit eats plants. A fox eats the rabbit. The fox got its energy from the rabbit. The rabbit got it from plants. The plants got it from the sun. This is a .

Look at teeth for clues. Flat teeth grind plants. Sharp, pointy teeth tear meat. Your teeth have both kinds. People can eat plants and meat.

Think it through
A Meal on the Prairie

A grasshopper chews a blade of grass. A frog snaps up the grasshopper. A snake swallows the frog. A hawk swoops down and grabs the snake.

Where did the hawk's energy come from at the very start of the chain?

Words to know
energy
what living things use to move, grow and stay warm; it comes from food
food chain
a line that shows who eats what
Check yourself

1. A cow has flat teeth. What does it eat?

Why: Flat teeth grind plants. Cows chew grass all day.

2. Where does a fox get its energy?

Why: A fox cannot make food from sunlight. It gets energy by eating rabbits and mice.

3. Which one is a food chain?

Why: A food chain shows who eats what. The rabbit eats grass, and the fox eats the rabbit.
Section 3

Changing the Land

5.6

Plants and Animals Change Places

Main ideaLiving things change the places where they live.

Have you seen a bumpy sidewalk? Tree roots did that. Roots grow thick and strong. They push the concrete up. Slowly, the sidewalk cracks.

A squirrel buries in the fall. It forgets some of them. In spring, a tiny oak pops up. The squirrel planted a tree without knowing it.

A beaver cuts down small trees with its teeth. It builds a across a stream. Water backs up. A pond forms. Frogs and ducks move in. One animal changed the whole place.

Think it through
The Bumpy Sidewalk

A big tree grows next to a sidewalk. Each year the trunk gets wider. Its roots grow long under the ground. The sidewalk slabs tilt up. Now there is a bump you could trip on.

What pushed the sidewalk up? What might happen in ten more years?

Words to know
acorn
the seed of an oak tree
dam
a wall across a stream that holds back water
Check yourself

1. What cracks a sidewalk next to a tree?

Why: Roots grow thick and strong. They push the concrete up until it cracks.

2. A squirrel forgets a buried acorn. What may happen?

Why: An acorn is a seed. Left in the dirt, it can sprout into an oak.

3. What does a beaver dam make?

Why: The dam holds back the stream. Water backs up and makes a pond.
5.7

People Change the Land Too

Main ideaPeople change the land, and people can take care of it.

People change the land a lot. We build roads. We build houses. We cut trees. We plant corn and beans in big fields. The land under Chicago was once and marsh. Now it is a big city.

Changes can hurt living things. When a field becomes a parking lot, mice and birds lose their homes. Trash in a creek can hurt fish.

People can help too. We can plant trees. We can pick up trash. We can leave a corner of the yard wild for bees. In Illinois, people plant new prairies. Butterflies come back.

Think it through
Two Schoolyards

Schoolyard A is all blacktop. Schoolyard B has a garden, a few trees and a patch of tall grass. On a spring day, you count the birds and bees at each one.

Which schoolyard has more living things? Why do you think so?

Words to know
prairie
land covered with tall grass and wildflowers, with few trees
Check yourself

1. What was the land under Chicago long ago?

Why: Before the city, the land was tall grass and wet marsh. People changed it.

2. A field becomes a parking lot. What happens to the mice?

Why: The field was their shelter and food. Blacktop takes that away.

3. Which is a way to take care of the land?

Why: Trees and clean creeks give living things food, water and shelter.

Living Things
Big questionHow do living things fit the places where they live?
The story

Who Lives on the Prairie?

Grass taller than you. Buzzing, hopping, singing. Who is out here?

In June, Ms. Ortiz took her class to a prairie near Chicago. The grass was tall. Some of it was taller than the kids. Purple and yellow flowers poked up. The wind made the grass wave like water.

Jamal stood very still. A grasshopper jumped past his shoe. A bee landed on a purple flower. It walked around. Then it flew to another flower. Yellow dust stuck to its legs.

Then they heard a song. A small bird sat on a tall stem. It sang and sang. Ms. Ortiz said it was a meadowlark. Its nest was hidden down in the grass. Baby birds were waiting for food.

Near the path, a rabbit froze. Two smaller rabbits froze too. They looked just like their mother. Only smaller. Ms. Ortiz said, "This is their home. It gives them everything they need." The class walked on, quietly.

Talk about itThe bee, the meadowlark and the rabbits all live in the prairie. What does the prairie give each one?
Section 1

Parents and Young

6.1

Young Look Like Their Parents

Main ideaYoung plants and animals look like their parents, but not exactly.

A kitten looks like a cat. It has fur, four legs and whiskers. But it is smaller. Its fur may be a different color. It is like its , but not the same.

A baby rabbit looks like its mother. A puppy looks like a dog. A baby oak tree has oak leaves. things grow up to look more like their parents.

Some young look very different at first. A tadpole has a tail and no legs. Then it grows legs. Its tail shrinks. It becomes a frog, like its parents.

Think it through
A Litter of Puppies

A brown dog has five puppies. Three are brown. One is black. One is white with brown spots. All five have floppy ears and a long tail, just like their mother.

How are the puppies like their mother? How are they different?

Words to know
parent
a mother or a father
young
a baby animal or a new, small plant
Check yourself

1. A kitten is like its mother. But it may have...

Why: Young look like their parents, but not exactly. Color and size can be different.

2. What grows from an acorn?

Why: An acorn is an oak seed. The young tree grows oak leaves like its parent.

3. A tadpole grows up to be...

Why: A tadpole grows legs and loses its tail. It becomes a frog like its parents.
6.2

Parents Take Care of Young

Main ideaMany animal parents feed, warm and protect their young.

Baby birds cannot fly. They cannot find food. They open their beaks wide and chirp. The parents fly back and forth all day. They bring worms and bugs.

A mother cat keeps her kittens warm. She licks them clean. A mother deer hides her fawn in tall grass. She comes back to feed it milk. She works to it.

Not all animals do this. A sea turtle lays her eggs on a beach and leaves. The babies alone. They crawl to the sea by themselves.

Words to know
protect

hatch

Check yourself

1. Why do baby birds chirp with open beaks?

Why: Baby birds cannot find food. Chirping tells the parents to bring worms.

2. A mother deer hides her fawn in tall grass. This keeps the fawn...

Why: Hiding protects the fawn. The mother comes back to feed it.

3. Which animal parent leaves its eggs?

Why: A sea turtle lays eggs on the beach and goes back to the sea. The babies hatch alone.

Body Parts That Help

6.3

Beaks and Claws

Main ideaAnimals have body parts that help them get food and stay safe.

Look at a bird’s . A duck’s beak is wide and flat. It scoops food from water. A hawk’s beak is sharp and hooked. It tears meat. A finch’s beak is short and thick. It cracks seeds.

help too. A cat’s claws grip and climb. A squirrel’s claws hold onto bark. An eagle’s claws grab fish. A mole’s wide claws dig through dirt.

Each body part fits a job. You can guess what an animal eats by looking at its beak or claws. Scientists do this all the time.

Words to know

Check yourself

1. A hawk's beak is sharp and hooked. What does it eat?

Why: A hooked beak tears meat. Hawks eat mice and other small animals.

2. What do a mole's wide claws do?

Why: Moles live under the ground. Wide claws work like little shovels.

3. A bird has a short, thick beak. What does it likely eat?

Why: A short, thick beak cracks hard seeds, like a finch's beak.
6.4

Thorns, Fur and Shells

Main ideaPlants and animals have parts that protect them.

A rose has . A deer wants to eat the leaves. Ouch! The thorns poke its nose. The deer walks away. Thorns protect the plant.

A rabbit has thick fur. Fur keeps it warm in an Illinois winter. A turtle has a hard shell. It pulls its head inside. A fox cannot bite through.

A skunk has a stinky spray. A porcupine has sharp quills. A cactus has spines and a thick skin that holds water. Each part helps the living thing .

A Turtle by the Pond

A raccoon finds a turtle by the pond. The turtle pulls its head and legs into its shell. The raccoon pokes and paws at it. The shell stays shut. The raccoon gives up and leaves.

What did the shell do for the turtle? Name another animal with a part that protects it.

Words to know
thorn

survive
to stay alive
Check yourself

1. Why does a rose have thorns?

Why: Thorns poke any animal that tries to eat the leaves. The plant stays safe.

2. What does thick fur do for a rabbit?

Why: Fur holds in heat. It helps the rabbit survive cold winters.

3. A cactus has a thick skin. It helps the cactus...

Why: The desert is dry. Thick skin keeps the cactus from losing its water.
Section 3

Homes for Living Things

6.5

Pond and Forest

Main ideaDifferent kinds of living things live in different places.

A is the place where a living thing lives. It gives food, water, air and shelter. A pond is a habitat. A forest is a habitat.

In a pond, frogs sit on lily pads. Ducks dip for plants. Dragonflies zip over the water. Fish hide in the weeds. Cattails grow at the edge.

In a forest, tall trees make shade. Owls sleep in holes in the trunks. Deer eat leaves. Squirrels leap from branch to branch. Mushrooms grow on old logs.

Think it through
The Wrong Home

Imagine a duck in a forest with no water. Imagine an owl in a pond with no trees. Now switch them. Put the duck on the pond and the owl in the forest.

Why does each animal fit its own habitat? What does each one need?

Words to know
habitat
the place where a plant or animal lives and gets what it needs
Check yourself

1. What is a habitat?

Why: A habitat gives a living thing its food, water, air and shelter.

2. Which animal fits a pond habitat?

Why: Frogs need water. A pond gives them water, bugs to eat and lily pads to sit on.

3. Why do owls live in forests?

Why: An owl needs shelter. A hole in a tree trunk is a safe place to rest.
6.6

Prairie and Desert

Main ideaA prairie and a desert are very different habitats with their own living things.

Illinois is called the Prairie State. A is a sea of tall grass and flowers. Few trees grow there. Grasshoppers, meadowlarks and rabbits live on the prairie.

Prairie grass has very deep roots. Some roots go down deeper than a grown-up is tall. Deep roots find water. They help the grass live through dry summers and cold winters.

A is dry. Little rain falls. Days are hot. A cactus stores water in its stem. A lizard hides under a rock in the heat. A kangaroo rat gets water from the seeds it eats.

Words to know
prairie
land of tall grass and wildflowers, with few trees
desert

Check yourself

1. Why does prairie grass have deep roots?

Why: Deep roots reach water far below. That helps the grass live through dry summers.

2. A desert is...

Why: Deserts get little rain. Plants and animals there must save water.

3. How does a cactus live in a dry desert?

Why: A cactus keeps water inside its thick stem for the long dry times.
6.7

Pollen and Traveling Seeds

Main ideaAnimals help plants make seeds and carry seeds to new places.

Remember the bee on the purple flower? Yellow dust stuck to its legs. That dust is . The bee carried it to the next flower. Now that flower can make seeds.

Bees, butterflies and hummingbirds do this. They are . The flower gives them sweet nectar. They give the flower a ride for its pollen. Both win.

Seeds travel too. A dandelion seed floats on the wind. A burr sticks to a dog’s fur. A bird eats a berry and drops the seed far away. New plants grow in new places.

Think it through
Socks After a Hike

You walk through tall grass in the fall. Back home, your socks are covered in little sticky seeds. You pull them off and drop them in the yard.

How did the seeds travel? What might grow in your yard next spring?

Words to know
pollen
yellow dust from a flower that helps it make seeds
pollinator
an animal that carries pollen from flower to flower
Check yourself

1. What is pollen?

Why: Pollen is the yellow dust on a flower. When it moves to another flower, seeds can form.

2. What does a flower give a bee?

Why: The bee drinks nectar. In return, it carries the flower's pollen.

3. How does a dandelion seed travel?

Why: Dandelion seeds have fluffy tops. The wind carries them to new places.
