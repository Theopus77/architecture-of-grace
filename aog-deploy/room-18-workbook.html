Unit 1 · Knowing & Accepting Yourself

The Identity Web. Emotions Are Messengers. Mistake vs. Flaw. Inner Critic vs. Inner Coach. Guilt vs. Shame. This unit practiced building an honest map of who you are — and learning that you are more than your worst moment.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 The Identity Web has five branches. Which one is NOT a branch?
B. The five branches are Things I Love · Good At · Matter to Me · People Don’t Know · Still Figuring Out. There is no “bad at” branch — the web is a map of who you are, not a list of what you can’t do.
2 “Emotions are ______.”
A. Every emotion is a messenger carrying information. Fear says something feels unsafe. Sadness says something I care about was lost. When you feel an emotion, ask: “What is this feeling trying to tell me?”
3 On the Emotions Are Messengers chart, ANGER means —
C. Anger = a boundary was crossed. Sadness = something I care about was lost. Embarrassment = I care about belonging. Loneliness = I need connection. Emotions are not problems. They are data.
4 “I got the answer wrong” is a ______. “I am stupid” is a ______.
A. A mistake is something you DID. A flaw is something you ARE. The Inner Critic’s trick is turning mistakes into flaw statements. Catching that trick is the whole lesson.
5 The Inner Coach asks three questions. Which one is on the list?
D. WHAT happened? WHAT can I learn? WHAT is one next step? The Coach is not softer than the Critic — it is honest AND kind. It points toward repair and growth, not hiding.
6 Guilt says ______. Shame says ______.
B. Guilt is about what you DID and points toward repair: fix it, do better, try again. Shame is about who you ARE and points toward hiding. When the Critic speaks, ask: “Is this attacking my behavior or my identity?”
7 Kenji told a secret. That night he thinks: “I don’t deserve to have friends.” Which column is that in?
A. “I told a secret and I need to make it right” is guilt — useful, and it points toward repair. “I don’t deserve friends” is shame — it points toward hiding. Kenji can keep the guilt and put the shame down.
8 “I am more than my ______.”
C. One bad moment feels louder than all the others. The longer story includes everything else — the times you tried, the times you came back, the people you helped. Tariq is not “the kid who falls.”

Unit 2 · Self-Compassion & Self-Forgiveness

The Heavy Backpack and the four myths. The Three-Part Accountability Map. The Friend Test. The Stone Practice. The support web. This unit practiced owning a mistake without punishing yourself for it — and putting the weight down.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 In the Heavy Backpack lesson, forgiveness is —
D. Myth 4, busted: you do not have to feel ready before you start. Forgiveness is a decision, not a feeling. That is why you can begin even when it still hurts.
2 Forgiveness is “not for the person who hurt us. For ______.”
B. Forgiveness is releasing resentment or self-blame — not to excuse behavior, but to free YOURSELF from carrying the weight of it. Each book comes out of the backpack. The load gets lighter.
3 “Accountability is guilt in action. Self-punishment is ______ in action.”
A. Accountability = own it + understand the impact + repair = DONE. Self-punishment = using the mistake as evidence + replaying it = STUCK. Self-punishment keeps you stuck, so the repair never gets done.
4 The Three-Part Accountability Map asks: What exactly happened? What was the impact? And —
D. Step 1: what EXACTLY happened — honest, not dramatized. Step 2: the actual IMPACT. Step 3: what REPAIR is possible. When the map is complete, the self-punishment can stop. Isabella can stop apologizing for the gnome.
5 The Friend Test: “Would you say that to your best friend?” Most people discover —
C. Zara would tell a friend, “You are so brave for trying.” She would never say it to herself. Self-compassion has three parts: self-kindness, common humanity (everyone struggles), and mindfulness (notice it without dramatizing or hiding).
6 Which one is NOT self-compassion?
A. Liam’s mom said, “You’re amazing no matter what,” and he felt worse. Empty cheerleading is not compassion. Self-compassion is honest AND kind — it names what happened and still speaks with care.
7 The Stone Practice teaches that letting go is —
B. Some things you put down dozens of times before they stay down. Connor’s stone keeps coming back — that is not failure. That is what the practice looks like. You put it down again.
8 When self-forgiveness feels impossible, asking for help is —
D. Some things are too big to carry alone. The one sentence to start: “I have been really hard on myself about something and I cannot seem to let it go. Can I talk to you?” Asking for support is the bravest thing in this curriculum.

Unit 3 · Empathy & Forgiving Others

Your hurt is real. Intent vs. impact. Forgiveness vs. trust — two separate decisions. The Forgiveness Path. The Three-Panel Story. Say It Straight. I Choose. This unit practiced dealing with hurt from other people without letting it change who you are.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 Before Unit 3 says anything about forgiving, the first lesson says —
B. Priya’s mom said, “Just ignore it.” What Priya needed first was for the hurt to be real. That is the whole first lesson — nothing more, not yet.
2 Intent is ______. Impact is ______.
A. If someone bumps into you by accident, their intent was not to hurt you — but you still fell. The hurt is real even when it wasn’t meant. Understanding why someone hurt you is an explanation, never an excuse.
3 Forgiveness is ______. Trust is ______.
A. Two separate decisions. Forgiveness is internal and can happen alone, in your own heart. Trust is relational and must be rebuilt through demonstrated change. You can forgive someone and still choose not to be close to them.
4 “Forgiveness is never required when ______.”
A. The safety statement comes first: your safety is more important than any lesson. If someone is still hurting you, the priority is safety and adult support — not forgiveness.
5 Leila says: “I can try to forgive Jada. But I’m not going to tell her secrets anymore.” Leila is —
D. Trust is never obligatory — and never owed. Leila can put down the resentment and still keep herself protected. The boundary statement: “I am choosing not to carry resentment. I am also not in a place to be close to this person right now, and that is okay.”
6 On the Forgiveness Path, moving backward —
B. Mia’s anger came back three months later, as real as day one. The path is a squiggle, not a straight line: Still hurting · Beginning to understand · Some days are better · Choosing to put it down today · The hurt is getting smaller · I am okay. You put it down again.
7 In the Three-Panel Story, Panel 1 is what happened. Panel 2 is the story I told myself. Panel 3 is —
A. You did not choose what happened to you. You get to choose what it means. Panel 1 stays true and stays private. Panel 3 is you being the author of your own story.
8 Which “I Choose” statement is valid?
C. Jasmine wrote: “I choose not to forgive yet. Because I am still hurting. And that is where I am on the path.” One of the most honest answers in the room. The only requirement is that the choice is yours and you know you are making it.

Unit 4 · Grace & Generosity

Grace vs. doormat. Mercy. The limit formula. The Pause Practice. The grace gap and the five-day micro-action. Difficult vs. unsafe. This unit practiced choosing generosity on purpose — from strength, never from fear.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 Grace is —
D. A choice, not a requirement. A strength, not a weakness. Mateo had done nothing to earn a seat — and on day twenty-two Ryn moved her tray anyway. That is grace.
2 Grace vs. doormat. A doormat —
B. A person extending grace has full choice and chooses generosity on purpose. If someone keeps hurting you and you keep doing nothing, that is not grace — that is a boundary problem. Devon playing worse on purpose is doormat, not grace.
3 Mercy is —
A. Mercy requires power — only a strong person can choose not to use their strength. Imani covered for Zach and said, “Just don’t leave me with it again.” Mercy, with a limit attached.
4 The most important words in the limit formula are —
D. When ___, I feel ___ because ___. Going forward, I WILL ___. The limit is what YOU will do — not what you demand from the other person. It works whether or not they agree, change, or even hear it.
5 A limit from strength passes three tests. Which is one of them?
C. Test 1: self-respect or retaliation? Test 2: does it focus on what I will do? Test 3: can I hold it even if they don’t change? A limit from fear collapses when the other person pushes back. A limit from strength doesn’t need them to.
6 The Pause Practice: NOTICE the assumption, PAUSE, ASK what else could explain it — and then —
A. Charitable interpretation is not naive — it is a deliberate choice when there are several possible explanations. Imani’s first thought was “She’s mad at me.” Then she remembered: Maya had a math test that morning.
7 A grace gap is —
B. Be specific: the new student who sits alone, the kid who never gets called on, the lunch monitor whose name nobody knows. Then: “Every day for five days, I will ___.” Small, sustainable, specific.
8 Grace applies to a DIFFICULT person. For an UNSAFE person, what applies instead?
D. Difficult = hard to be around, sometimes unkind, can be handled with care. Unsafe = repeatedly causes harm. With difficult people: limit your exposure, not your kindness. With unsafe people: adults and limits — never the doormat.

Year-End · Integration

Regulate. Reflect. Repair — in that order. The four-tool card. The four pillars — IDENTITY · SELF-COMPASSION · FORGIVENESS · GRACE — and the community charter: Room 18 built this. The Critic does not get the last word.

Part A · Choose the answer

1 The Regulation & Repair rule, in order:
B. You cannot do hard emotional work — repair, accountability, forgiveness — from outside your Window of Tolerance. An apology delivered while flooded sounds defensive. Regulate first. Then do the work. Every time.
2 The four tools on your year-end card: a regulation strategy, your recovery arc, a repair step, and —
A. My Toolkit (WS-R1). My Recovery Map (WS-R2). My Repair Plan (WS-R3). My Limit Statements (WS-R4). Four tools, in your own handwriting — the framework you carry out the door.
3 The Repair Protocol has five steps. The one most often skipped is —
C. Repair does not automatically mean the friendship goes back to how it was. Re-entry is not assumed — it is agreed. Jordan apologized; Sam is still guarded; that Step 5 conversation has not happened yet.
4 The four pillars on the Room 18 charter are —
A. One chart, four pillars, and under each one the class wrote what it learned in its own words. “The Inner Critic is loud, not true.” “Shame is always lying.” “Safe always comes first.” Room 18 built this.
5 The last line of the year: “You are the ______ of your own grace.”
D. The work continues. You know how now. The room we built does not get torn down — it gets added onto.
