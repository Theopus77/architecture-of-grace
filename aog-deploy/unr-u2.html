Big Choices Go Wrong

Genesis 6–11

This unit tells two old stories from Genesis. First comes Noah and his big boat. Then comes a tall tower in a city called Babel. In both stories, people make big choices. Some choices go wrong. Some go right.

You will read the stories from Genesis itself. You will meet an old book called 1 Enoch. You will meet a Jewish writer from long ago named Josephus. And you will hear how Bible scholars, like Michael Heiser, read these stories.

By the end, you will be able to retell both stories in order. You will know words like ark, covenant and nation. And you will think about taking turns, listening and being kind.

The big question

When big choices go wrong in Genesis, what does the story say God does next?

The story

A Rainbow After the Storm

The storm is over. A rainbow shines. Grandma has a story to tell.

Rain taps on the window. Thunder rumbles far away. Then the storm stops. Maya and her little brother Jon run to the window. The sun peeks out. A rainbow stretches across the sky. Red, orange, yellow, green, blue and purple.

Grandma comes to look too. She smiles. "That reminds me of an old story," she says. "It is in the Bible, in a book called Genesis. It is the story of Noah." Maya and Jon sit on the rug. They love Grandma's stories.

Grandma tells how the world got full of unkindness. She tells how Noah listened to God. She tells about a big boat full of animals. Jon giggles at the animal part. "Two by two!" he says.

"And at the end," Grandma says, "the story says God put a rainbow in the sky. It was a sign of a promise." Maya looks back at the window. The rainbow is fading now. But she will remember the story.

Trouble on the Earth

3.1

Some Heavenly Ones Chose Wrong

Main ideaGenesis 6 says some heavenly ones chose wrong and came down to earth.

The Bible’s first book is . Chapter 6 tells a short, puzzling story. It says the came down to earth. They chose wives from the people there.

Long ago, a Jewish writer named Josephus read this story. He said the sons of God were angels. The old book 1 Enoch says they were heavenly ones too. Some Bible scholars today, like Michael Heiser, think so as well.

Other readers see it another way. Some think the sons of God were people, not heavenly ones. Readers still talk about this story today.

The story

The Block Tower

Three friends want to build the tallest tower ever. First they have to find a way to share.

It is block time in Room 12. Sofia, Ben and Amir want to build a tower. A tall one! They want it to touch the ceiling. They pull out every block in the bin.

But they cannot agree. Sofia wants only red blocks. Ben wants to build fast. Amir is still learning English. He tries to say an idea, but the others do not understand. Everyone talks at once. The tower wobbles. Then it falls down.

Their teacher, Ms. Lee, sits down with them. "What if you take turns?" she asks. "And what if you listen?" Amir shows them with his hands. He wants a wide bottom, so the tower will stand.

They try again. Each child adds one block. Then the next child adds one. Amir teaches them a word from his home language. The new tower is not the tallest. But it stands, and they built it together.
