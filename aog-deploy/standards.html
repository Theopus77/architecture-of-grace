Architecture of Grace · For review committees

Standards & Alignment

Every lesson in the K–12 curriculum, with the CASEL competency it carries and the Illinois SEL benchmark it is written against — lesson by lesson, and with what is not yet mapped stated in the same table.

Who this is for. Curriculum directors, review committees, and whoever has to put a standards column in an adoption rubric.

On this page in Spanish. The framing, the headings and every explanation switch with the language button. The table cells do not. Lesson titles are the titles as printed in the books, and the Illinois benchmark text is the wording the Illinois State Board of Education publishes — in English. An unofficial translation of a state standard would misrepresent it, and a lesson title that appears in no book would send a teacher looking for something that does not exist. Both stay in the original on purpose.
Looking for the academic courses? Science, Mathematics, English Language Arts, Social Studies, U.S. History, Economics, Spanish and Family & Consumer Sciences are mapped unit by unit to NGSS, the Illinois Learning Standards, ACTFL, CEE and the National FCS Standards on the Standards Crosswalk.

What this page is

A lesson-level crosswalk covering 140 lessons across five grade bands. It is assembled from the curriculum itself — the alignment block printed inside each lesson, and the K–12 crosswalk generated from the five curriculum volumes. Nothing on this page was written for the page.

136 of 140 lessons carry an Illinois SEL benchmark, drawing on 75 distinct benchmarks across all three Illinois goals. 119 of 140 name a primary CASEL competency. 67 are additionally mapped to the companion novel — the chapter the lesson sits against, and the anchor chart built from it.

Nine framework-level crosswalk documents — Illinois SEL, CASEL, Danielson, MTSS, restorative practices, IDEA — are listed further down and open without a form.

What this page is not

This is an alignment document, not an efficacy claim. It shows which standard each lesson is written against. It does not show that the lesson produces an outcome, and no efficacy study of this curriculum has been conducted — see Evidence & Roadmap, which says so at more length and commits to not claiming otherwise.

Alignment here means the benchmark was selected by the author while writing the lesson and printed with it. It has not been reviewed by ISBE, by CASEL, or by any third party, and neither organization has endorsed this curriculum.

What is not mapped

  • MTSS tiers are not mapped at the lesson level. There is a framework-level MTSS crosswalk above, but the table on this page carries no per-lesson tier. The counselor flag is a safety requirement attached to specific lessons; it is not a tier-of-support designation, and reading it as one would misstate what it means.
  • Danielson is not mapped at the lesson level either — it maps to teaching practice rather than to student standards, so the framework-level document above is the right grain for it.
  • 21 of 140 lessons do not name a CASEL competency in their source lesson, almost all of them in the 11–12 band. The alignment is not absent from the teaching; it is absent from the printed block, and the block is what this page reads.
  • 4 of 140 lessons print no Illinois benchmark.
  • The adult edition is out of scope. Illinois SEL Standards and CASEL are K–12 frameworks; Book 6 is mapped to its own 12-session arc instead.
  • No Spanish edition of this page yet. The lessons are bilingual; this crosswalk is not.
