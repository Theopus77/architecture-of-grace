Functions and Slope

A function is a rule with one output for every input. A linear function is a starting amount plus a steady rate — b and m — and it can be told four ways: in words, in an equation, in a table and on a graph. Find the start, find the per, and say what they mean. b is where you start. m is how fast you go. y = mx + b. Every table, graph, story and equation in this unit is asking for those two numbers and what they mean.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

A repeated number anywhere in the table breaks the function.

Repeated OUTPUTS are fine — lots of inputs can give the same answer. What breaks a function is one INPUT with two different outputs, because then the rule cannot tell you what to do.

Check the x column for repeats, not the y.

⚠️ People often think…

Slope is run over rise.

Rise over run — the y's go on top. Flip it by accident and you get the reciprocal, which usually still looks like a reasonable answer, which is why this one is so expensive.

Rise first, because you climb before you walk away.

⚠️ People often think…

In a table, the slope is just how much y jumps.

Only if x jumps by 1. Check the x steps first: if x goes up by 2 and y goes up by 6, the slope is 3, not 6.

Divide the y jump by the x jump. Every time.

⚠️ People often think…

A vertical line has a slope of zero.

It is the other way around. A HORIZONTAL line has slope 0 — it rises nothing. A VERTICAL line is undefined, because the run is zero and you cannot divide by zero.

Flat: zero. Straight up: undefined.

A gym charges $20 to join, then $5 a month. Write the rule.

  2. That is b, the starting amount.
  3. The $5 repeats every month — that is m.
  4. y = 5x + 20. The word “per” always rides with x.

🪜 From a rule to a line — the ladder

Function (one output per input) → show it four ways (words, equation, table, graph) → linear means a constant change → slope m = rise ÷ run = change in y ÷ change in x → y-intercept b = y when x is 0 → y = mx + b → compare two functions by their m and their b.

🤝 The whole topic in one idea

A linear function is a starting amount plus a steady rate. b is where you start; m is how fast you go. Every table, graph, equation and story in this unit is asking you to find those two numbers and say what they mean.

⚠️ Traps the test loves

  • A repeated OUTPUT is fine. A repeated INPUT with different outputs breaks a function.
  • Slope is rise over run — y’s on top. Upside down gives the reciprocal.
  • In a table, check the x steps. If x jumps by 2, divide the y jump by 2.
  • Swapping m and b: the number with “per” goes with x.
  • Horizontal line: slope 0. Vertical line: undefined. Not the other way around.
  • A distance-time graph is not a picture of a hill.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A table, a mapping or a graph — “is this a function?”
  • Two points — “what is the slope?” (subtract in the same order).
  • A story — “write the equation” and “what does the slope mean here?”
  • Two functions in two different forms — “which has the greater rate of change?”
  • Several equations or tables — “which is linear?”
  • A graph with no numbers — “describe what is happening.”

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Decide whether a table or graph shows a function, and say why?
  • Find slope from two points, from a table and from a graph?
  • Find the y-intercept, even when x = 0 is not in the table?
  • Write y = mx + b from a story and say what m and b mean, with units?
  • Compare two functions given in different forms?
  • Tell linear from nonlinear in a table and in an equation?
