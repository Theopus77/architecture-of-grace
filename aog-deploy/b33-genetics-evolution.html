Variation first, selection second

A population, already varied. Mutation and shuffling made these differences before anything happened to them.

Variation comes first, by mutation and shuffling. Selection comes second. Need never enters it — the environment cannot invent a trait, it can only decide which of the traits already present get passed on. And individuals do not evolve; populations do.

What people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Organisms develop traits because they need them.

Need cannot reach into a genome. The trait was already there in somebody, by chance, before the environment ever asked for it. The environment then sorted — it did not order.

Chance supplies. The environment sorts.

⚠️ People often think…

Two Bb parents having four brown-eyed children breaks the Punnett square.

A Punnett square gives PROBABILITY per child, not a quota per family. Three in four brown each time means four in a row is one chance in about three — unremarkable. Each conception is its own independent roll.

Probability per child, not a quota.

⚠️ People often think…

A dominant allele is the common one in a population.

Dominant describes how it behaves when paired, not how often it shows up. Polydactyly is dominant and rare; having the recessive form of most traits is the majority. Frequency and dominance are unrelated.

Dominant is behavior, not frequency.

⚠️ People often think…

Selection removes a harmful recessive allele from a population quickly.

Selection acts on the PHENOTYPE, and a heterozygous carrier shows nothing. The allele rides along invisibly for generations — which is why rare recessive conditions never fully disappear.

Carriers hide the allele from selection.

Antibiotics stop working on a bacterial population. What happened?

  1. Before the drug: a few bacteria already carried a resistance mutation, by chance.
  2. The drug arrives and kills the ones without it.
  3. The survivors reproduce, and they all carry it.
  4. The bacteria did not learn anything. The population’s make-up changed. ✓

Helpful Hints

🪜 Genetics to evolution — the ladder

DNA is a code of paired bases; a gene is one recipe → transcription copies it to mRNA in the nucleus; translation reads codons at a ribosome and builds a protein → a mutation changes the code, and that is where new variation comes from → each gene comes in alleles; you carry two; a Punnett square gives the odds for a child → meiosis halves and shuffles, so offspring vary → natural selection keeps the heritable variants that fit the environment, and allele frequencies shift → fossils, homologous structures and DNA say it has been happening all along.

Code, copy, read, vary, shuffle, select, prove.

🤝 The whole idea

Variation comes first, by mutation and shuffling. The environment never invents a trait — it only decides which of the traits already present get passed on.

Variation first. Selection second. Need never.

⚠️ Tricky ones

  • Organisms do NOT change because they need to. The trait was already there; the environment sorted.
  • A Punnett square gives PROBABILITY. Four brown-eyed kids from two Bb parents does not break it.
  • Selection acts on PHENOTYPE. A recessive allele can hide for generations in heterozygous carriers.
  • Dominant does not mean common. A dominant allele can be rare; a recessive one can be the majority.
  • Transcription is DNA → RNA (nucleus). Translation is RNA → protein (ribosome). Replication is DNA → DNA.
  • Individuals don’t evolve. POPULATIONS do — the allele frequencies change across generations.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • A Punnett square with one box blank — “fill it and give the phenotype ratio.”
  • A DNA strand — “write the complementary strand” or “the mRNA.”
  • Beak-depth data before and after a drought — “explain what happened, using the term natural selection.”
  • An allele-frequency line rising over decades — “what does the rise show?”
  • “Give two lines of evidence for common ancestry.”
  • “Why can two brown-eyed parents have a blue-eyed child?” (both Bb).
