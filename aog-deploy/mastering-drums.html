How to use this course

There are 12 short lessons. Do them in order, with the Drum Bench open next to this page.

Every lesson works the same way:

  1. Read the short part at the top. It says what the button does.
  2. Try it: do the numbered steps, one at a time, on the real machine.
  3. Listen for: what you should hear if it worked.
  4. Check yourself: tick the boxes. If you can't tick one, do the steps again before moving on.

Before you start

  • Use headphones or real speakers. Laptop speakers can't play a kick drum, so you'll miss half of what the lessons ask you to hear.
  • Press SAVE once (top row of buttons). It downloads a backup of what's on the machine now, because some lessons clear the grid.

Words you'll see

WordWhat it means
PadOne of the 8 big buttons with drum names on them (Kick, Snare…). Tap it to hear that sound.
GridThe rows of little boxes at the bottom. Click boxes to build a beat.
StepOne box across a row. There are 16 in a row.
BeatA count of 1, 2, 3 or 4. Each beat is 4 steps.

WordWhat it means

Lesson 1 — Find your way around

The machine looks busy, but it's just 9 areas stacked from top to bottom. Learn where they are first; the later lessons explain each one.

#WhereWhat's there
1Top leftThe green screen. It shows the tempo, the bank you're on, and messages.
2Top row of buttonsRUN (start/stop), REC, METRO, SWING, TAP, CLEAR GRID, SAVE, TO THE DECKS, OPEN
3Second rowSELECT A B C D (the 4 banks), then MIX TUNE DECAY TRIM (what the sliders do), then SAMPLE, REV, ERASE, MUTE, SOLO
4Third rowSEG 1–8 (your 8 patterns), LEN, TC, COPY, PASTE, and the TRACK buttons
5Middle8 sliders, one over each pad
6Under the sliders8 pads: Kick, Snare, Hat C, Hat O, Clap, Tom, Rim, Bell
7Under the padsA dark screen strip (it shows a sound's shape when you trim it)
8NextOUTPUT EQ (tone knobs for the whole machine)
9BottomThe grid: one row for each pad, 16 boxes across

Under the machine you'll also find the TEMPO slider, the grit button and the STARTER PATTERNS.

One thing to know now: each grid row belongs to the pad in the same position. Row 1 (KICK) plays pad 1, row 2 (SNARE) plays pad 2, and so on down to row 8 (BELL) and pad 8.

Try it:

  1. Tap each pad once, left to right. Say its name as you hear it.
  2. Tap Hat O (pad 4). While it's still ringing, tap Hat C (pad 3).

Listen for: in step 2, the open hat stops instantly when you hit the closed hat. On a real drum kit, one foot can't hold the hi-hat open and closed at once, so the machine cuts it off the same way.

Check yourself:

  • I can point to RUN, the SELECT row, SAMPLE, the sliders, the pads and the grid.
  • I can name all 8 pads.
  • I heard Hat C cut off Hat O.

Lesson 2 — Your first beat

You build a beat by clicking boxes on the grid. Tapping pads only lets you hear the sounds; it doesn't write anything down.

Each click moves a box to its next colour:

ClickColourWhat it does
1stAmberPlays at normal volume
2ndBright amberPlays louder (an accent)
3rdGreenPlays softer (a ghost note)
4thDarkOff again

Try it:

  1. Press CLEAR GRID (top row). The grid goes empty.
  2. Press RUN. A light starts walking across the grid, left to right, over and over.
  3. On the KICK row, click boxes 1, 5, 9 and 13 once each. (The small numbers 1, 2, 3, 4 above the grid sit over those boxes.)
  4. On the SNARE row, click boxes 5 and 13 once each.
  1. On the HAT C row, click every other box: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15.
  2. Click the SNARE box at 13 once more. It turns bright amber.
  3. Click the HAT C box at 15 twice more. It turns green.
  4. Press RUN again to stop.

Listen for: "boom, tss, CRACK, tss" repeating. The last snare hits harder than the first one, and the last hat is quieter than the rest.

Check yourself:

  • My beat plays kick, snare and hats.
  • I can hear one loud snare (bright amber) and one quiet hat (green).
  • I know that clicking a green box once more turns it off.

Lesson 3 — Speed and counting

This lesson covers four buttons that deal with time: TEMPO, TAP, METRO and LEN.

Counting the grid. Say this out loud while a beat plays, one word per box: "1 e & a, 2 e & a,

3 e & a, 4 e & a". The numbers land on boxes 1, 5, 9 and 13.

ButtonWhereWhat it does
TEMPOThe slider under the machineSpeed, from 40 to 240 beats per minute. Hip-hop is usually 84–96; house music is about 124.
TAPTop rowTap it in time with any song and the tempo jumps to match (tap at least twice).
METROTop rowTurns on a click on every beat. Use it whenever you play along live.
LENThird rowMakes the pattern shorter. Each press: 16 → 12 → 8 → 6 → 4 boxes. Unused boxes go dim.

Try it:

  1. Under the machine, press the starter pattern Four on the floor. Press RUN.
  2. Drag TEMPO slowly down to 70, then up to 128. Same beat, different mood.
  3. Play any song on your phone. Tap TAP along with its kick drum 4 times. Look at the tempo on the green screen.
  1. Press METRO. You hear a click on every beat. Say "1 e & a…" along with it.
  2. Press LEN until the screen says LEN 12. Listen: the beat now loops in 3 instead of 4. Keep pressing LEN until it says LEN 16 again.

Check yourself:

  • I can count "1 e & a" along with the grid light.
  • I can set the tempo with the slider and with TAP.
  • I put LEN back to 16.

Lesson 4 — Swing: make it groove

SWING (top row) makes a stiff beat bounce. It pushes the "&" and "a" of every beat a little later, the way a real drummer would. Each press moves to the next setting, and the button shows the number.

SWINGSounds
50%Straight, like a robot
54%Barely loose
58%Relaxed; a lot of hip-hop sits here
63%Bouncy, head-nodding
67%Full shuffle
71%Heavy and lazy

After 71% it goes back to 50%. Swing only changes how the beat sounds; the boxes on the grid don't move.

Try it:

  1. Press the starter pattern Backbeat (it has a hat on every box). Press RUN.
  2. Press SWING once. Listen for 2 loops. Press it again. Keep going until you get back to
  • 50%.
  1. Pick the one you like best (most people like 58% or 63%). Leave it there.

Listen for: the hats going from even "ticka-ticka" to a bouncy "tick-a-tick-a".

TC (third row, short for "time correct") only matters when you play pads live in Lesson 6, so leave it alone for now.

Check yourself:

  • I can hear the difference between 50% and 63%.
  • I know which swing I like best.

Lesson 5 — The sliders: volume, pitch and length

There's one slider above each pad. The four buttons MIX TUNE DECAY TRIM (second row) decide what all 8 sliders do. The lit button is the current job. The number under each slider shows its setting.

PressThe sliders now changeStarts at
MIXHow loud each drum is (0–100)84
TUNEHow high or low each drum is (−7 to +7)0
DECAYHow long each drum rings (20–100)85
TRIMWhere each sound starts (you'll use this for samples in Lesson 7)0%

Try it (one job at a time):

  1. Press the starter pattern Backbeat. Press RUN.
  2. Volume: press MIX. Pull the Hat C slider (3rd one) down to about 55. The kick and snare now stand out more.
  3. Pitch: press TUNE. Pull the Kick slider (1st one) down to −4. The kick gets deeper.
  4. Length: press DECAY. Pull the Hat C slider down to about 35. The hats get short and tight.
  5. Press MIX again when you're done, so the sliders go back to volume.

Good starting settings: kick and snare loud (85–95), hats quieter (50–65), kick tuned down 3 or 4, hats short (30–45).

These settings stay with each pad even when you switch banks.

Check yourself:

  • I can make one drum quieter with MIX.
  • I can make the kick deeper with TUNE.
  • I can make the hats shorter with DECAY.
  • I pressed MIX again at the end.

Lesson 6 — Play the pads live

Instead of clicking boxes, you can tap the pads in time and the machine writes the boxes for you. This is called recording.

TC decides how neat your taps are made. Press it (third row) to change it:

TCWhat happens to your taps
1/8Snapped hard onto the nearest safe spot. Easiest; start here.
1/16Snapped to the nearest box. The normal setting.
1/32Only lightly tidied.
OFFKept exactly where you tapped.

Try it: record hi-hats

  1. Press the starter pattern Four on the floor.
  2. Click off every lit box in the HAT C row so only kick and snare are left.
  3. Press TC until it says TC 1/8.
  4. Press METRO (you'll hear a click) and press RUN.
  5. Press REC. The green screen now shows REC.
  6. Tap the Hat C pad along with the click for two loops. Watch boxes light up in the HAT C row as you tap.
  7. Press REC again to stop recording. The beat keeps playing with your hats in it.

Tip: record one drum at a time. Kick first, then snare, then hats.

Fixing and hiding drums

ButtonHow to use it
ERASEWhile the beat plays, press ERASE, then tap a pad just as the hit you don't want goes past. It disappears. Press ERASE again to finish.
MUTEPress MUTE, then tap a pad to silence it (tap again to bring it back). Press MUTE again to go back to normal.
SOLOPress SOLO, then tap a pad to hear only that pad. Press SOLO again to go back to normal.

Check yourself:

  • I recorded hats by tapping the pad with REC on.
  • I removed one hit with ERASE.
  • I muted the kick, then brought it back.

Lesson 7 — Load and use your own sounds (samples)

A sample is any short sound you load in yourself: a clap, a knock, your voice, a pen click. This lesson walks through it in five parts: pick a bank, get a sound file, load it, trim it, and put it in a beat.

Part A — Pick a bank

The SELECT A B C D buttons (second row) switch between 4 sets of 8 pads.

BankWhat's on its pads
AThe built-in drum kit
BThe same kit, tuned differently
CEmpty. For your own sounds.
DEmpty. For your own sounds.

Put your samples in C or D so the built-in kit in A stays safe.

Part B — Get a sound file onto the computer

The machine loads sound files from the computer. It can't use a microphone directly. The easiest way:

  1. On your iPhone, open Voice Memos.
  2. Record one short sound, such as a hand clap, a knock on a desk, or a "tss" with your mouth. Start the sound right away and stop recording right after it.
  3. Tap the recording, tap Share, then AirDrop it to your Mac. It lands in your Downloads folder.
  4. Record 3 or 4 different sounds the same way.

MP3, M4A (what Voice Memos makes) and WAV files all work. Keep each sound under 2.5

seconds; anything longer gets cut off at 2.5.

Part C — Load a sound onto a pad

  1. Press C in the SELECT row. The green screen says BANK C IS EMPTY, and the pads say EMPTY.
  2. Tap pad 1. Because it's empty, a file picker opens.
  3. Go to Downloads and choose one of your sounds.
  4. The green screen shows END and the length, for example END 0.42s. The pad now shows the first letters of the file's name.
  5. Tap pad 1. You hear your sound.
  6. Do the same for pads 2, 3 and 4 with your other sounds.

To replace a sound on a pad that already has one: tap the pad (it plays, and that selects it), then press SAMPLE in the second row and choose the new file.

Part D — Trim it so it hits right away

Most recordings have a little silence at the start. That makes the sound late. Trimming cuts it off.

  1. Press TRIM (second row).
  2. Tap pad 1.
  3. Look at the dark screen strip under the pads. It now shows the shape of your sound, with two marks: S (start) and E (end).
  4. Drag S to the right until it sits just before the tall part of the shape. When you let go, the sound plays so you can check it.
  1. If the sound has a long tail, drag E to the left to cut it shorter.
  2. Press MIX when you're done, so the sliders go back to volume.

Listen for: the sound starts the instant you tap the pad, with no delay.

Part E — Put your sound in a beat

The key idea: each grid row plays the pad in the same position, whatever bank is lit. The row names stay the same (KICK, SNARE, HAT C…) even when your own sound is on that pad.

Your sound is onClick boxes on this row
Pad 1KICK
Pad 2SNARE
Pad 3HAT C
Pad 4HAT O
Pad 5CLAP
Pad 6TOM
Pad 7RIM
Pad 8BELL
  1. Make sure C is lit.
  2. Press RUN.
  3. Click boxes on the KICK row (pad 1) and the SNARE row (pad 2) to build a beat from your own sounds.
  4. Now press A. The same beat plays with the built-in drums. Press C again to hear yours.

What the green screen can tell you

Screen saysMeaning
END 0.42sLoaded. The sound is 0.42 seconds long.
Screen saysMeaning
MEM FULLOut of room. All your sounds together can't be more than 10 seconds. Trim them shorter or use fewer.
NO SAMPLEThat file couldn't be read. Try an MP3, M4A or WAV.
7.6s (bottom line)Seconds of room left, out of 10.

Check yourself:

  • I recorded a sound on my phone and got it into Downloads.
  • I loaded it onto pad 1 of bank C and heard it.
  • I trimmed the silence off the start.
  • I programmed a beat with my own sounds, then switched to A and back to C.

Lesson 8 — The gritty sound (made simple)

This lesson is about two things that change the sound of your drums, not the beat. Take them one at a time.

Part A — The grit switch

Under the three sliders (TEMPO, OUTPUT FILTER, ROOM VOLUME) there's one round button. It says one of two things:

When it saysYou hear
12-bit · drop-sampleRough and gritty, like the old machine. This is the normal setting.
smoothed (modern)Clean and soft, like a modern app.

The grit is easiest to hear on a drum that has been tuned down.

Try it:

  1. Press Heartbeat under STARTER PATTERNS. It's only a kick drum. Press RUN.
  2. Press TUNE in the SELECT row. The sliders now change pitch.
  1. Pull the first slider (over the Kick pad) all the way down. The number under it says −7. The kick is now deep and low.
  2. Press the round button so it says smoothed (modern). Listen for four beats.
  3. Press it again so it says 12-bit · drop-sample. Listen for four beats.

Listen for: the 12-bit kick sounds dirtier and crunchier; the smoothed one sounds cleaner. That crunch is the SP-1200 sound. Leave the button on 12-bit from now on.

Part B — Pads 1 and 2 are brighter

Look at the pads. Pads 1 and 2 say RAW. Pads 3 to 8 don't.

  • A sound on pad 1 or 2 keeps all its sparkle (the bright, crisp top).
  • A sound on pads 3 to 8 gets its top rounded off, so it sounds warmer and darker.

This only matters for your own sounds (the ones you load in Lesson 7). The built-in drums already sound the way they're meant to.

Rule to remember: put the sound you want to be bright and crisp on pad 1 or 2.

Try it:

  1. Drag the OUTPUT FILTER slider (under the machine) all the way to the right. This stops the whole mix from being darkened, so you can hear the difference.
  2. Press C in the SELECT row.
  3. Tap pad 1. If a file picker opens, choose one of your own short sounds (a clap or a shaker works best). If it plays a sound instead, press SAMPLE (in the SELECT row) and choose the file.
  4. Tap pad 5. Load the same file again, the same way.
  5. Tap pad 1, then pad 5, then pad 1, then pad 5.

Listen for: pad 1 sounds brighter and crisper; pad 5 sounds a little duller and warmer. Same sound, different pad.

Check yourself

  • I can find the grit button and say which setting is the old-machine sound.
  • I heard the kick get crunchier on 12-bit when it was tuned down.
  • I heard pad 1 sound brighter than pad 5 with the same file.

If you can't hear a difference, use headphones and turn ROOM VOLUME up. Laptop speakers hide both effects.

Why it works (optional): the old machine stored sound roughly ("12-bit") and changed pitch by skipping ("drop-sample"), which adds grit. Its outputs 1 and 2 skipped a tone- rounding chip that outputs 3 to 8 went through. Producers loved both, so the Drum Bench copies them.

Lesson 9 — Make the whole beat sound good

These controls change the sound of everything the machine plays at once. Always set each drum's volume first (MIX, Lesson 5), then use these.

Part A — Brighter or darker: OUTPUT FILTER

The OUTPUT FILTER slider is under the machine, next to TEMPO. It starts fairly dark (about 4,200).

  • Drag it right = brighter, crisper, more modern.
  • Drag it left = darker and more muffled, like an old tape.

Try it: press Backbeat and RUN. Drag OUTPUT FILTER all the way left, then all the way right. Stop where you like it (most people like it about three-quarters of the way right).

Part B — The tone knobs: OUTPUT EQ

The OUTPUT EQ box is under the pads. Each slider makes one part of the sound louder (right) or quieter (left). Zero means no change.

SliderChanges
LOWThe deep boom of the kick
LOW-MIDMuddiness. Its SWEEP slider picks exactly where.
HIGH-MIDThe crack of the snare. Its SWEEP slider picks exactly where.
HIGHThe sizzle of the hats
HP FILTERCuts the very lowest rumble. Helps on phone speakers.
FLAT (button)Puts every slider back to zero

Try it: three simple fixes

  1. Less muddy: LOW-MID to −4, its SWEEP to 300.
  2. Snare stands out more: HIGH-MID to +3, its SWEEP to 2500.
  3. Better on a phone: HP FILTER to 80.

Then press FLAT to hear the difference (write your numbers down first, then set them back). Yours should sound clearer, not just louder.

Rule: small moves. Anything past 8 on a slider is usually too much; press FLAT and start again.

Part C — ROOM VOLUME

This is just how loud it is in the room. It doesn't change the beat itself.

Check yourself:

  • I set OUTPUT FILTER where I like it.
  • I made the beat less muddy with LOW-MID.
  • I used FLAT to compare before and after.

Lesson 10 — Turn beats into a song

A song is several beats (called segments) played one after another (called a track). The machine holds 8 segments.

Part A — Make more than one beat

The SEG 1–8 buttons (third row) switch between your 8 beats. Each one has its own grid, speed and swing. The lit number is the one you're working on.

The fast way to make a new beat that fits: copy your main beat, then change a little.

  1. Press SEG 1. Make your main beat (or press a starter pattern).
  2. Press COPY. The screen says SEG 01 COPIED.
  3. Press SEG 2, then PASTE. Segment 2 is now the same beat.
  4. Change segment 2 a little: add an open hat (HAT O row) on box 15 and a green ghost snare or two.
  5. Press SEG 3, then PASTE again. Press LEN until it says LEN 8, and add snare hits on the last few boxes. That's a short drum fill.
  6. Press SEG 4. Make a quiet version: kick and hats only.

Part B — Put them in order

Button (third row)What it does
+ SEGAdds the segment you're on to the end of the song
CLREmpties the song list and starts over
PLAY TRACKPlays the song list in order (press RUN too)
  1. Press CLR.
  2. Press SEG 1, then + SEG three times. Press SEG 3, then + SEG once.
  3. Press SEG 1, + SEG twice. SEG 2, + SEG once. SEG 3, + SEG once.
  4. Press SEG 4, + SEG four times.
  5. Press RUN, then PLAY TRACK.

The bottom line of the green screen shows your song, for example TRK 2/12 111311234444: it's playing part 2 of 12, and those digits are the segment numbers in order.

Check yourself:

  • I made 4 segments using COPY and PASTE.
  • I put them in order with + SEG.
  • My song plays through with PLAY TRACK, and the fill leads back into the main beat.

Lesson 11 — Save your work and send it to the turntables

The browser keeps your last session by itself, but only on this computer. To keep your work safe, or move it to another computer, use SAVE.

Button (top row)What it does
SAVEDownloads a file called drum-bench.json into Downloads. The screen says FILE SAVED.
OPENLoads a drum-bench.json file back in. The screen says OPENED.
TO THE DECKSRecords about 20 seconds of your beat and sends it to the Turntables page.

The saved file keeps everything: all 8 segments, your song order, the slider settings, the EQ, and your own samples.

Try it: save and reopen

  1. Press SAVE.
  2. In Downloads, rename the file to something you'll recognise, like my-first-song.json.
  3. Press CLEAR GRID (your beat disappears).
  4. Press OPEN and choose my-first-song.json. Everything comes back.

Try it: send a beat to the turntables

  1. If you want the whole song, turn on PLAY TRACK. If you want just one beat, leave it off.
  2. Press TO THE DECKS. The screen says BOUNCING, then SENT.
  3. Open the Turntables page. Near the bottom, a box says From the drum bench. Press
  • Onto deck A.

If any pad is on SOLO, only that pad is sent. That's handy for sending just the hats.

Check yourself:

  • I saved, cleared, and opened my work again.
  • I sent a beat to the Turntables and loaded it onto a deck.

Practice plan

Twenty minutes a day for four weeks.

WeekEach day
1One lesson a day: Lessons 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.
2Lessons 6 and 7 (take two days on Lesson 7). Then build one beat from Lesson 12.
3Lessons 8, 9, 10. Then one beat from Lesson 12.
4Lesson 11. Then make one short song a day, each with a few of your own sounds.

You've mastered it when you can:

  • Build any beat from Lesson 12 without looking
  • Hear the difference between swing 50% and 63%
  • Use loud and soft boxes on purpose
  • Record a part live with REC
  • Load your own sound, trim it, and use it in a beat
  • Make a 4-part song and play it with PLAY TRACK
  • Save it, open it again, and send it to the turntables
