Everyday Food and Sometimes Food

No food is bad. Some foods just come around less often.

A plate, and it has parts. About half of it is fruit and vegetables. A quarter is grains — bread, rice, tortillas, pasta. A quarter is protein — beans, eggs, meat, fish, nuts. Milk or something like it sits beside it.

Everyday food and sometimes food. That is the whole idea, and it is about HOW OFTEN a food turns up, not about whether it is good or bad. Everyday foods are the ones your body would like most days — fruit, vegetables, grains, beans and eggs and meat, milk or what your family drinks instead, and water. Sometimes foods turn up at a birthday, on a Friday, at a grandmother's house. They are still food. They are still allowed. Nobody in this room is going to tell you that a cake is bad, because a cake is not bad — it is just not Tuesday.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Some foods are good foods and some foods are bad foods.

Food does not have a character. A carrot is not being good and a biscuit is not being naughty — they are both just food, and they do different things. What actually changes is how often each one shows up. Fruit and vegetables and grains and beans can turn up every single day and your body is glad of it. Cake turns up at a birthday. Both of those are true at the same time, and neither of them makes you a good or a bad person. Calling a food bad usually just makes people feel bad about eating, and feeling bad about eating has never helped anybody eat better.

Everyday and sometimes. Not good and bad.

⚠️ People often think…

You should always finish everything on your plate.

Your stomach knows how much you need. Your plate does not — somebody else filled it, and they were guessing. Full is also a feeling that arrives a little late, which is why you can feel fine and then suddenly feel too full a few minutes later. If you practice eating past full because the plate still has food on it, you get better at ignoring the one signal that was actually telling you the truth. Take a smaller amount and go back for more if you are still hungry. And if you hate wasting food — good, that is worth caring about — the answer is a smaller serving, not a fuller stomach.

Your stomach knows before your plate does. Take less, go back.

Build a plate

1 Watch one

Make a lunch out of rice, black beans, a tortilla, an orange and some spinach.

  1. Start with the half. Fruit and vegetables: the orange and the spinach. That is half the plate.
  2. Now a quarter of grains. Rice and tortilla are both grains, so pick one — say the tortilla.
  3. Now a quarter of protein. Black beans. Beans are protein even though they grow like a vegetable.
  4. Count the colors: orange, dark green, black, and the pale tortilla. Four.
  5. Beans and spinach in a tortilla, with an orange. ✓ Half, a quarter, a quarter — and water to drink.

A plate is about half fruit and vegetables, a quarter grains and a quarter protein, with milk or something like it beside it → more colors means more jobs done → water is the everyday drink → hunger and fullness are signals, and full arrives a little late → and food is everyday or sometimes, which is about how often it turns up and not about good and bad.

Half, a quarter, a quarter. Everyday and sometimes, not good and bad.

🔑 The one idea

Almost every lesson a child gets about food sorts it into good and bad, and then the child has to live inside that sorting at every birthday party for the rest of their life. This room does not do that. Everyday and sometimes says the true thing — some foods belong most days and some turn up less often — without attaching a verdict to the person eating them. You still learn the plate, the colors and the water. You just do not learn to feel bad.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • No food is a bad food. Everyday and sometimes describe HOW OFTEN a food turns up, and nothing else.
  • You do not have to finish your plate. Your stomach knows how much you need; the plate was filled by somebody guessing.
  • Beans are counted as PROTEIN, not as a vegetable, even though they grow in the ground.
  • Full arrives a little late, which is why eating slowly helps and has nothing to do with manners.
  • A drink does not fill you up the way food does, so the sugar in a sugary drink slips past unnoticed.
  • Not liking a new food today does not mean not liking it. Most people need several tries.
  • An allergy is not fussiness and not a choice. Never share food with somebody who has one without asking an adult.
