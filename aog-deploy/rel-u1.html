Families and Their Special Days

Holidays and Places

Families keep many special days. Some light candles. Some share a big meal. Some keep no religious days at all. Every family's days matter to them.

In this unit, you will learn about special days like Christmas, Hanukkah, Eid, Diwali, Lunar New Year and Vaisakhi. You will visit places where people gather, like a church, a synagogue, a mosque, a temple and a gurdwara.

You will learn fair words, like "some families" and "Muslims celebrate." You will learn to ask a friend kind questions and to be a good guest.

The big question

How do families keep special days and places, and how can we learn about them kindly?

Special Days at Home

Holidays
Big questionWhy do families keep special days, and how can we ask a friend about theirs?
The story

A Light for Every Month

Mia's class makes a big calendar and finds something surprising.

Mia's class makes a big calendar. It hangs on the wall. Each child adds a special day from home. They draw it in the right month.

Sam draws candles in December. His family keeps Hanukkah. Priya draws small clay lamps in the fall. Her family keeps Diwali. Omar draws a moon for Eid.

Mia draws a tree with lights for Christmas. Leo draws red lanterns for Lunar New Year. Jas draws a parade in April for Vaisakhi. Ben says his family has no holiday. They have a big pancake day on his birthday.

The class steps back. There are lights in many months! Their teacher smiles. "Every family has its own days," she says. "Each one matters to that family."

Talk about itWhat is one day that is special in your home? What do you do that day?
Section 1

Many Families, Many Days

1.1

Days That Matter

Main ideaFamilies keep different special days, and each day matters to them.

A holiday is a special day. Families do special things on it. They eat, sing and spend time together.

Different families keep different days. Some days come from a religion. A religion is what a group of people believe and do together.

Some families keep no religious days. They may have other special days, like birthdays. Every family’s days are real to them.

Think it through
A Picture in Words

Look at the big calendar on the wall. There are candles in December. There is a moon in spring. There is a lantern in winter. Each picture is a day that one family keeps.

Pick one picture. What might that family do on their day?

Words to know
holiday
a special day that a family or group keeps each year
religion
what a group of people believe and do together, like prayer and holidays
Check yourself

1. What is a holiday?

Why: A holiday is a special day kept each year.

2. Do all families keep the same special days?

Why: Different families keep different days.

3. Ben's family keeps no religious holiday. What is true?

Why: Some families keep no religious days. Their days are real to them.
1.2

Saying "Some Families"

Main ideaWe say "some families" or "Muslims celebrate" to speak fairly about each day.

Words can be kind and true. We say "some families keep Diwali." We do not say "everyone keeps Diwali."

We name who keeps a day. "Muslims celebrate Eid." "Christians celebrate Christmas." "Jewish families light Hanukkah candles." This tells who, and it is fair to all.

Think it through
Two Ways to Say It

Kai says, "Everybody has a Christmas tree." Nia says, "Many Christian families put up a Christmas tree."

Which way fits more families? Why?

Words to know
celebrate
to do happy, special things to mark a day
Check yourself

1. Which words are fair to all families?

Why: "Some families" is true. Not every family keeps the same day.

2. Who celebrates Eid?

Why: Muslims celebrate Eid.

3. What does celebrate mean?

Why: Celebrate means to mark a day with happy, special things.
Section 2

Lights in the Dark

1.3

Christmas and Hanukkah

Main ideaChristians keep Christmas and Jewish families keep Hanukkah, often in December.

Christmas is on December 25 for many Christians. Christians believe Jesus was born on this day long ago. Many families give gifts and sing. Some put up a tree with lights.

Hanukkah is a Jewish holiday. It lasts eight nights. Families light a menorah. They add one more candle each night. Many eat potato pancakes called latkes.

From the source
A Line Read at Christmas
For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord.
The Bible, Luke 2:11, King James Version (1611)

Christians read these words at Christmas. Whose birth do they tell about?

Words to know
menorah
a candle holder; at Hanukkah it holds candles for eight nights, plus one helper candle
Christmas
the day many Christians remember the birth of Jesus
Check yourself

1. How many nights does Hanukkah last?

Why: Hanukkah lasts eight nights.

2. Which holiday do many Christians keep on December 25?

Why: Many Christians keep Christmas on December 25.

3. What do Jewish families light at Hanukkah?

Why: At Hanukkah, Jewish families light a menorah.
1.4

Diwali, Festival of Lights

Main ideaMany Hindus, Sikhs and Jains keep Diwali with small lamps and bright colors.

Diwali is a festival of lights. It comes in the fall. Many Hindus keep it. Many Sikhs and Jains mark it too.

Families light small clay lamps called diyas. They make bright patterns on the floor. They share sweets. Many Hindus say the lights show good winning over bad.

Think it through
Lamps by the Door

Look at the front step. A row of tiny clay lamps glows. Each has a small flame. On the floor is a flower pattern in pink, yellow and blue powder.

Which holiday is this? What clues tell you?

Words to know
Diwali
a fall festival of lights kept by many Hindus, Sikhs and Jains
diya
a small clay lamp lit at Diwali
Check yourself

1. What is a diya?

Why: A diya is a small clay lamp lit at Diwali.

2. When does Diwali usually come?

Why: Diwali comes in the fall.

3. Who keeps Diwali?

Why: Many Hindus keep Diwali. Many Sikhs and Jains mark it too.
Section 3

New Moons and New Years

1.5

Eid al-Fitr

Main ideaMuslims celebrate Eid al-Fitr at the end of Ramadan, a month of fasting.

Ramadan is a special month for Muslims. Many grown-ups fast. They do not eat or drink from dawn to sunset. Each evening, families share a meal.

When Ramadan ends, Muslims celebrate Eid al-Fitr. Families pray together. They wear new or nice clothes. They visit, share food and give to people in need.

From the source
The Month of Ramadan
The Qur'an says that fasting is asked of believers in the month of Ramadan. It says God wants ease for people, not hardship.
The Qur'an, Surah 2 (Al-Baqarah), verses 183-185, retold in plain words (paraphrased)

What does this passage say Muslims do in Ramadan?

Words to know
Ramadan
a month when many Muslims fast from dawn to sunset
fast
to not eat or drink for a time
Check yourself

1. What do Muslims celebrate when Ramadan ends?

Why: Eid al-Fitr comes at the end of Ramadan.

2. What does fast mean?

Why: To fast means to not eat or drink for a time.

3. In Ramadan, when do many Muslims share a meal?

Why: Many Muslims fast until sunset, then share a meal.
1.6

Lunar New Year and Vaisakhi

Main ideaMany families mark a new year in winter or spring, like Lunar New Year and Vaisakhi.

Lunar New Year starts with a new moon in winter. Many Chinese, Korean and Vietnamese families keep it. They clean the house. They eat a big meal. Children may get red envelopes with money.

Vaisakhi comes in April. It is a special day for Sikhs. Sikhs visit the gurdwara, their place of worship. Many join a parade with music and prayer songs.

Read the numbers
Special Days on Our Calendar

Common calendar facts; some dates move each year

Which day comes first in the year? Which comes last?

Words to know
Lunar New Year
a new year that starts with a new moon in winter
Vaisakhi
a spring day in April that is special for Sikhs
Check yourself

1. What might a child get at Lunar New Year?

Why: Many children get red envelopes at Lunar New Year.

2. Vaisakhi is a special day for which group?

Why: Vaisakhi is special for Sikhs.

3. Lunar New Year starts with what?

Why: Lunar New Year starts with a new moon.
Section 4

Asking a Friend Kindly

1.7

Good Questions

Main ideaWe can ask a friend kindly about their special day and listen well.

Your friend’s day may be new to you. You can ask about it! Ask kind questions. "What do you do on your special day?"

Listen to the answer. Do not say a day is weird. Say "thank you for telling me." Your friend may not keep any holiday. That is okay too.

Think it through
At the Lunch Table

Omar says, "Tomorrow is Eid!" Lily does not know Eid. She could say, "That's weird." Or she could say, "Cool! What do you do on Eid?"

What should Lily say? Why is that kind?

Words to know
respect
to treat people and what matters to them with care
Check yourself

1. Which question is kind?

Why: A kind question asks and listens.

2. What does respect mean?

Why: Respect means treating people with care.

3. Your friend keeps no holiday. What do you do?

Why: Some families keep no holiday. That is okay.
1.8

Being Together

Main ideaMost special days share lights, food, songs and being together.

The days are different. But many share things. Many have lights. Many have good food. Many have songs or prayers.

Most of all, many special days bring people together. Families, friends and neighbors meet. They share and help each other.

Think it through
Same and Different

Hanukkah has candles. Diwali has clay lamps. Christmas has lights on a tree. Eid has a big family meal. Lunar New Year has a big family meal too.

What is the same about these days? What is different?

Words to know
tradition
something a family or group does the same way year after year
Check yourself

1. What do Hanukkah and Diwali both have?

Why: Both use lights: candles and clay lamps.

2. What is a tradition?

Why: A tradition is done the same way year after year.

3. Eid and Lunar New Year both have what?

Why: Both have a big family meal.

Places Where People Gather

Sacred Places
Big questionWhat do people do in places of worship, and how can we be a good guest?
The story

Five Doors on One Street

Theo walks down one Chicago street and sees five special doors.

Theo walks with his grandpa down a long Chicago street. "Look," says Grandpa. "Five doors. Each opens on a special day."

On Friday, people go into a mosque. Some take off their shoes. Friday evening, families walk to a synagogue. On Saturday, they read from a big scroll.

On Sunday, bells ring at a church. People sing inside. Down the block is a Hindu temple with bright colors. Next is a gurdwara. People cover their heads and share a free meal.

"Do we go in one?" Theo asks. "Not every family does," says Grandpa. "Some do, and some do not. But we can learn about all of them."

Talk about itWhat did Theo see at each door? Which place would you like to learn about?
Section 1

Houses of Worship

2.1

A Place to Gather

Main ideaA place of worship is where a group meets to pray, sing, read and learn.

Worship means to pray, sing or show love to God or to what is holy. Many religions have a special building for it.

People gather there. They pray. They sing. They read from holy books. They learn and eat together. Not every family goes to one. That is okay.

Think it through
What Do You Hear?

Close your eyes. You hear people singing together. Someone reads out loud. Then it is quiet while people pray. After, you smell food and hear friends talk.

What are the people doing in this place?

Words to know
worship
to pray, sing or show love to God or to what is holy
holy
very special and set apart, for people of a religion
Check yourself

1. What is a place of worship?

Why: People gather there to pray, sing and learn.

2. Does every family go to a place of worship?

Why: Not every family goes. That is okay.

3. What does worship mean?

Why: Worship means to pray, sing or show love to God or what is holy.
2.2

Holy Days to Gather

Main ideaMany groups gather on a special day each week.

Many Muslims gather at a mosque at midday on Friday. They pray together. Then they may visit with friends.

The Jewish Sabbath, Shabbat, starts Friday at sunset. It ends Saturday night. Many Jewish families go to a synagogue. Many Christians go to church on Sunday.

Read the numbers
Weekly Gathering Days

Common weekly practice; some groups gather on other days too

Which group gathers last in the week?

Words to know
Sabbath
a day of rest; for Jewish families, from Friday sunset to Saturday night
Check yourself

1. When do many Muslims gather to pray together?

Why: Many Muslims pray together at a mosque on Friday.

2. When does Shabbat start?

Why: Shabbat starts Friday at sunset.

3. Many Christians go to church on which day?

Why: Many Christians go to church on Sunday.
Section 2

Church, Synagogue, Mosque

2.3

Church and Synagogue

Main ideaChristians gather in churches; Jewish people gather in synagogues.

A church is where Christians worship. Many have a cross. Some have tall towers and bells. Inside, people sing, pray and read from the Bible.

A synagogue is where Jewish people worship. Inside is a cabinet called the ark. It holds the Torah scroll. The Torah is the first five books of the Hebrew Bible.

From the source
Glad to Go
I was glad when they said unto me, Let us go into the house of the LORD.
The Bible, Psalm 122:1, King James Version (1611)

This song is in both the Jewish and Christian Bible. How does the singer feel about going?

Words to know
church
a building where Christians worship
synagogue
a building where Jewish people worship and learn
Torah
the first five books of the Hebrew Bible, written on a scroll
Check yourself

1. What is kept in the ark in a synagogue?

Why: The ark holds the Torah scroll.

2. Where do Christians worship?

Why: Christians worship in a church.

3. What is the Torah?

Why: The Torah is the first five books of the Hebrew Bible.
2.4

The Mosque

Main ideaMuslims pray in a mosque, often on soft rugs, facing toward Mecca.

A mosque is where Muslims pray. Many have a round dome. Some have a tall tower called a minaret.

People take off their shoes. They wash before they pray. They stand in rows on soft rugs. They all face toward Mecca, a holy city in Saudi Arabia.

Think it through
Rows on the Rug

Look inside. There are no chairs. A big soft rug covers the floor. People stand in straight rows, side by side. Shoes wait in a rack by the door.

Why do you think the shoes are by the door?

Words to know
mosque
a building where Muslims pray
Mecca
a holy city for Muslims; they face toward it to pray
Check yourself

1. What do people do with their shoes in a mosque?

Why: People take off their shoes in a mosque.

2. Which way do Muslims face to pray?

Why: Muslims face toward Mecca to pray.

3. What is a minaret?

Why: A minaret is a tall tower on some mosques.
Section 3

Temple and Gurdwara

2.5

A Hindu Temple

Main ideaHindus visit a temple to pray and see images of the gods.

A Hindu temple is often bright and full of carvings. People take off their shoes. Some ring a bell as they come in.

Inside are images of gods and goddesses. Hindus bring gifts like flowers, fruit and light. They pray and sing. Many Hindus pray at home too.

Think it through
Gifts at the Temple

A girl holds a plate. On it are orange flowers, a banana and a tiny flame. She rings a bell. Then she steps up to pray.

What gifts does she bring? What might they show?

Words to know
temple
a building where some groups, like Hindus, worship
Check yourself

1. What might Hindus bring to a temple?

Why: Hindus bring gifts like flowers and fruit.

2. Where do Hindus worship?

Why: Hindus pray at temples and at home.

3. What might someone ring when they come into a Hindu temple?

Why: Some ring a bell as they come in.
2.6

The Gurdwara

Main ideaSikhs gather at a gurdwara, where anyone can share a free meal.

A gurdwara is where Sikhs worship. Their holy book is the Guru Granth Sahib. People sing its hymns.

Everyone covers their head and takes off their shoes. After prayer comes langar. Langar is a free meal. Everyone sits together and eats. Anyone may come, of any faith.

Think it through
Everyone at One Meal

Look at the long hall. People sit side by side on the floor. Helpers bring bowls of lentils and warm bread. No one pays. A grandma, a student and a visitor eat together.

What does this meal show about Sikh ideas of sharing?

Words to know
gurdwara
a building where Sikhs worship
langar
a free meal at a gurdwara, shared with anyone who comes
Check yourself

1. What is langar?

Why: Langar is a free meal at a gurdwara.

2. What is the Sikh holy book?

Why: Sikhs read the Guru Granth Sahib.

3. Who can eat langar?

Why: Anyone may come to langar.
Section 4

Being a Good Guest

2.7

Guest Manners

Main ideaA good guest uses a quiet voice and follows the rules of the place.

You might visit a place of worship with a friend. Be a good guest! Use a quiet voice. Watch what others do.

Some places ask you to take off your shoes. Some ask you to cover your head. If you are not sure, ask kindly. You can watch without joining in.

Think it through
Visiting with a Friend

Ava visits a gurdwara with her friend Harpreet. She sees a basket of head scarves by the door. People are taking off their shoes. It is quiet inside.

What should Ava do? How can she find out?

Words to know
guest
a visitor to someone's home or place
Check yourself

1. What voice should a guest use?

Why: A good guest uses a quiet voice.

2. You are not sure what to do. What helps?

Why: Asking kindly helps.

3. At a gurdwara, what do guests do?

Why: Everyone covers their head at a gurdwara.
2.8

A Walk in Chicago

Main ideaChicago has many kinds of places of worship, close together.

Chicago has people from all over the world. So it has many places of worship. You can find churches, synagogues, mosques, temples and gurdwaras.

Near Chicago, in Wilmette, is the Baha’i House of Worship. It is white and has nine sides. People of all faiths may visit it.

Think it through
Our Street Walk

Pretend to walk one block. First, a church with a bell. Next, a synagogue with a star on the door. Then a mosque with a dome. Last, a gurdwara with a tall flag.

Which place did you pass first? Which one would you ask about?

Words to know
neighbor
a person who lives near you
Check yourself

1. Why does Chicago have many places of worship?

Why: People from all over the world live in Chicago.

2. What is in Wilmette, near Chicago?

Why: The Baha'i House of Worship is in Wilmette.

3. How many sides does the Baha'i House of Worship have?

Why: It has nine sides.
