Room 36 in one minute

  • WhatSocial and emotional learning for grades 6–8: mistakes, kindness, and making things right.
  • The bookA novel, The Year of Two Voices (Book 3). It follows one seventh-grade class, Room 36, through a school year. Each lesson connects to it.
  • How much28 lessons, about one a week, September to June. Each takes 35 to 50 minutes.
  • FaithThis is the Universal Edition. It has no religious content.
  • PrivateWhat a student writes stays theirs. It never leaves their device or journal.
  • Unit 1 · September – OctoberIdentity & The Weight of Perception“Who defines me — my mistakes, my social circle, or my character?”
  • Unit 2 · November – DecemberSelf-Compassion & The Internal Critic“How do I hold myself honestly — without cruelty — when I fall short?”
  • Unit 3 · January – MarchEmpathy & Forgiving Others“How do I deal with the hurt other people cause — without being destroyed by it?”
  • Unit 4 · April – JuneGrace & Generosity“Given everything I now know — what kind of person do I choose to become?”

Lessons with extra care

Five lessons touch harder feelings. The school plans each one carefully. Here is what it does.

U2·L6★★ Highest care

Rewriting the Script

December, week 2

  • The school counselor is in the room for the whole lesson.
  • If a student steps out, the counselor goes with them. The teacher stays with the class.
  • Help lines are posted where every student can see them.
  • No substitute teacher. Never the day before a break, holiday or weekend.
  • The teacher and counselor talk it over the same day.
  • Students write on their own. The writing stays theirs.

U3·L6★★ Highest care

The Agency of Release — Letter of Release

March, week 1

  • The school counselor is in the room before the first student arrives, and stays.
  • The letter is private and never sent. No sharing, no pairs.
  • The lesson is never split in two. If time is short, the letter waits.
  • Help lines are posted. Never the day before a break, holiday or weekend.
  • The teacher and counselor talk it over the same day.

U3·L3★ Elevated care

Forgiveness vs. Reconciliation

February, week 1

  • The counselor is in the building and can be in the room within a minute, for the whole lesson.
  • No substitute teacher. Not within a day of a school-wide safety event.
  • Students are never asked to share their own stories of forgiveness.
  • The teacher and counselor talk it over the same day.

U4·L6★ Elevated care

Grace Audit — Final Project

June, week 1

  • The counselor is ready to help the same day.
  • Taught early in the week (Monday to Wednesday) when possible.

U4·L7★ Elevated care

Community Charter & Year-End Celebration

June, week 2

  • After the lesson, the teacher and counselor look at the year-end check-in together.
  • They plan who may want to keep talking with the counselor next year.

If a child tells an adult they are being hurt

Every school employee in Illinois is a mandated reporter.

When they have reason to believe a child is abused or neglected, they must call the DCFS Hotline: 1-800-25-ABUSE (1-800-252-2873).

They do not investigate first. They do not hand the call to someone else.

How the school handles it, step by step

What stays private

Nothing a student writes is collected, read, or kept.

  • Journals and worksheets stay with the student. Never graded.
  • On a screen, a worksheet stays on the student’s device. At most it tells the teacher “I finished”: a name, the sheet and the time. No words.
  • Any student may pass on any activity, at any moment. No explaining, no penalty.
  • The future-self letter: in October each student writes it and seals it. The teacher keeps the envelope safe, unopened, and gives it back in June.
  • Check-ins are short questions with set answers, no writing. A check-in score is the teacher’s starting point. It is never a label, a placement or a diagnosis.
  • There are no accounts, no ads, no trackers and no company server. If your school connects its own Google Sheet, results go only there.
See every check-in and survey The full privacy policy

Saying no to a lesson

  1. At least 10 days before the first lesson, the school sends a letter home. It names the lessons with extra care.
  2. For the lessons marked ★ or ★★, the school asks for your written yes or no. No answer is not a yes.
  3. You can say no to any lesson. Your child does a quiet activity with an adult instead.
  4. Grades and standing do not change either way.
