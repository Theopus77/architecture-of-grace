Who you are talking to changes the words

A hallway. A friend your age on one side, an adult you do not know on the other. You are about to greet both.

That choice has a name: register. Register is how close or how formal your words are. Spanish puts it in the first word you say — tú for people you are close to or equal with, usted for adults you do not know and for people in charge.

Four things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

Usted is the polite word you add at the end.

The word is only half of it. The verb changes too. ¿Cómo estás? becomes ¿Cómo está usted?, and ¿Cómo te llamas? becomes ¿Cómo se llama usted?

Change the verb, not only the word.

⚠️ People often think…

It is buenos días, buenos tardes and buenos noches.

Día is a masculine word, so it takes buenos. Tarde and noche are feminine, so they take buenas. Buenos días, buenas tardes, buenas noches.

Días takes buenos. Tardes and noches take buenas.

⚠️ People often think…

Usted is for old people.

Age is not the test. A clerk of twenty you have never met takes usted. Your seven-year-old brother takes tú. The test is how close you are, and who is in charge.

Not age — closeness, and who is in charge.

⚠️ People often think…

The ¿ at the front of a question is optional.

A written Spanish question needs both marks: ¿ at the front and ? at the end. The first one tells the reader what is coming before a single word is read, so leaving it off is a spelling error, not a style choice.

Two marks, or the question is misspelled.

Helpful Hints

🧭 The unit in one line

Greet by the hour → give your name → ask the name back → say how you are → say where you are from → choose tú or usted → say please and thank you → leave with hasta luego. The sounds and the marks (ñ, ll, rr, silent h, ¿ ¡, the accent) are the tools you need to read every one of those words out loud.

Hola · Me llamo · ¿Y tú? · Soy de · Hasta luego.

🤝 The one idea

In Spanish you choose your words for the person in front of you. The clock chooses the greeting; the person chooses tú or usted. Say the word, then hand the question back — ¿Y tú? — and the conversation keeps going.

⚠️ Traps to avoid

  • Buenos días, but buenas tardes and buenas noches. Día is a masculine word; tarde and noche are feminine. The ending has to match.
  • ¿Cómo estás? is the tú question. To an adult you have just met, say ¿Cómo está usted? Learn the two forms together, never a week apart.
  • The h is silent in every Spanish word. Hola is OH-lah, not HOH-lah. Do not drop the letter when you write, and do not say it when you read.
  • B and v sound the same as each other in Spanish. Hearing a word will not tell you which one to write; you have to learn the spelling.
  • Ñ is a letter, not an n wearing a hat. Año means “year”; ano is a different word. The little wave changes the word.
  • A written Spanish question needs two marks: ¿ at the front and ? at the end. Same for ¡ and ! Leaving off the first one is a spelling error, not a style choice.
  • The accent mark is not decoration. Sí is “yes” and si is “if”; tú is “you” and tu is “your”; cómo is “how” and como is “as, like”.

🎯 How you will be asked

  • “It is 9:00 in the morning. What do you say?” — the clock picks the greeting.
  • “Tú or usted?” with a person named — a classmate, a nurse, the principal.
  • A short conversation with the lines scrambled: put them back in order.
  • A sentence with a mistake in it — find it and fix it (¿Cómo estás? said to a doctor; a missing ¿; a missing accent).
  • “Read this word out loud” — usually one with h, ll, ñ or rr in it.

✅ Before the test, can you…

  • Give the right greeting for 8 a.m., 3 p.m. and 9 p.m.?
  • Ask a name, give yours, and hand the question back — in Spanish, out loud?
  • Say the tú form and the usted form of the same question, one after the other?
  • Name three people at school you would address as usted, and say why?
  • Read hola, mañana, me llamo and perro out loud with the right sounds?
  • Write a Spanish question with both marks and every accent in place?

📚 Go to the sources

Hear the words, then look them up. These open another website and need the internet. On Forvo, type hola or mañana and listen to real speakers from several countries say the same word.

  • Forvo — a free library of words pronounced by native speakers; search hola, buenos días, mañana, perro
  • Diccionario de la lengua española — the Royal Spanish Academy’s own dictionary, free to search
  • Diccionario panhispánico de dudas — the Academy’s answers to questions like tú, usted and vos; written in Spanish
  • SpanishDict — a free English–Spanish dictionary with recorded audio for most words and short grammar notes
