The Quiz Room

Every social studies quiz in one place — three fill-in-the-blank papers with word banks — the judicial branch, the bill road and impeachment, and the whole Constitution review — plus the four charts. This room keeps score. Nothing else on the site does, and nothing here is saved, sent, or seen by anyone.

Before you start

This room is not the lesson. It is the rehearsal.

On the activity pages there are hints on every question, nothing is ever called wrong, and a miss teaches you what that answer WOULD have been right for. That is where the learning happens, and it is where to go when something is still shaky. In here there are no hints and nothing is marked until you press the button — you find out how you did at the end, because that is what a quiz is. There is no clock: a rehearsal is for thinking, not for hurrying. Get it wrong in this room, on purpose, as many times as you like. That is what a rehearsal is for.

Nothing in this room is recorded. No score is saved on this computer, none is sent to your teacher, and closing the tab forgets all of it. Your teacher sees your work from the activity pages, never a number from here.

Judicial Branch Quiz

The same fifteen questions, in the same order, served by the same two word banks — the quiz as you will actually sit it. Tap a word, then tap the blank it belongs in, or drag it if you have a mouse; tap a filled blank to send its word back. Every word is used exactly once, which is a real clue on the paper too. The whole Constitution review

from Put It All Together
Fill in the blanks — three banks, the sheet in order

Everything on the review sheet

Twenty-four blanks across three word banks, in the order your review sheet asks them: the plan and its parts, then Congress and the presidency, then the courts and the amendments. Every word is used exactly once, so a bank with one word left answers itself — that is a real clue on paper too. No clock, and nothing is checked until you press the button.

04

The four charts

from Put It All Together
Fill them in — and print them, blank or finished

Separation of powers, checks, federalism, amendments

The same charts your teacher hands out, except these ones talk back. Tap a card, then tap the column it belongs in — or drag it, if you have a mouse. Put one in the wrong column and nothing is lost: it tells you which column it really lives in, and why that one pulled you. Press Print this chart only for a clean sheet — blank for a class set, or finished, whichever state it is in.
