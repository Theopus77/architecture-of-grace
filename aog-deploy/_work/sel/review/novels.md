# The Architecture of Grace — series editorial pass (Books 1–6)

Read in full: room-12.json (Book 1), room-18.json (Book 2), room-36.json (Book 3), room-104.json (Book 4), room-207.json (Book 5), the-dwelling.json (Book 6). ≈255k words.

This is an error pass, not a rewrite. Every item gives book, chapter, an exact quote (find-and-replace ready) and a proposed fix. Items are in priority order within each section. Where one fact is wrong in several places, the item lists every occurrence so the fix can be applied consistently.

A note on the canonical timeline used below (established by Books 1–2 and mostly honoured by Book 5):
- Marcus, Aaliyah: Marcus is 6 in first grade; Aaliyah is 3 (three years younger). Marcus tells his parents about the bike in November of third grade (age 8). The bike happened when he was 6, "in early August". The ice-cream anniversary ritual starts in fifth grade (Book 2 ch 14).
- Maya lives with her grandmother from "the spring" before first grade (Book 1 ch 12); her mother is absent/unwell, father not around.
- Kezia lives with her grandmother; an unnamed, ungendered "grown-up" has been kept out of the house since Kezia was about 4.
- Mia moves to Pittsburgh in the middle of third grade (age 9).
- Pepper (Darius's dog, female) arrives May of third grade (age 9) and dies October of senior year (Book 5 ch 5).
- Ms. Calloway has taught 14 years as of first grade; she moves up with the cohort at each stage.
- Mr. Patel is the cohort's third-grade homeroom teacher (Book 2), then their seventh-grade English teacher (Book 3), retiring after their eighth-grade year.

## 1. CONTINUITY ERRORS

### 1.1 Aaliyah becomes a neighbour instead of Marcus's sister (Book 6) — SERIES-BREAKING
- **Book 6, ch. 11 "Marcus, and the Whole Truth"**: "there was a girl named Aaliyah, a neighbor, a year younger, and there was a small theft and a smaller lie, and the lie was that Marcus said Aaliyah had broken the bike, when in truth Marcus had broken it himself" … "Aaliyah's family moved away when Marcus was nine. He never told her." … "he found her at twenty-five … living two states away".
  In Books 1–5 Aaliyah is Marcus's little sister (three years younger), lives in his house, and was told the whole story at ten (Book 4 ch 15). She cannot have "moved away when Marcus was nine" or be found "two states away."
  Also **Book 6, ch. 11**: "his mother took him for ice cream at Mason's, not as a reward" — in Books 2/4/5 Marcus buys Aaliyah the ice cream.
  Also **Book 6, ch. 1**: "the one about a bicycle and a girl named Aaliyah and a thing he said when he was six that was not true" (fine as written, but the chapter 11 reveal contradicts it).
  **Fix**: The Book 6 keystone needs a different wronged party. The cleanest repair that keeps the chapter's mechanism (repair made to family, never to the person harmed): make the person Marcus blamed a *neighbour kid* with a new name (e.g. "a boy named Jaylen from down the street" — Jaylen is already a Book 1 secondary character who could plausibly have moved), and keep Aaliyah as the sister who *was* told. Replace "a girl named Aaliyah, a neighbor, a year younger" → "a boy named Jaylen, a neighbor, a year older, who had been riding with them"; every later "Aaliyah"/"she"/"her" in ch. 11 → "Jaylen"/"he"/"him"; "Aaliyah's family moved away" → "Jaylen's family moved away". Adjust the note's "a girl named Aaliyah" in ch. 1 to "his sister Aaliyah — and, it turned out, somebody else" or simply drop the clause. Change "his mother took him for ice cream at Mason's" → "he started taking Aaliyah for ice cream at Mason's". Ms. Calloway's line in ch. 13 "They told her about Marcus and Aaliyah" → "about Marcus and Jaylen".

## 3. SAFETY

Overall the series is careful: adults are always brought in, no methods are described, help-seeking is modelled and rewarded, and the 988 Lifeline is named where appropriate (Book 3 ch 12, 16, 20). Items a school or a safe-messaging reviewer would still want addressed:

**K–2 (Book 1)**
- ch. 17 (Kezia): "the grown-up did some things that hurt Kezia and her grandmother, and the grown-up has not been allowed in their house for almost two years." Appropriately vague; keep. One line a school may flag as heavier than the read-aloud level: "Loving them does not mean you have to be in danger. You can love them from a long way away, your whole life, and never be unsafe again." — consider softening "in danger" → "unsafe".
- ch. 23: "doesn't text back … Their phone was off." — phones as examples in a first-grade lesson; → "doesn't wave back … They did not hear you."
- No other flags. Biscuit's death (ch. 16) and Marcus's LEGO throw (ch. 10) are age-appropriate.

**3–5 (Book 2)**
- ch. 27 ("Boundaries from Strength") item 9 — secret-keeping guidance is exactly what schools want; keep.
- ch. 13 (Jordan and Dr. Mendel): careful and positive; keep.
- ch. 4: "Priya would say it to her boyfriend in college" — harmless.
- ch. 15/20 use the phrase "HIGHEST RISK lesson" in narrative voice; consider "the hardest lesson" so a child reader is not primed to worry.
- No content a school would flag.

**6–8 (Book 3)**
- ch. 12: Theo's script section names Ms. Wong and "The 988 Lifeline" — good. ch. 15: "an uncle who had hurt his sister Lena when Theo was small" — handled with three adults present; keep vague as is.
- Brands/people: "Instagram" (ch. 3, 10), "FaceTimed" (ch. 22), "Meditations by Marcus Aurelius" and "James Marcia … identity foreclosure" (ch. 2; attribution is correct). None are a problem, but "Instagram" could become "her feed" if the series wants to stay brand-neutral.

**9–10 (Book 4)**
- ch. 11 (the party): alcohol is named, no one drinks on page, parents are consulted, and the decision is Marcus's own — this is model handling.
- ch. 13: Mistake Wall cards "My dad has been drinking again and I have not told anyone." / "I have not eaten lunch in two weeks." — the text immediately shows the adult response ("a kid would get checked in on"). Keep.
- Real names: "The House on Mango Street", "To Kill a Mockingbird", "Mockingjay", "Hunger Games" — fine. "Lincoln Park High" is the name of several real high schools (Chicago; Michigan; New Jersey) — low risk, but if the town is meant to be fictional consider "Linden Park".

**11–12 (Book 5)**
- ch. 3: "a boy in her grade who had been telling her, in pieces over a few weeks, about being scared he might hurt himself. … I am going to walk you to Ms. Calloway right now. … The boy had been seen by professionals within an hour. He was okay." — Conforms to safe-messaging guidance (no method, immediate adult involvement, recovery shown). Keep. One suggestion: add a line naming that the boy "got help, and kept getting it" so recovery is not presented as instantaneous.

- Real-world references: "University of Pittsburgh" (ch. 26), "COVID" (ch. 2), "AA meetings" (ch. 19), "Google Docs" (ch. 4), "Common App" (ch. 4) — none problematic.

This is within AFSP guidance. Recommend one explicit sentence in ch. 7 that she "saw a doctor and started treatment" so the recovery is not attributed solely to the friends, and consider a resources line (988) in the front matter for an adult book that contains this.
- ch. 13 (Ms. Calloway's death): illness unnamed, death "in her sleep"; fine.
- ch. 9 (Jordan/Mia): fine.

## 4. VOICE (nine sentences across the series)

1. **Book 1, ch. 11**: "It will follow them, in different forms, all the way through Book Five, when they are seventeen and eighteen and about to leave home, and they will still be doing it then." — series-meta in a K–2 read-aloud; the narrator usually keeps this lighter. → "It will follow them for years, even when they are big and about to leave home."
2. **Book 1, ch. 27**: "(She still has it. She is forty-three now, and she still has it. We will talk about it more in Book Five.)" — same issue; → "(She still has it. She is grown up now, and she still has it.)"
3. **Book 1, ch. 23**: "It saves you, on average, about eight unnecessary mad feelings per day. Multiply that by 365. Multiply that by a lifetime." — arithmetic aside pitched above the K–2 reader; → "It saves you a lot of mad feelings. Every day. For your whole life."
4. **Book 2, ch. 4**: "Marcus would say it to himself in a hospital waiting room in tenth grade. Priya would say it to her boyfriend in college." — the hospital waiting room never happens in any later book; → drop the Marcus clause or → "Marcus would say it to himself in a college dorm."
5. **Book 3, ch. 4**: "the kind of light venom that twelve-year-old girls have a particular skill for" — the narrator generalising about girls in a way the rest of the series avoids; → "the kind of light venom that twelve-year-olds have a particular skill for".
6. **Book 4, ch. 9**: the chapter is third-person throughout ("Kezia had said, okay.") but signs off "— Kezia"; same in **Book 5, ch. 8** ("— Theo"). Either remove the sign-off or rewrite in first person like the other signed chapters.
7. **Book 4, ch. 14**: "Amara — Amara is me, I am writing this — saw the cohort holding the small private moment" — a first-person chapter tripping over itself; → "I saw the cohort holding the small private moment".
8. **Book 5, ch. 16**: "— J.A.R., in the back of the room, watching (Just kidding. J.A.R. is not in the room. The narrator is gone. But the narrator, for one moment…" — this breaks the promise in the Note ("I am here, on this page, and then I am gone until the last page") and the joke undercuts the chapter's mood. → delete the parenthetical and the sign-off.
9. **Book 6, ch. 5**: "He married again, eventually, at thirty-six, a woman named Priya — no, I am teasing, that would be too neat, and this book does not do neat." — the only wink of its kind in the adult book; consider cutting to keep the narrator's stated restraint.
