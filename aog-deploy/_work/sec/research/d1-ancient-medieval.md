# Dossier 1: Ancient Mystery Cults and Medieval Orders

Eleusis, Mithras, the Pythagoreans, and the Knights Templar. Source dossier for the Architecture of Grace K-12 course "Secret Societies".

Compiled 2026-09-30.

**How this was researched.** Every fact below was checked with live web searches against the holding institution, a scholarly edition, or a well-sourced reference. Direct page fetching was blocked in this research environment (archive.org, Gutenberg, Perseus, LacusCurtius, Gallica and loc.gov were all unreachable). So:
- a quotation appears **only** where a search result gave the exact published translation;
- a URL is listed only if it appeared in a search result;
- anything I could not confirm is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

Before a lesson goes live, a person should open each URL once and check the page reference.

## Summary

1. All four groups were "secret" in different ways. Eleusis kept a **ritual** secret, the Mithraists kept a **membership** secret, the Pythagoreans kept a **teaching** secret, and the Templars were public, but their **chapter meetings and reception ceremony** were closed. That closed ceremony is what their accusers used against them.
2. The Eleusinian Mysteries ran for about a thousand years. Thousands of people were initiated, and the core secret was kept so well that we still do not know exactly what happened in the Telesterion (the initiation hall).
3. Mithraism left no scripture. Almost everything we know comes from buildings (mithraea), images, inscriptions, and hostile Christian writers. It is a real "archaeology of a secret".
4. The Pythagoreans are known mostly from writers who lived 600 to 800 years later (Iamblichus, Porphyry, Diogenes Laertius). Separating record from legend is the main teaching point.
5. The Templars are the best-documented group in this dossier. We have their Rule, papal bulls, royal arrest orders, and hundreds of pages of interrogation transcripts, printed by Michelet in 1841-1851 and now free online.
6. The Chinon Parchment (Vatican, found 2001 by Barbara Frale) shows that papal cardinals **absolved** Jacques de Molay and other leaders in August 1308. It did not end the case. The order was still suppressed in 1312 and Molay was burned in 1314.
7. Most "Templar mystery" material is modern. Examples: Friday the 13th, hidden treasure, Rosslyn Chapel, Oak Island, the Priory of Sion (a proven 1960s forgery), and Masonic descent (first claimed in 1737).
8. Illinois link: Chicago hosted the Masonic Knights Templar's 21st Triennial Conclave in 1880. Material from it is held by the Scottish Rite Masonic Museum & Library. Chicago hosted the conclave again in 1910.
9. Teaching opportunity: the owner has read Bill Cooper for decades. Cooper's *Behold a Pale Horse* (1991) traces a single secret conspiracy back to ancient mystery schools. This dossier gives the primary evidence that lets older students test that kind of claim themselves.
10. Grade fit: Eleusis symbols and Mithraic grade symbols work from 3-5; the Pythagorean "rules" and codes work from 3-5; the Templar trial works best from 6-8 up; the conspiracy and myth-busting material is 9-12.

## Primary sources

### A. Eleusinian Mysteries

**P1. *Homeric Hymn to Demeter* (Hymn 2), c. 7th-6th c. BC.** Trans. Hugh G. Evelyn-White, *Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns and Homerica* (Loeb, 1914). Public domain.
- Where: Perseids/Perseus CTS reader, lines 475-495: https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0013/tlg002/perseus-eng2/475-495 ; also theoi.com: https://theoi.com/Text/HomericHymns1.html
- What it shows: the founding myth (Persephone taken, Demeter grieving, the gift of the rites). It is the ancient text that states outright that the rites must not be told.
- Exact quotation (Evelyn-White, near the end of the hymn): "Happy is he among men upon earth who has seen these mysteries; but he who is uninitiate and who has no part in them, never has lot of like good things once he is dead, down in the darkness and gloom."
- Band: myth 3-5; quotation 6-8.

**P2. Ninnion Tablet (Ninnion Pinax), red-figure clay votive plaque, c. 370 BC.** National Archaeological Museum, Athens, inv. 11036. Found at Eleusis in 1895. 44.5 x 33 cm.
- Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninnion_Tablet (for orientation); Oxford Classical Art Research Centre record: https://www.carc.ox.ac.uk/record/07DB1472-B21D-409A-8D98-604BBF95736A (contents UNVERIFIED; the page could not be opened)
- What it shows: a procession of initiates, torches, and Demeter and Persephone. A woman named Ninnion dedicated it. It is one of the very few surviving pictures of the initiation. Wikipedia calls it "the only known original representation"; treat that wording as DISPUTED and say "one of the rare".
- Band: 3-5 ("What can you see? What is hidden?").

**P3. Andocides, *On the Mysteries* (Oration 1), c. 400/399 BC.**
- Where: the Bibliotheke (Univ. of Arkansas) Andocides texts: https://cmuntz.hosted.uark.edu/texts/andocides/index.html ; review of scholarship: BMCR 2012.12.40 https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2012/2012.12.40
- What it shows: a real court speech from a man accused in the 415 BC scandal. Young aristocrats were accused of acting out the Mysteries at private parties. Revealing the rites was a crime that could bring death or exile. This is courtroom evidence of how seriously Athens protected the secret.
- Band: 9-10.

**P4. Aristotle on initiation, fragment preserved by Synesius, *Dio* 8 (c. AD 400).** Aristotle fr. 15 Rose (fragment number from standard scholarship; UNVERIFIED against Rose directly).
- Where: Livius page for Synesius, *Dio*: https://livius.org/sources/content/synesius/synesius-dio/synesius-dio-7 (section number UNVERIFIED)
- What it shows: Aristotle's idea that initiates did not *learn* (mathein) something but *experienced* (pathein) something and were put into a state of mind. This is the best ancient explanation of why the secret was hard to "leak": it was an experience, not a password.
- Band: 11-12 (philosophy).

**P5. Cicero, *De Legibus* 2.36 (c. 52 BC).** Cicero, an initiate, praises the Athenian mysteries as having taught people to live with joy and die with better hope.
- Where: Latin on The Latin Library (/cicero/leg2.shtml). URL not confirmed in a search result; exact Latin not retrieved. **Quotation UNVERIFIED; do not quote until checked.**
- Band: 11-12.

**P6. Clement of Alexandria, *Protrepticus* (Exhortation to the Greeks) 2, c. AD 195.** Trans. G. W. Butterworth, Loeb Classical Library 92 (1919). Perseus catalog: https://catalog.perseus.org/catalog/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0555.tlg001
- What it shows: a hostile Christian author claims to reveal the Eleusinian password formula (the *synthema*: fasting, drinking the kykeon, handling objects in a basket). Scholars use it cautiously. Exact wording **UNVERIFIED**; I could not retrieve the text.
- Band: 9-10 (source-reliability lesson: can we trust an enemy's "leak"?).

### B. Mithras

**P7. Mithraeum beneath the Basilica of San Clemente, Rome (early 3rd c. AD).** Built in a 1st-c. Roman house under a 4th-c. church, under a 12th-c. church. Its altar has the tauroctony (Mithras killing the bull) on the front, the torch-bearers Cautes and Cautopates on the sides, and a serpent on the back.
- Where: https://mithraeum.eu/monument/13 ; the Basilica is open to visitors (lower levels ticketed).
- What it shows: a secret meeting cave literally buried under two churches. Father Mullooly (Irish Dominican) began digging in 1857. Water flooding delayed full excavation until 1914, when a drainage tunnel was built.
- Band: 3-5 (layers of a city as a "layer cake").

**P8. The London Mithraeum, Walbrook (mid-3rd c. AD).** Excavated 1954 by W. F. Grimes and Audrey Williams. The marble head of Mithras was found on the last scheduled day of the dig. Crowds, parliamentary debate and Cabinet discussion followed. The temple was rebuilt on its original site by Bloomberg (opened 2017; opening year from general knowledge, UNVERIFIED here).
- Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Mithraeum ; British Online Archives note on the 70th anniversary: https://britishonlinearchives.com/posts/category/notable-days/826/70-years-since-marble-head-of-mithras-unearthed-in-walbrook
- Band: 3-5.

**P9. Mithraeum of Dura-Europos, Syria (dedicated AD 168-169), now reconstructed at Yale University Art Gallery.** Found in 1933-34 by the Yale-French Academy excavation. A dated dedication by Palmyrene archers serving in the Roman army. It has two tauroctony reliefs, plus paintings of Mithras as a mounted archer.
- Where: https://duraeuropos.artgallery.yale.edu/Mithraeum ; relief object record: https://artgallery.yale.edu/collections/objects/6890
- What it shows: a whole secret temple you can walk into in the United States. There is a 1933-34 photograph of Franz Cumont and Michael Rostovtzeff standing in front of it.
- Band: 6-8.

**P10. Mithraeum of Felicissimus, Ostia (2nd half 3rd c. AD).** Excavated by Guido and Raissa Calza in 1940. The floor mosaic is divided into seven panels, one for each grade: Raven (Corax), Bridegroom (Nymphus), Soldier (Miles), Lion (Leo), Persian (Perses), Runner of the Sun (Heliodromus), Father (Pater). Each panel has symbols and its planet.
- Where: Parco archeologico di Ostia antica panel: https://ostiaantica.cultura.gov.it/en/educational-panels-and-maps/larea-dei-culti-orientali-e-il-quartiere-residenziale-di-porta-laurentina/mitreo-di-felicissimo/ ; https://www.mithraeum.eu/monument/7
- What it shows: the clearest surviving "ladder" of a secret society's ranks.
- Band: 3-5 ("design your own seven symbols").

**P11. Tertullian, *De Corona* (The Chaplet) 15, c. AD 200.**
- Where: New Advent (ANF trans.): https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0304.htm ; parallel Latin/English/French: https://bkv.unifr.ch/works/cpl-21/compare/the-chaplet/16/de-corona-militis
- What it shows: a Christian opponent describes a Mithraic "soldier" being offered a crown at sword point in a cave. The initiate pushes it away, saying Mithras is his crown, and never wears a crown afterward. Tertullian uses it to shame Christian soldiers. Paraphrase only; check the exact ANF wording on New Advent before quoting.
- Band: 9-10.

### C. Pythagoreans

**P12. Polybius, *Histories* 2.39 (2nd c. BC).** Reports that the Pythagorean "meeting houses" (*synedria*) in Magna Graecia were burned and the region fell into disorder.
- Where: LacusCurtius (Univ. of Chicago server, Bill Thayer), Loeb trans. W. R. Paton. URL for 2.39 **UNVERIFIED** (the server could not be reached).
- What it shows: the earliest surviving *historian* (not philosopher) to record violence against the brotherhood. It is about 300 years after the events.
- Band: 9-10.

**P13. Iamblichus, *On the Pythagorean Life* (c. AD 300).** Trans. Thomas Taylor (1818, public domain); modern trans. Dillon & Hershbell (1991).
- What it shows: the five-year silence for new members; the Cylon story (a rejected applicant leads the attack); rules like the *akousmata* ("things heard"). It also tells the Hippasus legend: he was drowned or cursed for revealing a mathematical secret. Iamblichus wrote 800 years later, so treat it as a source for **legend and reputation**, not a diary.
- URL **UNVERIFIED** (archive.org copies exist but could not be opened).
- Band: rules 3-5; source criticism 9-10.

**P14. Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of Eminent Philosophers* Book 8 (3rd c. AD).** Trans. R. D. Hicks (Loeb, 1925).
- What it shows: a gossipy collection of Pythagoras stories, including rules about beans. It is useful beside Iamblichus. URL **UNVERIFIED**.
- Band: 6-8.

**P15. Cicero, *De Natura Deorum* 1.10.** The origin of *ipse dixit* ("he himself said it"). Pythagoreans reportedly ended arguments by citing the master. Text **UNVERIFIED** in this session (standard reference; check before quoting).
- Band: 6-8 (a lesson on "because the leader said so").

### D. Knights Templar

**P16. William of Tyre, *Historia rerum in partibus transmarinis gestarum*, Book 12 ch. 7 (written c. 1170s-1180s).** Account of the Templars' founding.
- Where: Fordham Internet Medieval Sourcebook: https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/tyre-templars.asp
- What it shows: the founding by Hugh of Payns and Geoffrey of St Omer, which he dates to 1118. Nine knights, poverty, lodging in the royal palace on the Temple Mount. It also shows William's later hostility: by his time he thought the order had grown proud and rich.
- Band: 6-8.

**P17. The Latin Rule (Primitive Rule), Council of Troyes, c. 1129.** 72 clauses, based mostly on the Rule of St Benedict.
- Where: Fordham: https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/templars-primitiverule.asp ; full English with the later French statutes (including the *reception ceremony*): J. M. Upton-Ward, *The Rule of the Templars* (Boydell, 1992), ISBN 9780851157016.
- What it shows: rules on meals, silence, clothing, horses, and no hunting except lions. It shows the Templars as rule-bound religious men, not occultists. The reception clauses in Upton-Ward are the "secret ceremony" the accusers later twisted.
- Band: 3-5 for selected rules (compare with classroom rules); full text 9-10.

**P18. Bernard of Clairvaux, *De laude novae militiae* (In Praise of the New Knighthood), c. 1129-1136.**
- Where: overview at ORB: https://www.the-orb.arlima.net/encyclop/religion/monastic/bernard.html (full English translation in Cistercian Fathers series; URL UNVERIFIED)
- What it shows: the most famous monk in Europe answering Hugh of Payns. He defends "monks who fight" and introduces the idea of *malicidium* (killing the evil, not the man).
- Band: 11-12 (ethics of holy war; handle with care).

**P19. Philip IV's secret arrest order, 14 September 1307, and instructions to royal officers.** Original held in the Archives nationales, Paris (shelfmark **UNVERIFIED**). English translation in Malcolm Barber & Keith Bate, *The Templars* (Manchester Medieval Sources, 2002). Discussed in Barber, *The Trial of the Templars* (Cambridge, 2nd ed. 2006), ch. "Arrests": https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/trial-of-the-templars/3CEF95FD22DD129F4B4BFE3A6D5074D8
- What it shows: the opening pile-up of horror words ("A bitter thing, a lamentable thing, a thing which is horrible to contemplate, terrible to hear of..."). This is propaganda written a month before the arrests. It works well for teaching loaded language. Quoted as it appears in the Barber summary; the full original French wording is UNVERIFIED.
- Band: 9-10.

**P20. Jules Michelet (ed.), *Procès des Templiers*, 2 vols. (Paris: Imprimerie royale, 1841-1851), Collection de documents inédits sur l'histoire de France.**
- Where: Project Gutenberg, vol. 1: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/22919 ; vol. 2: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/22920 (produced from BnF/Gallica images)
- What it shows: the Latin minutes of the papal commission (1309-1311). It includes Jacques de Molay's own appearances and the testimony of 231 knights and serving brothers. Hundreds of real voices, many of them ordinary farm managers and servants, not warriors.
- Band: 11-12 (Latin; excerpts in translation at 9-10).

**P21. The Chinon Parchment, 17-20 August 1308.** Vatican Apostolic Archive, Archivum Arcis, Armarium D 217 (original); D 218 is an authenticated copy. Issued by Cardinals Bérenger Frédol, Étienne de Suisy and Landolfo Brancacci. Identified by Barbara Frale in September 2001.
- Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinon_Parchment (for orientation); Google Arts & Culture image: https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/le-parchemin-de-chinon/TgEQuKxdDIbFlA ; History News Network report: https://www.historynewsnetwork.org/article/53043
- What it shows: the cardinals questioned Molay and other leaders at Chinon castle and **absolved** them and restored them to the sacraments. Frale's reading is that Clement V meant to reform the order, not destroy it, and was overruled by royal pressure.
- Band: 6-8 (the discovery story); document analysis 9-12.

**P22. *Processus contra Templarios* (Vatican Secret Archives / Scrinium, 2007).** Facsimile edition of the Templar trial documents, including the Chinon Parchment. 799 numbered copies plus one for Pope Benedict XVI. **Copy no. 164 is in the Rare Book Collection of the Law Library of Congress**, Washington DC.
- Where: Library of Congress Law blog, "Templar Secrets at the Law Library of Congress" (Sept 2011): https://blogs.loc.gov/law/2011/09/templar-secrets-at-the-law-library-of-congress/ ; Catholic News Agency on the launch: https://www.catholicnewsagency.com/news/book_published_from_vatican_secret_archives
- Band: 6-8 (as a story about a book that cost about $8,400 and had wax-seal replicas).

**P23. Papal bulls of Clement V at the Council of Vienne: *Vox in excelso* (22 March 1312) and *Ad providam* (2 May 1312).**
- Where: English text of the Vienne decrees, Tanner ed. (PDF, Univ. of Trieste course copy): https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/320815/mod_resource/content/1/Concilio%20Vienne%2C%201311%20%28inglese%29.pdf ; EWTN: https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/council-of-vienne-1542
- What it shows: the pope suppressed the order by apostolic ordinance, "not by definitive sentence". The Templars were never found guilty as an order. *Ad providam* gave the property to the Hospitallers, with separate arrangements in Iberia.
- Band: 9-10.

**P24. Plaque at the execution site, Square du Vert-Galant, Pont Neuf, Paris (modern).** Text: "A cet endroit / Jacques de Molay / Dernier grand maître / de l'ordre du temple / a été brûlé le 18 Mars 1314."
- What it shows: how a place remembers. The island (Île aux Juifs) was joined to the Île de la Cité when the Pont Neuf was built (1578-1604).
- Band: 6-8.

**P25. Geoffroi de Paris, rhymed chronicle (early 14th c.).** A clerk at Philip IV's court and an eyewitness-era source. He says Molay declared at the stake that God would avenge them. Edition and URL **UNVERIFIED**; cited through secondary reporting.
- Band: 9-10 (compare with the later "curse" legend).

### E. Illinois / Chicago and modern-myth sources

**P26. *The Daily Conclave Souvenir* (Chicago, 1880)**, an illustrated newspaper of the 21st Triennial Conclave, Grand Encampment of Knights Templar of the U.S. Also the souvenir calling-card booklet of James G. Elwood of the Illinois commandery, and a c. 1880 conclave scrapbook. Held by the Scottish Rite Masonic Museum & Library, Lexington MA.
- Where: https://srmml.libraryhost.com/repositories/2/resources/3236 ; https://srmml.libraryhost.com/repositories/2/archival_objects/663322 ; https://srmml.libraryhost.com/repositories/2/archival_objects/662744
- What it shows: a 19th-century American fraternal order dressed as medieval knights, with tents on the Chicago lakefront. It is the bridge from medieval Templars to modern fraternal "Templars", which are not the same organization.
- Band: 3-5.

**P27. *Dossiers Secrets d'Henri Lobineau*, deposited at the Bibliothèque nationale de France, 27 April 1967.** A 27-page forgery by Pierre Plantard and Philippe de Chérisey. De Chérisey admitted the parchments were fakes in 1971. Plantard admitted under oath in a 1993 judicial inquiry that his later "Priory" claims were invented.
- Where: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dossiers_Secrets_d%27Henri_Lobineau ; CESNUR analysis: https://cesnur.org/2005/pippo.htm
- What it shows: a *planted* document in a national library, designed to be "found". This is the perfect companion to the genuine Chinon discovery.
- Band: 9-10.

**P28. Milton William Cooper, *Behold a Pale Horse* (Light Technology Publishing, 1991).**
- Where: publisher reprints; critical account: *The New Republic*, "A Pioneer of Paranoia": https://newrepublic.com/article/150922/pioneer-paranoia
- What it shows: a modern primary source *for the history of conspiracy belief*. Cooper argues that a secret society descending from early civilizations and mystery schools controls world events. Documented: early printings reproduced the forged, antisemitic *Protocols of the Elders of Zion* and told readers to read "Illuminati" in place of "Jews". Whether later printings dropped that chapter is UNVERIFIED: the earlier claim here that it "was removed" has no source, and reports on current printings describe it as still included (source check, 00-source-check.md; see D9 row 21). Check an in-print copy.
- Band: 11-12 only, with the teacher framing below.

| 7 | Mithras was born of a virgin on 25 December, like Jesus. | UNSUPPORTED | Mithraic art shows him born from a rock (*petra genetrix*). A December 25 link comes from Sol Invictus calendars, not Mithraic texts. Popular internet claim. |
| 8 | Pythagoreans kept mathematical discoveries secret, and Hippasus was killed for revealing irrational numbers. | DISPUTED / LEGEND | Late sources only (Iamblichus, P13). Burkert, *Lore and Science in Ancient Pythagoreanism* (Harvard, 1972) treats this as legend. |
| 9 | Pythagoreans avoided beans. | DOCUMENTED as reported rule | Diogenes Laertius 8 (P14); Iamblichus. The reason was debated even in antiquity. |
| 10 | Pythagorean meeting houses were burned in anti-Pythagorean violence. | DOCUMENTED (late); details DISPUTED | Polybius 2.39 (P12); dating c. 509 vs c. 450 BC debated |
| 11 | The Templars were founded in 1118. | DISPUTED | William of Tyre says 1118 (P16). Most historians (e.g. Barber) prefer 1119 or early 1120. |
| 12 | There were only nine Templars for nine years. | DISPUTED | William of Tyre says so. Other evidence (for example, Hugh recruiting in Europe in 1127-29) suggests the story simplifies. |
| 13 | The Templars ran an early international banking system. | SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS | Deposits, transfers, and the Paris Temple acting as a royal treasury (Barber, *The New Knighthood*, 1994) |
| 14 | The Templars were arrested on Friday 13 October 1307, and this created the Friday-the-13th superstition. | Arrest date DOCUMENTED; superstition link UNSUPPORTED | Written Friday-13th fear appears in the 19th century. Thomas Lawson's novel *Friday, the Thirteenth* (1907) spread it. The Templar link was popularized much later (Helen Nicholson, Cardiff, quoted in Live Science). |
| 15 | Templars confessed to spitting on the cross, denying Christ, and idol worship. | DOCUMENTED (the confessions); truth of charges DISPUTED to largely rejected | Many confessions came under torture or its threat, and many were retracted. Michelet (P20). Barber, *Trial of the Templars*, argues the charges were fabricated. Frale argues a real but misunderstood "hazing" ritual of denial. |
| 16 | "Baphomet" was a goat-headed idol of the Templars. | UNSUPPORTED (the goat image) | "Baphomet" appears in a few trial confessions, widely read as an Old French corruption of "Mahomet". The goat-headed figure was drawn by Éliphas Lévi in 1856, 540 years later. |
| 17 | The Chinon Parchment proves the pope declared the Templars innocent. | DISPUTED / OVERSTATED | It records absolution of named leaders from sin (P21). It did not quash the trial. The order was still suppressed "without definitive sentence" (P23). Frale's wider reading is supported by Barber, Demurger and Cardini, but popular headlines ("Templars not heretics") went further than the document. |
| 18 | The Templars were officially found guilty by the Church. | UNSUPPORTED | *Vox in excelso* suppressed the order by papal provision, not by condemnation (P23). |
| 19 | Templar property went to Philip IV. | Mostly UNSUPPORTED | *Ad providam* gave it to the Hospitallers. Philip extracted large "costs" payments. Iberian lands went to new orders (Montesa; Order of Christ in Portugal). |
| 20 | Molay cursed Pope Clement, Nogaret and Philip IV to meet him before God within a year. | LEGEND | Geoffroi de Paris (P25) says only that Molay said God would avenge them. A fuller curse appears c. 1330 (Ferreto de' Ferreti), spoken by an anonymous Templar. Nogaret had already died in 1313. Clement (April 1314) and Philip (November 1314) did die that year, which fed the legend. |
| 21 | The Templars survived in Portugal. | DOCUMENTED (institutionally) | The Order of Christ (1319) took over Templar property and some personnel with papal approval. |
| 22 | The Templars survived secretly and became the Freemasons. | UNSUPPORTED | No documentary link. The claim first appears in 18th-c. Masonic writing: Ramsay 1737 (crusaders), then von Hund's Strict Observance from 1751. |
| 23 | Templar fleet and treasure escaped from La Rochelle in 1307. | UNSUPPORTED (the fleet); one testimony exists | One Templar deposition (Jean de Châlon, 1308) spoke of a brother fleeing with treasure (details UNVERIFIED in this session). No corroboration. No Templar treasure has ever been found. |
| 24 | Templars fought at Bannockburn (1314), built Rosslyn Chapel's secrets, or buried treasure on Oak Island. | UNSUPPORTED | Rosslyn was begun in 1446, more than a century after the suppression. No contemporary evidence for Bannockburn Templars. Oak Island has no medieval finds tied to the Templars. |
| 25 | The Priory of Sion was a real medieval society behind the Templars. | DISPROVEN | The forgery was deposited in 1967 and its makers confessed (1971, 1993) (P27). |
| 26 | Frale's claim (2009) that the Templars held the Shroud of Turin. | DISPUTED | Argued in her book; not accepted by most historians. |
| 27 | One continuous secret society runs from ancient mystery schools to modern elites (Cooper and others). | UNSUPPORTED | The groups in this dossier had different gods, members, centuries and purposes. There are gaps of 800+ years between them. Each ended or changed in documented ways. |

## Story hooks

1. **The last day of the dig (London, 18 September 1954).** Workers are about to pour foundations for an office block. On the final scheduled day, the marble head of a young god in a Phrygian cap comes out of the mud. Within days, queues of Londoners stretch around the block, and Parliament and the Cabinet argue over whether to save the temple. (3-5 and up)
2. **A priest with a lamp under his church (Rome, 1857 onward).** Father Joseph Mullooly digs below San Clemente and finds a lost church. Below that, he hears running water, and behind it a Roman house with a secret cave-temple, too flooded to reach for decades. Today visitors walk down through three layers of time and can still hear the water. (3-5)
3. **Dawn, Friday 13 October 1307.** Sealed letters, sent a month earlier with orders not to open them until the day, are opened across France. At dawn, royal officers knock on the doors of every Templar house at once. Most of the brothers are older men managing farms. (6-8)
4. **The document in the wrong drawer (Vatican, September 2001).** Paleographer Barbara Frale finds a parchment with Templar interrogation records. It had been misfiled for centuries. It shows the pope's own cardinals quietly forgiving the Grand Master seven centuries earlier. (6-8)
5. **The island that disappeared (Paris, 18 March 1314).** Jacques de Molay and Geoffroi de Charney are burned on a tiny island in the Seine. That island no longer exists: it became part of the Île de la Cité when the Pont Neuf was built. Today a small plaque in a park is all that marks the place. (6-8)
6. **The party that became a trial (Athens, 415 BC).** Just as the great fleet is about to sail for Sicily, Athens wakes to find its street statues smashed. Rumors say rich young men have been acting out the holy Mysteries at dinner parties. Andocides later stands in court to defend his life. (9-10)
7. **A woman named Ninnion (Eleusis, c. 370 BC).** She pays for a clay painting of the initiation and dedicates it to the goddesses. It lies in the ground for 2,200 years until 1895. It is one of the only pictures of what initiates saw on the way in. (3-5)
8. **The crown pushed away (Roman army camp, c. AD 200).** In a dark cave, a soldier is offered a crown on the tip of a sword. He is taught to push it off his head and say that his god is his only crown. (9-10)
9. **Knights on the lakefront (Chicago, 1880).** Thousands of American Masonic "Knights Templar" in plumed hats camp in tents by Lake Michigan for their Triennial Conclave. They print their own daily souvenir newspaper. They are playing at being medieval knights. (3-5)
10. **The forgery in the national library (Paris, 27 April 1967).** Two men slip a typed "secret history" into the Bibliothèque nationale, hoping someone will find it. Fifteen years later it becomes the basis of a bestseller. Decades after that, one of them admits under oath that it was made up. (9-10)

## Illinois & Chicago

Illinois ties for the ancient and medieval groups are thin. Only confirmed items are listed.

- **1880: 21st Triennial Conclave, Grand Encampment of Knights Templar of the United States, Chicago.** Documented by the *Daily Conclave Souvenir* newspaper, a c. 1880 scrapbook, and the souvenir booklet of James G. Elwood of the Illinois commandery (Scottish Rite Masonic Museum & Library, P26). These are the modern Masonic Knights Templar, a Christian degree body within American Freemasonry. They have **no documented descent** from the medieval order. The lesson point is the difference between *inspired by* and *descended from*.
- **1910: Knights Templar Triennial Conclave, Chicago.** Newspaper coverage (e.g. *The Lamont Record*, Oklahoma, 11 Aug 1910, via the Gateway to Oklahoma History: https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc957229/m1/2/) describes a "Templar Way" from Randolph to Van Buren Street, lined with white columns and arches. That includes an arch at the La Salle Hotel topped with mounted knights 14 feet high. By the triennial sequence (21st in 1880) this was the 31st Triennial Conclave (source check: derived, confirm in the Grand Encampment proceedings); exact dates UNVERIFIED. The Chicago History Museum image server has related items (https://images.chicagohistory.org/asset/146401/), but I could not open it, so its content is UNVERIFIED.
- **University of Chicago:** Bill Thayer's LacusCurtius site (penelope.uchicago.edu) hosts public-domain Loeb translations of Polybius and other ancient authors used here. It is a hosting connection, not a historical one.
- Not found: any mithraic or Eleusinian object confirmed in an Illinois collection. A search of the Art Institute of Chicago for Mithras reliefs found nothing. Do not claim one.

## Hard-to-find finds

1. **Processus contra Templarios, copy no. 164, Law Library of Congress (P22).** One of only 799 numbered facsimiles of the full Templar trial dossier, with replica seals. It is in a U.S. public library, so a class could write to the Law Library for images. Most people think these papers are locked in the Vatican.
2. **Michelet's *Procès des Templiers*, free on Project Gutenberg (P20).** Hundreds of pages of word-for-word 14th-century testimony from 231 brothers, including farm managers and cooks. It is the Templars' own voices, rarely used in schools because it is in Latin. A teacher could pick two or three short depositions for translated excerpts.
3. **The Felicissimus mosaic's seven-grade ladder (P10).** It is a floor you can "read": a secret society's whole rank structure, in pictures, with no words needed. It is ideal for K-5 symbol work and is rarely shown outside specialist books.
4. **The 1880 Chicago *Daily Conclave Souvenir* and the Elwood calling-card booklet (P26).** Ephemera from a Chicago event, held in a specialist Masonic library. It gives the course a local, visual, 19th-century hook, and a clean way to teach "modern groups that borrow old names".
5. **The planted forgery of 1967 (P27), paired with the genuine 2001 Chinon find (P21).** One document in a national library was faked to be found. One document in a papal archive was real and overlooked. Side by side, they make the strongest source-evaluation lesson in the dossier.
6. **Dura-Europos Mithraeum at Yale (P9).** A complete secret temple, rebuilt indoors in the U.S., with a dated inscription from soldier-archers. It also has a 1930s photo of the excavators in front of it.
7. **Andocides, *On the Mysteries* (P3).** A real defense speech by a man accused of mocking a secret rite. It is one of the earliest surviving "secret society scandal" trials.

## Teacher notes on sensitive material (for course designers)

- **Bill Cooper.** Present him respectfully as a widely read author whose claims can be tested against sources. Students can compare his "mystery schools to modern elites" story with the documented, separate endings of each group here.
- **The *Protocols*.** The fact that early printings reproduced the *Protocols* is documented and must be named plainly as an antisemitic forgery (a British inquiry and a 1935 Bern trial found it plagiarized; check the details in the relevant dossier). It belongs to the 11-12 band and a separate hate-and-conspiracy unit, never quoted at length.
- **Bernard's "malicide" and crusading violence.** Grades 11-12, with context about the crusades and their victims.
- **Burnings and torture in the Templar trial.** For 6-8, keep to "arrested, questioned harshly, many later said their confessions were forced". Details belong in 9-12.
- **K-2 angle.** Use symbols (raven, lion, torch, wheat ear), a "clubhouse rule" book modeled on the Templar Rule, and the difference between a surprise-party secret (safe) and a secret someone asks you to keep that makes you feel bad (unsafe: tell a trusted grown-up). No violence, no trials.

## Open questions

1. What exactly happened in the Telesterion at Eleusis? Still unknown. The evidence is circumstantial (P1, P2, P6).
2. Did Mithraism come from Persia or was it invented in Rome? This is the live scholarly debate (Cumont vs Beck/Ulansey/Clauss).
3. When was the anti-Pythagorean violence? c. 509 BC vs c. 450 BC, or several waves.
4. Was there a real Templar reception rite involving a staged "denial" (Frale), or were the charges wholly invented (Barber's earlier view)?
