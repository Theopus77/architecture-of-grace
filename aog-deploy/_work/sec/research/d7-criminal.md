## Summary

1. The Sicilian Mafia grew up in western Sicily (Palermo and its lemon and orange groves) in the decades after Italian unification (1860). It sold "protection" where the new state was weak. Its members call it "Cosa Nostra" ("our thing").
2. Two early investigations still matter: Leopoldo Franchetti's private inquiry of 1876, published in 1877, which described an "industry of violence"; and police chief Ermanno Sangiorgi's report of 1898-1900, which mapped Mafia "families" (cosche) around Palermo and their initiation ritual.
3. Omertà, the code of silence, was the Mafia's shield. Every story here in which people won is a story of someone breaking that silence, or protecting those who did.
4. The "Black Hand" (1900-1915) was a method, not one organization: extortion letters signed with a black hand, sent mostly to Italian immigrants in New York, Chicago and other cities. NYPD Lieutenant Joseph Petrosino fought it and was shot dead in Palermo on 12 March 1909 (VERIFIED 2026-09-30, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Petrosino).
5. In Chicago, Prohibition (1920-1933) made the Torrio-Capone organization (later called "the Outfit") rich. The St. Valentine's Day Massacre (14 February 1929) was never solved in court. Capone fell in 1931 to federal income-tax charges, not murder charges.
6. The Kefauver Committee (1950-51) brought organized crime onto national television. Its claim that one nationwide "Mafia" ran all of it is still debated by historians.
7. The Apalachin, New York meeting (14 November 1957) and Joseph Valachi's televised testimony (1963) convinced the public and the FBI that a national Italian-American crime network existed.
8. In Palermo, the "anti-Mafia pool" of magistrates (Chinnici, Caponnetto, Falcone, Borsellino and others) built the Maxi Trial (1986-87): 475 indicted (460 still on trial at the verdict), 346 convicted and 114 acquitted. CORRECTED 2026-09-30: was "475 defendants, 344 convictions"; most Italian sources give 346 convicted / 114 acquitted, 19 life sentences, 2,665 years (some print 2,655; one gives 342, English Wikipedia 338) (https://www.ilsussidiario.net/news/maxiprocesso-di-palermo-cose-celebrato-tra-il-1986-e-il-1992-lotta-contro-cosa-nostra/2965299/amp/ ; https://www.geopop.it/maxiprocesso-palermo-mafia-quando-falcone-borsellino-duro-colpo-cosa-nostra/ ; l'Unità front page 17 Dec 1987, https://archivio.unita.news/assets/main/1987/12/17/page_001.pdf, seen only as a search result). Falcone and Borsellino were murdered in 1992. Their deaths set off a lasting citizens' anti-Mafia movement.
9. Chinese-American tongs began mostly as mutual-aid and brotherhood associations. Some ran gambling and fought "tong wars". Triads descend from the 18th-century Heaven and Earth Society (Tiandihui). Their story is not simply "gangsters".
10. Best teaching frame: these are secret societies whose secrecy protected harm. Compare them with the fraternal and religious orders in other dossiers, where secrecy protected belonging.

### Sicily

1. **Franchetti, Leopoldo. *Condizioni politiche e amministrative della Sicilia*.** Vol. 1 of Franchetti and Sonnino, *La Sicilia nel 1876*. Florence: G. Barbèra, 1877. Where: digitized copies are commonly found on Internet Archive and Google Books (search the full title), and on Italy's Biblioteca digitale; modern Italian reprints exist (Donzelli, 1993 and later). What it shows: a sharp outsider's analysis of violence as a business ("industria della violenza"), where landlords, estate managers (*gabellotti*) and armed guards (*campieri*) sold protection. It is the root of the "Mafia as a protection industry" thesis (Gambetta 1993). Band: **11-12** (excerpts in translation, 9-10). Status: VERIFIED 2026-09-30 (work, date and "industry of violence" idea: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_Franchetti); the Italian phrase's page STILL UNVERIFIED.
2. **Sonnino, Sidney. *I contadini in Sicilia*.** Vol. 2 of the same 1877 work. What it shows: the poverty of Sicilian peasants. It explains why protection rackets could take root, and why so many emigrated to America. Band: **9-10**. Status: STILL UNVERIFIED (checked 2026-09-30: not reached; shared search budget ran out and page fetches were blocked).
3. **Calà Ulloa, Pietro. Report of 3 August 1838 from Trapani to the Bourbon Minister of Justice.** Where: quoted in most Mafia histories (Lupo, *History of the Mafia*; Dickie, *Cosa Nostra*). ## Story hooks

Each is a true scene. Details marked in the ledger or timeline still need checking before printing.

1. **Palermo, the evening of 12 March 1909 (6-8).** A New York detective, Joseph Petrosino, waits alone near the garden in Piazza Marina. He has come secretly to gather criminal records to deport extortionists from America. His trip was leaked in a New York newspaper. Shots ring out. Back home, a city lines the streets for his funeral. *Question: why did Petrosino think records, not guns, were his best weapon?*
2. **A train near Palermo, 1 February 1893, and a son who would not stop (9-10).** Emanuele Notarbartolo, an honest banker who had tried to clean up the Bank of Sicily, is stabbed on a train. His son Leopoldo spends years in court, facing a powerful politician. *Theme: one family breaking omertà.*
3. **A dark office, Chicago, 1930-31 (6-8).** Treasury agent Frank Wilson searches old ledgers for proof of Capone's income. He finds bound ledgers from a gambling house; one page reads "Frank paid $17,500 for Al". CORRECTED 2026-09-30: was a ledger with "Frank" and "Snorky"; the "Frank paid $17,500 for Al" notation is reported from Wilson's work (https://www.grantthornton.co.uk/insights/the-accountant-and-al-capone/), not yet checked against Wilson's 1933 summary report (https://www.irs.gov/privacy-disclosure/historical-documents-relating-to-alphonse-al-capone-chicago). Accountants win where bullets failed. *Hook for a math and civics lesson.*
4. **A garage wall on Clark Street, and a microscope at Northwestern (6-8).** After 14 February 1929, Calvin Goddard compares bullet markings under a comparison microscope. Chicago gets a crime lab. *Keep the scene about the science, not the killings.*
5. **A trooper's hunch, Apalachin, New York, 14 November 1957 (6-8).** Sergeant Edgar Croswell notices expensive cars and a local motel filling up. He sets up a roadblock. Men in suits run into the woods. *Theme: an ordinary officer paying attention.*
6. **A bunker courtroom, Palermo, 10 February 1986 (9-10).** A green-painted, bomb-proof hall built next to the prison. Defendants sit in cages along the walls. Hundreds of names are read out. Witnesses speak because a few magistrates kept files, protected them, and refused to be frightened. *Theme: a state finally saying "we see you".*
7. **Sheets on the balconies, Palermo, summer 1992 (6-8).** After Falcone and Borsellino are killed, ordinary families hang white bedsheets from their windows with messages against the Mafia. Twelve years later, students put stickers on lampposts: a whole people who pays protection money is a people without dignity (English paraphrase; the Italian is "Un intero popolo che paga il pizzo è un popolo senza dignità", VERIFIED 2026-09-30, https://addiopizzo.org/ladesivo-addiopizzo/). *Theme: how neighbours break silence together.*
8. **A priest who played football with the kids, Brancaccio, 1993 (6-8).** Father Pino Puglisi opens a centre so children in a Mafia-run neighbourhood have somewhere else to go. He is killed on his 56th birthday. The Church later beatifies him. *Theme: offering young people a different belonging.*
9. **A new Chinatown, Chicago, 1912-1928 (6-8).** Chinese merchants move south from the Loop to Cermak and Wentworth. The On Leong Merchants Association builds a green-and-red-tiled "Chinese City Hall" as a meeting place, school and shelter. *Theme: a "tong" as community, and a building you can visit.*
10. **The White Hand, Chicago, 1907-08 (6-8).** Italian-American doctors, lawyers and businessmen form a society to fight the Black Hand: they offer rewards and press police to act. *Theme: a community standing up for itself against both criminals and prejudice.* (Founders' names UNVERIFIED.)

## Hard-to-find finds

1. **The Sangiorgi report (1898-1900), published by Salvatore Lupo, 2011.** Lost in the state archives for a century. It proves the Mafia's structure, families and initiation existed 85 years before Buscetta. Almost no English-language school material uses it. Pair it with Dickie's English summary.
2. **Schlegel, *Thian Ti Hwui* (Batavia, 1866).** Translations of seized ritual books, poems and certificates of the Hung League. It is a real "secret document seized by the state", excellent for grades 9-12 on how outsiders learn secrets (and misread them).
3. **Franchetti, 1877.** A 150-year-old outsider who explained the Mafia as a market for violence. Its "industry of violence" idea still frames the best modern scholarship. Few US teachers know it exists.
4. **The Kefauver Chicago hearings volume (1950-51).** Sworn testimony from Chicago police officers and officials, including the "richest cop" episode, in a government document most people never open. Also a media-literacy source: the first TV hearings.
5. **IRS and NARA Chicago Capone case files, and Irey's *The Tax Dodgers* (1948).** They replace the Hollywood "Untouchables" myth with the true story: accountants, ledgers and a tax ruling from 1927.
6. **W. P. Morgan, *Triad Societies in Hong Kong* (1960).** A Hong Kong Government Press police study: rare, and full of the numbers, ranks and hand signs that lessons on codes can use without glamour.
7. **The Lo Piccolo "decalogue" (2007).** A written rulebook of a modern secret criminal society (reports describe it as typed, and some experts doubted it; https://spokesman.com/stories/2007/nov/11/mafia-code-of-conduct-found). CORRECTED 2026-09-30: was "handwritten". It makes the "secret rules" concrete and shows they still exist. Verify the exact text before use.
8. **Chinese-language community archives (Him Mark Lai collection, Berkeley; CHSA; MOCA).** They show tongs and associations as members saw them: welfare, defence against exclusion laws, burial of the dead. This gives the "members' view" the brief asks for.
