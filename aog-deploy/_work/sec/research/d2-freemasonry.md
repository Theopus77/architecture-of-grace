## Summary

1. Freemasonry grew out of the working stonemasons of medieval Britain. Their "Old Charges" were rules and legends of the craft; the oldest is the Regius Poem (British Library Royal MS 17 A I, c. 1390).
2. The first real lodge records come from Scotland. William Schaw, the king's Master of Works, issued statutes in 1598 and 1599 that told lodges to keep written records. Lodge minutes survive from January 1599.
3. Gentlemen who never cut stone began to join. Elias Ashmole's diary (16 October 1646) is the earliest clear English record of one.
4. A "Grand Lodge" formed in London. The traditional date is 24 June 1717, but that date rests on James Anderson's 1738 account; historians Prescott and Sommers (2017) argue the Grand Lodge really began around 1721.
5. Anderson's *Constitutions* (1723) set the rules. Benjamin Franklin reprinted it in Philadelphia in 1734, the first Masonic book printed in America.
6. Secrecy invited outsiders to publish "exposés". Samuel Prichard's *Masonry Dissected* (London, 1730) was a best-seller.
7. The Catholic Church first condemned Freemasonry in Clement XII's bull *In eminenti* (28 April 1738).
8. Some founders were Masons (Washington, Franklin, Hancock); others were not (John Adams said so in writing in 1798; there is no record of Jefferson joining). Prince Hall and fourteen other free Black men were made Masons in Boston in 1775.
9. In 1826 William Morgan of Batavia, New York, vanished after planning an exposé. The scandal produced the Anti-Masonic Party, which held the first national presidential nominating convention (Baltimore, 26 to 28 September 1831).
10. The eye over the pyramid on the dollar comes from the Great Seal design process (1776 to 1782). The eye was suggested by a non-Mason, Pierre Du Simitière. The claim that the Seal is a "Masonic emblem" is not supported by the design record.

### A. The working masons (1390 to 1700)

**1. The Regius Poem ("Halliwell Manuscript").** Anonymous, *Hic incipiunt constituciones artis gemetriae secundum Euclydem* ("Here begin the constitutions of the art of geometry according to Euclid"), vellum, c. 1390. British Library, **Royal MS 17 A I**. 33 folios, about 5 x 3.5 inches.
- Catalogue: https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002107232 (URL confirmed by search; the record returned was for this manuscript).
- Transcription with modern English: https://phoenixmasonry.org/regius_poem.htm (URL confirmed by search).
- Shows: a craft rulebook in rhyme. Honest work, fair pay, keeping the master's counsel, good manners at church and at table. Came into the British Museum with the Royal Library in 1757. It sat in the catalogue as a poem of "moral duties" until James O. Halliwell, who was not a Mason, spotted its subject in 1838-39 and published it in 1840 (*The Early History of Freemasonry in England*, 2nd ed. 1844).
- Short quotation (Middle English): "Fyftene artyculus þey þer sow3ton, and fyftene poyntys þer þey wro3ton" ("Fifteen articles they there sought, and fifteen points there they wrought"). QUOTE CHECK against the BL images or Halliwell 1840. STILL UNVERIFIED (checked 2026-09-30: archive hosts blocked; no transcription seen). Date c. 1390 and shelfmark Royal MS 17 A I VERIFIED 2026-09-30 (https://phoenixmasonry.org/regius_poem.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonic_manuscripts); note one source gives the size as about 4 x 5.5 inches.
- Band: **3-5** (a medieval rulebook for builders; "rules for being a good worker and neighbor").

**2. The Cooke Manuscript.** Old Charges in prose, c. 1450. British Library **Add MS 23198**. Sold to the British Museum on 14 October 1859 by Mrs Caroline Baker for £4 (recorded in Sir Frederic Madden's diary). First printed by Matthew Cooke, 1861; facsimile by Quatuor Coronati Lodge No. 2076, 1890.
- Catalogue: https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-002097175 (URL confirmed by search; BL notes full digital coverage, images at times unavailable).
- Transcript: https://phoenixmasonry.org/description_and_transcript_of_cooke_ms.htm
- Shows: the legend history masons told about themselves (geometry, Euclid, Solomon's temple). Good for "stories groups tell about their own past".
- Band: **6-8**.

**3. First Schaw Statutes, 28 December 1598.** Signed by William Schaw (1549-1602), Master of Works to James VI. Original in the records of the Lodge of Edinburgh (Mary's Chapel) No. 1.
- Discussion and text: https://www.thesquaremagazine.com/mag/?p=23624 (URL confirmed by search). No public scan was found. **UNVERIFIED** whether a digitized image exists online.
- Shows: safety rules, fines for bad work, and the order to keep written records. This is why lodge minutes begin in 1599.
- Band: **3-5** (workplace rules and safety).

**4. Second Schaw Statutes, 28 December 1599.** Addressed partly to the Lodge of Kilwinning.
- Text and article: https://www.thesquaremagazine.com/mag/article/202204the-second-william-schaw-statutes-1599/ (URL confirmed by search).
- Short quotation (Scots): the warden of the lodge of Kilwinning is to "tak tryall of ye airt of memorie and science yrof, of everie fellowe of craft and everie prenteiss" (the text continues "according ayr of yr vocations"). VERIFIED 2026-09-30 (https://www.thesquaremagazine.com/mag/article/202204the-second-william-schaw-statutes-1599/ ; transcription, not a scan).
- Shows: masons were tested on a memory skill. Historian David Stevenson (*The Origins of Freemasonry: Scotland's Century, 1590-1710*, Cambridge UP, 1988) links this to Renaissance "memory palace" methods. That link is an interpretation (DISPUTED in degree, widely cited).
- Band: **6-8** (memory palaces are also a fine 3-5 activity).

**5. Aitchison's Haven lodge minute, 9 January 1599.** Grand Lodge of Scotland collection. Records Robert Widderspone made a Fellow of Craft. Oldest surviving lodge minute. The lodge closed in 1852.
- Article: https://Freemasonry.bcy.ca/aqc/aitchison-lodge.pdf (URL confirmed by search).
- Band: **6-8**.

**6. Elias Ashmole, diary entry, 16 October 1646.** Bodleian Library, Ashmole manuscripts. Standard edition: C. H. Josten, ed., *Elias Ashmole (1617-1692): His Autobiographical and Historical Notes*, 5 vols, Oxford, 1966. **UNVERIFIED**: exact Bodleian shelfmark and page in Josten.
- Quotation: "I was made a Free Mason at Warrington in Lancashire, with Coll: Henry Mainwaring of Karincham in Cheshire." The entry then lists those present (Richard Penket, Warden; James Collier; Richard Sankey; Henry Littler; John Ellam; Richard Ellam; Hugh Brewer). VERIFIED 2026-09-30 against the 1881 *Masonic Magazine* reprint and Wikipedia "Elias Ashmole" (search-result text; "Karincham" is glossed [Kermincham]); still compare with Josten before printing.
- Reprinted in the *Masonic Magazine*, 1 December 1881: https://masonicperiodicals.org/periodicals/mmg/issues/mmg_01121881/page/1/ (URL confirmed by search).
- Shows: a scholar, not a builder, joining a lodge. Ashmole later founded the Ashmolean Museum. His diary mentions Masonry once more, in 1682.
- Band: **6-8**.

**7. Edinburgh Register House Manuscript, 1696.** National Records of Scotland (found in Register House). Titled "Some Questions that Masons use to put to those who profess to have the Mason Word before they will acknowledge them".
- Article: https://www.thesquaremagazine.com/mag/article/202206the-edinburgh-register-house-manuscript/ (URL confirmed by search). **UNVERIFIED**: NRS reference number.
- Shows: the earliest known question-and-answer test and admission ceremony. Proof that the "Mason Word" was a real password for proving you were a trained mason.
- Band: **6-8** (the password idea itself works for **K-2**: see Story hooks).

### B. Grand Lodge, rules and exposés (1717 to 1738)

**8. James Anderson, *The Constitutions of the Free-Masons*, London, 1723.** Copy and exhibit: Library and Museum of Freemasonry, London: https://museumfreemasonry.org.uk/node/1367 (URL confirmed by search). Transcription of the Charges: https://www.1723constitutions.com/1723-constitutions/the-charges/ (URL confirmed by search).
- Quotation, First Charge "Concerning GOD and RELIGION": "A Mason is oblig'd by his Tenure, to obey the moral Law; and if he rightly understands the Art, he will never be a stupid Atheist nor an irreligious Libertine." QUOTE CHECK spelling against a facsimile.
- Shows: the new club opened itself to men of different Christian churches ("that Religion in which all Men agree"), which was unusual in 1723. Also rules against talking politics in lodge.
- Band: **9-10**.

**9. Benjamin Franklin's 1734 Philadelphia reprint of Anderson's *Constitutions*.** First Masonic book printed in America.
- Free e-edition: University of Nebraska-Lincoln, Zea E-Books in American Studies: https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/zeaamericanstudies/27 (URL confirmed by search).
- Catalogue: Historical Society of Pennsylvania: https://discover.hsp.org/Record/marc-231856/Cite

- Band: **6-8**.

**10. Samuel Prichard, *Masonry Dissected: being a universal and genuine description of all its branches, from the original to this present time*, London: J. Wilford, 1730.**
- Folger Shakespeare Library catalogue: https://catalog.folger.edu/record/888374 ; Wellcome Collection digitized editions: https://wellcomecollection.org/works/dke2r5ra and https://wellcomecollection.org/works/cwx3pvrk (URLs confirmed by search; **UNVERIFIED** which edition each Wellcome record is).
- Shows: the first exposé to print all three degrees. It sold so well it went through many editions (a 14th edition is catalogued). Historians note that some lodges may have used it to learn the ritual.

**11. Graham Manuscript, 1726.** Library and Museum of Freemasonry, London. A catechism that tells a legend of Noah's sons, not the later Hiram story.

- Band: **9-10**.

**12. Pope Clement XII, *In eminenti apostolatus specula*, Rome, 28 April 1738.** Latin original, Vatican archives.
- Latin/French text: https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/In_eminenti_apostolatus_specula ; Spanish text and museum page: UNED Virtual Museum of the History of Freemasonry, https://www.uned.es/universidad/inicio/en/unidad/museo-virtual-historia-masoneria/sala-ix-religion-y-masoneria/bula-in-eminenti-contra-la-francmasoneria.html (URLs confirmed by search).

## Story hooks

1. **The password at the building site (K-2, 3-5).** Scotland, around 1600. A mason walks to a new job far from home. How do the men there know he has trained for seven years? He whispers the "Mason Word". The 1696 Edinburgh Register House manuscript shows the questions they asked. Lesson: a password that proves you belong, and the difference between a *surprise* secret (a password, a birthday present) and an *unsafe* secret (one that hides someone getting hurt).
2. **The poem in the wrong drawer (3-5).** London, 1838. A young scholar, James Halliwell, is paging through a little vellum book the library calls "a poem of moral duties." He realizes it is a rulebook for medieval masons, the oldest ever found. It had been in the King's library for centuries. Lesson: librarians and detectives; how things get lost in plain sight.
3. **The memory test at Kilwinning (3-5, 6-8).** December 1599. William Schaw, the king's builder, orders the lodge of Kilwinning to test every apprentice on "the art of memory". Lesson: build your own memory palace of the classroom.
4. **Ashmole's diary, Warrington, 16 October 1646 (6-8).** In the middle of a civil war, a royalist officer and scholar writes one line: he has been "made a Free Mason". He lists the seven men in the room. Lesson: how one diary sentence becomes historical proof.
5. **Twenty-year-old George in Fredericksburg (3-5).** 4 November 1752. The lodge treasurer writes in the ledger that "Mr. George Washington" joined and paid two pounds three shillings. Forty-six years later, the old general writes that he has hardly been inside a lodge in thirty years. Lesson: what a club meant to him young and old.
6. **Prince Hall's long wait for a letter (6-8).** Boston, March 1775. Prince Hall and fourteen other free Black men are made Masons in a British army lodge. When the army leaves, they have no charter. Hall writes to London. The charter is issued in 1784 and finally arrives by ship in 1787. Lesson: persistence, and building your own institutions when doors are shut.
7. **The man who never came home (6-8).** Batavia, New York, September 1826. William Morgan, a bricklayer, plans to print Masonic secrets. He is arrested, taken to the jail in Canandaigua over a small debt, let out at night, pushed into a carriage, and driven toward Fort Niagara. He is never seen again. Lesson: rumor, evidence and how a mystery can change a nation's politics.
8. **"A good enough Morgan" (9-10).** October 1827. A body washes up at Oak Orchard Creek. Anti-Masons say it is Morgan. A widow identifies her husband, Timothy Munroe. Editor Thurlow Weed is accused of using the body for politics. Two versions of his reply survive. Lesson: comparing sources.
9. **The first convention (6-8 civics).** Baltimore, September 1831. About 110 delegates from 13 states meet to choose a president. (CORRECTED 2026-09-30: was "111 delegates"; counts vary by source.) It is the first national nominating convention in U.S. history, and the party is built on opposing a secret society. Their nominee turns out to be a former Mason.
10. **The eye on your dollar (3-5).** Philadelphia, August 1776. Franklin, Adams and Jefferson ask an artist for a seal. The artist, not a Mason, draws an eye in a triangle. Lesson: design a seal for the class; then check the claim "Masons put the eye on the dollar" with evidence.

## Illinois & Chicago

Only items confirmed by search are listed.

- **First Grand Lodge of Illinois.** Convention at Vandalia, 9 December 1822; Grand Lodge opened 1 December 1823 with Shadrach Bond (the first governor of Illinois) as Grand Master. It stopped meeting around 1827 as the Anti-Masonic wave spread. Status: DOCUMENTED per Grand Lodge histories (e.g. https://ilmason.org/illinois-freemasonry ; https://www.universalfreemasonry.org/en/encyclopedia/illinois). **UNVERIFIED**: original minute books.
- **Re-founding, Jacksonville, 6 April 1840.** Six of eight chartered lodges present. This is today's Grand Lodge of Illinois. Abraham Jonas of Quincy was an early Grand Master ("father of Freemasonry in Illinois": https://www.hsqac.org/?p=2109).
- **Nauvoo, March 1842.** Jonas installed the Nauvoo Lodge and made Joseph Smith a Mason "at sight" (Joseph Smith Papers minutes). Nauvoo Masonry grew fast and soon clashed with the Grand Lodge; the details are complex and belong in 9-12.
- **Chicago's first lodges.** Masonry reached Chicago around 1843 (per WTTW "Ask Geoffrey", https://news.wttw.com/2017/08/09/ask-geoffrey-history-freemasonry-chicago). By 1846 three lodges (Lafayette No. 18, Apollo No. 32, Oriental No. 33) issued a joint circular letter. Oriental Lodge No. 33 still meets.
- **Masonic Temple, Chicago (1892).** Burnham and Root; State and Randolph; 21 stories, 302 feet; tallest building in Chicago 1895 to 1899; Masonic meeting rooms at the top; demolished 1939 (https://www.preservationchicago.org/masonic-temple-by-burnham-root-built-1892-demolished-1939). Good **3-5** hook: "a skyscraper with a secret club on top."
- **Illinois as the center of anti-secret-society activism.** Jonathan Blanchard, first president of Wheaton College, led the National Christian Association (founded 1868), whose paper *Christian Cynosure* was published in Chicago. The NCA paid for the 1882 Morgan Monument in Batavia, NY. Wheaton College Archives hold Blanchard's papers. This is a strong, little-known Illinois link to the Morgan story.
- **Lincoln.** Not a Mason. The Tyrian Lodge petition story is unconfirmed (see Claims ledger).

## Hard-to-find finds

1. **Joseph Smith Papers, Nauvoo Lodge minutes, 15 to 16 March 1842.** A digitized Illinois lodge record that most Masonic histories only summarize. It lets students see a Grand Master from Quincy making a religious founder a Mason in two days.
2. **Wheaton College Archives: Jonathan Blanchard Papers and the *Christian Cynosure*.** Puts Illinois at the center of the long anti-Masonic tradition that runs from Batavia in 1826 to the monument of 1882. A local archive a Chicago-area class could visit.
3. **John Adams's letter of 22 June 1798 (Founders Online).** A founder saying in his own hand that he was "never … initiated". One document settles a common myth. It pairs well with Washington's 1798 "once or twice" letter.
4. **The 20 August 1776 Great Seal committee report (Jefferson Papers) plus Hunt's 1909 State Department history.** Together they let students trace the eye from a non-Mason artist's sketch to the 1935 dollar, and then read Wallace's own words about seeing it as Masonic. Myth-busting built on primary sources.
5. **Aitchison's Haven minute (9 January 1599) and the Second Schaw Statutes' "airt of memorie".** The very first written lodge minute and the first hint of something beyond building skill. Rarely shown in schools.
6. **Proceedings of the 1831 Anti-Masonic Convention (Boston, 1832).** The founding document of the American party convention, created by a movement against a secret society.
7. **The Cooke Manuscript's sale slip: £4 on 14 October 1859**, noted in the Keeper of Manuscripts' diary. A small, human detail about how treasures reach museums.
8. **The Morgan Monument inscription (1882)**: a public stone stating as fact a murder no court proved. A ready-made media-literacy exercise.

## Open questions

- What exactly happened to William Morgan? Drowned in the Niagara River, paid to leave, or something else? The evidence does not settle it.
- Is 1717 or 1721 the right founding date for the first Grand Lodge? Masonic bodies and some historians still defend 1717.
- How direct is the line from working stonemasons' lodges to gentlemen's lodges: continuous growth (the Scottish evidence) or a new club borrowing old legends (the English evidence)?
- Did the eye of Providence appear in Masonic art before 1782? A survey of dated 18th-century aprons and certificates (Library and Museum of Freemasonry London; Scottish Rite Masonic Museum and Library, Lexington) would answer it.
- Exact wording to verify from scans before publishing: Ashmole (Josten ed.), Washington ledger, Anderson First Charge, Adams 1798, Washington 1798, Morgan Monument inscription, Weed's reply (Weed's *Autobiography*, 1883).
- Is there an online scan of the first Schaw Statutes, the Edinburgh Register House MS, or the Prince Hall warrant?
- How many signers of the Declaration were Masons? Counts vary; a teacher should cite a named source (Grand Lodge of Pennsylvania: 9 proven) rather than a round number.
- Which issues of the *Anti-Masonic Enquirer* and Illinois newspapers of 1827-1840 are digitized (Chronicling America, Illinois Digital Newspaper Collections)? Could not be checked from this environment.

### Grade-band notes for the course team

- **K-2:** passwords and secret handshakes as belonging; the square and compass as real builders' tools; clubhouses; surprise secrets vs. unsafe secrets (always tell a trusted adult about a secret that makes you feel scared or hurt). Do not show ritual texts, oaths or the Morgan story.
- **3-5:** medieval masons and cathedrals; the Regius "rules for good workers"; memory palaces; young Washington; the Great Seal design story; the Chicago Masonic Temple skyscraper.
- **6-8:** Ashmole; Franklin's printing; Prince Hall; Adams's letter; Morgan's disappearance (without graphic detail); the first convention; checking the dollar-bill myth.
- **9-10 / 11-12:** exposés and oaths; the papal ban; the Illuminati panic of 1798; comparing Weed's two quotes; the 1717 vs. 1721 debate; Nauvoo Masonry.
