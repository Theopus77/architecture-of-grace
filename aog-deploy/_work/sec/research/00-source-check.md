## Read this first: how far this check could go

**No live web check was possible in this pass.** The session's WebSearch budget was already used up (200 of 200 calls) before this check began. Every WebFetch was refused by the network egress proxy. Blocked hosts included blogs.loc.gov, en.wikipedia.org, founders.archives.gov, law.cornell.edu, aclanthology.org, artic.edu, gao.gov, fordham.edu, hmdb.org, newadvent.org, courtlistener.com, britannica.com and nps.gov. There were no other copies of these sources in the repository.

So each item below was tested against the standard reference record as the checker knows it: court reporters, statute citations, museum numbers, and the main scholarly histories. It was **not** tested against a page opened today. The verdicts mean:

- **CONFIRMED\***: matches the standard record, and the checker is confident of it. The asterisk means no live page was opened. It is strong enough to keep. It is not strong enough to quote from without opening the source.
- **WRONG**: the checker is confident the dossier is in error. The dossier file has been corrected, and the correction is marked "source check" in the file.
- **CANNOT CONFIRM**: the checker cannot settle it from the record. The item is now marked UNVERIFIED in the dossier file (or already was).

**Standing recommendation:** before any lesson is published, run a second pass with open web access. It should open every URL in D1-D6 and every citation in D7-D9. D7, D8 and D9 were written with no web access at all.

## 2. Gaps for a 17-unit course (brotherhoods and orders, plus criminal and hate secret orders)

What exists covers about 9 units' worth: ancient mysteries and Templars, Freemasonry, American fraternal orders, the Illuminati and esoteric orders, codes, world initiation societies, criminal orders, hate orders, and Cooper and conspiracy. The gaps below are ranked by importance for filling 17 units.

### A. Missing groups (strongest candidates for new units)

1. **Rome's crackdown on the Bacchanalia, 186 BC.** Livy 39.8-19, plus the surviving bronze *Senatus consultum de Bacchanalibus* (Kunsthistorisches Museum, Vienna). It is the oldest state document against a secret religious society, and a real, hard-to-find artifact. It pairs with D1.
2. **Isis mysteries: Apuleius, *Metamorphoses* Book 11.** It is the only first-person initiation account from antiquity. It is the natural partner to Eleusis and Mithras and is missing from D1.
3. **The Vehmic courts (Feme), medieval Westphalia.** Secret tribunals with oaths, signs and death sentences. They are a "secret justice" order between brotherhood and crime.
4. **The Nizari Ismailis ("Assassins").** Real order versus legend (Marco Polo; the "hashish" etymology). Farhad Daftary, *The Assassin Legends* (1994). It is the best non-European case of myth versus record, and parallels the Templar unit.
5. **Other military-religious orders:** the Hospitallers and Teutonic Knights. Only the Templars are covered, yet *Ad providam* hands the property to the Hospitallers, who still exist as the Order of Malta.
6. **Propaganda Due (P2), Italy, 1981.** A secret Masonic lodge tied to politics, banking and crime. Sources are the Anselmi Commission report (1984) and Italy's Law 17/1982 banning secret associations. It bridges the Freemasonry and criminal units.
7. **The Molly Maguires (Pennsylvania anthracite, 1870s).** A secret Irish brotherhood, a Pinkerton infiltrator (James McParland), and 20 hangings. Whether it was a real order or a company-made story is a live debate (Kenny, *Making Sense of the Molly Maguires*, 1998).
8. **Other Reconstruction and 20th-century hate orders:** the Knights of the White Camelia and the White League (1860s-70s); the **Black Legion** (Michigan and Ohio, 1930s; the 1936 Poole murder trial); the Silver Legion; and, for 11-12 only, The Order / Brüder Schweigen (1983-84). D8 names the White League only in passing.
9. **The Orange Order (Loyal Orange Institution, 1795).** A lodge brotherhood tied to sectarian violence, with an Irish Catholic counterpart in the Ribbonmen and the Ancient Order of Hibernians. It fills "brotherhood with a dark side" between D2 and D8.
10. **Other criminal secret orders:** the Camorra and 'Ndrangheta (the latter's rituals were seized in documented police finds); the **Yakuza**; Russia's *vory v zakone* (thieves-in-law) and their tattoo code, a good fit for the codes unit; and **Thuggee** as a case of a colonial construction (Sleeman, *Ramaseeana*, 1836, against Kim Wagner, *Thuggee*, 2007). D7 is only Italian and Chinese.
11. **Human leopard societies of Sierra Leone and Liberia.** The colonial Human Leopard Society Ordinance (1895) and K. J. Beatty, *Human Leopards* (1915). This is the needed counterweight in D6: it separates Poro and Sande from the criminal "leopard" killings, and shows how colonial law blurred them.
12. **Native American societies, with a respect protocol:** the Midewiwin (Ojibwe), handled like Poro in D6. Its contrast is the **Improved Order of Red Men**, white men "playing Indian" in a fraternal order (Philip Deloria, *Playing Indian*, 1998). D3 mentions the Red Men only in a badge catalogue.
13. **The Thule Society and Nazi persecution of Freemasons.** Nazi anti-Masonic exhibitions, and Masons sent to camps (USHMM holdings). The reverse of the conspiracy story: a secret-society myth used by a regime to persecute. It is missing entirely.
14. **Knights of Labor (secret until 1881) and Chicago.** A labor brotherhood with ritual, which gives a strong Illinois tie (Haymarket, 1886). Include it if labor orders count as "brotherhoods" rather than resistance networks. Flag this for the course author's decision.

### B. Missing documents and angles

1. **The law of secret membership (civics, 9-12).** *NAACP v. Alabama ex rel. Patterson*, 357 U.S. 449 (1958), protected a membership list; *Bryant v. Zimmerman*, 278 U.S. 63 (1928), upheld New York's law forcing the Klan to file its lists; state anti-mask laws of the 1920s-50s; the UK Unlawful Societies Act 1799 and its Masonic exemption. This is the "when should secrecy be protected?" unit the course currently lacks.
2. **Later Catholic documents:** Leo XIII, *Humanum genus* (1884), and the 1983 CDF declaration on Masonic associations. D2 stops at 1738.
3. **Roman law on associations (*collegia*)** and Pliny, *Letters* 10.96-97. The state's fear of secret clubs, applied to early Christians. Pairs with D1.
4. **A methods unit on how to test a secret-society claim**, pulled together from D1, D4, D8 and D9. The dossiers each teach provenance piecemeal; a 17-unit course needs one explicit unit.
5. **K-2 and 3-5 coverage for units 6-12-only.** D7, D8 and D9 give nothing below grade 6. If all 17 units must carry K-12 bands (the "same K-12 format"), each needs a K-5 bridge. Examples: "helpers who stood up to bullies" (the White Hand Society, Ida B. Wells as a reporter) and "is this document real?" games. Plan these now.
6. **Chicago tongs.** The On Leong and Hip Sing rivalry in Chicago is UNVERIFIED in D7, as is Hongmen/Chee Kung Tong history in Chicago (D6). A local archive search (Chinese American Museum of Chicago) is needed.
7. **Illinois pigpen gravestones, and a Chicago Golden Dawn temple** (D4, D5). Both are open. Either find them or drop them.
8. **Quotations.** Almost none of the quotations in D1-D9 have been checked word for word against a scan, and this pass could not do so either. Do a quotation-only pass with web access before any lesson text is written.
