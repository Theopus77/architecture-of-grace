# Dossier D4: The Bavarian Illuminati, the Illuminati Legend, and the Esoteric Orders (Rosicrucians, Golden Dawn)

Prepared for the Architecture of Grace K-12 course "Secret Societies." Research date: 2026-09-30.

**How to read this dossier.** Every claim carries a status:

- **DOCUMENTED**: primary evidence exists and is cited.
- **SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS**: historians agree, based on primary evidence.
- **DISPUTED**: serious people disagree; both sides named.
- **UNSUPPORTED / DISPROVEN**: no evidence, or the evidence shows otherwise.
- **UNVERIFIED**: I could not confirm it in this session. Check before it goes in a lesson.

Quotations are marked **"verify against image"** where I saw the text only as a transcription in search results. ## Summary

1. The Order of the Illuminati was real. Adam Weishaupt, a law professor at the University of Ingolstadt in Bavaria, founded it on 1 May 1776 (SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS; the date comes from the order's own papers).
2. Its first and, for years, only grade was the "Minerval," named for Minerva, goddess of wisdom. Members took classical code names (Weishaupt was "Spartacus"; Zwack was "Cato"; Knigge was "Philo").
3. Its aim, in its own papers, was moral and intellectual improvement of an elite and quiet influence over appointments, against church control of education. Critics read the same papers as a plot against church and throne.
4. Baron Adolph Knigge joined in 1780, wrote most of the higher degrees, grew the order into the hundreds, and left on 1 July 1784 after quarrels with Weishaupt.
5. Elector Karl Theodor of Bavaria banned unauthorized secret societies on 22 June 1784, then named Freemasons and Illuminati specifically on 2 March 1785. Weishaupt lost his post and fled, first to Regensburg and later to Gotha.
6. Police searched Franz Xaver von Zwack's house in Landshut on 11-12 October 1786. The government printed the seized papers in 1787 as *Einige Originalschriften des Illuminatenordens*, with a supplement (*Nachtrag*). Printing a secret group's papers to discredit it was a new public-relations move.
7. The Bavarian order was finished by about 1785-1787 (SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS). No documentary evidence shows it continued.
8. After the French Revolution, Abbé Augustin Barruel (*Memoirs*, 1797-98) and Professor John Robison of Edinburgh (*Proofs of a Conspiracy*, 1797) blamed the Revolution partly on the Illuminati. Their books crossed the Atlantic and set off the 1798-1800 New England "Illuminati scare" (Jedidiah Morse, Timothy Dwight), which even reached George Washington and Thomas Jefferson.
9. The modern pop-culture "Illuminati" was largely built in the 1960s-70s by Discordian pranksters (Greg Hill, Kerry Thornley) and by Robert Anton Wilson and Robert Shea, who worked in **Chicago** at *Playboy*. Their *Illuminatus!* trilogy (1975) was satire; many readers took it straight.
10. The Rosicrucian manifestos (*Fama* 1614, *Confessio* 1615, *Chymical Wedding* 1616) described a secret brotherhood that probably never existed as an organization; the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn (London, 1888) was a real magical order whose founding documents are themselves a historical puzzle.

### A. The Bavarian Illuminati from the inside

**1. *Einige Originalschriften des Illuminatenordens, welche bey dem gewesenen Regierungsrath Zwack durch vorgenommene Hausvisitation zu Landshut den 11. und 12. Oktob. etc. 1786 vorgefunden worden. Auf Höchsten Befehl Seiner Churfürstlichen Durchleucht zum Druck befördert.*** München, 1787.
- Where: Bayerische Staatsbibliothek (BSB), digitized; also listed on Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek. A related BSB item, *Bemerkungen über einige Originalschriften des Illuminaten-Ordens*, carries URN urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb10381766-7.
- URLs: https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/2JQCYT6D6HH3SG3PTWLJ7ZNENVLZC5P4?lang=en ; modern reprint record: https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783112529249/html ; an English working translation with page images is posted at https://ficino.sourcelibrary.org/book/original-writings-of-the-order-of-the-illuminati-zwack (a new, non-scholarly site: use only as a reading aid, cite the BSB original).
- Shows: the order's statutes, the Minerval rules, code-name lists, the Persian calendar the order used, Weishaupt's letters. It is the single most important document: the real Illuminati in their own words, published by their enemy.
- Band: 9-10 (in translated excerpts); the *idea* of a government publishing a club's secret papers works for 6-8.

**2. *Nachtrag von weitern Originalschriften, welche die Illuminatensekte überhaupt, sonderbar aber den Stifter derselben Adam Weishaupt ... betreffen*** München, 1787 (two parts).
- Where: BSB and other German libraries; listed in UPenn Online Books "Illuminatenorden" author page: https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Illuminatenorden
- Shows: further seized letters, including the most damaging personal material used to discredit Weishaupt.
- Band: 11-12 (contains adult personal matter; teacher summary only).

**3. Adolph Freiherr Knigge, *Philo's endliche Antwort auf verschiedene Anforderungen und Fragen, die an ihn ergangen, seine Verbindung mit dem Orden der Illuminaten betreffend*** Hannover, 1788.
- Where: German digital libraries (search BSB / Göttingen SUB / Google Books by title). URL: UNVERIFIED in this session.
- Shows: an insider explains why he joined and why he quit. Rare chance to hear a founder-level member's side.
- Band: 9-10.

**4. The "Schwedenkiste" (Swedish Box): the Illuminati archive of Johann Joachim Christoph Bode and Duke Ernst II of Gotha.**
- Where: Geheimes Staatsarchiv Preußischer Kulturbesitz (Berlin; formerly held at Merseburg). Organized into 20 volumes; seized by the Nazis 1935-36, taken by Soviet forces after 1945, mostly returned in the 1950s; volume 10 reportedly remains in Moscow.
- Research access: FactGrid database item "Schwedenkiste, 20 Volumes": https://database.factgrid.de/wiki/Item:Q2030 ; Gotha Research Centre (University of Erfurt), Arbeitsstelle Illuminatenforschung: https://www.uni-erfurt.de/forschungszentrum-gotha/forschung/arbeitsstellen-und-netzwerke/arbeitsstelle-illuminatenforschung ; DFG project "Illuminati Essays in the Context of the Late Enlightenment: An Unknown Source Corpus" (Markus Meumann, Olaf Simons), about 150 unprinted member essays from 1783-1787: https://gepris.dfg.de/gepris/projekt/222346431
- Shows: what members actually wrote when asked to think about ethics, economics and reform. It undercuts the "master plot" picture: much of it reads like homework.
- Band: 9-10 (as a story of an archive's journey, 6-8).

**5. "Satirical Image about the Illuminati and their Propaganda Efforts (c. 1785/86)."**
- Where: German History Intersections (German Historical Institute Washington), image 56: https://germanhistory-intersections.org/en/knowledge-and-education/ghis:image-56
- Shows: a contemporary cartoon from the years of the ban. Details of artist and holding library: UNVERIFIED (page blocked in this session; open it to confirm).

### B. The counter-revolutionary conspiracy books

**6. John Robison, *Proofs of a Conspiracy against all the Religions and Governments of Europe, carried on in the secret meetings of Free Masons, Illuminati, and Reading Societies. Collected from good authorities.*** Edinburgh: William Creech; London: T. Cadell jun. and W. Davies, 1797. American editions Philadelphia and New York, 1798.
- Where: Wellcome Collection (https://wellcomecollection.org/works/g422h5nu); Internet Archive PDF (https://ia600905.us.archive.org/0/items/proofsofaconspiracy/PROOFS_OF_A_CONSPIRACY_John_Robison.pdf); Project Gutenberg #47605 (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/47605), made from Internet Archive images.
- Shows: a respected scientist (Robison was professor of natural philosophy at Edinburgh and secretary of the Royal Society of Edinburgh) using the 1787 Bavarian publications, often loosely, to build a conspiracy case. Great for "how does a real fact become a big theory?"
- Band: 11-12 (full); 9-10 (excerpts).

**7. Augustin Barruel, *Memoirs, Illustrating the History of Jacobinism*, trans. Robert Clifford.** London, 1797-98, 4 vols. (French original *Mémoires pour servir à l'histoire du Jacobinisme*, 1797-98). Volume III treats the Illuminati.
- Where: Internet Archive: https://archive.org/details/BarruelMemoirsIllustratingTheHistoryOfJacobinism ; WorldCat: https://search.worldcat.org/oclc/519407209
- Shows: the founding text of the modern "one secret group behind the revolution" pattern. Barruel quotes the Bavarian papers at length, which is why Jefferson could reread the same quotes and reach the opposite view.
- Band: 11-12.

### C. The American scare, 1798-1800

**8. Jedidiah Morse, *A Sermon, Delivered at the New North Church in Boston, in the Morning, and in the Afternoon at Charlestown, May 9th, 1798, being the Day Recommended by John Adams, President of the United States of America, for Solemn Humiliation, Fasting and Prayer.*** Boston, 1798. Evans 34148 and 34149 (two impressions).
- Where: Early American Imprints, Series I (Evans), in university libraries; bibliographic record: https://www.grubstreetproject.net/publications/W29144/

- Band: 9-10.

**10. G. W. Snyder to George Washington, 22 August 1798, and Washington's replies (first reply dated 25 September 1798 in most editions, though one secondary source gives 10 October: verify; second reply 24 October 1798).**
- Where: Founders Online, *Papers of George Washington, Retirement Series*, vol. 2: https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Washington/06-02-02-0435 ; also printed in Julius F. Sachse, *Washington's Masonic Correspondence as Found among the Washington Papers in the Library of Congress* (Philadelphia, 1915), Project Gutenberg #29949: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/29949 ; originals in the George Washington Papers, Library of Congress.

**11. Thomas Jefferson to Bishop James Madison, 31 January 1800, Philadelphia.**
- Where: Founders Online, *Papers of Thomas Jefferson*, vol. 31: https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Jefferson/01-31-02-0297 ; Madison's thanks, 11 February 1800: https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Jefferson/01-31-02-0310

Bishop Madison called Jefferson's reading "the most satisfactory" analysis he had seen. A perfect paired text with Morse or Robison: same evidence, opposite conclusions.
- Band: 9-10.

**12. John Jay to Jedidiah Morse, 24 April 1800.** Founders Online: https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Jay/01-07-02-0039

### D. Rosicrucian and esoteric sources

### E. Symbol evidence

**19. Great Seal of the United States, first committee report, 20 August 1776, and 1782 final design.**
- Where: U.S. State Department, Office of the Historian, Great Seal booklet: https://diplomacy.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Great-Seal-PDF.pdf ; greatseal.com eye page: https://www.greatseal.com/symbols/eye.html
- Shows: the "Eye of Providence" was proposed in 1776 by consultant Pierre Eugène du Simitière to the first committee (Franklin, Adams, Jefferson) and adopted in 1782 on the reverse of the seal. It was a common Christian image for God watching over people, not an Illuminati mark.
- Band: 3-5 (symbols on money); K-2 (what a symbol is).

## Claims ledger

| # | Claim | Status | Evidence / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Illuminati were founded by Adam Weishaupt on 1 May 1776 at Ingolstadt. | SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS | Order's own papers (item 1); all modern scholarship. |
| 2 | The date 1 May 1776 links the order to the U.S. Declaration of Independence or to May Day socialism. | UNSUPPORTED | Coincidence of year only. No document links them. |
| 3 | Weishaupt had been trained by, or was secretly, a Jesuit. | DISPUTED / partly false | He was educated at a Jesuit school; he was not a Jesuit and opposed their influence at Ingolstadt. Knigge accused him of "Jesuitism" as an insult about his controlling style. |
| 4 | The Illuminati began inside Freemasonry. | DISPROVEN as stated | Founded separately; from about 1777-1780 it recruited through and inside Masonic lodges (SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS). |
| 5 | The order had thousands of members including Goethe and Herder. | DISPUTED in detail | Membership estimates run from a few hundred to about 2,000+. Goethe and Herder appear in membership records compiled by researchers (FactGrid); how active they were is debated. Check FactGrid before naming anyone. |
| 6 | The Minerval degree was named for Minerva; the owl was its emblem. | SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS (Minerva name); owl emblem widely reported, verify image | Minerva naming is in the statutes. For the owl, show a real period image before use. |
| 7 | The Illuminati planned the French Revolution. | UNSUPPORTED / DISPROVEN | The order was dissolved by 1785-87, four years before 1789. Barruel and Robison offered no document showing direction of events in France. Modern historians reject it. |
| 8 | A courier (Lanz) was struck by lightning in 1785 and carried papers that exposed the order. | DISPUTED | Lanz's death by lightning near Regensburg on 20 July 1785 is repeated in older German histories (e.g., Leopold Engel's 1906 history, Wikisource). The claim that decisive papers were found on his body is not supported by the 1787 official publications, which credit the Zwack house search. Teach as "a story people told," not a fact. |
| 9 | The government seized the papers in a house search and printed them. | DOCUMENTED | Title page of item 1 names the Landshut search, 11-12 October 1786, and the Elector's order. |
| 10 | Bavaria banned the group in 1784-85. | DOCUMENTED | Edicts of 22 June 1784 and 2 March 1785 (text not examined in this session; widely cited). |
| 11 | The order survived secretly and still runs the world. | UNSUPPORTED | No continuous documents after c. 1787. Weishaupt lived quietly at Gotha until 1830. Every modern "Illuminati" group claiming descent is a new creation. |
| 12 | Robison and Barruel wrote independently and reached the same conclusion. | SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS (roughly) | Written almost at the same time; Barruel said he saw Robison's book late in his work. Both drew on the same Bavarian publications. |
| 13 | The 1798 Illuminati scare in America was partly political. | SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS | Stauffer (1918) and later historians: Federalist clergy used it against Jefferson's Democratic-Republicans during the Quasi-War and the Alien and Sedition Acts year. |
| 14 | George Washington believed the Illuminati controlled American Masonic lodges. | DISPROVEN | His 24 October 1798 letter to Snyder says he did not believe American lodges, as societies, spread those ideas, though individuals might (text: verify against image). |
| 15 | Thomas Jefferson defended Weishaupt. | DOCUMENTED | Letter of 31 January 1800 (item 11). |
| 16 | The eye and pyramid on the dollar bill are Illuminati symbols. | DISPROVEN | Eye proposed 1776 by du Simitière; pyramid and eye adopted 1782 on the Great Seal reverse (Charles Thomson and William Barton). Put on the $1 bill in 1935. Neither designer is linked to the order. The "MDCCLXXVI" on the pyramid is 1776, the year of independence. |
| 17 | The "Novus Ordo Seclorum" motto means "New World Order." | DISPROVEN | It adapts Virgil (Eclogue IV); Thomson's 1782 notes explain it as the beginning of the new American era. Standard translation: "a new order of the ages." |

| 19 | The first *Principia Discordia* was run off on Jim Garrison's office Xerox machine. | DISPUTED | Told by Discordians and repeated in reference works; linked to Thornley's time in New Orleans. Details (1963 vs. 1965 edition, whose machine) vary. Treat as a story the authors told. |

| 22 | Modern Rosicrucian groups (AMORC, Rosicrucian Fellowship, etc.) descend from the 1614 brotherhood. | UNSUPPORTED as historical lineage | These are 19th-20th century foundations. They are real organizations with members; their spiritual claims are matters of belief, not documented history. Describe respectfully. |

| 26 | Aleister Crowley founded the Golden Dawn. | DISPROVEN | Founded 1888 by Westcott, Mathers and W. R. Woodman; Crowley joined in 1898. |

## Story hooks

1. **The professor's club, Ingolstadt, 1 May 1776.** A 28-year-old law professor, frustrated that his university still let church censors decide what students could read, meets a handful of students to start a club for "illumination." He picks the code name Spartacus, after a slave who rebelled. (Band 6-8.)
2. **The house search, Landshut, 11-12 October 1786.** Officers go through the house of a government official, Franz Xaver von Zwack ("Cato"). They find the club's rulebooks, code-name lists, and letters. Months later the government prints them all for anyone to buy. Question for students: is that fair? (Band 6-8.)
3. **Lightning on the road to Regensburg, 20 July 1785.** A priest walking with Weishaupt is killed by lightning. Later writers said secret papers were sewn into his clothes. The official record points to a house search instead. How do we tell a good story from a true one? (Band 6-8.)
4. **The minister's fast-day sermon, Boston, 9 May 1798.** It is a national day of fasting called by President Adams. Jedidiah Morse, the geography-book author, tells his congregation a foreign secret society is working in America. (Band 9-10.)
5. **A letter to Mount Vernon, August 1798.** A Lutheran minister in Maryland mails the retired President a Scottish book and a warning. Washington, a Freemason, writes back twice. (Band 6-8.)
6. **Jefferson reads the same book differently, Philadelphia, January 1800.** Jefferson reads Barruel's alarming quotes and decides Weishaupt was a sincere, if odd, reformer who hid because his government jailed free thinkers. Same evidence, opposite conclusion. (Band 9-10.)
7. **The tomb that opened after 120 years (Kassel, 1614).** A little pamphlet tells of brothers who find a hidden vault lit by an inner sun, with the unspoiled body of their founder, Christian Rosenkreutz. Across Europe, readers print open letters asking to join. No one answers. (Band 3-5 as a legend; 6-8 as history.)
8. **A coded notebook, London, 1887.** A London coroner, William Wynn Westcott, decodes an old cipher notebook that describes rituals, and a German address. Out of it comes a magical society that a future Nobel Prize poet (Yeats) will join. (Band 9-10.)
9. **The Chicago office full of strange letters, late 1960s.** Two editors at *Playboy* in Chicago sort mail from readers sure that secret groups run everything. Over a payday drink, one asks: what if they are all right at once? They start writing *Illuminatus!* (Band 9-10.)
10. **The Swedish Box.** A wooden crate of Illuminati papers travels from Gotha to Sweden, back to Germany, is seized by the Nazis, taken by the Soviet army, and mostly returned; one volume is still in Moscow. (Band 6-8.)

## Illinois & Chicago

Only connections I am confident of:

- **Robert Anton Wilson and Robert Shea at *Playboy*, Chicago (Wilson 1965-1971).** They co-edited "The Playboy Forum" letters page and began *Illuminatus!* in 1969, drawing on conspiracy letters sent to the magazine. Status: SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS (both authors' accounts; biographies). This makes Chicago the birthplace of the modern pop "Illuminati." Good 9-10 hook; the novel itself is 11-12 / adult.
- **Nothing found** tying the 18th-century Bavarian order or the 1798 scare to Illinois (Illinois was not yet a territory; it was part of the Northwest Territory).
- **Possible, UNVERIFIED:** a Golden Dawn-lineage temple (often named "Thme") operated in Chicago around 1897-1900s under Mathers' authority. I could not confirm this in this session. Do not use until checked (see Open questions).
- **Possible, UNVERIFIED:** Chicago libraries (Newberry Library; the University of Chicago's Special Collections) may hold 18th-century Illuminati imprints or Robison/Barruel American editions. Check WorldCat for Chicago holdings before claiming it; this would allow a field-trip lesson.

## Hard-to-find finds

1. **The 1787 *Originalschriften* as a digitized original (BSB / Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek).** Most "Illuminati" videos never show a single real document. This is the real rulebook and letters, printed by the police state that crushed the group. It lets students see both the order's idealism and why authorities were alarmed.
2. **The Schwedenkiste and the 150 unprinted member essays (Gotha / FactGrid / Geheimes Staatsarchiv).** A little-known archive with a thriller of a journey (Sweden, Nazis, Soviets, a missing volume in Moscow). The Gotha project shows members writing ordinary Enlightenment essays about ethics and economics, the most powerful antidote to the "world plot" story.
3. **Washington-Snyder correspondence, 1798 (Founders Online; Sachse 1915).** A retired President, himself a Mason, answering an Illuminati warning. It teaches that the scare was mainstream, and that Washington drew a careful line between "some people" and "the lodges."
4. **Jefferson to Bishop Madison, 31 January 1800.** Almost never used in schools. A founder reading a conspiracy book critically. Pairs perfectly with Morse's sermon of 1798 for a "same evidence, different reading" lesson.
5. **Morse's fast-day sermon (Evans 34148/34149) and Dwight's July 4 discourse.** Original pamphlets in the Early American Imprints series. Shows how a European book became an American pulpit message within a year.
6. **The 1614 Kassel *Fama* (Wellcome digitized copy; NY Masonic Library exhibit) and Vaughan's 1652 English edition (Internet Archive; Marsh's Library, Dublin).** Real first printings of the Rosicrucian legend, for comparing a legend with the paper it was printed on.
7. **The Golden Dawn fonds (AIM25 ref. GBR 1991 GD).** Original papers of a Victorian secret order, including (per standard histories) material around the Cipher Manuscripts, which were themselves a code puzzle. Links neatly to the K-5 "codes and ciphers" strand.
8. **The 1785/86 satirical cartoon (German History Intersections, image 56).** A contemporary picture of how Germans mocked the Illuminati at the time: a primary-source image for middle school.

## Suggested K-12 band map (from this dossier)

- **K-2:** What is a symbol? The Eye on the dollar bill and what it meant to the people who drew it (God watching over the new country). Code names (Weishaupt picked "Spartacus"): make a club name. Safe secrets (a surprise party) vs. unsafe secrets (someone asks you to hide being hurt): always tell a trusted adult.
- **3-5:** A simple substitution cipher like the Golden Dawn notebook used (make one; no ritual content). The Rosenkreutz tomb as a legend. Why do clubs have rules? Compare with the Minerval rules in plain words.
- **6-8:** The real Illuminati: a professor's club, a ban, a house search, a government that printed the secret papers. Media literacy: celebrities and "Illuminati" claims. Reading a 1785 cartoon.
- **9-10:** 1798 scare: Morse, Dwight, Washington, Jefferson. Robison as a scientist who got history wrong. Chicago and *Illuminatus!* as satire.
- **11-12:** Barruel and the birth of conspiracy thinking; how the legend was later merged with antisemitic forgeries; Golden Dawn and the Sprengel forgery debate; evaluating historians' disagreements.
