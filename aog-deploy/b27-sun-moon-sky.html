A shadow is a clock

Morning. The sun is low in the east, so the shadow stretches out long.

Short shadow means the sun is high, so it is near noon. Long shadow means morning or evening. The sky does the same thing every day, so you can read it.

Two things people get wrong

⚠️ People often think…

The stars go away during the day.

They are right where they always were. The sun’s light is so bright it washes them out, the way a flashlight beam hides a candle. Nothing moved.

The sun hides them. It does not send them away.

⚠️ People often think…

The moon changes shape — sometimes it is a sliver and sometimes it is round.

The moon is a round ball the whole time. Half of it is always lit. What changes is how much of the lit half we can see from here.

Always round. We just see less of the lit side.

Helpful Hints

🪜 Day, month, year — the ladder

The sun comes up in the east and goes down in the west, every day → the moon changes shape, sliver to full to sliver, every month → the stars come out when it’s dark and each season has its own → summer days are long, winter days are short, every year.

Sun for the day, moon for the month, seasons for the year.

🤝 The whole idea

The sky does the same things over and over. If you watch and count, you can tell what it will do next.

Watch, count, predict.

⚠️ Tricky ones

  • The sun does NOT move — Earth spins. It only looks like the sun moves.
  • The moon is always round. We just see different amounts of its lit side.
  • The stars are up in the day too. The sun’s light hides them.
  • The moon can be out in the day. The sun is never out at night.
  • Short shadow = noon. Long shadow = morning or evening.

🎯 How the test will ask

  • Pictures of the moon — “put them in order” (sliver, half, full).
  • A picture of a kid and a shadow — “what time is it?” (short = noon).
  • “Which season has the most daylight?” (summer).
  • “Where do the stars go in the day?” (nowhere — the sun hides them).
